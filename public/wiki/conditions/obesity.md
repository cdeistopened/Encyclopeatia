# Obesity

The conventional understanding of obesity—too many calories, too little exercise—misses the mechanism entirely. Ray Peat's framework identifies obesity as a metabolic disorder driven by [[PUFA|polyunsaturated fats]], [[estrogen]] dominance, and [[hypothyroidism]], not by caloric excess. Pig farmers discovered the essential principle decades ago: feeding polyunsaturated fats to animals caused rapid weight gain on less food. The same mechanism operates in humans, and the obesity epidemic tracks almost perfectly with the rise in seed oil consumption.

## The Thyroid Mechanism

[[Thyroid]] function is the master variable in body composition. When thyroid hormone is adequate, cells oxidize glucose efficiently, produce heat, and maintain structural integrity. When thyroid function declines—whether from PUFA exposure, estrogen dominance, or nutrient deficiency—metabolism slows, the body retains water, and fat accumulates.

As Peat noted in his email correspondence, the weight gain of hypothyroidism is not purely fat. "That sounds like standard hypothyroid symptoms—the body simply adjusts to holding more water, while losing sodium quickly." Much of what appears as obesity is actually edema: water retention driven by inadequate metabolic heat production. This explains why thyroid correction sometimes produces dramatic weight loss that cannot be accounted for by fat metabolism alone—the body releases the water it no longer needs to retain.

Peat described cases where correction worked in both directions. Men at 120-130 pounds who could not gain weight "restored muscle and fat with thyroid"—because their cells could finally use the nutrients they consumed. The underweight and the overweight often share the same underlying deficiency, manifesting differently based on the individual's hormonal milieu.

## PUFA: The Fattening Fat

Polyunsaturated fats promote obesity through mechanisms that go beyond their caloric content. Their primary action is metabolic suppression: PUFA blocks [[thyroid]] function at the gland, in transport, and at the cellular receptor level. With thyroid suppressed, metabolic rate drops, and the body burns fewer calories at rest—producing weight gain even without increased food intake.

The pig farming industry understood this empirically before the biochemistry was worked out. As Peat frequently noted, farmers found that feeding corn and soy oils to pigs produced rapid fattening at lower cost than traditional feeds. The fats did not just provide calories—they slowed the animals' metabolism so dramatically that less total food was needed to produce the same weight gain.

In humans, the effect compounds over decades. As PUFA accumulates in tissue (a process that takes approximately four years to fully reverse), each stress response liberates stored polyunsaturated fats into circulation, where they further suppress thyroid function and block glucose oxidation. As Peat explained, "Stored PUFA released by stress slow metabolism." The person diets, exercises, and restricts calories—all of which are stressors that mobilize stored PUFA—and finds that their metabolism slows further in response. The obesity worsens despite the effort.

## Estrogen and Fat Distribution

[[Estrogen]] drives fat accumulation specifically and strategically. It shifts metabolism from sugar-burning to fat-burning—which sounds beneficial until you understand the context. As Peat described in a [[2012-08-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-hot-flashes-night-sweats-the-relationship-to-stress-aging-pm|2012 episode on hot flashes and aging]], "Estrogen shifts metabolism from sugar to fat-burning as a starvation defense; it suppresses thyroid." The fat-burning state estrogen promotes is not the efficient fat oxidation of a healthy metabolism but the stress-driven mobilization pattern of an organism preparing for famine.

Fat cells themselves produce estrogen through the aromatase enzyme. More fat means more estrogen production, which means more fat storage—a self-amplifying cycle. PUFA makes this worse by increasing aromatase activity and by making fat cells more inflammatory, which further stimulates local estrogen production.

The distribution of fat reflects hormonal status. Abdominal fat accumulation—the "metabolic syndrome" pattern—corresponds to cortisol and estrogen dominance. Peripheral fat (hips, thighs) reflects a different hormonal pattern. The conventional approach of targeting body composition through caloric restriction addresses none of these hormonal drivers.

## Why Dieting Fails

Caloric restriction produces weight loss initially but activates the stress-hormone cascade that makes obesity self-perpetuating. When calories drop, blood sugar falls. [[Cortisol]] rises to maintain blood sugar through gluconeogenesis—breaking down muscle tissue while preserving fat stores (which the body treats as essential reserves for the perceived famine). [[Adrenaline]] mobilizes free fatty acids from adipose tissue—releasing stored PUFA that suppresses thyroid function.

The result is predictable: the body loses muscle, retains fat, and lowers its metabolic rate to match the reduced caloric intake. When normal eating resumes, the lowered metabolic rate ensures that calories that were previously maintenance-level now produce surplus—and the weight returns, typically with less muscle and more fat than before.

Exercise produces a similar paradox when the metabolic foundation is inadequate. Intense exercise is a stressor that mobilizes stored fatty acids, raises cortisol, and depletes glycogen. In a person with high tissue PUFA and marginal thyroid function, exercise releases thyroid-suppressing fats, breaks down muscle protein, and increases the stress hormones that promote fat storage. The harder they work, the worse the metabolic environment becomes.

## Body Composition Versus Scale Weight

Peat's framework distinguishes between meaningful changes in body composition and misleading changes in scale weight. In a [[2013-02-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-weight-gain|2013 episode]], he described people whose waists, thighs, and hips measured smaller even while their scale weight increased—because they were replacing water retention and fat with denser muscle tissue. Thyroid support specifically promotes this recomposition: muscle replaces water, structural protein replaces edema, and the body becomes denser and smaller while potentially weighing the same or more.

This means the common practice of judging dietary changes by scale weight can be actively misleading. A person beginning thyroid support or reducing PUFA intake might temporarily weigh more as their body shifts from the edematous, hypothyroid state to a denser, more metabolically active composition.

## The Metabolic Solution

Peat's approach to obesity addresses the metabolic suppression that causes it rather than the caloric surplus that results from it:

**PUFA elimination** removes the primary metabolic brake. As Peat noted, "Niacinamide lowers free fatty acids; coconut oil increases fat oxidation by stopping PUFA's antithyroid effect; aspirin reduces stress-related free fatty acids." Each of these substances works not by burning fat directly but by preventing PUFA from suppressing the metabolic machinery that burns fat naturally.

**[[Thyroid]] support** restores the metabolic rate that PUFA and estrogen have suppressed. Temperature and pulse monitoring guides dosing—the goal is waking temperature approaching 98.6°F with a resting pulse around 80-85, indicating efficient oxidative metabolism.

**Adequate [[calcium]]** from dairy—particularly low-fat milk and cheese—supports weight normalization. The calcium-to-phosphate ratio matters; dairy provides calcium in a form that opposes the parathyroid hormone elevation associated with fat accumulation.

**Frequent feeding with sugar and protein** prevents the stress-hormone cascade that mobilizes PUFA and breaks down muscle. Orange juice, milk, eggs, and fruit maintain blood sugar without requiring cortisol-driven gluconeogenesis.

**[[Coconut oil]]** provides medium-chain saturated fats that the body burns immediately, helping displace stored PUFA while directly boosting metabolic rate through thyroid-independent mechanisms.

**Raw carrot salad** reduces intestinal reabsorption of estrogen, lowering the estrogen-driven fat storage signal.

**[[Progesterone]]** opposes estrogen's fat-promoting effects and supports thyroid function. In women, adequate progesterone during the luteal phase prevents the estrogen-dominant state that promotes fat storage.

The goal is not caloric restriction but metabolic restoration—creating conditions where the body naturally maintains appropriate composition because its energy systems are functioning efficiently. A person with adequate thyroid, low tissue PUFA, and balanced hormones does not need to restrict calories to maintain a healthy body composition, any more than a healthy child does.
