# Psoriasis

Dermatology counts psoriasis as one of the model autoimmune diseases. T-cells lose tolerance, IL-17 and IL-23 cytokines drive keratinocyte hyperproliferation, plaques with the silvery scale form at extensor surfaces and scalp, HLA-Cw6 confers susceptibility, and roughly thirty percent of patients develop joint involvement (psoriatic arthritis) that can erode small joints irreversibly. The therapeutic ladder runs from topical steroids and vitamin D analogs to phototherapy, then methotrexate and cyclosporine, then the biologic era — TNF, IL-17, and IL-23 inhibitors at $40,000 to $80,000 per year per patient. The framing is auto-aggressive immunity in a genetically predisposed host; the intervention is permanent immune suppression aimed at the most recently identified cytokine.

[[Ray Peat|Peat]] read the disease in opposite order. The cytokine cascade is what tissue does when stuck in a chronically inflamed, energetically failing state, not a primary aggression. The hyperproliferative keratinocyte — a cell that has dropped its twenty-eight-day cycle to three or four days and sheds prematurely as the silvery scale — is doing exactly what any vitamin-A-starved, serotonin-saturated, prostaglandin-pressured cell does. The HLA-Cw6 allele is a susceptibility factor that determines which tissue expresses the underlying metabolic crisis, not the cause of the crisis.

The clinical signature pointing back at the metabolic frame is the gut-skin axis. Psoriasis improves dramatically in summer sun, worsens with stress and infection, flares with alcohol and seed-oil-heavy diets, remits during pregnancy in roughly half of patients, and shares strong epidemiologic overlap with inflammatory bowel disease, celiac disease, and metabolic syndrome. None of these patterns fits a primary T-cell aggression model. All of them fit a model in which the gut is leaking [[endotoxin]] into the portal circulation, [[serotonin]] is elevated, [[arachidonic-acid|arachidonic acid]] from years of PUFA accumulation is being released into inflamed tissue, [[cortisol]] is chronically up but functionally insufficient, and the skin — a tissue that turns over rapidly under any of these signals — is expressing the systemic state.

Peat said this plainly on a 2011 *Ask Your Herb Doctor* episode when a caller asked about eczema and leaky gut. "Celiac disease is one of the causes of both skin disease and arthritis. Celiac disease will allow endotoxin into your circulation. Low thyroid is probably the most common thing associated with ordinary eczema. When your thyroid is low, the circulation in your intestine is poor, serotonin is high, and the combination of endotoxin and serotonin will cause the skin to have abnormal growth patterns." When Sarah Murray asked whether psoriasis fell into the same category, Peat said simply, "I think so." On a 2015 episode he widened the frame: "Swelling has an anabolic effect and turns on cell division. Uncontrollable cell division can produce everything from cancer to various skin diseases like dandruff, psoriasis, or an overgrowth of fibrous tissue. The control of cell growth is deeply controlled by water and pH." Keratinocyte hyperproliferation, by another name. The same metabolic crisis in another tissue would be called atherosclerosis or carcinoma; in the dermis it is called psoriasis.

## What's happening: the four-day cycle

Healthy epidermal turnover takes about twenty-eight days. A basal keratinocyte divides, the daughter cell migrates upward through the spinous and granular layers, synthesizes keratin, loses its nucleus, flattens, and is shed from the stratum corneum. In psoriatic plaques, the cycle compresses to roughly three to seven days. Immature keratinocytes pile up before they can lose their nuclei or finish keratinization. The result is the parakeratotic scale (cells with retained nuclei where there should be none), the suprapapillary thinning, the Munro microabscesses of neutrophils in the stratum corneum, and the dilated capillaries that bleed pinpoint when the scale is scratched off (Auspitz sign).

Mainstream dermatology reads the proliferation as T-cell-driven and treats the IL-17/IL-23/TNF cytokine cascade as causal — the biologics era confirms that interrupting any link clears most plaques in most patients within weeks. Peat reframed the cascade without denying its reality. T-cells and cytokines are downstream amplifiers; suppressing them clears the plaque the way suppressing fever clears the temperature reading. This is why psoriasis returns when biologics are withdrawn, why secondary biologic failure is common, and why methotrexate (a non-specific DNA-synthesis blocker) "works" without targeting any specific immune mechanism. The drugs block the symptom; the metabolic state underneath is unchanged.

What controls keratinocyte differentiation in a healthy state? In Peat's framework, the same factors that control any cell's differentiation: [[vitamin-a|vitamin A]], [[thyroid|thyroid hormone]], [[progesterone]], and adequate intracellular acidity from oxidative metabolism. Strip any of these and the cell defaults to division and keratin production — the cellular surrender Peat identified in *Blocking Tissue Destruction*: "Keratinization is what cells do when they can't afford to do anything more complex."

## The gut-skin axis

The strongest clinical lever Peat identified was the gut. The skin and the intestinal lining share embryologic origin (both derive from ectoderm and endoderm interactions that produce barrier epithelia), and they share signaling chemistry. "The gut and the skin have some antigens and enzymes that are so closely connected that when one is inflamed, the other gets inflamed," Peat told Andrew Murray in a 2016 episode. "Thyroid and Vitamin D are the most common solutions to hypersensitivity of the gut and skin."

The mechanism chains in one direction. Hypothyroid metabolism slows intestinal transit. Slow transit and a high-fiber diet feed gram-negative bacteria in the small intestine (SIBO) and lower bowel. These bacteria continuously release lipopolysaccharide (LPS, endotoxin). LPS leaks across an intestinal barrier weakened by chronic inflammation, low [[vitamin-a]] (necessary for tight-junction maintenance), and gluten-derived zonulin signaling. The portal blood carries LPS to the liver, where Kupffer cells generate TNF-α, IL-6, and nitric oxide. Serotonin synthesis from tryptophan in the gut wall is upregulated by inflammation; 95% of body serotonin is gut-derived and acts as a proinflammatory amplifier in distant tissues. Cortisol rises to suppress the inflammatory state but breaks down skin collagen and amplifies [[estrogen]]-driven keratinocyte proliferation. Skin tissue receives the signal: serotonin telling it to divide, prostaglandins (from arachidonic acid released from inflamed membranes) telling it to inflame, cortisol-resistance leaving it without the brake.

This sequence explains the epidemiologic pattern. Celiac disease coincides with psoriasis at roughly 2–4× the population rate, IBD at 7–10×, and roughly 60% of psoriasis patients show SIBO on lactulose breath testing. The gluten-free diet anecdotes ubiquitous in patient forums are not placebo — gluten withdrawal lowers zonulin signaling and the endotoxin load reaching the liver. The same skin disease that responds to a $60,000 biologic responds in a substantial minority of cases to removing one food.

## Peat's causal cluster

**Vitamin A deficiency, often functional rather than dietary.** Vitamin A keeps cells out of the proliferative-immature default state. "A deficiency tends to cause an increased rate of cell division, with the production of abnormal cells, and a substitution of keratinized cells for other types," Peat wrote in [[blocking-tissue-destruction|*Blocking Tissue Destruction*]]. A psoriatic plaque is the textbook expression of that paragraph. The deficiency is often functional — PUFA displaces vitamin A from its transthyretin carrier, hypothyroidism slows carotene-to-retinol conversion, and chronic stress depletes the steroid pipeline vitamin A feeds. Full mechanism at [[vitamin-a]].

**Hypothyroidism, often subclinical.** Low thyroid slows intestinal circulation, raises serotonin, and creates the metabolic state in which skin grows abnormally. Most psoriasis patients run cold, sluggish, constipated, and tired without being told their thyroid is the issue — subclinical hypothyroidism is invisible to TSH-based screening but appears clearly on basal temperature, pulse, and Achilles tendon reflex. Full hub at [[hypothyroidism]].

**Serotonin amplification.** Gut serotonin rises with SIBO and intestinal inflammation; systemic load rises with chronic stress and cortisol-driven muscle catabolism releasing tryptophan. Serotonin promotes keratinocyte proliferation directly through skin 5-HT receptors, amplifies the prostaglandin cascade, and drives the itch that triggers scratching and the Koebner phenomenon (psoriasis appearing at sites of skin trauma). Full mechanism at [[serotonin]].

**Endotoxin from gut dysbiosis.** The chronic LPS load sits behind the systemic inflammation, traveling through Kupffer-cell TNF-α and IL-6 to skin tissue where it amplifies the proliferative response. Daily endotoxin reduction ([[endotoxin-reduction|carrot salad and bowel-transit work]]) is the slow upstream lever; antibiotic decontamination is the fast one for acute flares.

**Cortisol elevation with functional insufficiency.** Chronic stress drives cortisol up, but the protective steroids upstream (pregnenolone, progesterone, DHEA) deplete first, leaving the tissue with the catabolic side of the cortisol profile without enough of the anti-inflammatory side. This is also why topical steroid clearance is fast but recurrence is fast — the steroid suppresses the symptom without correcting the steroid-pool depletion underneath. Full mechanism at [[cortisol]].

**PUFA accumulation.** Decades of dietary linoleic acid accumulate in membranes. Under inflammatory signals, [[arachidonic-acid|arachidonic acid]] is released by phospholipase A2 and converted by COX-2 to the prostaglandin series that amplifies inflammation and keratinocyte proliferation, plus the leukotriene B4 chemotaxis that brings neutrophils into the plaque. The mainstream prescription of fish oil or evening primrose oil adds more polyunsaturated substrate to a system already overloaded with it. Full mechanism at [[pufa]].

**Estrogen-progesterone imbalance.** "Vitamin A and estrogen are antagonistic, and while estrogen promotes keratinization (shedding of skin cells), vitamin A opposes it," Peat wrote in one [[vitamin-a|email]]. The same chemistry drives the dramatic clearance during pregnancy (massive placental progesterone) and the menopausal worsening (progesterone collapse), and explains the response to topical progesterone described in Peat's 1984 patent.

## Peat's protocol

The interventions cluster around five upstream levers. Each carries to its own vault page for dosing detail. The protocol is a clinical pattern, not a one-size prescription; severe disease and psoriatic arthritis require dermatology coordination (see the safety note below).

**Vitamin A in clinical doses.** 25,000–50,000 IU daily of preformed retinol (retinyl palmitate or halibut liver oil — never cod liver oil because of its PUFA content), with short-term ramps to 100,000 IU under guidance for severe presentations. Vitamin E (100–400 IU mixed tocopherols) co-supplemented to protect against oxidative loss. Liver once or twice weekly as the food anchor. Effects are twofold: vitamin A slows keratinocyte turnover by restoring differentiation, and supplies the cofactor for the pregnenolone-progesterone-DHEA pipeline that opposes estrogen and cortisol. Caution: contraindicated in pregnancy, suppresses thyroid in excess (must be balanced with thyroid replacement), fat-soluble toxicity is real over months without monitoring. Full case at [[vitamin-a]].

**Thyroid replacement when hypothyroid.** Desiccated thyroid (Armour, Acella, NP Thyroid, or compounded) preferred over T4 monotherapy, dosed to a normal pulse (75–90 at rest), basal temperature (98°F oral on waking, 98.6°F mid-afternoon), and resolution of cold extremities. Thyroid normalizes intestinal transit (the upstream lever for endotoxin), restores carotene-to-retinol conversion, and lowers serotonin output. Full protocol at [[hypothyroidism]] and [[thyroid-dosing]].

**Sun exposure — natural UVB, not artificial phototherapy.** Roughly 20–40 minutes of midday sun on as much skin as the season permits, built up gradually. Sun acts through multiple mechanisms: UVB stimulates vitamin D synthesis, red and near-infrared wavelengths penetrate deeper to stimulate mitochondrial cytochrome-c-oxidase activity, local heat improves circulation, and broad-spectrum daylight resets the cortisol-melatonin diurnal cycle. Peat distinguished sharply between natural sunlight (anti-inflammatory across multiple mechanisms) and narrowband UVB phototherapy boxes ("a controlled burn" that ages skin and raises cancer risk over time). The Dead Sea remission effect is the natural-spectrum case, not the medical-UVB case.

**Daily aspirin.** 325–650 mg daily with food, balanced by vitamin K2 (≈1 mg per 325 mg aspirin). Aspirin works on every arm of the cascade simultaneously: COX inhibition lowers prostaglandin production, aromatase inhibition lowers tissue estrogen synthesis, mitochondrial uncoupling shifts metabolism toward oxidative respiration, and direct anti-serotonin effects support the gut work. Full pharmacology at [[aspirin]].

**Endotoxin reduction.** Raw carrot salad once daily after the first meal (one medium carrot, julienned lengthwise, with olive or coconut oil, vinegar, salt), well-cooked bamboo shoots and mushrooms on rotation, one to two formed bowel movements daily, magnesium glycinate 300–600 mg/day for transit, gelatin or bone broth at 10–30 g/day. Severe-flare additions: short-course low-dose tetracycline or rifaximin under clinician oversight, activated charcoal 1–2 g between meals during acute flares. Full protocol at [[endotoxin-reduction]].

**Cyproheptadine when serotonin signs predominate.** 1–4 mg at bedtime. Indicated when the pattern includes severe itch, IBS, migraine, insomnia, anxiety, or clear stress-correlated flares. The antiserotonin effect is what does the work; the antihistamine sedation is the side effect. Full protocol at [[cyproheptadine]].

**PUFA elimination — the long arc.** No seed oils, no nuts in quantity, no fish oil, no flax. Cooking fats limited to butter, coconut oil, olive oil (moderation), beef tallow. PUFA half-life in adipose is roughly two years; significant tissue clearance takes three to five years. The slowest and most important lever for long-term disease modification. Full case at [[pufa-depletion]] and [[pufa]].

**Topical progesterone and tocopherol — the 1984 patent route.** Peat's [[ray-peat-patent-1984|US Patent 4,507,290]] explicitly named psoriasis as an indication for his progesterone-tocopherol composition: 10–20% progesterone solubilized in 80–90% mixed-tocopherol vitamin E. Applied to plaques, the progesterone opposes local estrogen-driven keratinization, the tocopherol protects PUFA from peroxidation. This is the direct ancestor of the commercial Progest-E formulation. For acute localized flares, crushed aspirin dissolved in vodka or water and dabbed on plaques delivers COX inhibition directly; coconut oil with mixed tocopherols soothes the scale and provides antimicrobial medium-chain fatty acids.

## Psoriatic arthritis overlap

Roughly thirty percent of psoriasis patients develop psoriatic arthritis — joint involvement that can erode small joints irreversibly if untreated. In Peat's framework, this is not a separate disease but the same biology expressed in synovial rather than cutaneous tissue. The 1986 patent he held on the topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol formulation explicitly covered "arthritis associated with psoriasis" alongside RA, OA, and lupus arthritis — not as an afterthought but as recognition that the underlying mechanism is shared. The full protocol for the arthritic component (aspirin, thyroid, gelatin, progesterone-DHEA topical, PUFA elimination, cyproheptadine, magnesium-niacinamide) is documented at [[arthritis-protocol]]. The skin and joint components respond to the same upstream interventions because they are the same disease.

## What to avoid

- **Long-term topical steroids** beyond brief flare control. They thin skin, cause rebound flares on withdrawal, and suppress the cortisol axis without correcting the steroid-pool depletion underneath.
- **Methotrexate when alternatives exist.** Folate antagonism is broadly cytotoxic, depletes the methyl donors the liver needs for detoxification, and accumulates hepatotoxicity and cytopenias. Reserve for severe disease that has not responded to the upstream protocol.
- **Biologics (TNF, IL-17, IL-23 inhibitors) for mild-to-moderate disease.** Permanent single-cytokine suppression at $40,000–$80,000/year, increased serious-infection risk (especially TB reactivation), demyelinating events with TNF inhibitors, secondary failure from anti-drug antibodies. Reasonable for severe recalcitrant disease; not reasonable as first-line for moderate plaque psoriasis.
- **Fish oil and EPA/DHA.** Marketed on the "anti-inflammatory omega-3" framing; in Peat's reading these add polyunsaturated substrate to a tissue already overloaded.
- **Soy and isoflavone-heavy plant proteins.** Phytoestrogen load adds to the estrogenic pressure on keratinocyte proliferation.
- **Alcohol, especially beer.** Wheat-derived gluten plus direct hepatotoxic load worsens the gut-leak axis and the liver's capacity to clear estrogen and endotoxin.
- **High-dose oral vitamin D without sun.** Sunlight delivers the entire spectrum including the anti-inflammatory red/near-infrared wavelengths. When oral D is used, D3 only, with vitamin K2 co-supplementation.

## Tracking signal

The protocol generates measurable signal within weeks if the metabolic frame is correct.

- **Plaque thickness and silvery scale** should reduce within two to four weeks of vitamin A loading plus thyroid optimization plus daily sun. Photograph monthly; the eye accommodates to gradual change.
- **Itch intensity** falls quickly with cyproheptadine and with endotoxin reduction. A 0–10 daily score captures this better than the dermatologist's quarterly PASI score.
- **PASI (Psoriasis Area and Severity Index)** is the dermatology standard; a 50–75% reduction at three months is the conventional response threshold. Patients on the upstream protocol often hit this without any topical or systemic immunosuppressant.
- **Basal temperature and pulse rate** are the upstream metric. If these are not rising into the 98°F / 75–90 bpm range during the protocol, the thyroid arm is undertreated.
- **Bowel transit** — one to two formed bowel movements daily within thirty days indicates the gut-skin axis is opening. Persistent constipation is the most common reason the protocol stalls.
- **Carotenemic palms** receding indicates carotene-to-retinol conversion is normalizing — a signal that thyroid and vitamin A are now balanced.
- **Joint involvement** (psoriatic arthritis) often resolves on the same protocol within two to three months. New joint involvement during the protocol is a signal to add the arthritis-specific elements (gelatin, magnesium-niacinamide, topical DHEA-progesterone).

## Counter-arguments

The biologic era has fundamentally changed mainstream psoriasis treatment. IL-17 and IL-23 inhibitors clear 75–90% of plaque area in 70–90% of patients within twelve to sixteen weeks, with PASI-90 responder rates running 60–80% — clearance the Peat-era protocol almost certainly does not match for severe disease on the same timeline. A patient with severe recalcitrant plaques and psoriatic arthritis already eroding joints will get faster and more dramatic clearance on a biologic than on aspirin, vitamin A, and carrot salad. The mainstream framing has produced effective drugs because the cytokine cascade is real and pharmacologically accessible.

Peat's reading places the drugs differently on the decision tree, not in opposition to them. The biologic suppresses the symptom rapidly, at high cost, with real long-term risks (serious infection, demyelinating events with TNF inhibitors, anti-drug antibody formation), and without correcting the underlying state. The upstream protocol corrects the underlying state slowly, at low cost, with low risk, and with broader benefit — the gut-thyroid-PUFA-serotonin work also addresses the comorbidities (metabolic syndrome, IBD, depression, cardiovascular disease) that account for most of the excess mortality in psoriasis patients. Reasonable approach for moderate disease: upstream protocol first, biologics reserved for severe or recalcitrant cases that have failed it. Reasonable approach for severe disease with joint erosion: biologic to halt joint damage while building the upstream protocol underneath, with eventual taper as the goal.

The HLA-Cw6 association is real (~60% of early-onset patients carry the allele vs. 15% of controls). The mainstream reading is genetic predetermination of autoimmune susceptibility; the Peat-compatible reading is that HLA-Cw6 determines which tissue expresses the underlying metabolic crisis. This framing keeps the upstream levers (food, sun, sleep, thyroid, gut) visible and prevents the patient from being told the disease is genetically predetermined and lifelong immunosuppression is the only path.

## Data gaps

- No RCT has tested the full Peat protocol (vitamin A + thyroid + sun + aspirin + endotoxin reduction + PUFA elimination) against biologic therapy. Anecdotal case reports and clinical-pattern evidence are the available data.
- Serotonin-driven keratinocyte proliferation is established mechanistically for 5-HT2A and 5-HT7 skin receptors, but the relative contribution to psoriasis specifically is not quantified.
- The HLA-Cw6 mechanism is unresolved on both readings. The mainstream story has no identified auto-antigen; the metabolic-expression story has no clear molecular bridge.
- Modern dosing data for natural-form retinol in severe psoriasis is sparse — the 1970s–80s clinical interest in oral retinoids shifted entirely to pharmaceutical analogs (acitretin, isotretinoin) with different risk profiles.
- The gut microbiome-psoriasis literature is rich but mostly correlational. Causal studies (germ-free animal models, fecal transplant) are early.
- Long-term safety data for the integrated protocol over 10–20 years is absent, though individual components have decades of safety records.

## Safety

Severe psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis warrant dermatology and rheumatology coordination — joint erosion is irreversible, and the timeline on which the upstream protocol works may not be fast enough to halt active joint damage. Vitamin A above 25,000 IU daily, sustained over months, requires monitoring (liver enzymes, calcium, mood changes) and is contraindicated in pregnancy. Thyroid replacement requires clinician oversight to avoid hyperthyroid overshoot. Aspirin requires food and vitamin K2 co-supplementation; gastrointestinal bleeding risk is real at sustained doses, particularly in patients on SSRIs or other antiplatelet agents. Patients on biologics or methotrexate considering taper or withdrawal must do so under dermatology supervision — rebound flares can be severe (including erythrodermic and pustular psoriasis, both medical emergencies). The Peat protocol is best implemented as the long-term substrate beneath conventional treatment, not as a single-step substitution.
