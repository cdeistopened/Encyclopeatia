# Cold and Flu

Ray Peat almost never wrote a stand-alone essay on the common cold. The material is scattered — buried in the 1988 [[natural-immunity-and-viral-infections|*Natural Immunity and Viral Infections*]] newsletter, in the 1989 [[repairing-immunodeficiencies|*Repairing Immunodeficiencies*]] piece, in the 1993 [[the-bean-syndrome|*Bean Syndrome*]] newsletter where he interrupts a discussion of intestinal toxins to note that "flu symptoms" are really bowel symptoms, and in the dense run of *Ask the Herb Doctor* and *Politics and Science* episodes from the first two years of the COVID-19 pandemic. The scatter is itself revealing. Peat did not think of "the common cold" as a disease entity. He thought of it as one expression — among many — of a metabolic state in which the body has dropped below the energetic threshold required to suppress the viruses it has been carrying all year.

"If your cells are already in a weakened, excitable state, they are the most susceptible to viral infection. If the cell is stable, even if it's infected by a virus, it will turn off the process rather than letting the virus propagate" — from the [[2021-09-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-september-2021-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, September 2021]]. The cold is the cellular state. The virus is the opportunist.

## The inflammation is the pathogen

The orthodox model treats a cold or flu as an infection: virus enters, replicates, symptoms are the body's reaction. Peat inverted the causality. "Inflammation is a pathogen in any kind of sickness, especially in this virus infection. The symptoms are basically inflammatory. The angiotensin system and the interferon system turn on other inflammatory agents like histamine. All of the defensive reactions triggered by these respiratory infections amplify inflammation, causing congestion, constriction, swelling, and exudation of serum into the lungs" — from the *Politics and Science*, March 24, 2020.

The claim is more biophysical than it sounds. A virus needs a specific cellular environment to assemble. "The highest energy states of the cell tend to exclude water soluble substances, and to absorb oil soluble materials. The components of a virus have very specific affinities for water and oil, and they can be assembled only in a very special solvent environment" — from [[natural-immunity-and-viral-infections|*Natural Immunity and Viral Infections*]] (1988). A high-energy, well-respired cell is hostile to viral assembly. A low-energy cell — [[pufa]]-loaded, hypothyroid, hypoglycemic, flooded with [[cortisol]] and [[serotonin]] — is permissive.

What we call "the flu" is the body's inflammatory cascade reacting to that permissiveness: [[surfactant-protein]], [[ace2]] depletion, [[angiotensin]] rising unopposed, [[nitric-oxide]] flooding tissues, [[serotonin]] and [[histamine]] from gut and mast cells, [[prostaglandins]] driving fever and aches. Peat's clinical interventions target this cascade directly. See [[inflammation]] for the underlying pathology.

## The gut-to-lung axis: why "respiratory" viruses are really intestinal

One of Peat's most consistent and least-quoted positions is that what we call a "respiratory" infection usually begins in the bowel. The argument traces back to Walter Alvarez, the early-twentieth-century gastroenterologist whose dog-distemper experiments Peat cited repeatedly.

"I think there is a great similarity between the variety of viruses that like to attack the intestine. Things we think of as respiratory viruses, I think, are primarily intestinal viruses. The famous gastroenterologist Walter Alvarez experimented on dogs about 80 years ago. When he gave them a respiratory virus, he found they would get a runny nose and inflammation in their head without any virus present in their nose. He found the virus was growing in their intestine and producing symptoms in the upper respiratory system" — from the [[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2016]]. He reinforced the point in the *Politics and Science*, March 18, 2020: "An early 20th-century gastro doctor demonstrated that in canine distemper — thought of as a respiratory disease — there was already a well-established infection in the intestine before any virus occurred in the respiratory membranes. They would start drooling and sniffling because of a signal from an inflamed intestine."

The systemic case is in [[the-bean-syndrome|*The Bean Syndrome*]] (1993): "Injury to the bowel causes 'flu-like' symptoms. There have been reports that the viruses of 'respiratory diseases' appear in the intestine before they appear in the respiratory tract... Since the bowel becomes inflamed in influenza, it is reasonable to think that some of the symptoms of 'the flu' are produced by absorbed bowel toxins."

The mechanism converges on [[endotoxin]]. Endotoxin and viral inflammation activate the same angiotensin–serotonin–prostaglandin pathway, so the body has no clean way to distinguish them. Peat wrote in [[natural-immunity-and-viral-infections|*Natural Immunity and Viral Infections*]] (1988): "Unopposed [cortisol] excess causes destruction of these enzymes, eliminating much of the intestine's barrier function, and leading to allergies. This action of cortisol against the thymus and against the bowel's detoxifying enzymes very likely accounts for the common association of allergies with virus infections."

This is why Peat's first-line cold intervention is intestinal, not pulmonary. "What seems to be a nose cold is often irritation in the intestine. I've seen people who learned that at the first sign of a cold — sore throat or runny nose — if they ate a carrot, they could break the pattern and not develop the cold" — from the [[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2016]]. The raw [[carrot-salad|carrot]] scrubs the bowel of endotoxin-producing bacteria and their secretions, interrupting the loop before it amplifies into systemic illness.

## Winter sickness: the bioenergetic origin

The single most vivid passage Peat ever wrote on this subject is autobiographical. From [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] (p. ~82):

> In 1983, the ocean current called El Nino caused Oregon's weather to stay cloudy and rainy through the summer. I didn't have my usual summer recovery from "winter sickness," and by November I was becoming very sick. During a two week trip I felt perfectly well, but except for that short time I kept getting sicker and sicker (with inflammation of arteries, dental abscesses, asthma, migraines, and colitis). Lying in bed one day trying to understand what had happened during that two-week trip, I remembered that I had used the vitamin E containing a residue of [[pregnenolone]] on the trip, but used pure vitamin E after I got home. I immediately got out of bed and took a pinch of pregnenolone. In about an hour, all of my symptoms had stopped, and for over a week I was healthy and energetic.

This is the founding story for what Peat called *winter sickness* — and his definition of it bears restating, because it is far broader than any flu virus. From [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] again: "When stress reduces circulation to the bowel, causing injury to the barrier function of the intestinal cells, endotoxin can enter the blood, contributing to a shock state, with further impairment of circulation. In old age and in 'winter sickness,' something like a chronic borderline state of shock can develop." Winter sickness is a chronic-borderline-shock state mediated by endotoxin and depleted protective steroids — the body permissive to viral infection, bacterial superinfection, allergy, asthma, depression, and the seasonal complaints mainstream medicine treats as separate conditions.

The seasonal pattern tracks closely with [[vitamin-d]] status. "The reason there is a flu season from winter until late spring is that in temperate zones, sun exposure goes down. Most people become Vitamin D deficient and extremely susceptible to infection. By summer, Vitamin D rises, and viral immunity rises strongly, ending the flu season" — from the [[2020-03-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-coronavirus|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 20, 2020]]. [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] put the underlying mechanism in one line: "In the winter and at night the respiratory energy producing system is damaged, and the protective hormones decline, and the harmful stress hormones increase. The immune system becomes less active, and mortality increases."

## The bioenergetic theory of immunity

Peat's framework for immunity is in [[repairing-immunodeficiencies]] and the wider [[oxidative-metabolism]] page — the steroidal hierarchy ([[cholesterol]] → [[pregnenolone]] → [[progesterone]]/[[dhea]]), the [[thyroid]] master variable, the [[estrogen]]/cortisol immunosuppressive axis, the antibody-vs-cellular-immunity inversion. The cold/flu-specific implication is one sentence from [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]]: "Infectious diseases, then, are not so different from the degenerative diseases, since the failure of biological energy, and the resulting stress response, can be responsible for both classes of sickness." Infection and degeneration are the same physiology — both rooted in failed bioenergetic respiration.

One clinical sign worth pulling forward: chronic infection presents as low temperature, not fever. "Anything that intensifies metabolism tends to be an immune stimulant, other things being equal. A subnormal temperature is a common feature of chronic infection and early cancer, until a late stage of intense catabolism with fever" — from [[repairing-immunodeficiencies|*Repairing Immunodeficiencies*]] (1989). The body that runs cold is the body permissive to whatever virus it carries.

## Natural killer cells, not antibodies

Peat rejected the antibody-centric model of immunity as a marketing artifact. "The antibody theory came about more than 100 years ago with Paul Ehrlich's 'lock and key' idea, which was a way to sell chemical drugs. The antibody became the model for a specific drug cure. However, there is an alternative view gaining recognition: the Danger Theory (Polly Matzinger) or Damage Theory (Jamie Cunliffe). In this view, the immune system isn't on the outlook for 'pathogens.' The organism has a picture of itself that it wants to preserve. When it experiences damage, it recognizes that part of itself is out of place" — from the *Politics and Science*, March 24, 2020.

The operational point is in the [[2021-03-19-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-march-2021-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2021]]: "Within about three days of a viral infection, [natural killer cells] will go to work to kill the cells damaged by the virus. They don't give their attention to killing a virus, but to getting rid of the cells that are being used to multiply the virus. That's why 99.6% of people not only survive, but most don't even know they're sick. At the very earliest stage, the natural killer cells are knocking out the cells that would be multiplying the virus." Same episode names the two failure modes: "A Vitamin D deficiency or an estrogen excess lowers the function of the natural killer cells." The same two factors that drive winter sickness drive the failure of the cellular layer that should be silently clearing infections before symptoms appear.

Antibodies, by Peat's account, can even mislead. "Antibodies against specific tissues are probably part of a normal process to take care of damaged cells... I decided that 'auto-immune' antibodies were useful to indicate which organ was under stress, but shouldn't be taken as a sign of an 'immunological disease'" — from [[failures-of-immunity|*Failures of Immunity*]] (1992). Rising antibody titers in the face of obvious chronic illness indicate tissue damage and stress — not effective immunity.

## Aspirin: the "super antiviral"

For aspirin's full mechanism — COX inhibition, the mitochondrial uncoupling, the anti-aromatase effect, the Mead-acid hypothesis — see [[aspirin]]. This section covers only its application to cold and flu, where Peat treated it as the central tool.

The framing he kept returning to was economic, not pharmacological: "If [aspirin] were under patent, it would be considered a 'super antiviral' because it is active against a wide variety, including influenza, herpes, rhinovirus, and hepatitis" — [[2020-03-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-coronavirus|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 20, 2020]]. The same episode names ACE2/AT1 as the specific cold/flu mechanism: aspirin raises ACE2 (the anti-inflammatory enzyme the virus depletes) and lowers AT1 receptor expression, the same therapeutic direction as the prescription [[losartan]]. Peat's working hypothesis was that "all of our known protective, pro-immune but anti-inflammatory substances lower the expression of angiotensin receptor 1" — vitamin D, progesterone, and aspirin all converge on the same protective state (*Politics and Science*, March 18, 2020).

The clinical evidence runs back decades. From an [[2011-12-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-water-retention-and-salt|earlier 2011 episode]]: "Aspirin has been found to have a mild antiviral effect against several types of virus. Someone with AIDS sent me an old publication showing they had begun a trial of aspirin for HIV-infected people and were getting good results. It has been proven somewhat effective for several different kinds of virus, including the herpes/chickenpox type." COVID concentrated the question; in the [[2020-05-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-may-2020-kmud|May 2020 KMUD episode]] he said it plainly: "Aspirin is also not only anti-inflammatory but has well-established broad-spectrum antiviral activity."

**Dosing for cold and flu** (acute, not maintenance):

- Maintenance baseline: ~300 mg/day USP crystals, dissolved in warm water, taken with food — Peat's personal dose (*One Radio Network*, October 2019).
- Acute illness: 300–500 mg every 4–6 hours; the bedtime dose has particular value because it interrupts the overnight inflammatory cycle ([[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|*Ask the Herb Doctor* Allergy, March 2016]]).
- Cancer / severe inflammation: "People using it with cancer usually take a daily total of 6 grams or more" ([[aspirin|aspirin email]]).
- [[vitamin-k|Vitamin K2]]: non-negotiable above baby-aspirin level. 1 mg K2 per 325 mg aspirin.
- Stomach reactions: "I don't know anyone who has a stomach reaction when they dissolve the aspirin in hot water, and then take it with food" ([[aspirin|aspirin email]]).
- "Aspirin allergy" is usually not allergy: "I know people who had lifelong 'aspirin allergy' who now use it regularly. I think part of it is the metabolic problems caused by PUFA and low thyroid function" ([[aspirin|aspirin email]]) — i.e., the susceptibility is a [[pufa]] / [[thyroid]] state, not an immune reaction.

## Fever, and what aspirin actually does to it

One of the strongest contemporary objections to aspirin for cold and flu — that it suppresses a therapeutic fever — Peat addressed directly in the *Politics and Science*, March 31, 2020:

> Aspirin can often increase your thyroid function and cell respiration so much that it doesn't affect your fever, but it can lower the *need* for a fever. Since angiotensin overproduction is a basic harm factor of any infection, you're stopping the mechanism of the harm. That is what is lowering the fever. It isn't just mechanically turning off heat production. It actually increases oxidative metabolism and, at least temporarily, will increase thyroid function. So when it reduces a fever, I think it's most often because it's remedying the thing that is causing the fever.

The distinction is critical and gets lost in mainstream "should you take aspirin for a fever" debates. [[acetaminophen|Acetaminophen]] (Tylenol) genuinely suppresses fever by blocking heat production downstream. Aspirin removes the upstream inflammatory drivers — the prostaglandins, the angiotensin, the cytokines — that the body was raising its temperature to combat. The fever may stay (if the underlying metabolic stress is real) or fade (if the inflammation is what was driving it), but the intervention is qualitatively different from a simple antipyretic.

Fever itself, Peat affirmed in the same episode, is adaptive: "It activates various parts of your immune reaction. The ability to produce antibodies, for example, is increased with fever. And white blood cells can eat faster at a higher temperature. So in general, your resistance is increased with the fever." His framework treats the fever-temperature itself as data — a sign the metabolic apparatus is mobilizing — and treats the inflammation that produces the *need* for the fever as the actual target.

The pediatric prohibition on aspirin during flu, grounded in fear of [[reye-syndrome|Reye syndrome]], is covered in [[aspirin]]. The short version, from the [[aspirin-brain-and-cancer|*Aspirin, Brain, and Cancer*]] (2006) newsletter: the Australian Reye-syndrome data found "six times as many of them had been using acetaminophen as had used aspirin." The substitution went the wrong direction — safer drug replaced by more dangerous one on the strength of a manufactured story.

## Vitamin D: the seasonal architecture of immunity

The full vitamin D mechanism — PTH suppression, calcium balance, D3 vs D2 vs calcitriol, topical and dietary delivery — is in [[vitamin-d]]. For cold and flu specifically:

- **Target**: 50–60 ng/mL serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D. "Probably 95% of people I've seen are below that" — [[2020-03-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-coronavirus|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 20, 2020]].
- **Dose**: 5,000 IU/day during low-sun months for most adults; 10,000 IU/day for several months to repair a deficit ([[vitamin-d|vitamin D email]]).
- **Form**: D3 (cholecalciferol) only — not D2, never the activated calcitriol form.
- **Seasonal explanation**: "Flu season from winter until late spring" tracks the seasonal D collapse — same episode.
- **Why it works against viruses without being directly antiviral**: "Vitamin D is one of the most important opponents of inflammation. It directly prevents the types of inflammation caused by particles, coronavirus, influenza, and any infection" — [[2020-09-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-september-2020-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, September 2020]].
- **Calcium pairing**: "Vitamin D is anti-inflammatory, but it works by lowering parathyroid hormone. It can't do that if your phosphate intake is very high relative to calcium" — *Politics and Science*, March 31, 2020. Adequate dietary calcium plus low phosphate — fewer grains, beans, nuts; more milk, cheese, cooked greens. See [[milk]] for the calcium-with-D delivery system.

## What else matters

Around the aspirin / vitamin D core, Peat assembled a cluster of supports that recur across his cold and flu material. Each links to the hub page that walks the mechanism in detail; the lines below are the cold/flu-specific quotes.

- **[[vitamin-a]]** — "Vitamin D is the most important immune-supporting nutrient for Americans. In the world in general, vitamin A and zinc are rivals for protection of the membranes and direct immunity to germs, mechanical, or chemical irritants" — [[2021-09-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-september-2021-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, September 2021]]. Dose: ~5,000 IU/day from food (liver, eggs, dairy) for most; up to 100,000 IU/day for high-metabolism people in sunny climates ([[vitamin-a|vitamin A email]]).

- **[[thyroid]]** — the master variable. From the Eugene clinic passage in [[repairing-immunodeficiencies|*Repairing Immunodeficiencies*]] (1989): "We found that many people with acne, bladder or kidney infections, periodontitis and sinusitis seemed to be deficient in both thyroid and vitamin A even when they were taking supplements of those materials. At higher doses of both, many of those people quickly got over their infections." [[broda-barnes|Broda Barnes]] documented the hypothyroid-infection link in pre-antibiotic-era tuberculosis populations. Recurring infection is a flag for [[hypothyroidism]] until proven otherwise.

- **[[progesterone]]** — the antiviral steroid for cycling and post-menopausal women. GABAergic calming, anti-estrogenic thymus protection, anti-prostaglandin. "Thyroid, progesterone and pregnenolone help to normalize blood sugar by improving respiration, and should be the basic treatment for allergies" — [[immunity-hormones-and-yeast-infections|*Immunity, Hormones and Yeast Infections*]] (1985). Why women die less from respiratory infection before menopause: "Women have protective exposure from progesterone, which is anti-inflammatory. Men don't express progesterone in the same way" — [[2020-07-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-july-2020-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, July 2020]].

- **Sugar, salt, calorie sufficiency** — fasting is immunosuppressive. "Just 24 hours of fasting is enough to damage all of your functions, especially energy production. Energy is a basic thing for maintaining your non-inflammatory immune system" — *Politics and Science*, March 24, 2020. The Selye-era anaphylaxis experiments are decisive: blood glucose at ~300 mg% was "the completely protective level" against the deadliest allergens; insulin made the mildest allergen deadly ([[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2016]]). See [[anti-stress-diet]] for the sick-day metabolic substrate: orange juice with salt, milk with honey, gelatinous chicken soup.

- **[[carbon-dioxide]]** — "the most effective expectorant" per Goodman 1956, quoted in [[the-transparency-of-life-cataracts-as-a-model-of-age-related-disease|*The Transparency of Life*]]: "It not only lowers the viscosity of tenacious sputum, thereby facilitating expectoration, but it decreases the volume of sputum by promoting its active resorption by bronchial mucosa." Oxygen is anti-expectorant. The contemporary relevance — pure-oxygen ventilation in severe pneumonia displaces CO2 and amplifies inflammation — Peat pressed in the *Politics and Science*, March 24, 2020: "Several good research projects have found that if you hypoventilate people who are having problems in the hospital, letting the CO2 build up reduces inflammation and produces much better outcomes."

- **[[coconut-oil]]** — short and medium chain saturated fats. From [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] (p. ~88): their cellular effects include "induction of hormone receptors and other specialized cell functions, suppression of stress-induced enzymes, stimulation of energy production in fat cells, inhibition of cancer cell division and viral expression."

- **Soap** — the hygiene point Peat made repeatedly: "Soap is a surefire parasiticide for this type of virus. It is very virucidal on the skin" ([[2020-03-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-coronavirus|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 20, 2020]]). Pure coconut soap is what he used himself.

## Why some people get sick every winter

The chronic-cold-every-year question gets one sentence in the [[lungs|lungs email]]: "The liver, intestine, and lungs interact very closely, and supporting the liver with nourishment and adequate thyroid, while avoiding irritating foods such as salads, beans, and allergens, will usually prevent recurring respiratory problems." The recurrent winter cold is a marker of a chronic state — usually subclinical [[hypothyroidism]], usually with [[endotoxin]] leak from a stressed gut, usually exacerbated by the seasonal vitamin D collapse — and not a series of separate infections. The recurring "cold" is the same metabolic substrate failing in the same way on the same schedule.

This matters for the protocol layer. If a cold is an event, you treat it with one-off measures. If it is a chronic state intermittently breaking the surface, you treat it with year-round structural changes: [[anti-stress-diet|diet]], [[temperature-tracking|temperature and pulse tracking]], thyroid evaluation, PUFA depletion, daily vitamin D and vitamin A, daily [[carrot-salad|raw carrot]] for the bowel barrier. See [[immune-protocol]] for the operational sequence.

## Severe illness: the boundary of the home framework

Peat's framework has its outer edge, and he was honest about it. For pneumonia with hypoxia, sepsis, severe COVID, or multi-organ failure, the home protocol is insufficient and the right response is medical evaluation. But Peat also documented the iatrogenic disasters that occurred when COVID-era hospitals applied standard intensive-care protocols to inflammatory respiratory illness. From the [[2020-09-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-september-2020-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, September 2020]]: "The extremely high mortality rate in the early weeks occurred when some hospitals were putting a tube into all of the COVID-diagnosed patients, resulting in 60% to 100% mortality. Nearby hospitals, where they had someone actually thinking about what's needed when you have inflamed lungs, had essentially zero mortality, showing that almost 100% of the mortality was caused by ignorant application of these respirators."

His framework for severe inflammatory respiratory illness centered on [[losartan]] (angiotensin receptor blocker), [[cinanserin]] (serotonin blocker the Chinese used during COVID — "The Chinese for years have been using Cinanserin, which is a serotonin blocker. They find that it's helpful for people with the established respiratory corona infection. Losartan, which is a high blood pressure drug, is the most well-known blocker of the angiotensin 1 receptor" — *Politics and Science*, March 18, 2020), [[cyproheptadine]] (broad anti-histamine and anti-serotonin), and [[emodin]] (from cascara — "specifically blocks the coronavirus spike protein and the angiotensin-converting enzyme" — [[2021-03-19-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-march-2021-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2021]]) — alongside high-dose [[aspirin]] with vitamin K, intravenous glucose, and CO2-supplemented respiratory support. These are prescription decisions for a hospital setting, included here for context, not as a self-treatment recommendation.

## Counter-arguments

The cold-and-flu position above contradicts most contemporary cold-and-flu wisdom on several specific points. The article would be incomplete without naming them.

**Vitamin C megadosing.** The dominant lay protocol (since Linus Pauling) is 1,000–10,000 mg of ascorbic acid at the onset of a cold. Peat opposed it sharply. Isolated ascorbic acid is a strong reducing agent that mobilizes stored iron and heavy metals, drives [[lipid-peroxidation]], and can produce cold-like symptoms — he himself developed bleeding colitis from chronic high-dose ascorbic acid (recounted in [[supplement-safety|*Supplement Safety*]]). Vitamin C from food arrives with bioflavonoids that prevent the reducing-agent damage. "I took a 500mg tablet and immediately got cold symptoms. I found that I could consume 4,000mg a day of natural Vitamin C by eating simply and eliminating grains, with no problem at all. But 2mg of the commercial stuff produced reactions" — *Politics and Science*, March 24, 2020. Food vitamin C, yes. Synthetic megadose, no.

**Acetaminophen for fever.** As noted above, the Reye-syndrome reversal flipped the safety profile in the wrong direction. Acetaminophen lacks aspirin's anti-inflammatory, mitochondrial, and anti-estrogenic properties while adding liver toxicity. The wrong NSAID substitute, produced by pharmaceutical marketing.

**Antibiotics for the common cold.** Mainstream over-prescription of antibiotics for viral illness is widely criticized, and Peat shared the basic criticism — most colds and flus don't need them. But he held a nuanced position. Because endotoxin-driven inflammation is a major component of "the flu" symptom complex, a low-dose, short course of antibiotics can sometimes interrupt the cascade. "You usually don't need the standard dose to clear out a bowel infection if it has just come on. Sometimes a third of the standard dose of penicillin, tetracycline, or erythromycin will work" — [[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2016]]. Not a recommendation for self-prescription, only an acknowledgment that the standard "no antibiotics for viral illness" rule misses the gut-amplification mechanism.

**"Feed a cold, starve a fever."** Peat's position is "feed both, especially a fever." Starvation activates [[cortisol]], mobilizes free fatty acids, accelerates catabolic destruction of immune tissue. Small frequent meals, salt + sugar + protein, milk + honey + orange juice at bedtime if appetite is absent.

**Cold plunges, intermittent fasting, prolonged bed rest in the dark.** All three drive the same problem: [[cortisol]]-mediated thymus atrophy. The full mechanism is in [[cortisol]]; the cold/flu-specific implication is that wellness-culture practices marketed as "anti-inflammatory" (cold exposure, fasting, restrictive diet) are immunosuppressive during illness. The right move is food + light + warmth, not deprivation.

**Annual flu vaccination.** Peat was openly skeptical of seasonal flu vaccination on the grounds that mutation rates outrun vaccine development cycles, that aluminum adjuvants drive systemic inflammation, and that "vaccinated people had a much greater rate of coronavirus infections the following year" in the studies he reviewed ([[2021-03-19-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-march-2021-kmud|*Ask the Herb Doctor*, March 2021]]). This is the most politically loaded position in the cluster. The article states Peat's view without expanding it into a vaccine-policy essay; readers should be aware contemporary public-health consensus is opposite.

## Data gaps

Peat's framework was developed before COVID-19, before long COVID, and before mRNA vaccines became the dominant pandemic response. He commented on all three in interviews but in the form of preliminary readings, not systematic positions. The framework also leaves several common cold-and-flu questions explicitly unanswered.

- **Pediatric dosing.** Peat almost never specified doses for children. The aspirin/Reye-syndrome controversy makes pediatric aspirin use a contested topic. His framework — calm the inflammation, support thyroid, raise vitamin D — is sound in principle for kids, but the *quantities* require pediatric medical supervision.

- **Pregnancy.** Peat strongly recommended progesterone, vitamin D, and milk in pregnancy, but did not write a focused piece on respiratory infections in pregnant women. Aspirin use in pregnancy has standard medical guidelines (low-dose sometimes prescribed; high-dose contraindicated near term).

- **Immunocompromised individuals.** [[repairing-immunodeficiencies|*Repairing Immunodeficiencies*]] (1989) is closest to addressing this directly but was written about AIDS-era patients. Modern immunocompromise (transplant recipients, chemotherapy patients, biologic-drug-treated autoimmune patients) presents different metabolic realities Peat did not address specifically.

- **Long COVID / post-viral syndrome.** The [[2021-09-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-september-2021-kmud|September 2021 episode]] addresses one caller with persistent symptoms using the same framework, but no Peat newsletter or article is devoted to post-viral chronic fatigue. The framework is the right place to start; the specifics need a separate research thread.

- **Specific viral pathogens.** Peat consistently said the virus's identity doesn't matter much — the inflammatory cascade is the same. For most readers most of the time this is correct; for mononucleosis, severe shingles, or hepatitis C, it isn't sufficient.

- **Bacterial superinfection.** Peat acknowledged that most flu deaths are bacterial pneumonia superinfections but did not write extensively about when to escalate to antibiotics. A productive cough with chest pain and a fever above 103°F for more than 48 hours warrants a doctor visit.

- **Zinc.** Peat acknowledged zinc as a membrane-protective immune nutrient ("vitamin A and zinc are rivals for protection of the membranes") but never wrote a focused piece on zinc lozenges for colds — a topic with a substantial mainstream literature.

- **Sore throat as a primary symptom.** Peat almost never wrote about sore throat in any detail. It fits the general gut-to-lung inflammatory model and responds to the general protocol (aspirin, salt, vitamin A), but Peat-specific guidance does not exist in the corpus.
