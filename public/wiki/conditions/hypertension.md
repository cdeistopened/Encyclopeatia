# Hypertension

The conventional advice to restrict salt for high blood pressure represents one of medicine's most persistent errors. In Ray Peat's framework—drawing on the research of David McCarron—hypertension correlates with low [[calcium]] intake, not high [[sodium]] intake. The people with the highest blood pressure eat the *least* salt. Salt restriction activates the hormonal systems that actually raise blood pressure, making the treatment worse than the disease.

As Peat explained in a [[2011-12-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-water-retention-and-salt|2011 episode on water retention and salt]], "David McCarron, 30 or 40 years ago, was studying the issue. He thought that if you look at the figures carefully, the people who ate the *least* salt had the highest blood pressure, and the people who ate the *most* salt had the lowest blood pressure."

## The Calcium-PTH-Aldosterone Axis

The mechanism Peat identified involves [[parathyroid hormone]] (PTH) and [[aldosterone]]. In a [[2012-06-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-cellular-repair|2012 episode]], he explained the cascade: "Parathyroid hormone comes down when you eat more calcium and have adequate Vitamin D. Parathyroid hormone increases aldosterone. If you restrict calcium, your parathyroid hormone goes up, making your aldosterone go up, which increases blood pressure and retains sodium."

This inverts the conventional understanding entirely. Aldosterone—a mineralocorticoid hormone—retains sodium by raising blood pressure. But aldosterone rises in response to *calcium* deficiency, not sodium excess. The person restricting salt often has low calcium intake, elevated PTH, elevated aldosterone, and the hypertension that aldosterone produces.

The body then retains sodium to maintain blood volume—but this is compensation, not cause. Treating the sodium retention while ignoring the calcium deficiency and PTH elevation misses the root cause entirely.

## McCarron's Data

McCarron's insight came from looking at the government's own data with fresh eyes. In a [[2008-12-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-cholesterol-is-an-important-molecule|2008 episode]], Peat recounted: "He showed that it wasn't sodium excess causing high blood pressure, but calcium deficiency. When people didn't eat milk and cheese, they would often eat salty snack foods."

The correlation between salt and hypertension was real but spurious—a third variable (low calcium from avoiding dairy) explained both the salty snack consumption and the high blood pressure. Restricting salt while continuing to avoid dairy does nothing to address the calcium deficiency that drives the hormonal dysfunction.

## Thyroid and Blood Pressure

[[Hypothyroidism]] contributes to hypertension through multiple mechanisms. Low thyroid function causes [[sodium]] wasting, which the body compensates for by activating aldosterone. It impairs kidney function, reducing the organ's ability to regulate blood pressure. It increases [[stress]] hormones like [[adrenaline]] and [[cortisol]] that constrict blood vessels.

As noted in the [[sodium]] article, "Low thyroid women lose sodium easily. Thyroid causes sodium retention, which binds water and keeps blood volume up, perfusing kidneys properly so they stop signaling to raise blood pressure."

The paradox: adequate salt intake can *lower* blood pressure by restoring blood volume and reducing the stress signals from inadequately perfused kidneys.

## Correcting the Metabolic Basis

Peat's approach to hypertension addresses nutritional and hormonal factors:

**Adequate calcium intake** is primary—from [[milk]], [[cheese]], or supplements like powdered eggshell. Two grams daily suppresses PTH and reduces aldosterone.

**Vitamin D** optimization (around 50 ng/mL serum level) supports calcium metabolism and further suppresses PTH.

**Vitamin K** helps direct calcium to bones rather than soft tissues, as Peat noted in a [[2014-01-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-questions-and-answers|2014 episode]]: "A big dose of Vitamin K can correct osteoporosis and take calcium out of the blood vessels."

**Liberal salt intake** (salting food to taste) prevents the stress response to perceived sodium deficiency.

**Thyroid optimization** restores normal kidney function and reduces compensatory stress hormones.

**[[Potassium]] from fruit** balances sodium and supports proper cellular function.
