# Schizophrenia

> Parent: [[mental-health-cluster]]. See also [[bipolar]], [[anxiety-depression.research]].

## Bottom line

Peat dismantles the dopamine theory of schizophrenia using the same critique he applies to the serotonin theory of depression: it is a drug-industry construction backfilled from how neuroleptics happened to work, not a primary insight into the disease. PET studies showing reduced prefrontal glucose metabolism in chronic schizophrenia fit Peat's frame — schizophrenia is the extreme expression of the brain-stress-syndrome where high-energy frontal-cortex function fails first.

## The dopamine-theory critique

> "The fact that some patients hallucinate caused many psychiatrists to believe that hallucinogenic chemicals, interfering with nerve transmitter substances such as dopamine or serotonin, were going to provide insight into psychotic states. The dopamine [theory] …" — *Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities*

Same structural problem as the serotonin theory of depression: the theory was built around the drugs, not derived from the disease. Peat's preferred frame is metabolic.

## What hallucination actually is, in Peat's frame

> "There's a point at which thoughts flow freely and luminously, but meaningfully, that can happen when nutrients and hormones are optimal; coffee and vitamin B1 support that kind of function. **Hallucination suggests that there is distortion in their meaning, probably when energy isn't being produced as fast as it's used.**" — Peat, *Hallucinations* email

Thought generation depends on rapid ATP turnover in the prefrontal cortex. When generation outpaces energy supply, signal degrades into hallucinatory or delusional content. The same lens applies to delirium, severe sleep deprivation, and altitude sickness — all of which can produce psychotic symptoms in non-schizophrenic individuals.

## Therapies that have actually helped

> "Therapies that have been successful in treating 'schizophrenia' include penicillin, sleep therapy, hyperbaric oxygen, carbon dioxide therapy, thyroid, acetazolamide, lithium and vitamins. These all make fundamental contributions to the restoration of biological energy. Antibiotics, for example, lower endotoxin formation in the intestine, protect against the induction by endotoxin of serotonin, histamine, estrogen, and cortisol. Acetazolamide causes the tissues to retain carbon dioxide, and increased carbon dioxide acidifies cells, preventing serotonin secretion." — *Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities*

| Therapy | Mechanism in Peat's frame |
|---|---|
| **Penicillin / antibiotics** | Lower endotoxin → reduce systemic serotonin, histamine, estrogen, cortisol induction |
| **Sleep therapy** | Restore deep slow-wave sleep, which is depleted in schizophrenia (same pattern as depression and old age) |
| **Hyperbaric oxygen** | Direct restoration of oxidative metabolism in energy-starved frontal tissue |
| **CO₂ therapy** | Acidifies cells, blocks serotonin release, stabilizes excitable neurons |
| **Thyroid** | Restores oxidative metabolism, lowers serotonin and adrenalin, raises body temperature |
| **Acetazolamide** | Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor → tissues retain CO₂ (same mechanism as CO₂ therapy) — there is a 1984 paper (Inoue et al., *Folia Psychiatr Neurol Jpn*) on acetazolamide for atypical psychosis |
| **Lithium** | Sodium-mimic, anti-adrenergic, antiserotonin, mitochondrial-energy-restoring |
| **Vitamins** | Niacinamide, B1, etc. — each supports oxidative phosphorylation directly |

The unifying principle is that all of these restore biological energy. The dopamine antagonists do not appear on Peat's list — they suppress symptoms by blunting prefrontal function, which is the opposite of restoring energy.

## The morning-temperature pattern

From the same article: "in some schizophrenics, the morning temperature is high, corresponding to very high levels of adrenalin and cortisol." Same diagnostic pattern as both phases of bipolar — sympathetic-overdrive masking metabolic deficit. The post-breakfast temperature drop test (see [[bipolar]]) is applicable here too.

## What Peat would do additionally

Beyond the article's specific therapy list:

- **Reduce PUFA** (polyunsaturated fats raise estrogen and serotonin via free-fatty-acid mobilization)
- **Adequate protein with glycine** (gelatin) to balance tryptophan
- **Calcium-rich anti-fermentation diet** — fruit, milk, cheese
- **Aspirin** for anti-prostaglandin effect (Feldberg's 1976 prostaglandin hypothesis of schizophrenia is in Peat's references)
- **Progesterone / pregnenolone** as neurosteroid support
- **Niacinamide** — long history in orthomolecular psychiatry (Hoffer), restores NAD/NADH balance

## Counter-arguments

- **First-episode psychosis** has clear genetic and developmental components (in utero infection, perinatal hypoxia, cannabis use in adolescence) that Peat's metabolic frame downstreams but doesn't fully account for.
- **Antipsychotics are real medical interventions** that prevent re-hospitalization and reduce mortality. Peat does not call them out as actively harmful in the corpus, but his omission from the "what works" list reads as quiet rejection — which is contestable.
- **Hyperbaric oxygen for schizophrenia** is largely not validated in modern controlled trials. Peat's evidence base is from older literature.
- **Acetazolamide for atypical psychosis** (Inoue 1984) is a single-paper basis; not a robust therapy.
- **Niacinamide / orthomolecular** approaches were tested in the 1970s in formal trials and largely failed (Canadian Mental Health Association review). Peat's reference frame is older than the negative replications.

## Data gaps

- No modern controlled trials of the "biological-energy-restoration" cluster against neuroleptics.
- The role of endotoxin in schizophrenia onset is suggestive (animal models, post-natal infection epidemiology) but not causally established.
- Whether "negative-symptom" schizophrenia (avolition, anhedonia, flat affect) responds differently to thyroid/metabolic intervention vs. positive symptoms (hallucination, delusion) is unaddressed in Peat.
- Modern psychiatric mechanism research has moved toward NMDA hypofunction and glutamate; Peat's serotonin/dopamine critique pre-dates this and may be partially superseded.
