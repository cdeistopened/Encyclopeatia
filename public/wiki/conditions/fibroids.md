# Uterine Fibroids

Uterine fibroids — leiomyomata, myomas — are the most common benign tumors in women of reproductive age. Mainstream gynecology describes them as clonal proliferations of myometrial smooth muscle, present in 70–80% of women by age 50, and the leading indication for hysterectomy in the United States. Roughly 600,000 hysterectomies are performed in the U.S. annually; about a third are for fibroids. The disease is presented as etiologically obscure ("multifactorial") and surgically tractable — a logic that produces one of the largest interventional-surgery footprints in modern medicine while leaving the underlying physiology unaddressed.

Ray Peat's framework folds fibroids into a much broader pattern. The leiomyoma is the myometrial reading of the disorder mapped at [[fibrosis]]: a chronically estrogen-stimulated smooth muscle compartment that has lost the metabolic conditions for normal remodeling and has gone into the collagen-depositing, locally-aromatizing terminus that the same disorder reaches in the peritoneum (as [[endometriosis]]), in the breast (fibrocystic disease), in the lung (scleroderma-associated fibrosis), and in the skin (keloid). What makes the uterine reading distinctive is that the smooth muscle compartment expresses [[aromatase]] particularly avidly, the tumor becomes a pseudo-organ producing its own estrogen, and the surgical specialty equipped to handle it treats the visible tumor as the cause rather than as the late expression of a metabolic state.

This page assumes the estrogen-aromatase loop covered at [[endometriosis]] and the connective-tissue terminus described at [[fibrosis]]. What follows is the fibroid-specific layer: local aromatase and the 4-hydroxyestradiol pathway, the progesterone-receptor anomaly, the surgical-intervention critique, Peat's case reports of shrinkage under hyperthyroid protocol, and why fibroids regress at menopause but can be made to regress faster.

## The leiomyoma as myometrial fibrosis

Histologically, the leiomyoma is smooth muscle cells embedded in a dense, disorganized matrix of collagen types I and III, fibronectin, and proteoglycans, with elevated lysyl-oxidase cross-linking. Mass for mass, a fibroid contains more collagen than normal myometrium and more disordered collagen than scar — a fibrotic tumor of muscle origin, which the older name *fibromyoma* captured before the gynecology literature shortened it.

Peat read this through the [[fibrosis]] frame directly. From the 2012 *Politics and Science* ([progesterone part 3](app/public/transcripts/politics-and-science/polished/2012-02-07-politics-and-science-politics-science-progesterone-part-3.md)): "Alexander Lipschutz was the person who did the most comprehensive work showing that where estrogen causes fibrosis—fibroids first and eventually cancer—it acts first on the uterus, then on the breasts, then on the lungs, kidneys, brain. It's just a matter of where estrogen concentrates that it tends to cause fibromas and cancers. In every one of those situations, progesterone was protective." That sentence collapses what current gynecology treats as four or five distinct diseases into a single mechanism with different anatomic addresses.

The 2001 [*Fibrosis*](sources/newsletters/fibrosis.md) makes the cross-tissue link explicit: "Retroperitoneal fibrosis, which can seriously damage the kidneys and other abdominal organs, has been compared to uterine fibroid tumors, and it is commonly treated successfully with the antiestrogenic drug, tamoxifen." A fibrotic lesion outside the uterus and a uterine fibroid both respond to the same antiestrogen because they are running the same disorder. The same logic underlies Peat's keloid observation: "I have seen keloids regress quickly following the application of vitamin E with antiestrogenic hormones" ([*Fibrosis*](sources/newsletters/fibrosis.md)).

The uterine smooth muscle is particularly susceptible to this disorder because it is one of the most estrogen-responsive tissues in the body, and under hypothyroid conditions, local aromatase activity rises substantially above what circulating estradiol alone would predict.

## Local aromatase and the 4-hydroxyestradiol pathway

The decisive biological finding for understanding leiomyomata is the same finding that grounds the modern view of [[endometriosis]]: the lesion makes its own estrogen. Leiomyoma cells express [[aromatase]] (CYP19) at levels well above adjacent normal myometrium, converting circulating androstenedione and testosterone into estradiol locally, in concentrations the lesion uses for self-stimulation. Standard serum estradiol can read normal — even low — while the tumor bathes itself in concentrations many-fold higher. This is the "tissue-bound estrogen" picture Peat had been working out since the 1980s; mainstream endocrinology only began to accept it after the Bulun-Northwestern aromatase findings of the 1990s.

A second enzymatic anomaly compounds the first. Leiomyoma and adjacent myometrial cells preferentially 4-hydroxylate estradiol rather than 2-hydroxylating it. The 2-hydroxylation pathway produces a weak, rapidly-cleared estrogen; the 4-hydroxylation pathway produces 4-OH-estradiol, a potent estrogen further metabolized into reactive semiquinone and quinone intermediates that bind DNA covalently — a direct mutagenic mechanism. The 1995 Liehr et al. *PNAS* paper — "4-Hydroxylation of Estradiol by Human Uterine Myometrium and Myoma Microsomes: Implications for the Mechanism of Uterine Tumorigenesis" — appears in Peat's reference lists for both [*Epilepsy and Progesterone*](sources/newsletters/epilepsy-and-progesterone.md) and [*The Problem of Alzheimer's Disease as a Clue to Immortality*](sources/newsletters/the-problem-of-alzheimers-disease-as-a-clue-to-immortality.md). The fibroid is not merely estrogen-responsive — it is estrogen-*generating* and estrogen-*mutagenically-activating*. The MED12 and HMGA2 mutations catalogued in mainstream cytogenetics are the downstream genomic effect of this upstream chemistry.

The local aromatase pathway is stimulated by [[prostaglandins]] (especially PGE2), cortisol, and the cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α from chronically inflamed connective tissue. The loop is tight: the fibroid generates prostaglandins from stored [[pufa]] via cyclooxygenase, prostaglandins upregulate aromatase, aromatase makes more estradiol, estradiol increases cyclooxygenase expression. From a control-systems perspective, the leiomyoma is a tissue that has built its own positive-feedback estrogen amplifier and decoupled from systemic regulation.

## The progesterone-receptor anomaly

Fibroids overexpress both estrogen and progesterone receptors. They proliferate in response to estrogen; they also proliferate in response to certain synthetic progestins. This is the basis of the gynecology consensus that "progesterone causes fibroid growth" and the main reason mainstream practice avoids progesterone in fibroid management.

The confusion turns on the difference between *natural progesterone* and *progestins*. Synthetic progestins (medroxyprogesterone acetate, norethindrone) bind the progesterone receptor but also bind androgen and glucocorticoid receptors, induce different gene programs, and can have proliferative effects on uterine smooth muscle. The levonorgestrel IUD — promoted for fibroid-associated bleeding — delivers a synthetic progestin at high local concentrations. "Progestin-containing IUDs sometimes shrink fibroids" is not equivalent to "progesterone shrinks fibroids."

Peat's distinction is sharp. From the 2012 [*Politics and Science*](app/public/transcripts/politics-and-science/polished/2012-01-22-politics-and-science-politics-science-progesterone-part-1.md), when Barkhausen asked about the pregnancy-and-fibroid pattern: "Progesterone was recommended for many years for treating fibroids. Partly that involves increased thyroid function. Progesterone is needed for the thyroid to work, and the thyroid working causes the liver to lower the estrogen level systemically. Sometimes just one dose of progesterone is all it takes to free the thyroid to function, stimulate new progesterone synthesis, and lower estrogen." That is a four-part mechanism in one sentence: progesterone enables thyroid; thyroid raises estrogen clearance; estrogen falls; the loop unwinds. The natural-progesterone effect on a fibroid is not direct mitogenic suppression — it is restoration of the metabolic context in which the lesion's positive-feedback amplifier shuts off.

The 1995 [*Comparison of Progesterone and Estrogen*](sources/newsletters/comparison-of-progesterone-and-estrogen.md) puts the protective action profile compactly: progesterone is the "anti-tumor, anti-aging hormone. Shrinks fibroids of breast & uterus, cystic ovaries; effective in some types of cancers, such as cervical & breast cancers & estrogen-induced tumors."

The PR-A vs PR-B isoform problem that complicates the [[endometriosis]] picture also applies here. Leiomyomata show altered isoform ratios. Whether natural progesterone alone is sufficient, or whether the receptor environment must first be normalized by thyroid, is the practical question. Peat's case reports consistently pair progesterone with thyroid — never progesterone alone — which is consistent with the receptor-restoration interpretation.

## The hyperthyroid-overdose strategy: Peat's case series

The most distinctive clinical claim in Peat's fibroid discussion is the deliberate use of supraphysiological thyroid to drive shrinkage. He returned to the same case repeatedly from 2014 onward — a 40-year-old woman with a tennis-ball-sized fibroid blocking her uterus who wanted to get pregnant.

The fullest version is from the 2014 [*One Radio Network*](app/public/transcripts/one-radio-network/polished/2014-01-01-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-patrick-timpone-dr-peat-answers-questions-regarding-health-die.md) with Patrick Timpone: "I had an experience with a woman who was about 40 at the time... She had a fibroid plugging the end of her uterus by the fallopian tubes about the size of a tennis ball. I explained the physiology to her. She adjusted, taking enough thyroid that she could keep her estrogen level down while keeping the other hormones up. It happens that her pulse averaged about 110 per minute. Her doctor told her she would die from keeping her pulse that fast. But she had an ultrasound every month, and every month that she kept her thyroid at that level, the fibroid shrank. I think she started around March or April, and by August the fibroid had disappeared and she was pregnant." The 2019 [*One Radio Network*](app/public/transcripts/one-radio-network/polished/2019-02-19-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-thyroid-pufas-oj-and-sugar.md) compressed it: "She took Armour thyroid, gradually working up until her resting heart rate was 110. The tumor shrank, and she had a perfect pregnancy. That was a deliberate overdose to lower estrogen."

The 2022 [*Ask the Herb Doctor*](app/public/transcripts/ask-the-herb-doctor/polished/2022-01-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-january-2022-kmud.md), retold in [the 2025-11-28 episode](app/public/transcripts/ask-the-herb-doctor/polished/2025-11-28-ask-the-herb-doctor-january-2022.md), adds the clinical-defiance detail: "Every month the doctor — besides telling her she was going to die from taking too much thyroid — made an image showing that the fibroid was shrunken." By the fifth month it had reached a quarter of its original size and she was pregnant.

The strategy generalizes. From the [*Generative Energy* episode #58](app/public/transcripts/generative-energy/polished/#58： Bioenergetic Nutrition Continued ｜ Authoritarianism ｜ Intention and Learning with Ray Peat, PhD.md): "I've seen fairly large uterine fibroids shrink when someone was willing to stay slightly hyperthyroid, keeping a resting pulse around 110 for three months."

Three months of resting pulse 110 is, in conventional thyroidology, an emergency. Peat treated it as a deliberate, monitored, time-limited intervention with two constraints: the hyperthyroid state had to be biochemically deep enough to suppress hepatic estrogen recirculation and raise tissue oxidative metabolism above the threshold where local aromatase shuts down; the duration had to be bounded because the metabolic cost of sustained hyperthyroidism is real. The aim was not pulse 110 forever — it was to shrink the lesion sufficiently to return to a normal pulse on standard replacement doses.

The 2015 [*Ask the Herb Doctor*](app/public/transcripts/ask-the-herb-doctor/polished/2015-02-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-uses-of-urea.md) compresses the etiology into one sentence: "Low thyroid causes the ratio of estrogen to progesterone to be very high. Endometriosis, uterine fibroids, and breast fibrocystic disease all go with high estrogen, low progesterone, and low thyroid function." That sentence is the entire framework distilled.

## Mainstream framing and its limits

The mainstream menu for symptomatic fibroids is wide. Each option treats a different aspect of the symptom while leaving the metabolic state alone.

*Watchful waiting* is effectively a bet on menopause — fibroids reliably shrink 30–50% in the postmenopausal estrogen-withdrawal state, but the waiting interval averages many years for a woman in her thirties. The framework predicts that interval can be compressed; the natural menopausal shrinkage is the same process the protocol accelerates.

*Combined oral contraceptives, progestin-only pills, and the levonorgestrel IUD* are aimed at bleeding rather than mass. The synthetic progestins reduce bleeding through endometrial atrophy but do not consistently shrink the fibroid, and the IUD delivers a continuous estrogen-disrupting signal Peat flagged at [[endometriosis]]: it interrupts the ovarian progesterone production that is the body's only built-in defense against the estrogen-dominant state maintaining the fibroid.

*GnRH agonists (Lupron) and antagonists (elagolix, relugolix)* induce a temporary chemical menopause. Fibroids shrink in 50–70% of patients over three-to-six months; bleeding decreases; preoperative use reduces surgical blood loss. Limitations: bone density loss caps therapy at six months without "add-back" estrogen-progestin (which partially defeats the purpose), and the fibroid regrows after discontinuation. The framework reads this exactly as it reads Lupron for endometriosis: the drug suppresses ovarian estradiol while leaving local aromatase intact, so the leiomyoma continues making estrogen from circulating androgens.

*Myomectomy* preserves the uterus but recurrence runs 15–30% at five years, up to 50% at ten. Removing the visible lesion while leaving the metabolic environment untouched allows new lesions to grow from the same myometrium. The procedure may be necessary for fertility-blocking distortion but is not curative.

*Uterine artery embolization (UAE)* causes ischemic necrosis of the tumor through radiologically-guided vascular occlusion. Symptom relief is real; fibroid volume drops 40–60% at one year. The intervention treats the lesion's vascular dependence — fibroids are heavily neovascularized under [[estrogen]]-induced VEGF — without addressing why the neovascularization happened. Five-year reintervention rates run 15–30%. *MRI-guided focused ultrasound (MRgFUS)* delivers thermal ablation with similar durability profile.

*Hysterectomy* is the "definitive" treatment in the narrow sense: no uterus, no uterine fibroid. The broader metabolic environment continues unchanged; post-hysterectomy patients show elevated cardiovascular, ovarian, and pelvic floor morbidity, and in the framework, continued fibrotic-disease tendency in other tissues. Peat's general critique of hysterectomy, from the 2022 [*Ask the Herb Doctor*](app/public/transcripts/ask-the-herb-doctor/polished/2022-05-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-may-2022-kmud.md) episode — 36 women advised to have hysterectomy for abnormal cervical cells whose tests normalized after one month on thyroid — extends to fibroid-driven hysterectomy: the clinical conditions that prompt the recommendation are reversible more often than the surgical specialty allows for.

The structural problem is that gynecology training is largely surgical, and the metabolic primary-care specialty that would integrate [[thyroid]] status, progesterone production, prostaglandin chemistry, and xenoestrogen exposure does not exist. The rate-limiting interventions — natural progesterone, T3, vitamin K, aspirin, low PUFA, xenoestrogen avoidance — sit between specialties and so are rarely tried.

## The protocol

The interventions follow from the mechanism. Each addresses a node of the local-aromatase / prostaglandin / TGF-β / matrix-deposition loop.

**Thyroid.** The keystone. The magnitude required for fibroid shrinkage in Peat's case series is meaningfully higher than for ordinary hypothyroidism correction — the 2014 case ran resting pulse near 110, sustained for months. Practical execution combines Cynoplus (T4+T3) for steady state with Cytomel (T3 alone) for acute control, titrated against pulse, temperature, and monthly ultrasound. The 2022 [*Ask the Herb Doctor*](app/public/transcripts/ask-the-herb-doctor/polished/2022-05-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-may-2022-kmud.md) cautions about T4's two-week half-life apply: small starting doses, slow titration, daily pulse/temperature tracking. See [[thyroid]] and [[hypothyroidism]].

**Progesterone.** Natural progesterone, not progestins. [[progest-e|Progest-E]] (10% progesterone in vitamin E) provides oral bioavailability that creams and micronized capsules do not. Therapeutic doses run 100–300 mg/day during the luteal phase, sometimes continuously across the cycle during active shrinkage. Mechanism: aromatase inhibition, 17β-HSD2 induction, cyclooxygenase suppression, direct receptor antagonism. The [*Comparison of Progesterone and Estrogen*](sources/newsletters/comparison-of-progesterone-and-estrogen.md) "shrinks fibroids of breast & uterus" claim is the action at therapeutic doses, not at homeopathic ones. See [[progesterone]].

**Vitamin K.** K2 (MK-4, MK-7) with adequate K1. Soft-tissue anti-calcification factor, anti-fibrotic cofactor, and — relevant to fibroid bleeding — mandatory clotting cofactor. Therapeutic range 5–15 mg/day; maintenance lower. See [[vitamin-k]].

**Aspirin.** 325–650 mg multiple times daily for the prostaglandin-and-aromatase brake described at [[endometriosis]]. The same cyclooxygenase inhibition that cuts cramping cuts the fibroid's local estrogen amplifier. Vitamin K co-administration mandatory. See [[aspirin]].

**Low PUFA.** Removing dietary soybean, corn, safflower, sunflower, canola, fish, and flax oils stops new prostaglandin formation immediately; depleting stored adipose PUFA takes years. The fibroid's cyclooxygenase has nothing to convert if substrate is absent. See [[pufa]] and the [[pufa-depletion]] protocol.

**Xenoestrogen avoidance.** Dioxins, BPA, phthalates, DDT residues, parabens — chronic estrogen-receptor activation no internal regulation neutralizes. Filtered water, glass over plastic, organic produce for high-residue items, label-reading on personal care. See [[xenoestrogens]].

**Adjuncts.** [[Carrot-salad|Raw carrot]] daily (interrupts enterohepatic estrogen recirculation). Adequate sugar (prevents PUFA-mobilizing hypoglycemia). [[Niacinamide]] (anti-lipolytic). [[Glycine]] from gelatin and bone broth (matrix turnover substrate). [[Vitamin-e|Vitamin E]] (antiestrogen, fibrinolytic, anti-PUFA-peroxidation — the same vitamin Peat paired with antiestrogenic hormones for keloid regression). [[Caffeine]] in conditions where stress-axis is stabilized.

For the integrated dietary frame see [[anti-stress-diet]] and [[estrogen-detox]].

## Distinction from endometriosis and adenomyosis

The boundary between the four estrogen-driven uterine and pelvic conditions — [[endometriosis]], [[adenomyosis]], leiomyomata, and endometrial hyperplasia — is more a question of which tissue compartment took on the lesion character than four distinct diseases. The framework applies the same five-part intervention to all four; what changes is anatomic readout. Endometriosis is the *peritoneal* expression (extrauterine endometrial-character tissue, cardinal symptom pelvic pain). Adenomyosis is the *deep-myometrial* expression (endometrial glands embedded in the uterine wall, cardinal symptom heavy bleeding plus diffuse tenderness). The leiomyoma is the *circumscribed-myometrial* expression (a discrete smooth-muscle-and-collagen mass, cardinal symptoms bulk-and-pressure plus bleeding). Endometrial hyperplasia is the *endometrial* expression (proliferation of the lining itself, closest direct progression to frank cancer).

All four can coexist in the same uterus — a single woman can have endometriotic implants on the ovary, adenomyosis in the posterior wall, a fundal fibroid, and hyperplastic endometrial lining simultaneously, because all four are reading the same systemic disorder onto different compartments. Symptom management of any one without metabolic correction allows the others to progress.

## Pregnancy and fertility

Fibroids interact with pregnancy at two scales. Mass-effect interactions are real: a fibroid obstructing the cornual uterus can prevent fertilization, a submucosal fibroid distorting the cavity can prevent implantation, a fundal fibroid can cause malpresentation, and rapid pregnancy growth can produce red-degeneration pain. The tennis-ball case was a mass-effect fertility problem solved by shrinking the lesion enough to clear the obstruction.

The metabolic interaction makes pregnancy unreliable as a shrinkage strategy. Pregnancy is an enormous progesterone surge — corpus luteum initially, then placental production at concentrations that swamp local aromatase. In about a third of pregnancies the fibroid shrinks; another third stable; the remaining third grows, sometimes substantially. Growth phenotype reflects a fibroid in which placental androgen substrate exceeds the progesterone brake. The framework reads variable response as a marker of how deep the underlying disorder is.

John Barkhausen's question in the 2012 [*Politics and Science*](app/public/transcripts/politics-and-science/polished/2012-01-22-politics-and-science-politics-science-progesterone-part-1.md) — "Is that perhaps why some women who get fibroids notice that the fibroids go away if they get pregnant?" — drew Peat's clearest statement: "Progesterone was recommended for many years for treating fibroids. Partly that involves increased thyroid function. Progesterone is needed for the thyroid to work, and the thyroid working causes the liver to lower the estrogen level systemically." Pregnancy is a natural progesterone-thyroid intervention; the framework reproduces it with supplementation outside pregnancy.

The fertility-protocol implication is that women with fibroid-associated infertility should not be steered first to surgical myomectomy or IVF. Metabolic correction has a meaningful chance of restoring spontaneous fertility — the 2014 tennis-ball patient got pregnant naturally after lesion shrinkage — and starts the new pregnancy from a metabolically stronger baseline. The 2022 caller's wife with "fibroids and a couple of miscarriages because of the fibroid" represents the combined picture: some miscarriage risk is mass-effect, some is metabolic (the same hypothyroid-low-progesterone state that maintains the fibroid is a common cause of recurrent first-trimester loss). The protocol addresses both arms.

## Counter-arguments

The mainstream literature pushes back at several points; the strongest objections deserve to be heard.

**Modern myomectomy efficacy.** Open, laparoscopic, and hysteroscopic myomectomy in skilled hands deliver high symptom control with acceptable complication rates for the indicated patient. The framework's objection is not that the surgery fails — it works — but that the 15–30% five-year recurrence reflects regrowth from a still-permissive metabolic environment. For women with severe symptoms and near-term fertility pressure, surgery-first may be defensible; for asymptomatic and mildly symptomatic women, the calculus is much weaker.

**GnRH analog evidence.** The RCT evidence for Lupron, elagolix, and relugolix-combination therapy is robust — meta-analyses confirm fibroid volume reduction and bleeding reduction during treatment. Post-discontinuation regrowth exceeds 50% within months. The role of GnRH agents as pre-surgical bridging is defensible; as primary therapy, the framework reads them as suppressing the ovarian estrogen variable while leaving local aromatase intact.

**UAE outcome data.** The REST and EMMY trials show UAE-vs-hysterectomy parity for one-year symptom relief with lower morbidity and shorter recovery. UAE has earned a real place. The framework predicts that interrupting vascular supply without addressing metabolic cause allows new fibroids to form in non-embolized myometrium, and the five-year reintervention rate of 15–30% is consistent with that prediction. The limitations show up at ten and twenty years more than at one.

**Asymptomatic-vs-symptomatic surgical threshold.** Mainstream gynecology already does not operate on asymptomatic fibroids regardless of size, except for rapidly growing postmenopausal lesions (leiomyosarcoma differential) and infertility-associated cases. The conservative threshold is closer to the framework's position than the historical "every fibroid out" pattern. The remaining disagreement is over the symptomatic threshold — at what severity is surgery justified before metabolic correction is tried? The framework pushes that threshold substantially higher.

**The natural-progesterone-shrinks-fibroids claim.** Mainstream trials of progesterone or progestins for fibroid mass are mixed or negative. The framework's response: those trials used synthetic progestins, not natural progesterone, and none used natural progesterone *combined with thyroid correction* — the actual protocol Peat described. The Burton Goldberg case-series literature referenced in the 2000 [*Progesterone Content Corrected*](sources/newsletters/progesterone-content-corrected.md) newsletter is suggestive but does not meet RCT standards. The integrated trial has not been done.

**The hyperthyroid-overdose strategy.** Sustained resting pulse 110 carries real risk: atrial fibrillation, accelerated bone loss, sympathetic exhaustion, potential cardiac decompensation. Mainstream endocrinology would not consider it defensible at any indication. The framework's response is that the intervention is bounded (months, not years), monitored (monthly ultrasounds, daily pulse), and titrated. The case reports are not controlled; the strategy should be presented as evidence of the lesion's dynamic maintenance by the metabolic state, not as a routine recommendation.

**Growth factors and genetic mutations.** MED12 mutations (70% of fibroids), HMGA2 dysregulation, altered TGF-β signaling, and monoclonal cytogenetics are well documented. Mainstream interpretation treats them as proximate cause; the framework treats them as downstream events — the chronic estrogen-and-4-hydroxyestradiol environment is itself mutagenic, and the resulting clonal proliferations carry molecular fingerprints the cytogenetics literature has catalogued. The mutations explain morphology and clonality but not the prevalence pattern, geographic distribution, or historical trajectory of the disease.

## Data gaps

**Progesterone dose-response.** The case reports describe success at hundreds of milligrams per day, but neither the minimum effective dose nor the time-to-response curve is systematically documented. Women trying the protocol operate on imprecise dosing guidance.

**Hyperthyroid-overdose case series.** Peat referenced the same 40-year-old patient across multiple interviews. Success rate, time-course, and adverse-event rate are not described in the corpus.

**Iron status interaction.** Fibroid-associated heavy bleeding commonly produces iron-deficiency anemia. Peat's broader iron-as-pro-oxidant framework does not directly address whether replacement should be aggressive (anemia) or cautious (iron concerns). Unresolved.

**Size threshold.** The tennis-ball case shrank to roughly a quarter in five months. Whether multi-pound fibroids distorting the abdominal contour respond at the same rate — or whether matrix cross-linking creates an irreversibility threshold — is not addressed.

**Post-shrinkage maintenance.** After clinical reduction, what protocol maintains the gain? Sustained therapeutic progesterone, or step-down to maintenance? The corpus does not engage this directly.

**Submucosal vs intramural vs subserosal location.** Different presentation (bleeding, bulk, pressure on adjacent organs) — same metabolic mechanism, but no documented evidence of differential protocol response.

**Postmenopausal fibroids.** Normally regress at menopause; when they do not (or appear de novo) leiomyosarcoma must be ruled out. The framework's [[tissue-bound-estrogen-in-aging]] account predicts that adipose, adrenal, and lesion-resident aromatase can maintain a fibroid past systemic estrogen withdrawal, but the postmenopausal management arm is underdeveloped in the corpus.

**Xenoestrogen contribution.** The dioxin-and-PCB endometriosis literature (Wisconsin rhesus studies) is more developed than the fibroid-specific xenoestrogen literature. The framework's prediction that environmental estrogens drive rising prevalence in industrialized populations is plausible but not directly demonstrated for leiomyomata.

**Cancer-risk linkage.** Ordinary fibroids are benign and leiomyosarcoma is rare. The broader fibrosis-to-cancer field-process framing from [*Fibrosis*](sources/newsletters/fibrosis.md) — "Fibrogenesis is related to carcinogenesis. Cancerization is a 'field process,' not primarily a unique 'genetic event'" — would predict elevated endometrial and breast cancer risk in severe fibroid history. Some epidemiology supports this; the case has not been worked through.
