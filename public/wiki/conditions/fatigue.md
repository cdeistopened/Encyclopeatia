# Fatigue

Fatigue is the subjective experience of inadequate cellular energy production. In Ray Peat's framework, chronic fatigue is not a mysterious syndrome requiring psychological intervention but a direct consequence of impaired oxidative [[metabolism]]—typically from [[hypothyroidism]], [[PUFA]] accumulation, or the combination of both. When cells cannot produce energy efficiently through mitochondrial respiration, every function requiring energy—which means every function—becomes difficult.

As Peat explained in a [[2009-04-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-thyroid-polyunsaturated-fats-and-oils|2009 episode]], hypothyroidism "is a slowing of the oxidative metabolism, which means your biological efficiency falls drastically. We rely almost entirely on oxidative metabolism."

## The Thyroid-Fatigue Connection

[[Thyroid]] hormone is essential for every cell's energy production. Without adequate T3 at the cellular level, mitochondria cannot oxidize glucose efficiently. As Peat noted in a [[2016-04-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-mitochondria-gaba-herbs-and-more|2016 episode]], "If you don't have adequate thyroid, you won't be getting adequate oxygen into that mitochondria, and basic energy production and cellular function will be decreased."

The decrease is not minor. Oxidative metabolism produces 18 times more ATP per glucose molecule than glycolysis. When thyroid-dependent respiration fails, cells fall back on the inefficient glycolytic pathway—producing [[lactic acid]] instead of [[carbon dioxide]] and generating a fraction of the energy the body requires.

This is why hypothyroid fatigue feels different from simple tiredness. It is not the pleasant tiredness after exertion that resolves with rest. It is the baseline inadequacy of energy for normal function—waking tired, struggling through the day, finding everything effortful that used to be easy.

## The Fatigue-Inflammation Cascade

Fatigue creates the conditions for tissue destruction. In a [[2013-06-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-heart-ii|2013 episode]], Peat described the progression: "Low thyroid allows muscles to fatigue easily because energy isn't being produced by respiration at the necessary level. When a muscle is fatigued, it activates processes to provide more energy, which liberates fats from storage as [[free fatty acids]]."

The freed fatty acids—particularly if polyunsaturated—compound the problem: "These free fatty acids further suppress the thyroid and start an inflammatory process. The muscle shifts from simply being fatigued to becoming inflamed and breaking down."

This cascade explains why fatigue worsens over time without treatment. Each episode of fatigue releases PUFA that suppresses the thyroid function needed to prevent future fatigue. The person feels worse despite resting, because the underlying metabolic suppression continues even during rest.

## PUFA and Mental Fatigue

The brain is particularly vulnerable to PUFA-induced energy failure. In a [[2011-12-15-eastwest-healing-eastwest-healing-questions-and-answers-ii|2011 episode]], Peat connected PUFA to cognitive fatigue: "The polyunsaturated fats interfere with attention and increase adrenaline, the sympathetic nervous system, and the stress hormones. That, along with hypothyroidism, keeps the front part of the brain from getting enough energy to focus."

The difficulty concentrating, the mental fog, the inability to sustain attention—these are not psychological but metabolic. The frontal lobes require substantial energy for executive function, and when PUFA and hypothyroidism restrict energy production, these high-demand functions fail first.

## Correcting the Metabolic Basis

Peat's approach to fatigue addresses energy production directly:

**Thyroid optimization** is primary. Temperature and pulse monitoring guides treatment—the goal is waking temperature approaching 98.6°F with a midday pulse around 80-85 bpm.

**PUFA elimination** removes the metabolic brake that suppresses thyroid function and blocks glucose oxidation.

**Adequate [[sugar]] intake** provides the glucose that cells need for efficient oxidation. [[Fruit]], [[orange juice]], and [[milk]] prevent the [[hypoglycemia]] that forces reliance on stress hormones for energy.

**[[Coconut oil]]** provides medium-chain fats that bypass the Randle cycle and boost energy production through thyroid-independent mechanisms.

**[[Caffeine]]** with food increases metabolic rate and can bridge the gap while underlying problems are addressed.

**[[Aspirin]]** blocks the inflammatory prostaglandins from PUFA that contribute to tissue damage during fatigue episodes.
