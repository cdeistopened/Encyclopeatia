# Depression

The pharmaceutical industry's framing of depression as a "serotonin deficiency" requiring drugs that increase serotonin represents one of the great inversions of modern medicine. In Ray Peat's framework, [[serotonin]] is not a "feel-good" neurotransmitter but a stress mediator—and depressed people typically have *too much* serotonin, not too little. The drugs designed to "treat" depression by increasing serotonin exposure often worsen the underlying metabolic dysfunction while masking symptoms.

As Peat explained in a [[2011-02-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-inflammation-ii|2011 episode]], "The Prozac-type drugs keep the platelets and nerve cells from binding and keeping serotonin out of the way, leading to overexposure."

## The Serotonin Myth

The "serotonin deficiency" theory of depression was never supported by direct evidence—it was a marketing construct built backward from the observation that SSRIs affected mood in some patients. Peat consistently argued the opposite: elevated serotonin is associated with depression, not its absence.

In a [[2019-10-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-brain-barriers|2019 episode]], Peat connected serotonin elevation to [[thyroid]] deficiency: "When thyroid function goes down, serotonin goes up. Either removing the thyroid or giving serotonin causes the energy failure that leads to a breakdown of the barrier function, leaking of substance, and eventually atrophy."

The Hans Selye research Peat cited was striking: Selye "could experimentally produce muscular dystrophy just by giving an animal serotonin." The same stress mediator promoted as a mood enhancer causes tissue degeneration when elevated chronically.

## The Thyroid-Depression Connection

[[Hypothyroidism]] produces depression through multiple mechanisms. Low thyroid function reduces cellular energy production throughout the brain, impairing every aspect of neural function. It elevates serotonin (which is suppressive, not stimulating). It raises [[estrogen]] (which further suppresses thyroid and elevates serotonin). It increases [[cortisol]] (catabolic and brain-damaging over time).

As Peat explained in a [[2020-02-17-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-menopause-estrogen-thyroid-coronavirus-and-glaucoma|2020 episode]], "If your adrenal and thyroid hormones are low, your energy is low, making everything stressful. Serotonin is a universal indicator of stress. It, along with estrogen, acts like beating a tired horse—they increase functions temporarily but risk harming vital functions."

The practical implication: checking and correcting thyroid function should precede any psychiatric treatment for depression.

## The Gut-Brain Axis

Peat emphasized that most serotonin originates in the intestine, not the brain. In a [[2019-06-01-jodellefit-jodellefit-cortisol-low-testosterone|2019 episode]], he explained: "In the alimentary canal, histamine and serotonin are massively produced. 95% of our serotonin comes from the intestine."

Intestinal inflammation—from [[PUFA]], [[endotoxin]], or problematic foods—increases serotonin production. This gut-derived serotonin affects the brain through the vagus nerve and through systemic circulation. Depression arising from intestinal dysfunction is not "all in your head"—it has a physical source in the gut.

## Why 5-HTP Is Harmful

When asked about 5-HTP (5-hydroxytryptophan), a "natural" supplement promoted for depression, Peat was direct: "Plain [[tryptophan]] is bad enough, but 5-hydroxy-tryptophan is one step closer to being serotonin."

The supplement bypasses the body's regulatory mechanisms, directly increasing serotonin synthesis. It addresses the supposed "deficiency" that doesn't exist while worsening the actual excess that contributes to depression.

## Correcting the Metabolic Basis

Peat's approach to depression addresses root causes rather than manipulating neurotransmitters:

**Thyroid optimization** restores cellular energy throughout the brain, naturally reducing the stress-driven serotonin elevation. Many depressed patients are functionally hypothyroid.

**[[Progesterone]]** opposes both estrogen and serotonin, providing neurosteroid support that prescription antidepressants cannot.

**Blood sugar stability** prevents the [[hypoglycemia]] that triggers stress hormones and serotonin release. Adequate [[sugar]] intake from [[fruit]] and [[orange juice]] maintains the glucose the brain requires.

**PUFA elimination** reduces intestinal inflammation and the serotonin production it drives.

**[[Aspirin]]** and **[[cyproheptadine]]** can provide symptomatic relief by blocking serotonin's effects while root causes are addressed.
