# Bipolar Disorder (Manic-Depressive Cycle)

> Parent: [[mental-health-cluster]]. See also [[mania]] (manic phase deep dive), [[anxiety-depression.research]].

## Bottom line

Peat does not treat bipolar as a discrete brain disease. He treats it as one of several manifestations of a broader "brain stress syndrome" — the manic and depressive phases are different expressions of the same underlying state: cellular respiration failing under chronically elevated adrenalin, cortisol, serotonin, and estrogen, with insufficient thyroid, progesterone, CO₂, and ATP. The most useful Peat-specific diagnostic in the corpus is morning-temperature behavior, and the most useful intervention frame is mineral-and-energy stabilization.

## The diagnostic Peat actually gives

From *Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities* (2009):

> "While the early morning body temperature will sometimes be low in hypothyroidism, I have found many exceptions to this. In protein deficiency, sodium deficiency, in menopause with flushing symptoms, and **in both phases of the manic depression cycle**, and in some schizophrenics, the morning temperature is high, corresponding to very high levels of adrenalin and cortisol. Taking the temperature before and after breakfast will show a reduction of temperature, the opposite of what occurs in simple hypothyroidism, because raising the blood sugar permits the adrenalin and cortisol to fall."

This gives a free at-home test: for ~5 days, log waking temp + pulse, then re-measure 30–60 minutes after a salty/sugary breakfast.

| Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|
| Morning temp low → rises after breakfast | Simple hypothyroidism |
| Morning temp **high** → drops after breakfast | Stress-driven (adrenalin/cortisol) — fits both phases of bipolar, some schizophrenia, menopause flushing, sodium/protein deficiency |
| Morning temp normal, no shift | Probably not metabolic |

## The phase asymmetry

Per Judd et al. (cited in transcripts), Bipolar I patients spend ~53% asymptomatic, ~32% depressed, ~15% manic/mixed; Bipolar II ~45% asymptomatic, ~50% depressed, ~5% hypomanic. Most clinical suffering is in the depressive trough, not the manic peak — but the manic phase is what triggers acute care and hospitalization.

## What's actually elevated in mania (Peat's frame)

- **Adrenalin / noradrenalin** — drives the high morning temperature, racing thoughts, reduced sleep need, vasoconstriction
- **Cortisol** — secondary to adrenalin and to CRH
- **Serotonin** — Peat is heterodox here: serotonin is *not* low in depression, it is generally elevated in stress states; mania may involve serotonin release from platelets and gut
- **Estrogen** in women, particularly around the cycle and perimenopause
- **CRH** — corticotropin-releasing hormone, more directly tied to mood disturbance than cortisol itself
- **Endotoxin / lactate** — gut-derived stressors that drive serotonin and inflammation centrally
- **Free fatty acids** — PUFA-derived, displace glucose oxidation (Randle cycle)

## Lever order (Peat-style)

For a stable patient considering metabolic-first interventions in addition to (not instead of) any prescribed mood stabilizer:

1. **Thyroid** — track waking + post-breakfast temp/pulse. If high-stress-low-thyroid pattern, supplemental T₃/T₄ or supportive nutrition (selenium, iodine, copper, salt).
2. **Sodium + sugar at meals and bedtime** — both lower adrenalin and raise body temperature; Peat is explicit that "salty and sugary foods taken at bedtime, or during the night, help to improve the quality and duration of sleep."
3. **Protein adequacy** — gelatin/glycine to balance tryptophan; correct outright protein deficiency (which itself produces the high-morning-temp pattern).
4. **Progesterone + pregnenolone** — neurosteroids; allopregnanolone (progesterone metabolite) is the endogenous GABA-A modulator. Allopregnanolone-derived brexanolone is FDA-approved for postpartum depression — partial mainstream vindication.
5. **DHEA** — Peat recommended pregnenolone + DHEA together in a depression-case email reply.
6. **Reduce PUFA** — polyunsaturated fats raise free fatty acid release, increase tryptophan-to-brain transport (→ serotonin), and amplify estrogen.
7. **Reduce endotoxin** — daily raw carrot, lower fermentable fibers, calcium-rich foods (milk, cheese, fruit).
8. **CO₂ retention** — bag breathing, altitude, acetazolamide where indicated; CO₂ inhibits serotonin release and stabilizes nerve cells.
9. **Aspirin** — anti-inflammatory, anti-prostaglandin, opposes estrogen.
10. **Light + warmth + deep slow-wave sleep** — torpor pattern (shared across hypothyroid/aged/depressed/schizophrenic) reverses with thyroid + body temperature.

## On lithium specifically

Peat explicitly puts lithium on the list of effective therapies for "schizophrenia" alongside thyroid, penicillin, hyperbaric oxygen, CO₂, acetazolamide, and vitamins. His mechanistic frame is striking:

> "Lithium is immediately above sodium [in the periodic table], meaning that it has the chemical properties of sodium, but with a smaller atomic radius, which makes its electrical charge more intense. Its physiological effects are so close to sodium's that we can get clues to sodium's actions by watching what lithium does. Chronic consumption of lithium blocks the release of adrenalin from the adrenal glands, and it also has extensive antiserotonin effects, inhibiting its release from some sites, and blocking its actions at others… Lithium has been demonstrated to restore the energy metabolism of mitochondria."

In a direct email reply when asked about low-dose lithium for mood volatility, his redirect was:

> "Good nutrition and thyroid function are the basic things. Keeping a record of waking and midday temperature and pulse rate for a few days can usually detect a thyroid metabolic problem, that could be destabilizing mineral metabolism."

So: lithium is permissible and even mechanistically coherent within the Peat frame (sodium-mimic + anti-adrenalin + anti-serotonin + mitochondrial-energy-restoring), but the diagnostic question Peat would ask first is whether the underlying mineral and thyroid metabolism could be stabilized without it.

## Counter-arguments

- **Genetic loading is real.** Bipolar has ~85% twin concordance for full bipolar I; this is not just an environmental energy-state.
- **Lithium has 70+ years of RCT data** for reduced suicide and hospitalization in bipolar. Replacing lithium with metabolic interventions in a stable patient is not casually defensible without psychiatric supervision.
- **Acute mania can be life-threatening.** Bipolar is one of the conditions where Peat's "metabolic-first" frame must be additive to, not substituted for, standard care.
- **Allopregnanolone for non-postpartum bipolar** is unstudied. Brexanolone's FDA approval is for postpartum depression specifically.
- **Peat's morning-temp test** has not been validated as a bipolar diagnostic; it's a generative hypothesis from his clinical frame.

## Data gaps

- No RCTs comparing thyroid + neurosteroid + dietary protocol against lithium for bipolar.
- The "high morning temp drops after breakfast" pattern is asserted from Peat's clinical observation, not formally studied as a diagnostic.
- Endotoxin → mood causation in humans is mostly inferential from animal models.
- The question of whether mania specifically (vs. depression) responds differently to the lever order is not addressed in Peat's writing.
