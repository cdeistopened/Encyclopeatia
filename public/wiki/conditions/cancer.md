# Cancer

In Ray Peat's framework, cancer is not a thing the body produces by accident; it is a state the body slides into when its capacity for [[respiration|oxidative respiration]] decays past a threshold and its tissues stiffen with cross-linked collagen, oxidized [[pufa|polyunsaturated fat]], free iron, and unopposed [[estrogen]]. Tumors are the visible local expression of an organism-wide failure — the "cancer field" or "cancer matrix" — in which lactate, prostaglandins, fibrin breakdown products, and stress-hormone signals have rebuilt the extracellular environment into something that selects for dedifferentiation and growth. The somatic-mutation orthodoxy, which treats cancer as a clone descended from a single rogue cell, mistakes the genomic wreckage that energy-failed tissue accumulates for the disease's origin. Peat's treatment framework follows from this inversion. Rather than destroying tumor mass as a foreign body to be exterminated, the goal is to restore the metabolic field that supports differentiated function: [[thyroid]] to drive oxidation, [[progesterone]] and saturated fat to oppose estrogen, [[aspirin]] and niacinamide to suppress the lactate-prostaglandin loop, [[carbon-dioxide|CO₂]] to acidify and stabilize cells, deliberate PUFA depletion over years, and a careful refusal of the iatrogenic insults — exploratory radiation, repeated biopsy, opiate analgesia, "starving the tumor" — that mainstream oncology delivers as standard care. The Peat-frame approach is not a substitute for oncology in acute solid-tumor surgery, debulking, or the leukemias; it is adjunctive, preventive, and — most clinically — recurrence-reducing. This hub assembles the framework. Three companion articles carry the specialized loads: [[estrogen-and-cancer]] for the hormonal axis, [[cancer-treatment-iatrogenesis]] for the case against standard oncology, and [[cancer-protocol]] for the actionable clinical stack.

## What cancer is in this framework

The central claim Peat made for fifty years is that cancer is a mitochondrial-respiratory disease whose genetic abnormalities are downstream consequences, not upstream causes. The frame is Otto Warburg's. In his 1924 experiments Warburg showed that tumor slices oxidized glucose at high rates yet still poured out [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]] in the presence of oxygen — a respiratory defect that produced what later workers would call the Warburg shift. The mainstream account, consolidated in textbooks during the 1950s and codified in Hanahan and Weinberg's "Hallmarks of Cancer" series, treats the Warburg shift as a metabolic adaptation cancer cells acquire after a series of initiating mutations gives them a growth advantage. Peat's reading, which the [[warburg-effect|Warburg effect]] page reconstructs in full, runs the causal arrow the other way: respiration fails first, the cell shifts to lactate production because it has lost the capacity to fully oxidize sugar, and the genomic instability that mainstream pathology reads as "the disease" is what tissues do when they have been forced into chronic glycolysis. As Peat put it in [[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy in its Origin and Treatment* (2013)]], "the presence of mutations is neither sufficient nor necessary for causing cancer, but tumors do eventually accumulate serious damage, which causes most of the tumor cells to die quickly. Biological stress, or excitotoxic energy deprivation, destabilizes the genome; genetic changes develop as a result of prolonged destructive influences."

The corollary is that the population from which tumors arise is much larger than the population of people called "cancer patients." Harry Rubin, the Berkeley cell biologist whose work Peat returned to repeatedly, was told by a pathologist that he had found diagnosable cancer in the tissues of every person over fifty he had autopsied — people killed in accidents, not people who died of cancer. The implication, Peat wrote, is that "if everyone has cancer by the age of 50, that means that cancer is harmless for most people, and that small cancers might frequently appear, and be spontaneously removed as part of the body's regular house-cleaning. One of the reasons that spontaneous regression of tumors seems so rare is undoubtedly that most tumors are quickly cut out by surgeons." A 2004 study cited in [[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix* (2015)]] found that 36% of women who had had a breast removed seven to twenty-two years earlier still had measurable circulating tumor cells in their blood, held in dormancy by tissue conditions that did not support their growth. The disease, on this reading, is not the presence of abnormal cells; it is the systemic state that breaks dormancy and allows them to proliferate.

The third element of the framework is that the tumor and the tissue around it constitute an organ-like structure — what Peat called the cancer matrix or cancer field — that has more in common with a failing heart or a chronic wound than with a foreign body. "Once it is accepted that cancer is a systemic disease, and that a tumor, or the place in the body where a tumor has been removed, is something more than a collection of defective cells, very different therapeutic approaches can be considered. Looking at the events in a failing heart, we can see that the potential repair cells recruited by the stressed heart are diverted by the conditions that they encounter there, and either die or become connective tissue cells, secreting collagen, rather than becoming new muscle cells" ([[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]). The same logic applies to the tumor site: cells recruited to a region of chronic injury, lactate, and stiff collagen are diverted from their normal trajectory and reconstitute the tumor when a local excision has been done. This is why, in Peat's reading, cancers return after surgical "cure" and why the surgical wound itself can become carcinogenic — the systemic conditions that produced the original tumor have not changed.

## The major cancer types in Peat's writing

Peat wrote on most of the common cancers but treated them as expressions of a single bioenergetic field rather than as distinct diseases. The unifying axis is hormonal-metabolic: tissues that are heavily estrogen-dependent or whose progenitor cells are heavily stress-hormone-sensitive develop tumors first.

**Breast** is the most heavily documented. Peat traced the post-1950 rise in breast-cancer incidence to the combination of estrogen replacement therapy, polyunsaturated cooking oils, atmospheric radiation from the atomic-bomb era, and the prenatal imprinting these caused on a generation of mothers. The dietary, hormonal, and radiation cases against breast cancer are bundled in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer* (2006)]] and reassembled with the political-history layer in [[estrogen-and-cancer]]. The single most-confirmed observation in Peat's reading of the breast-cancer literature is that surgical outcome is dramatically worse when mastectomy is performed in the follicular (estrogen-high, progesterone-low) phase of the menstrual cycle than in the luteal phase — an effect that mainstream cardiology and oncology have, as Peat noted, declined to investigate because it "doesn't involve lucrative products."

**Prostate** received its own newsletter ([[prostate-cancer|*Prostate Cancer* (2013)]]). Peat's framing is the inverse of the mainstream androgen-driven model: in the older man the relevant hormone is estrogen, not testosterone. Estrogen rises as testosterone falls; aromatase activity in fat tissue accelerates the conversion; and the prostate, like the breast, becomes a site of fibrosis, collagen cross-linking, and inflammation. The "androgen deprivation" treatment that mainstream urology delivers to advanced prostate cancer patients lowers testosterone but does not lower estrogen — and in the 1990s, when cancer specialists were asked what they would do if diagnosed with prostate cancer, "most of them said they would do nothing" ([[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]).

**Ovarian and endometrial** cancers follow the same hormonal logic and are the cleanest case study for the progesterone-as-cancer-protective story. [[a-physiological-approach-to-ovarian-cancer|*A Physiological Approach to Ovarian Cancer* (2010)]] and [[preventing-and-treating-cancer-with-progesterone|*Preventing and Treating Cancer with Progesterone* (2007)]] supply the case. The full hormonal-axis treatment is in [[estrogen-and-cancer]].

**Colon and liver** cancers connect Peat's framework to gut [[endotoxin]] and the [[liver]]'s failed estrogen-clearance capacity. The colon-cancer-aspirin literature is one of the few areas where Peat's framework has been partially absorbed by mainstream epidemiology: daily aspirin reduces colon-cancer incidence by roughly half in long-running cohorts, and the mechanism (suppression of cyclo-oxygenase and the [[prostaglandins|prostaglandin]] cascade) is exactly what Peat predicted.

**Lung** cancer is, for Peat, more about the [[pufa]] / oxidant load on a high-turnover epithelium than about tobacco specifically — a position the cigarette literature does not refute so much as fail to address. The same framework explains why incidence in non-smokers has risen in parallel with seed-oil consumption, and why vitamin E and aspirin show protective effects in the cohort studies.

**Leukemia and lymphoma** are Peat's atomic-radiation-and-childhood-leukemia story, traced from postwar atmospheric testing through prenatal imprinting onto the postwar baby boom. The mechanism is the same — oxidative damage to a high-turnover progenitor compartment — but the population dynamics differ enough that these deserve their own future articles. They are not deeply treated in this hub.

**Brain and skin** (melanoma in particular) are the contrarian outliers. Peat's brain-cancer writing is in [[aspirin-brain-and-cancer|*Aspirin, Brain, and Cancer* (2006)]] and centers on the glioma-prostaglandin-aspirin axis. His position on melanoma — that ultraviolet light is largely a marker for PUFA-induced lipid peroxidation in skin, not the proximate carcinogen — is the strongest of his contrarian cancer claims and deserves a separate future article.

## The causal cluster (Peat's six drivers)

The framework organizes the causation of cancer around six tightly linked upstream conditions, each of which has its own dedicated vault page. The point of this section is not to re-derive any of them but to show how they converge on the cancer state.

**1. Mitochondrial-respiratory damage.** The proximate cause, in Peat's reading, is the loss of [[respiration|oxidative respiration]] capacity. The cell cannot fully oxidize glucose to CO₂ and water; it produces [[lactic-acid|lactate]] instead. Mainstream pathology calls this the Warburg shift and treats it as a downstream adaptation; the [[warburg-effect]] page reconstructs the alternative reading in which the respiratory defect is primary and the genetic abnormalities follow. The damage to respiration is multifactorial: substitution of [[pufa|polyunsaturated]] fatty acids into [[cardiolipin]] (the inner mitochondrial membrane lipid that cytochrome c oxidase depends on), iron-driven oxidation of cytochromes, suppression by estrogen and serotonin, deficiency of [[thyroid]] hormone and [[carbon-dioxide]]. Once respiration fails, the cell can no longer maintain its differentiated state, and the cascade begins.

**2. Estrogen excess.** Estrogen is the single most-documented carcinogen in Peat's writing. The mechanisms are stacked: estrogen activates the [[aromatase|aromatase]] enzyme in fat tissue (generating more estrogen), it activates pyridine-nucleotide transhydrogenases that shift the cell from oxidative to glycolytic metabolism, it causes intracellular alkalinization and water uptake, it stimulates synthesis of lysyl oxidase and transglutaminase (the collagen-stiffening enzymes), and it directly stimulates the proliferation of breast, ovarian, endometrial, and (via aromatization of testosterone) prostate tissue. Peat wrote in [[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy*]]: "When a tissue is exposed to estrogen, within minutes it takes up water, and begins to synthesize fat, with a tendency to produce lactic acid at the same time. The alkalizing effect of lactic acid production is apparently what accounts for the uptake of water. Since it takes longer, at least 30 min, to produce a significant amount of new enzymes, these early changes are explained by the activation of existing enzymes by estrogen." The full hormonal-axis story is in [[estrogen-and-cancer]] and the substance-level case in [[estrogen]].

**3. PUFA and lipid peroxidation.** [[pufa|Polyunsaturated fatty acids]] are the structural carcinogens of Peat's framework. They substitute into mitochondrial cardiolipin, weakening respiration; they generate lipid-peroxidation products (4-HNE, MDA) that cross-link collagen and damage DNA; they serve as substrate for the [[prostaglandins|prostaglandin]] cascade that drives inflammation and HIF; they suppress the immune system; and they prime offspring for cancer when consumed during pregnancy and lactation. Peat cited the 1927 Bernstein-Elias finding that "rats eating a fat free diet had almost no spontaneous cancer" and the much larger subsequent animal and human literature showing the same dose-response in [[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy*]]. The PUFA story has its own dedicated page; the cancer-specific application is concentrated in the discussion of prenatal-imprinting in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]].

**4. Iron accumulation and oxidative stress.** [[iron|Iron]] accumulates in tissue across the lifespan (men more than women, until menopause) and catalyzes the Fenton-reaction oxidation of PUFA and DNA. Peat repeatedly cited the iron-mortality literature — the higher serum ferritin, the higher all-cause and cancer mortality — and recommended periodic blood donation as one of the simplest cancer-prevention interventions for men over forty. The iron / copper ratio matters more than iron alone; rising iron and falling copper together impair the cytochrome oxidase enzyme, contributing to the respiratory defect that defines the cancer state.

**5. Chronic inflammation and endotoxin.** [[inflammation]] is the systemic counterpart to the local cancer field, and gut [[endotoxin]] is its most underrated driver. Lipopolysaccharide leaking from the colon (the leak accelerated by PUFA, low thyroid, slow transit, and stress) reaches the liver via the portal vein, where it provokes Kupffer-cell activation, serotonin release, [[cortisol]] elevation, and free-fatty-acid mobilization. The chronic systemic inflammation that the mainstream literature now blames on "inflammaging" is, in Peat's reading, largely an endotoxin signal in a stressed-respiration tissue field. The intervention layer is bowel motility (raw carrot, gelatin, judicious cascara, occasional antibiotics) plus liver support; the protocol detail is in [[cancer-protocol]].

**6. Aging as cumulative respiratory decline.** The sixth driver is the umbrella that contains the other five. [[aging]] in Peat's framework is the gradual slide of every tissue into a partial Warburg state — the same metabolic failure that defines cancer, expressed in every tissue simultaneously and gradually rather than locally and catastrophically. The [[aging]] page reconstructs the cascade in detail (PUFA-cardiolipin substitution → cytochrome-oxidase loss → estrogen-cortisol dominance → fibrosis → calcification). The cancer-specific implication is that the same upstream interventions that slow aging — thyroid, progesterone, saturated fat, PUFA depletion, iron control, CO₂ elevation — also reduce cancer risk and recurrence. This is not a coincidence. They are the same intervention, applied to the same metabolic axis, at different points on the timescale.

## The cancer field and the fibrosis story

The signature Peat-frame concept that lives nowhere else in the vault is the "cancer field" — the systemic collagen-and-fibrosis-and-mechanical-stiffness milieu in which tumors arise and to which they return after local excision. The frame was anticipated by Johannes Müller in the 19th century and by J. W. Orr and D. W. Smithers in the 1940s and 50s, but it was Hans Selye's foreign-body experiments and the 21st-century mechanobiology literature (Ingber, Engler, Reinhart-King) that supplied the mechanism.

The center of the story is collagen — specifically, the *amount* of collagen in the body and its *stiffness*, both of which rise with age and rise faster in people who develop cancer. Peat wrote in [[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]: "The total collagen content of the body increases with aging, and the stiffness of that collagen also increases. The total collagen content in cancer patients is higher than in people without cancer. This suggests that the processes in the body that produce aging are acting more intensely in those who develop cancer. As the collagen accumulates in the extracellular matrix, the whole body becomes more favorable for the appearance of cancer." The clinical signature is "breast density" — high collagen content in mammary tissue — which the mammography literature has known for decades is a stronger predictor of breast-cancer risk than any other imaging parameter.

The mechanism that ties collagen stiffness to cancer is durotaxis. When cells are placed on a glass slide coated with collagen of varying stiffness, they migrate toward the stiffer regions and pull themselves harder against the stiffer substrate — what Don Ingber called "isotonic contraction." The contraction costs energy. The cell produces lactate to meet the demand. The lactate stimulates further collagen synthesis, lysyl-oxidase cross-linking, and new blood vessel formation. Within the same vicious cycle, the cell shifts toward the Warburg metabolism; HIF rises; prostaglandins rise; the local field becomes more cancer-permissive. Peat cited the Chae 2011 work showing that lysyl-oxidase-like-1 specifically links lactate accumulation to lung metastasis — a single enzyme is enough to invert the prognosis ([[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]).

The Selye foreign-body experiment supplies the most elegant demonstration. Selye implanted a short piece of narrow glass tubing under the skin of rats. A collagenous capsule formed around it (the standard foreign-body reaction). Inside the tube, a filament of tissue formed connecting the two ends of the capsule. When isolated from the organism, the filament aged rapidly — the same degenerative changes that develop in aged connective tissue. When Selye replaced the fluid around the filament with fresh lymph, the filament remained youthful even as the rat aged. The implication is that *isolation from the systemic regulatory environment causes accelerated aging and the collagenous capsule has a high probability of forming a cancer*. "Foreign body carcinogenesis" is the formal literature; methylcholanthrene (the classic chemical carcinogen), atomic-bomb radiation, and exogenous estrogen all stimulate the same collagen reaction and the same eventual progression to tumor. The mainstream framework treats these as separate carcinogenic mechanisms; Peat's reading is that they are one mechanism — chronic collagen accumulation in a tissue isolated from normal regulatory signals — with different proximate triggers.

This is also why surgical wounds can become carcinogenic. The wound is a region of high collagen synthesis, lactate, prostaglandins, growth-stimulating signals from dying cells, and locally elevated estrogen ("All sorts of stress, inflammation, and tissue injury increase the concentration of estrogen, both locally and systemically," [[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy*]]). Mainstream surgery has historically ignored this because the cells doctrine treats the tumor mass as the disease; once the mass is excised, the wound is considered an unrelated healing event. The Peat-frame reading is that the wound *is* the cancer field, freshly amplified, and that the recurrence of tumors at and near surgical sites is the predicted consequence.

## The Warburg signature

The molecular machinery of the Warburg state — aerobic glycolysis, lactate efflux, HCAR1 signaling, lactylation, the immune-suppressive tumor microenvironment, the angiogenic switch — is treated in detail at [[warburg-effect]] and [[lactic-acid]]. The hub-level points are three.

First, the Warburg shift is not unique to cancer. It is the metabolic signature of every state in which respiration has been compromised — hypoxia, wound healing, the embryonic blastocyst, the activated immune cell, the failing heart, the hypothyroid tissue, and the aged organism in general. Cancer is the terminal expression of this shift, not its only expression. (The eight non-cancer states the Warburg shift appears in are catalogued at [[warburg-effect]].) The corollary is that interventions that reverse the Warburg shift in non-cancer states — thyroid, methylene blue, niacinamide, [[dichloroacetate|DCA]], red light, CO₂ — should also reduce cancer risk and progression, and the literature consistently supports this prediction.

Second, lactate is not merely a metabolic waste product. It is an autocrine and paracrine signaling molecule that drives angiogenesis, suppresses immune surveillance via HCAR1 on T-cells, stimulates lysyl-oxidase-mediated collagen cross-linking, and activates HIF (which then feeds back to stabilize the glycolytic phenotype). The full molecular story — the post-2010 receptor literature, the lactylation-of-histones discovery, the sodium-lactate paradox of Ringer's solution — lives at [[lactic-acid]]. The hub-level point is that *suppressing lactate is a legitimate cancer therapy target*. Aspirin, thyroid, niacinamide, methylene blue, DCA, and CO₂ all reduce lactate by different routes; the [[cancer-protocol]] page integrates them.

Third, the Warburg shift is reversible. Harry Rubin's cell-culture work and Mintz's mouse-blastocyst experiments showed that cancer cells, when placed in a normal regulatory environment, can revert to differentiated behavior. The somatic-mutation framework cannot accommodate this; on its account, the mutations are irreversible and the cancer phenotype must be permanent. Reversibility is the strongest single piece of evidence that the genetic abnormalities are downstream, not upstream — and it is the warrant for treating cancer as a tissue-field problem rather than a cell-clone problem.

## Cancer as terminal aging

The [[aging]] article reconstructs in full the cascade by which the body slides into the Warburg state across decades: PUFA substitutes into cardiolipin, cytochrome oxidase loses activity, estrogen and cortisol become unopposed, collagen accumulates and stiffens, mitochondrial CO₂ output falls, lactate rises, every tissue gradually loses the capacity for differentiated function. The cancer-specific reading of this cascade is that cancer is the local terminal expression of the same axis. The tissue that fails first — usually a heavily estrogen-dependent, heavily PUFA-loaded, mechanically stressed tissue with high cell turnover — becomes the cancer site. The rest of the body is on the same trajectory at a slower rate.

The clinical implication is that cancer-prevention interventions and aging-reversal interventions are the same interventions. Thyroid restoration, PUFA depletion, [[progesterone]] in women and adequate testosterone (with aromatase suppression) in men, [[aspirin]] daily for the prostaglandin-suppression and anti-fibrotic effects, [[vitamin-e]] and [[vitamin-k]] for the antioxidant and clotting-modulation effects, CO₂ elevation through bag breathing or altitude, [[caffeine]] for the respiratory and anti-estrogenic effects, sunlight and red light for the cytochrome-oxidase support, [[methylene-blue]] in microdoses for the same — every item on this list shows up on both the aging-reversal protocol and the cancer-prevention protocol because they target the same underlying metabolic axis. The [[cancer-protocol]] page integrates the dose ranges and phasing.

The further implication is that cancer mortality cannot be reduced significantly without reducing the aging-equivalent mortality from heart disease, diabetes, and dementia. Mainstream oncology has spent fifty years and hundreds of billions of dollars pursuing cancer as if it were a separable disease. Peat's reading is that the war on cancer was always unwinnable on its own terms because it treated the symptom as the disease. Reducing cancer mortality at the population level requires the same intervention as reducing all degenerative-disease mortality at the population level: change the diet, suppress estrogen exposure, reduce iron load, support [[respiration|respiration]].

## Iatrogenesis: how mainstream treatment can make things worse

The mainstream oncology framework treats cancer as a foreign body to be exterminated. The three modalities — surgery, radiation, chemotherapy — are designed to destroy tumor mass. In Peat's reading, each modality also damages the metabolic field in ways that promote recurrence, and the cumulative effect of aggressive treatment is often worse than the disease the treatment was supposed to cure. The full case is in [[cancer-treatment-iatrogenesis]]; the hub-level points are below.

**Surgery** is the oldest modality and the one most clearly justified for solid-tumor debulking. Peat acknowledged this. But the surgical wound is itself a cancer-promoting environment — the lactate, prostaglandins, growth signals, locally elevated estrogen, and freshly stressed collagen that follow any incision create exactly the field in which dormant micrometastases break dormancy. Surgical stress also stimulates tumor growth via systemic catecholamine, cortisol, and prolactin release. The luteal-phase-vs-follicular-phase mastectomy outcome data are the cleanest evidence: identical surgery, different hormonal milieu, dramatically different long-term survival ([[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]], [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]]).

**Radiation** is the modality Peat opposed most consistently. The radiation-bystander-effect literature (Mothersill, Seymour, and the 2000s revisions) showed that ionizing radiation does not only damage the cells it hits — it causes injured cells to emit excitatory, inflammatory substances (serotonin, nitric oxide, prostaglandins) that injure neighboring cells. *In vivo* the injured cells also trigger systemic estrogen synthesis, cortisol release, and the migration of repair cells into the irradiated region. The mainstream framework treats the bystander effect as just another mutagenic mechanism; Peat's reading is that it is the experimental smoking gun for the systemic-cancer-field theory and the strongest case against radiation as a first-line modality. "Even the amount of radiation in a single dental x-ray is enough to activate the excitatory-inflammatory processes, and a 'therapeutic' x-ray to any part of the body excites similar, but much greater, processes throughout the body" ([[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy*]]).

**Chemotherapy** raises hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF), drives the same selective pressure that the Warburg shift selects for, and produces resistant clones in exactly the predicted way. "HIF is recognized as a fundamental problem in 'cancer therapy,' since HIF allows the cancer to resist the treatment, but the treatment increases HIF" ([[cancer-disorder-and-energy|*Cancer: Disorder and Energy*]]). The collagen-deposition effects of cytotoxic drugs amplify the same field problem; the bone-marrow toxicity adds an immune-surveillance failure on top of an already-suppressed immune state.

**Hormone-replacement therapy in cancer-prone populations** is the iatrogenesis case Peat made most aggressively. The Women's Health Initiative trial in 2002 found that estrogen-plus-progestin therapy increased breast cancer, stroke, blood clots, and dementia, and the U.S. saw a 7% decline in breast-cancer incidence in the three years after HRT use crashed. When mainstream gynecology rehabilitated HRT (with the "estrogen alone is fine" maneuver, then the "bioidentical estrogen is different" maneuver), breast-cancer incidence began rising again. The political history is in [[estrogen-and-cancer]]; the regulatory-capture frame is in [[regulatory-capture]].

**Aggressive screening that drives over-treatment** is the most volume-dependent iatrogenesis. The mammography literature documents a >1000% increase in ductal-carcinoma-*in-situ* diagnosis with no change in breast-cancer mortality — i.e., a vast expansion of "cancer" diagnosis with no benefit to the women diagnosed, accompanied by hundreds of thousands of unnecessary mastectomies, radiation courses, and chemotherapies ([[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]]). The PSA-prostate-cancer literature shows the same pattern. The post-1991 American Cancer Society "cure rate" statistics that Peat dismantled in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]] were built largely on the expansion of what counts as cancer.

## What Peat coordinated with mainstream care

Peat's framework is not a substitute for oncology in the acute setting. He acknowledged this explicitly in correspondence and in the newsletters. The framework operates in three distinct lanes.

**Prevention** is the lane where the framework is most clinically actionable and has the strongest population-scale evidence. The dietary, hormonal, and lifestyle interventions described in [[cancer-protocol]] are the same interventions Peat recommended for general metabolic health; the cancer-specific evidence is largely epidemiological (low-PUFA populations, traditional cycle-phase eating populations, sunlight-rich populations all have lower cancer rates) and mechanistic.

**Adjunct** during active treatment. Peat consistently advised patients receiving conventional treatment to optimize the metabolic field around the treatment — adequate sugar and protein during chemotherapy (against the "starve the tumor" instinct, which the Sauer-Dauchy and Schlappack literature shows accelerates metastasis), aspirin and progesterone to suppress the lactate-prostaglandin loop, thyroid to support respiration, CO₂ elevation, vitamin E and vitamin K for clotting modulation. The patient who is going through chemotherapy on a metabolically supportive protocol is, on Peat's reading, more likely to tolerate the treatment, less likely to develop resistance, and less likely to suffer the long-term cardiotoxic and cognitive sequelae.

**Recurrence reduction and post-treatment recovery** is the lane where the framework has the most distinctive contribution. The dormancy literature — circulating tumor cells held quiescent by tissue conditions, 36% of post-mastectomy women still carrying them years later — implies that recurrence prevention is largely a field-management problem. The interventions are the same as the prevention interventions, applied with more discipline.

Peat acknowledged the surgical-resection imperative for solid tumors, the role of debulking in patients with high tumor burden, and the role of conventional treatment for acute leukemias and aggressive lymphomas where the natural history would be rapid death without intervention. He did not advise patients to refuse oncology; he advised them to coordinate it with metabolic restoration and to refuse the iatrogenic excess (repeat biopsy, exploratory radiation, opiate analgesia, "starvation" diets, HRT in survivors) where the evidence for benefit was thin.

## The cancer industry critique

Peat's critique of the cancer industry has four distinct layers and they are worth separating.

**Statistical fraud.** The American Cancer Society's "declining cancer mortality" narrative — promulgated since the 1990s, rebuilt every few years with new "age standardization" tricks — does not survive scrutiny against the crude age-specific mortality data. Peat reconstructed this in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]]: cancer deaths in the U.S. rose from 505,300 in 1990 to 536,900 in 1994, faster than population growth, during the years the ACS claimed mortality was declining 0.5% per year. The trick was age-restandardization against the 1940 population (which had a disproportionately old age structure because the Depression had suppressed births) and the steady redefinition of what counts as cancer (ductal-carcinoma-*in-situ* and other "precancerous" lesions reclassified into the cancer category, then "cured" by surgery, generating spurious cure-rate increases). Dean Burk, Otto Warburg's long-time collaborator, was quoted on the ACS: "They lie like scoundrels."

**Mammography over-diagnosis.** The 1000-plus-percent increase in DCIS diagnosis since the introduction of mammography, with no corresponding change in breast-cancer mortality, is the cleanest single example of screening-driven over-treatment. Peat returned to it repeatedly in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]] and [[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]. The ionizing radiation dose from the screening itself — particularly in pre-menopausal women with dense breasts who are repeatedly exposed — is a non-trivial contributor to the cancer incidence the screening is supposed to detect.

**Surgical-stimulation-of-tumor-growth.** The Goldstein and Mascitelli 2011 paper documenting the surgery-cancer link, the cycle-phase mastectomy outcome data, and the older literature on surgery-induced catecholamine, cortisol, and prolactin release ([[the-cancer-matrix|*The Cancer Matrix*]]) constitute a literature mainstream oncology has not absorbed. G. W. Crile Jr. publicized the lumpectomy-vs-radical-mastectomy outcome data in the 1970s, and after decades of resistance the radical mastectomy was abandoned for breast cancer — but the more general implication, that surgical stress promotes tumor growth, has not been generalized.

**Regulatory capture.** The pharmaceutical-industry, NIH-curriculum, journal-advertising, and ACS-fundraising apparatus that maintains the somatic-mutation framework and suppresses the metabolic alternative is the political-economy layer of the critique. The [[regulatory-capture]] article carries the cross-industry case; the cancer-specific application overlaps the [[estrogen-and-cancer]] political history. Peat's framing, in [[breast-cancer|*Breast Cancer*]]: "The cancer industry, including major producers of chemotherapy drugs, subsidizes the American Cancer Society and 'Breast Cancer Awareness Week,' and it is in their interest to convince the public that early detection and conventional treatment with surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation are winning the war against cancer. There is always light at the end of the tunnel, in the war against cancer, just as there was in the Vietnam war. Their consistent effort to dissuade the government from acting to reduce the public's exposure to the known causes of cancer should make it clear that they are in the business of treating cancer, not eliminating it."

## The treatment framework (overview)

The actionable protocol is in [[cancer-protocol]]; the hub-level summary is below. The framework has three phases (pre-cancer prevention, active-cancer adjunct, post-treatment recovery) and operates along five intervention axes.

**Restore respiration.** Thyroid (T3 or NDT) to support oxidative metabolism, [[methylene-blue]] in microdoses as an electron-cycling agent, [[niacinamide]] to lower free fatty acids and support NAD+, [[carbon-dioxide]] elevation through bag breathing or altitude exposure, sun and red light for cytochrome-oxidase support, magnesium for ATP-dependent enzymes.

**Reduce estrogen.** [[progesterone]] (100–300 mg/day in active cancer, lower in prevention), aromatase suppression (aspirin, vitamin E, naringenin, low-PUFA diet), liver support for clearance ([[caffeine]], B-vitamins, adequate protein), bowel motility for estrogen excretion (raw carrot, gelatin, occasional cascara). In men: maintain testosterone, suppress aromatization.

**Reduce PUFA load.** This is a years-long project, not a months-long one. PUFA stored in adipose tissue and structural lipids has a half-life of years. The intervention is dietary substitution (coconut oil, butter, ghee, beef tallow) plus avoidance (no seed oils, no fish oil, no fatty fish, minimize nuts). The substitution alone is insufficient in the short term; aspirin and vitamin E protect against the released PUFA during the depletion phase.

**Suppress the lactate-prostaglandin loop.** [[aspirin]] daily (300–1500 mg, divided), [[vitamin-e]] for tocopherol-mediated antioxidant protection, [[vitamin-k]] for clotting modulation, [[caffeine]] for the adenosine and serotonin antagonism, gut-endotoxin reduction. Avoid serotonergic drugs (SSRIs in particular) in cancer patients.

**Support the metabolic field.** Adequate sugar (no carbohydrate restriction during active cancer — the Sauer-Dauchy literature is unambiguous), adequate protein (gelatin-enriched, not muscle-meat-dominant, to favor glycine over tryptophan), salt and calcium and magnesium (against the alkalosis and the chronic catecholamine drive), warmth (against the cold-induced stress hormone elevation), sleep, and the deliberate reduction of polyunsaturated, serotonergic, and estrogenic exposures.

The dose ranges and the cancer-type-specific adjustments are in [[cancer-protocol]].

## Tracking signal

In addition to standard oncology markers (tumor staging, CT/MRI imaging, tumor markers where applicable, circulating tumor DNA in research settings), the Peat-frame patient tracks the metabolic field itself.

**Temperature and pulse** — the basal metabolic rate proxy. Morning oral temperature trending toward 98.0–98.6 °F and resting pulse trending toward 75–85 are the most accessible signs that thyroid function and oxidative metabolism are recovering. Cold extremities are the warning sign.

**Mood, sleep, and energy** — the most sensitive proxy for the lactate-cortisol-estrogen axis. Anxiety, depression, insomnia, and chronic fatigue all rise as the stress-hormone state rises, and they fall first when the metabolic restoration is working.

**Weight stability** — particularly in women, weight gain in the post-menopausal period is one of the strongest risk factors for breast and endometrial cancer (because adipose tissue is the dominant site of post-menopausal estrogen synthesis). Stable weight in the desirable range, achieved without caloric restriction, is the goal.

**Lactate** — if measurable. The home dashboard described at [[lactic-acid]] (resting lactate, post-meal lactate response, post-exercise clearance) is the most direct readout of the Warburg-shift severity.

**Bowel function** — daily, formed, complete. Constipation drives endotoxin and estrogen reabsorption.

**Lab markers** — TSH below 1.5 (closer to 1.0 ideal), free T3 in the upper third of the reference range, ferritin below 100 (preferably 30–70), 25-OH vitamin D in the 50–80 ng/mL range, prolactin below 10 (in men) and within mid-range (in women), HDL above 60, triglycerides below 100.

## Counter-arguments

The dominant mainstream framework is the somatic-mutation theory consolidated by Bert Vogelstein and his collaborators since the 1980s. The framework treats cancer as a clonal disease descended from a single cell that has acquired the canonical "driver mutations" (TP53, RAS, MYC, EGFR, BRCA, etc.) plus a longer list of "passenger mutations." It is supported by extensive sequencing data, the success of targeted therapies (imatinib in CML, trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancer, EGFR inhibitors in lung), and the substantial improvements in survival for some specific cancers (childhood ALL, testicular cancer, certain lymphomas). The Peat-frame critique is not that mutations are unreal — they are unambiguously real — but that they are downstream of the metabolic-field collapse rather than upstream causes. The two frames can be partially reconciled at the *population-level prevention* lane (PUFA, estrogen, iron, smoking, alcohol reduction reduce cancer incidence regardless of which causal arrow one accepts) but diverge sharply at the *active-treatment* lane.

The immunotherapy era's "Warburg is obsolete" framing argues that PD-1 / PD-L1 / CTLA-4 checkpoint blockade and CAR-T cell therapies validate the immune-surveillance-failure model of cancer and that metabolic models are an anachronism. The Peat-frame response is that lactate is the dominant immune-suppressive signal in the tumor microenvironment (HCAR1 on T-cells, lactate-driven exhaustion) — exactly as the Warburg framework predicts — and that the checkpoint inhibitors work in the cancers where lactate suppression is biochemically reversible. The metabolic and immune frames are complementary, not competing.

The carcinogen-exposure / chemoprevention literature — tobacco, asbestos, aflatoxin, HPV, HCV, H. pylori, ionizing radiation, and the substance-specific carcinogens identified by IARC — is the strongest single piece of evidence that mainstream toxicology has been right about specific exposures. The Peat-frame integration is that the proximate carcinogens act through the same final common pathway (oxidative damage, PUFA peroxidation, inflammation, fibrosis, immune suppression) and that the population-level burden of cancer is driven mainly by *background metabolic state* — PUFA load, estrogen exposure, thyroid status, gut endotoxin — rather than by specific carcinogen exposure in most cases.

The genetic-determinism strand (BRCA, Lynch syndrome, Li-Fraumeni, the hereditary cancer syndromes) is a legitimate exception that the Peat framework does not deny. Mutations in DNA-repair pathways and tumor-suppressor genes do unambiguously raise cancer risk in carriers. The framework accommodates them by noting that even highly penetrant hereditary mutations express their cancer risk against a metabolic background — BRCA-positive women in low-estrogen, low-PUFA, traditional-cycle populations have historically had lower breast-cancer rates than BRCA-positive women in modern Western populations.

The mainstream "do everything as early as possible" treatment philosophy is the most defensible piece of standard oncology and the piece that the Peat framework most aggressively rejects. The defense rests on the early-stage survival curves for cancers where early intervention demonstrably saves lives — colorectal cancer with effective screening and polypectomy, testicular cancer with platinum-based therapy, certain leukemias. The Peat-frame reply is that the curves are dominated by cancers whose natural history is rapid and where local intervention is effective, and that the framework still applies to those cancers as adjunctive support; the disagreement is sharpest in cancers (slow-growing prostate, low-grade breast DCIS, indolent lymphomas, many thyroid cancers) where the over-treatment harms are largest and the natural-history-with-supportive-care outcomes are competitive.

## Data gaps

The Peat framework does not have RCT-grade survival-curve data competitive with modern oncology and it does not pretend to. The strongest evidence is mechanistic (Warburg's biochemistry, the lactate-signaling literature, the radiation-bystander-effect data, the cycle-phase mastectomy outcomes, the WHI breast-cancer crash) and epidemiological (low-PUFA populations, low-iron populations, traditional sunlight-and-warmth populations). It does not have a five-year survival curve from a phase-3 trial of "metabolic restoration vs. standard of care" in any cancer.

The framework is also weakest at *cancer-type stratification*. The estrogen-dependent cancers (breast, endometrial, ovarian, much of prostate) are the cleanest fit; the framework also fits colorectal and hepatic and many gynecological cancers well; it is more strained against the leukemias and lymphomas (which have their own immune-system and prenatal-radiation stories), the pediatric cancers (where Peat's writings are thin), and the rare cancers (small-cell lung, sarcomas, some brain cancers) where the natural history is so aggressive that the metabolic-field intervention has limited time to act.

There is no large-scale cohort study following Peat-protocol patients with biopsy-confirmed cancer through standardized survival endpoints. The case-report literature is anecdotal. The clinical-experience base is dispersed across the practitioner network that worked with Peat directly (and corresponded with him until his death in 2022) and is largely unpublished. The companion-volume project for which this hub is foundational identifies this as the single largest research gap in the framework.

The dosing literature for individual interventions (aspirin in cancer, niacinamide in cancer, methylene blue in cancer, DCA in cancer, progesterone in cancer) is uneven — strongest for aspirin (where mainstream epidemiology has independently confirmed the prevention effect), reasonable for DCA (where the University of Alberta trials supplied some clinical data before the program was wound down), thin for the others. The [[cancer-protocol]] page gives the dose ranges Peat actually used in correspondence, with the caveat that these are ranges, not RCT-validated point estimates.

The framework's safety claims (that the supportive metabolic restoration is harmless even if it does not affect tumor outcome) are well-grounded for most interventions but should not be over-stated. Progesterone is broadly safe in women; testosterone supplementation in older men requires aromatase monitoring; high-dose aspirin requires gastric-protection co-administration; methylene blue is incompatible with SSRIs (serotonin syndrome); thyroid replacement is dose-sensitive. The protocol is not "free."
