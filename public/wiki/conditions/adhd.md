# ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity)

> Parent: [[mental-health-cluster]]. See also [[hypothyroidism]], [[bipolar]].

## Bottom line

Peat lists "the hyperactivity-attention deficit syndrome" in the same brain-stress-syndrome family as schizophrenia, depression, and manic depression. His physiological explanation for why Ritalin "paradoxically" calms hyperactive children is the high-leverage frame here: the prefrontal cortex has extraordinary energy requirements, and when it can't meet them, executive function fails, motor inhibition fails, and the child is left with the lower-brain output. A stimulant restores enough frontal-cortex energy supply for executive function to come back online.

## The Peat-specific quote

From *Insomnia & Hyperactivity*:

> "Since the 1960s, a stimulant, Ritalin (methylphenidate) has often been prescribed for hyperactive kids, because it made them able to quietly pay attention. This effect was called 'paradoxical,' but — for scientific physiology — there was nothing paradoxical about it. The frontal lobes of the brain, the most highly evolved part, give us the ability to plan and to understand complex things that require prolonged attention. Without this higher part of the brain which has a very high energy requirement…"

The implication: the same restoration-of-frontal-energy logic that makes stimulants work would also make food, sleep, warmth, and thyroid work — they're cheaper and have better long-term profiles, but the mechanism is the same.

## Why this places ADHD in the brain-stress family

> "The idea that schizophrenia is a disease in itself tends to distract attention from the things it has in common with Alzheimer's disease, autism, depression, mania, the manic-depressive syndrome, **the hyperactivity-attention deficit syndrome**, and many other physical and mental problems." — *Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities*

Common features across the family: reduced deep slow-wave sleep, elevated adrenalin/cortisol, reduced prefrontal glucose metabolism, elevated serotonin, sensitivity to PUFA and endotoxin.

## Levers for ADHD (Peat-style)

1. **Frequent salty + sugary meals** — keep blood sugar stable, prevent the adrenalin spikes that drop frontal energy.
2. **Adequate protein** — gelatin/glycine; avoid soy (Peat-cited animal data: soy protein increases aggression in monkeys and chickens).
3. **Sleep + warmth** — high body temperature improves deep slow-wave sleep; hot bath, warm feet at bedtime.
4. **Reduce PUFA** — polyunsaturated fats amplify free-fatty-acid mobilization, displacing glucose oxidation in the brain.
5. **Reduce endotoxin** — daily raw carrot, low fermentable fiber, calcium-rich foods.
6. **Thyroid** — track waking + post-breakfast temp/pulse; subclinical hypothyroidism is common in children with ADHD-like presentations.
7. **Coffee + B1 (thiamine)** — Peat-cited as supporting healthy frontal-cortex function (from the *Hallucinations* email frame).
8. **Niacinamide** — supports NAD/NADH balance, oxidative metabolism.
9. **Sunlight, recess, outdoor movement** — cited in the corpus by adjacent voices (RLM, alternative-education guests) as central; matches Peat's general "high metabolic environment" frame.

## On stimulants (Ritalin, Adderall, Vyvanse) in the Peat frame

Stimulants do work mechanistically — they raise frontal-lobe energy supply long enough for executive function to come online. Peat's implicit critique is the same as his lithium critique: they are downstream tools, not the first lever. The corpus also includes RLM-podcast adjacent material noting that a measurable percentage of cocaine-addiction-program patients started on Ritalin as children — a long-tail risk that pushes toward food/sleep/thyroid first.

## What Peat would *not* recommend

- **L-tyrosine** (dopamine precursor) — likely overshoots the same axis as the stimulant
- **High-PUFA diet** (including fish oil, despite the popular ADHD literature)
- **Long fasting / ketogenic protocols in children** — pushes free fatty acids and cortisol
- **L-tryptophan / 5-HTP** — increases serotonin (which is already part of the brain-stress profile)
- **Soy-protein-heavy diets** — Peat-cited animal aggression data

## Counter-arguments

- ADHD has substantial genetic loading (60-90% twin heritability) and clear structural brain differences (reduced striatal volume, delayed cortical maturation). The energy-deficit frame may be downstream of these, not primary.
- Stimulant treatment of childhood ADHD has good long-term outcome data (academic, occupational, accident reduction); Peat's frame doesn't argue against this strongly, but the corpus lacks his explicit position on whether to *use* stimulants in any given child.
- Many ADHD children have *normal* thyroid panels by standard labs; the Peat morning-temp test is not a validated ADHD diagnostic.
- The "soy → aggression" point is from animal studies; the species translation is weak.

## Data gaps

- No formal Peat-style protocol study in pediatric ADHD.
- The relationship between subclinical hypothyroidism and ADHD diagnosis is studied but inconclusive; many ADHD kids don't have thyroid markers.
- Whether the gut-endotoxin axis is causal for ADHD specifically (vs. correlated) is an active research question.
