# Arthritis

Rheumatology divides arthritis into roughly a hundred named diseases distinguished by serology, joint distribution, and HLA type. Osteoarthritis is "wear-and-tear" cartilage breakdown. Rheumatoid arthritis is a "long-lasting autoimmune disorder" attacking the synovium symmetrically. Lupus arthritis is one of eleven diagnostic criteria for SLE. Ankylosing spondylitis is an HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathy. Psoriatic arthritis is the joint component of a skin disease. Each name comes with its own drug class — DMARDs and biologics for RA, NSAIDs and joint replacement for OA, immunosuppressants for lupus, TNF inhibitors for the spondyloarthropathies — and each receives separate research funding and separate patient organizations.

Peat refused the partition. "First of all, I don't make a clear distinction between any kind of arthritis, or even between heart failure, atherosclerosis, and various degenerative diseases," he told Andrew Murray on the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|2016 *Ask the Herb Doctor* episode on rheumatoid arthritis]]. "The interesting thing is to explain how a few basic metabolic problems have these very different-looking expressions." The basic metabolic problems recur on every page of the [[ray-peat|Peat]] corpus — [[inflammation]] driven by [[pufa|polyunsaturated fat]] peroxidation, [[estrogen]] excess relative to [[progesterone]], [[serotonin]] amplification of prostaglandin signaling, gut-derived [[endotoxin]] flooding past a compromised liver, hypothyroid suppression of [[oxidative-metabolism|oxidative respiration]] with compensatory [[lactic-acid|lactic acid]] generation, and a depleted protective steroid pool. Joint tissue is one of many compartments in which this state expresses itself. The synovium gets it; so does the heart, the brain, the breast, and the colon. The autoimmune description — rheumatoid factor antibodies, anti-CCP, immune cell infiltrates — describes what the body is doing about a metabolic disaster, not what caused it.

The clinical implication is that almost every arthritis case improves on a relatively short protocol: aspirin daily, thyroid replacement to a normal pulse and temperature, [[gelatin]]/[[glycine]] to balance the stress amino acids, sugar and salt to lower cortisol, progesterone and DHEA topically or orally when reproductive steroids are depleted, and PUFA-elimination as the long arc. Peat held US Patent 4,628,052 (December 1986) on a topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol formulation for arthritis. The patent case series records same-day pain relief in disabled patients with severe rheumatoid and post-traumatic disease — including a 60-year-old woman with two artificial joints and a 62-year-old man with documented cartilage damage refusing surgery.

## The major arthritides

### Osteoarthritis

Orthodoxy treats OA as a mechanical problem: cumulative load wears cartilage, Bouchard's and Heberden's nodes form, bone-on-bone contact produces pain, and joint replacement is the terminal therapy. Estrogen deficiency at menopause is offered for the female preponderance. The intervention pathway is NSAIDs, physical therapy, steroid injections, then surgery.

Peat read OA as the joint expression of a chronically inflammatory metabolic state, not a mechanical one. Mechanically stressed joints fail first, but they fail because their tissue is rendered fragile by the same processes that thin bones in osteoporosis and stiffen arteries in atherosclerosis. He noted in the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|2016 RA discussion]] that the conventional story collapses on inspection: "I started reading about osteoarthritis and saw studies published in books — not medical journals — making a clear association between estrogen and osteoarthritis. Even though journals consistently say osteoarthritis is caused by estrogen deficiency, looking at the research on what activates the osteoclasts that cause bone breakdown, endotoxin is a major activator." The Heberden's nodes at the distal interphalangeal joints — the most reliable physical sign of "primary" OA — are not in weight-bearing joints. Their preferential involvement in postmenopausal women indicates a systemic rather than mechanical cause.

### Rheumatoid arthritis

The orthodox account is autoimmune: T-cells lose tolerance to self-antigens, antibodies (rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP) form against synovial tissue, pannus erodes cartilage and bone, with systemic consequences in vasculitis, pleurisy, pericarditis, and accelerated atherosclerosis. The intervention pathway escalates from NSAIDs through methotrexate to TNF-inhibiting biologics and JAK inhibitors. RA cardiovascular mortality runs roughly twice the population baseline.

Peat reframed each step. The cardiovascular comorbidity is not a curious side effect of joint disease; it is the same disease in a different tissue. "The known atherosclerosis associated with rheumatoid arthritis doesn't seem to involve an attack on blood vessels by antibodies, but rather something going wrong in the whole organism causing simultaneous degeneration," he told Murray in the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|RA discussion]]. The rheumatoid factor antibody was a downstream marker of cellular leak: "I see it as a sign of stress that isn't being handled. The thyroid responds to the same stress signals that the local connective tissue responds to."

The mechanism for that leak compresses into a single chain: cell under chronic energetic stress activates [[fibrosis|stress proteins]] (heat shock proteins, glucose-regulated proteins) → membrane integrity loosened by inflammation → stress proteins escape into the interstitium → become immunogenic → antibodies form against what are now "self-from-outside-the-cell" structures → the same antibodies cross-react with thyroid antigens because the thyroglobulin-specific enzyme is also expressed in joint tissue. The autoantibodies are not the cause but the cleanup signal — the immune system finding misplaced intracellular machinery and tagging it. "I don't think you need a specific virus or bacteria to explain RA; when you have an energetically precarious tissue condition, any little cause can tip it over the edge."

The female preponderance (RA is roughly three times more common in women) and the dramatic remission during pregnancy fall out of this framework. Pregnancy produces massive placental progesterone. RA "frequently completely resolves during pregnancy and returns afterward. I think it's the huge production of progesterone that the placenta takes over during pregnancy," Peat noted. The two clinical anecdotes Peat opened his 1986 newsletter with sharpen the estrogen point further. Both were women, both began with completely disabling RA, and both experienced "total relief and clearing of the swellings" within a week or two of *stopping estrogen* — one had been told by her doctor that estrogen was protective and that she needed to *raise* the dose.

### Lupus arthritis

SLE is the autoimmune disease of the female reproductive years; joint inflammation is one of its most common manifestations. Peat's framework predicts exactly this pattern: an estrogen-saturated reproductive period, often punctuated by oral contraceptive use, in a population whose [[pufa|PUFA]] stores and gut [[endotoxin]] absorption have been steadily climbing for two generations. He recorded clinical remissions in the [[2009-08-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-you-are-what-you-eat-2009|2009 *Ask the Herb Doctor* episode]] with characteristic directness: "Regarding treatment, I have seen lupus patients over the last 30 years recover completely, or at least not die of kidney failure, by using thyroid, progesterone, and avoiding polyunsaturated fat (PUFA)."

### Ankylosing spondylitis and HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathies

Ankylosing spondylitis fuses the sacroiliac joints and vertebrae in a male-predominant pattern strongly associated with HLA-B27. Reactive, enteropathic, and psoriatic arthritis share the HLA-B27 association in lesser proportions. Orthodoxy makes the HLA allele predisposing; the precipitating cause is a Gram-negative gut or genitourinary infection triggering molecular mimicry against joint antigens.

Peat's framework absorbs this picture rather than refuting it. The strong association with gut and genitourinary infection points at the same [[endotoxin]] pathway he placed at the center of his inflammation model — bacterial lipopolysaccharide flooding past a compromised mucosal barrier, generating nitric oxide overproduction and the cytokine cascade. Norman Cousins's famous self-cure of ankylosing spondylitis with vitamin C and Marx Brothers films (*Anatomy of an Illness*, 1979) reads as an extreme case of this: massive vitamin C doses oppose endotoxin's nitric oxide effects, and laughter lowers cortisol acutely. The intervention worked because the underlying lesion was a treatable energetic crisis, not an immutable HLA configuration.

### Psoriatic arthritis

Psoriasis is, in Peat's reading, a hyperproliferative skin disease driven by [[serotonin]], [[estrogen]], and [[prolactin]] in a [[pufa|PUFA]]-laden organism. Psoriatic arthritis is the same biology in synovial rather than cutaneous tissue. Peat's 1986 patent explicitly covered "arthritis associated with psoriasis" alongside RA, OA, and lupus arthritis — not as oversight but as recognition that the underlying mechanism is shared.

## Peat's causal cluster

The cluster that produces arthritis in Peat's framework is the same one this vault traces under [[inflammation]], [[heart-disease]], [[fibrosis]], and [[cancer]]. The joint-specific weighting is what differs.

**Chronic estrogen excess relative to progesterone.** Estrogen "intensifies all of these changes of hypothyroidism, increasing permeability and edema," Peat wrote in [[progesterone-not-estrogen-is-the-coronary-protection-factor-of-women|*Progesterone, Not Estrogen*]]. The same vascular permeability that enables atherosclerotic plaque enables synovial inflammation. The two cases of complete RA remission on estrogen cessation in the 1986 newsletter are the strongest clinical evidence Peat offered for arthritis as an estrogen-driven disease. The female preponderance of RA, lupus, and finger OA falls out of the same chemistry. Full mechanism at [[estrogen]].

**PUFA accumulation generating chronic prostaglandin pressure.** "If our bodies are loaded with polyunsaturated fats (PUFA) — which we don't want — that tends to increase steadily with aging. As a person increases their stores of polyunsaturated fats, every little stress becomes more reductive and more inflammatory. The prostaglandins act to amplify any little stress," Peat told Murray in the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|2016 RA discussion]]. [[arachidonic-acid|Arachidonic acid]] released from inflamed synovial membranes becomes the substrate for the COX-2 prostaglandins. Removing the substrate (PUFA elimination) is the upstream move; inhibiting one downstream enzyme (COX-2 inhibitors, Vioxx, Celecoxib) is the move with the worst risk-benefit ratio in the toolkit. Full mechanism at [[pufa]].

**Serotonin amplification.** Peat noted in the [[2019-09-20-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-education-and-reeducation|2019 *Education and Reeducation* episode]] that antiserotonin drugs are "being used for all of the things now known to be promoted by serotonin. There are anti-serotonin products starting to be studied and recommended against cancer, arthritis, autoimmune diseases, depression, psychosis, and lung disease especially." Serotonin produces the same ATP collapse and lactate generation in synovium that the [[serotonin]] page documents in brain tissue.

**Endotoxin from gut bacterial overgrowth.** "When liver function slows down, it massively gets past the liver barriers and starts poisoning your systems, increasing nitric oxide and drastically lowering oxygen metabolism," Peat said in the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|RA discussion]]. Nitric oxide impairs mitochondrial oxidative metabolism; the affected tissues shift to glycolysis, generate lactic acid, and become inflammatory. Full mechanism at [[endotoxin]].

**Hypothyroidism and low CO2.** "Hypothyroidism is a common cause of adrenal insufficiency, but it also has some direct effects on the joint tissues. In chronic hypothyroidism (myxedema and cretinism), knees and elbows are often bent abnormally," Peat noted in the [[hormone-balancing-natural-treatment-and-cure-for-arthritis|1986 *Hormone Balancing* newsletter]]. The 71-year-old man in his opening case had arthritis resolve completely in less than two weeks on thyroid extract alone — no progesterone, no DHEA, no aspirin. Full hub at [[hypothyroidism]].

**Cortisol-progesterone-DHEA exhaustion.** Selye's rats developed arthritis when their adrenal glands were exhausted. Peat extended the picture: the precursor steroids (pregnenolone, progesterone, DHEA) deplete *before* cortisol because cortisol synthesis sits at the end of the steroid pathway. A patient under prolonged stress is depleted across the entire protective steroid pool. Replacement of the precursors restores the body's capacity to make cortisol without the side-effect profile of pharmacologic cortisone (osteoporosis, immune suppression, skin atrophy, cataracts).

## What raises arthritic inflammation

- **Dietary polyunsaturated fats.** Soybean, corn, canola, safflower, sunflower, cottonseed, peanut. Hidden in restaurant food, salad dressings, baked goods, "heart-healthy" margarines. Full case at [[pufa]] and [[seed-oils]].
- **Undigested protein crossing the gut.** Bacterial overgrowth, low stomach acid, and PUFA-damaged intestinal epithelium produce a gut that absorbs intact food proteins and bacterial fragments — the immune activation miscalled "autoimmunity."
- **Chronic stress producing cortisol and adrenaline cycles.** Sustained cortisol breaks down skeletal muscle to liberate tryptophan, cysteine, and methionine; tryptophan increases [[serotonin]]; serotonin amplifies inflammation. The gelatin/glycine countermove interrupts it at the amino acid balance.
- **Hypothyroidism**, especially the subclinical form rendered invisible by the post-1948 TSH ranges. Achilles tendon reflex, basal temperature, and pulse rate detect what TSH misses.
- **Exogenous estrogen** — oral contraceptives, HRT, and xenoestrogens (BPA, phthalates, soy isoflavones). The two cases of RA cured by estrogen cessation in the 1986 newsletter are clinical pointers.
- **High-iron diets.** Iron catalyzes the peroxidation of stored PUFA.
- **Vitamin K deficiency** during chronic aspirin use. Murray's rule of thumb is 1 mg K2 per 325 mg aspirin.
- **Trauma and surgery.** The 71-year-old man Peat opened the 1986 newsletter with developed arthritis after his house fell on him; case after case in the patent files began with falls, accidents, or surgery.

## Peat's protocol

The protocol below is the consolidated arthritis recommendation from the 1986 newsletter, the 1986 patent, the 2007 *Blocking Tissue Destruction* newsletter, and the 2016 *Ask the Herb Doctor* RA discussion. Each item carries to its own vault page for dosing detail.

**Aspirin.** The single most-recommended intervention. Peat dosed in the 325–1300 mg range depending on body weight and severity, balanced by vitamin K2 (≈1 mg per 325 mg aspirin). Aspirin works on every arm of the arthritis cascade at once: it inhibits the COX enzymes generating prostaglandins from arachidonic acid, suppresses aromatase (lowering estrogen), shifts mitochondria from fat to glucose oxidation, opposes serotonin's metabolic effects, and protects against endotoxin. Full pharmacology at [[aspirin]].

**Thyroid replacement when hypothyroid.** Desiccated thyroid (Armour, Acella) preferred over T4 monotherapy because it contains active T3 directly. Dosed to normal pulse (75–90 at rest), basal temperature (98°F oral on waking, 98.6°F mid-afternoon), and resolution of cold extremities. The 71-year-old in Peat's opening 1986 case had arthritis resolve on thyroid alone. Full protocol at [[hypothyroidism]].

**Gelatin and glycine to balance stress amino acids.** Hydrolyzed gelatin at 10–30 g/day, or bone broth from joint-bearing bones (not marrow, which is high in iron). Gelatin contains essentially no tryptophan and minimal cysteine and methionine, diluting the stress-protein amino acid profile of pure muscle meat. Glycine itself opposes serotonin and inflammation. Full case at [[gelatin]].

**Cyproheptadine (Periactin).** An old antihistamine with strong anti-serotonin activity, dosed at a quarter-tablet to one tablet (1–4 mg) at bedtime. Used when serotonin signs predominate — IBS, migraine, insomnia, anxiety, or RA flares concentrated at certain phases of the day. Full protocol at [[cyproheptadine]].

**Sugar and salt to lower cortisol.** A diet adequate in [[fructose]] (orange juice, ripe fruit) and milk sugar keeps blood glucose stable and suppresses cortisol pulsing. Adequate salt (3–6 g/day) opposes the [[aldosterone]] excess that accompanies chronic stress. Both moves are upstream of the inflammatory cascade.

**Magnesium and niacinamide.** Magnesium glycinate or carbonate at 300–600 mg/day; niacinamide (not niacin) at 100–500 mg twice daily. Both lower free fatty acid release and support oxidative metabolism.

**Vitamin D, K2, A, E.** Vitamin D suppresses parathyroid hormone; target 25-OH-D serum 50–75 ng/mL. K2 is co-essential with aspirin. A is depleted by chronic stress and is required for steroid synthesis. E protects PUFA against peroxidation during the long-arc PUFA reduction.

**DHEA and progesterone — topical or oral.** Peat's patented formulation (US 4,628,052) used 5% DHEA, 80% mixed tocopherols, 10% olive oil, and up to 3% progesterone, applied to affected joints. The patent case series records same-day pain relief in disabled patients. Oral dosing was 10–50 mg DHEA and 5–60 mg progesterone, often combined with pregnenolone (10–100 mg). Full protocols at [[dhea]] and [[progesterone]].

**Topical aspirin/DMSO.** Peat was cautious about DMSO — he experimented from 1965 onward but warned about rashes, histamine release, and breakdown products. Aspirin-in-water topical (crushed aspirin in vodka) is sometimes effective for localized flares; systemic aspirin is more reliable.

**Methylene blue** at low oral doses (1–5 mg) acts as an electron carrier supporting mitochondrial oxidative metabolism. A niche tool for refractory cases. See [[methylene-blue]].

**Carrot salad daily.** One large raw carrot, peeled, grated, with a tablespoon of coconut oil, vinegar, and salt. The fiber binds bacterial toxins and biliary estrogen for excretion, lowering endotoxin load. Daily. Full case at [[carrot-salad]].

The protocol is layered rather than sequential. Aspirin and thyroid restoration are the highest-leverage single moves. Gelatin and the dietary shift (PUFA out, fruit and milk in) are the long-arc background. DHEA-progesterone topical is the targeted intervention for acute flares.

## Joint replacement and surgical orthodoxy

Peat's framework is not opposed to surgical joint replacement when destruction is anatomically advanced. The 60-year-old woman in his patent case series had already received two artificial joints before topical DHEA-progesterone produced "complete relief" of her remaining symptoms. The orthopedic procedure addressed structural damage; the bioenergetic protocol addressed the upstream state that produced the damage and would, if unaddressed, produce the same destruction in the next joint. The distinction is preventive versus reactive. If the inflammatory chain is intercepted early — aspirin, thyroid, PUFA elimination, estrogen reduction — most joints never reach the point where replacement becomes the only option. Case 5 in the 1986 patent — a 62-year-old with arthroscopically confirmed cartilage damage who had declined surgery — climbed stairs and slept through the night within 24 hours of topical application.

## Tracking signal

Peat preferred clinical signals to laboratory markers.

- **Morning stiffness duration.** The most sensitive single sign. Untreated RA produces hours of stiffness on waking; effective treatment compresses this to minutes within weeks.
- **Pulse and basal temperature.** Resting pulse (target 75–90), oral temperature on waking (target ≈98°F) and mid-afternoon (target 98.6°F). The composite indicates whether thyroid replacement is at the right dose. Persistent low temperatures despite "normal" TSH means under-treatment.
- **Sleep quality and pain interrupting sleep.** Patent Case 5 used "ability to sleep through the night without analgesic" as the binary outcome.
- **Inflammatory marker drop.** CRP, ESR, fibrinogen. Useful but slow; morning stiffness and pulse-temperature signals lead them by weeks.
- **Mood and cognition.** Recovery on a working protocol typically improves these *before* the joint signs change.
- **Cold tolerance.** Warming of hands and feet on adequate thyroid replacement is the patient's most reliable subjective signal.

The trap is judging the protocol by single-symptom improvement. Aspirin alone can reduce joint pain while the underlying state worsens. A full-protocol patient should feel different across multiple axes — energy, sleep, temperature, mood, digestion — within weeks. If only the pain has moved, the cascade has not been intercepted; it has been suppressed.

## Counter-arguments

The strongest counter is genetic: HLA-DRB1 shared epitope alleles confer roughly fivefold increased RA risk, and HLA-B27 is present in roughly 90% of ankylosing spondylitis patients. Peat's framework absorbs this by treating HLA alleles as susceptibility markers — they determine which tissue compartment a chronic inflammatory state expresses in, not whether the state exists. But the framework owes a fuller account of why specific HLA configurations channel a generalized stress response into specific joint patterns.

The second strong counter is the response to TNF-α inhibitors. Etanercept, infliximab, adalimumab, and the JAK inhibitors produce dramatic remissions in patients who failed methotrexate. Peat's framework treats TNF-α as one cytokine in the inflammatory cascade he described (alongside interleukins 1 and 6); blocking it interrupts the cascade downstream. The catch is the indefinite duration and immune-suppression cost — recurrent infection, increased malignancy risk, paradoxical autoimmune phenomena. Mainstream rheumatology treats these as acceptable costs of "disease-modifying" treatment. Peat's framework treats them as evidence that the intervention is downstream and the upstream metabolic causes have not been addressed.

The autoimmune framing itself is the deepest disagreement. Mainstream rheumatology treats RA, lupus, and the spondyloarthropathies as primary disorders of immune tolerance. Peat treated them as descriptions of what an immune system does *correctly* when it encounters chronically leaking stress proteins from energetically compromised tissue. Restoring immune tolerance (rituximab, abatacept, biologics) is the mainstream therapeutic axis; restoring tissue integrity (so stress proteins do not leak) is Peat's. The two frameworks make different long-run predictions: mainstream predicts continued biologic dependence with intermittent flares; Peat predicts gradual drug taper as the metabolic state normalizes.

## Data gaps

Published RA cohorts show 6–15% overt hypothyroidism — high relative to baseline but not high enough to account for the disease on hypothyroidism alone. Either Peat's subclinical-hypothyroidism estimate is correct (RA studies miss the subclinical population through fraudulent TSH ranges) or the hypothyroid mechanism is one input among several. The 1986 case of complete RA resolution on thyroid extract alone is dramatic but n=1.

The HLA-B27 mechanism in ankylosing spondylitis is not yet absorbed into the framework. Why this MHC class I allele channels generalized stress into spinal joint fusion rather than peripheral inflammation is unspecified. Molecular mimicry between B27 and Klebsiella antigens is the leading mainstream candidate; Peat's framework would need to specify whether this is an instance of his endotoxin chain or something specific to MHC structure.

Peat's clinical anecdotes from the 1986 newsletter and patent case series are striking but uncontrolled. No proper trial of the DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol topical formulation has been done. The relationship between Peat's protocol and the modern gut-microbiome literature on arthritis (Prevotella copri in early RA, the dysbiosis signatures in psoriatic arthritis) is unmapped.
