# Alzheimer's Disease

The broader frame is at [[dementia|dementia]] — brain-energy failure expressed as protein misfolding, PUFA-iron-estrogen-hypothyroidism as the load-bearing variables, plaques and tangles as debris rather than disease, progesterone and thyroid and methylene blue and red light as the upstream interventions. This page narrows. Alzheimer's disease is the condition for which mainstream neurology has built the longest pharmacological track record and the longest record of failure, the condition for which Peat's inversion is most explicit, and the recent drug-approval cycle (Aduhelm 2021, lecanemab 2023, donanemab 2024) the cleanest natural experiment for whether the amyloid hypothesis can still deliver. What follows: how the diagnosis is constructed, why the amyloid orthodoxy survived four decades of negative trials, what the type-3-diabetes literature converged on independently, what the ApoE4 brain looks like decades before symptoms, and how Peat's stack maps onto the staging mainstream care now uses.

## What AD is in this framework

Mainstream neuropathology defines Alzheimer's by two structural findings at autopsy: extracellular amyloid plaques (Aβ42 peptide cleaved from amyloid precursor protein) and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (hyperphosphorylated tau dissociated from microtubules). Clinical diagnosis works backward from this pathology, scored against cognitive tests (MMSE, MoCA), staged by ADL deficits, and increasingly biomarker-confirmed via CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio, amyloid PET, and plasma p-tau217.

In Peat's reading the diagnostic apparatus is built on a pathological inventory whose causal arrows run the wrong way. From [[the-problem-of-alzheimers-disease-as-a-clue-to-immortality|*The Problem of Alzheimer's Disease as a Clue to Immortality* (2006)]], Peat laid out the AD brain's findings — "Death of neurons (increase of glial cells), Amyloid plaques (extracellular), associated with a particular variant of apolipoprotein E, the epsilon 4 allele, Fibrillary tangles (intracellular, or remaining after the rest of the cell has disappeared), Amyloid in blood vessels" — and placed alongside them the functional observations that orthodoxy treats as downstream: "The mitochondrial energy problem, cytochrome oxidase and its regulation; body temperature/pulse-rate cycle disturbance; lipid peroxidation; respiratory defect; altered amino acid uptake; memory impairment; dominance of the excitatory systems vs. the inhibitory adenosine/GABA/progesterone/pregnenolone system." The respiratory defect is the primary lesion. The amyloid plaque is cargo dropped by stalled microtubule transport. Clearing cargo without restoring transport clears symptom without touching cause.

The historical note in the same newsletter is load-bearing. "Although Alzheimer's disease until recently referred to a certain type of organic dementia occurring in people in their thirties, forties and fifties (presenile dementia), structural similarities seen in senile dementia have caused the term to lose its original meaning." Alois Alzheimer's 1906 case — Auguste Deter, age 51 — was a presenile dementia. The category in current use absorbs late-onset decline in 85-year-olds, early-onset familial cases with single-gene mutations, and mixed-vascular pictures that meet imaging criteria. From [[bse-mad-cow-scrapie-etc-stimulated-amyloid-degeneration-and-the-toxic-fats|*BSE, Mad Cow, Scrapie, etc.* (2007)]]: "Between 1979 and 2000, the number of people dying annually from Alzheimer's disease increased 50-fold. Very competent neuropathologists differ radically in their descriptions of the dementia epidemic." A diagnostic category that swells fifty-fold across a generation and absorbs heterogeneous pathologies is not a category whose causal mechanism is well-bounded.

The biomarker revolution of the 2010s — A/T/N classification, amyloid PET, CSF assays — promised tighter boundaries. In practice it tightened the biomarker correlation while leaving the clinical correlation messy. Roughly a quarter of cognitively healthy 80-year-olds have positive amyloid PET, and a substantial fraction of clinically diagnosed AD patients do not. The biomarker tracks the protein. The protein tracks something the framework does not yet explain.

## The amyloid hypothesis critique

The amyloid hypothesis as a load-bearing scientific commitment dates to 1991-92, when Hardy and Higgins articulated it after the cloning of APP and the discovery that APP mutations cause early-onset familial AD. The claim: Aβ accumulation drives the disease and everything downstream — tau pathology, synaptic loss, neuron death, dementia — follows from it. The therapeutic implication was direct: clear the amyloid, the cascade stops.

The track record is the most expensive series of negative trials in pharmaceutical history. Bapineuzumab failed Phase III in 2012. Solanezumab failed in 2014 (twice), 2016, and 2018. Crenezumab failed 2019. Gantenerumab failed 2022. Each reduced amyloid burden on PET to varying degrees. None produced a clinically meaningful slowing of decline.

Aducanumab (Aduhelm), FDA-approved June 2021 on accelerated approval over the unanimous objection of its own advisory committee, was the inflection point. Three of eleven committee members resigned in protest. Medicare restricted coverage to trial participants. Biogen withdrew the drug from market in early 2024 without it generating meaningful sales. The episode broke whatever consensus had survived the previous twenty-five years.

Lecanemab (Leqembi, July 2023) and donanemab (Kisunla, July 2024) are the post-Aduhelm survivors. Both produce statistically significant slowing on the CDR-SB scale — roughly 0.45 points difference at 18 months on an 18-point scale, about 27% slowing. Whether this is clinically meaningful is contested; the minimum clinically important difference is generally pegged at 1.0 to 2.0 points. Both produce ARIA — amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, the radiological term for brain edema (ARIA-E) and microhemorrhage (ARIA-H), occurring in 12-35% of treated patients, rising sharply in ApoE4 homozygotes. Several treated patients have died of cerebral hemorrhage. The drugs cost roughly $26,500 per year (lecanemab) and $32,000 per year (donanemab) before infusion and MRI surveillance, which add another $10,000-15,000.

Peat's framework treats this trial history as expected, not anomalous. From [[the-problem-of-alzheimers-disease-as-a-clue-to-immortality|*The Problem of Alzheimer's Disease*]]: "Until recently, the 'tangles' were commonly interpreted as the debris left after the death of a cell, rather than as one of the processes causing the death of the cell." If amyloid is debris dropped by a stalled transport system whose stalling is the disease, then clearing the debris without restoring the transport produces exactly what the trials produced: modest, expensive, side-effect-laden marginal benefit that does not bend the long-term trajectory. The ApoE4-amplified ARIA signal is, on the same reading, what happens when a system whose cholesterol carrier is already slightly destabilized is asked to absorb immune-mediated extraction of protein deposits from cerebrovasculature: the vessels bleed.

A separate methodological strand is harder to ignore. The Sylvain Lesné 2006 *Nature* paper that identified Aβ*56 as the "toxic oligomer" — cited more than 2,300 times and anchoring the soluble-oligomer extension of the amyloid hypothesis — was found in 2022 to contain manipulated Western blot images and was retracted in 2024. The literature built on that paper is still in the system. The framework predicts more of these. A research program whose drug pipeline has produced thirty years of failure and whose foundational toxic-oligomer paper turns out to be fraudulent is, on Peat's reading, in late-stage degeneration.

The orthodoxy still points to the genetic evidence — APP, PSEN1, PSEN2 mutations, Down syndrome (trisomy 21 carries APP) — as confirming amyloid causal upstream in at least the familial cases. The framework does not deny this. Familial cases are the cleanest amyloid signal in the spectrum and constitute roughly 1% of total AD diagnoses. The other 99% — the late-onset, sporadic, ApoE4-influenced, mixed-vascular cases that drive the public-health burden — are the cases the energy-failure reading was built to explain.

## Type 3 diabetes — brain insulin resistance

Suzanne de la Monte and Jack Wands at Brown coined "type 3 diabetes" in a 2008 paper after a decade of work showing the AD brain is functionally insulin-resistant. The findings are now mainstream: cerebral insulin receptor expression reduced in AD cortex, PI3K/Akt signaling impaired, IRS-1 phosphorylation mirroring peripheral type 2 diabetes, and brain glucose hypometabolism on FDG-PET as one of the earliest detectable abnormalities — visible in [[apoe4|ApoE4]] carriers decades before symptoms.

Peat had been making essentially the same claim for forty years with a different mechanistic emphasis. From [[bse-mad-cow-scrapie-etc-stimulated-amyloid-degeneration-and-the-toxic-fats|*BSE, Mad Cow, Scrapie, etc.* (2007)]]: "In the dementias, the brain has a greatly reduced ability to use glucose." The cause was not insulin resistance per se but the [[randle-cycle|Randle-cycle]] block that polyunsaturated free fatty acids impose on every cell exposed to them — pyruvate dehydrogenase suppressed, glucose dumped into lactate, mitochondria starved of acetyl-CoA. "Until the ability to use sugar is impaired, cells produce enough carbon dioxide to protect proteins against random glycation, but with each exposure to free polyunsaturated fatty acids, the ability to use glucose is damaged."

From [[homeostasis-and-aging|*Homeostasis and Aging* (1999)]]: "In Alzheimer's disease, brain respiratory metabolism is inhibited, creating a carbon dioxide deficiency with an excess of lactic acid and ammonia." The CO2 deficit reflects the failure of complete oxidation. Lactate accumulates because pyruvate cannot enter the mitochondrion in healthy volume. Ammonia rises because the urea cycle slows when ATP falls. Each metabolite further suppresses residual respiration; the spiral tightens.

Convergence and divergence are both load-bearing. The convergence: mainstream and Peat agree AD is a brain-energy disease whose substrate is impaired glucose utilization. The divergence: the mainstream nutritional response is the ketogenic diet — premise being that the dementing brain cannot use glucose and ketones bypass the problem. Peat's reading inverts this. From the [[2012-12-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-dementia-and-progesterone|2012 *Ask the Herb Doctor* episode]]: "as your liver gets less efficient, many people can only go an hour or even less without suffering the symptoms of starvation. Some people wake up every one or two hours during the night with nightmares or heart pounding, which happens when the liver runs out of its sugar supply." Fasting and ketosis mobilize stored PUFA into the bloodstream, where it is delivered to the brain at the time the brain is most vulnerable to the aldehydes PUFA generates. Ketogenic interventions provide a short-term substrate workaround at the cost of long-term substrate damage. The Peatian alternative — frequent, glucose-stable feeding with sucrose, milk, gelatin, and saturated fat (full diet at [[anti-stress-diet]]) — is engineered to keep liver glycogen full overnight, hold free fatty acids low, and provide the brain with the substrate it can actually oxidize.

## ApoE4 + brain glucose hypometabolism

The ApoE4 allele is the single largest genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's. Carriers have roughly 3-4× lifetime risk per allele, 10-15× for homozygotes (~2% of the population). The orthodox interpretation reads ApoE4 as pro-amyloidogenic: the variant clears Aβ less efficiently from brain parenchyma and cerebrovasculature, so plaque accumulates faster.

Peat's reading, from [[the-problem-of-alzheimers-disease-as-a-clue-to-immortality|*The Problem of Alzheimer's Disease*]]: "The platelets of Alzheimer's patients are less viscous, and lipids extracted from the brain are more fluid, and contain 30% less cholesterol than normal (on a molar basis, in relation to phospholipids)... I think the significance of the decreased cholesterol relates to its significance as precursor to pregnenolone and progesterone, and to the known association with Alzheimer's disease of a variant form of the cholesterol transporter protein, ApoE, which I suppose is a slightly less stable molecular form that is more susceptible to malfunction in stress." ApoE4 is a slightly less stable cholesterol carrier — adequate under non-stress conditions, vulnerable under chronic PUFA exposure, estrogen excess, hypothyroidism, and iron accumulation. The accumulated transport failure that orthodoxy reads as amyloid-clearance failure is, in this reading, the carrier itself failing under load.

The empirical signal that supports this is the FDG-PET data on cognitively healthy young ApoE4 carriers. Eric Reiman and colleagues at Banner have shown that cognitively normal ApoE4 homozygotes in their 20s and 30s already display reduced glucose metabolism in the posterior cingulate, parietal, and temporal cortex — the same regions that become hypometabolic in clinical AD decades later. The energy defect precedes the protein deposition by half a century. On the amyloid-primary reading this is hard to fit. On Peat's framework — ApoE4 as a vulnerability factor whose meaning depends on metabolic environment — the early hypometabolism is the expected signal: the ApoE4 brain runs at a slightly lower energy floor, and the floor drops further as PUFA accumulates, estrogen rises (in women), iron builds, and thyroid function declines. By the time amyloid is detectable, the brain has been running at a deficit for thirty years.

The therapeutic implication inverts standard advice. Mainstream guidance for ApoE4 carriers — particularly the Bredesen-protocol lineage and most ApoE4-targeted nutritional recommendations — emphasizes a low-saturated-fat, Mediterranean-style diet heavy in olive oil, fish, nuts, and seeds. Peat's framework reads this as the worst possible diet for the most-at-risk genotype: it loads the brain's most vulnerable substrate (the destabilized cholesterol carrier) with the lifelong delivery of polyunsaturated fats that drive the lipid-peroxidation chain whose products poison cytochrome oxidase. The ApoE4-appropriate diet, in this reading, is the same anti-PUFA, saturated-fat, glucose-stable, low-iron pattern recommended for everyone else, with possibly greater stringency on PUFA exclusion. The randomized evidence to settle the dispute does not exist.

## Peat's framework — the AD-specific load

The mechanism is at [[dementia|dementia]]; the AD-specific load on each variable is worth naming.

**Estrogen.** AD incidence runs 2-3× higher in women than men. Peat read the female excess as estrogen's signature (full architecture at [[estrogen|estrogen]] and the Seattle population data at [[dementia|dementia]]). The AD-specific signal is the hysterectomy correlation from [[the-problem-of-alzheimers-disease-as-a-clue-to-immortality|*The Problem of Alzheimer's Disease*]]: "the incidence of hysterectomies after the age of 55, which is a strong indication of a natural excess of estrogen, in the demented was 374% of the incidence in the non-demented." A mid-life hysterectomy is a downstream indicator of chronic estrogen excess; women who had hysterectomies were dramatically over-represented in the AD population decades later.

**PUFA, marine omega-3s specifically.** The dementia mother page covers the iron-catalyzed PUFA peroxidation chain. The AD-specific point: marine omega-3s, marketed as cognitive-protective, are the preferred substrate for [[acrolein|acrolein]]. From the [[2012-12-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-dementia-and-progesterone|2012 episode]]: "Acrolein is a very tiny fragment that comes mainly from the Omega-3 fatty acids... The fact that it comes from the oils that are being proposed as cures and treatments for Alzheimer's disease — fish oil supplements and other Omega-3 fats — are the main sources of acrolein." Fish-oil capsules are off the protocol at every AD stage.

**Hypothyroidism — the cold-brain misdiagnosis.** From the same newsletter: "When the body temperature is very much below normal, mental functioning is seriously limited. I think the first question that should be asked about a demented person is 'is this the cold brain syndrome, or is something else involved?'" Hypothyroid patients routinely misdiagnosed as demented; thyroid supplementation restores cognition in such patients. T3 specifically is the activator of cytochrome oxidase, the enzyme PUFA peroxidation poisons and that estrogen displaces. Diagnostics at [[hypothyroidism|hypothyroidism]].

**Loss of stimulation.** From [[2013-08-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-environmental-enrichment-bad-science|the 2013 *Environmental Enrichment* episode]]: "The observation that people with Alzheimer's have drastically reduced living variety — they tend not to socialize as much as resistant people — started me thinking about the general range of stimulation. Old people often develop the habit of staying at home." The animal literature on environmental enrichment shows substantial differences in brain weight, cortical thickness, and age-resistance between enriched and standard housing. The intervention: light, warmth, food, movement, social engagement.

## Peat's intervention stack — staged onto mainstream care

The action layer is at [[dementia-protocol]] and [[brain-orthomolecular-protocol]]; the AD-specific staging is worth naming because mainstream care now organizes around stage and the Peatian stack maps onto each differently.

**Preclinical AD (positive biomarkers, no cognitive symptoms).** Anti-PUFA diet; T3 for subclinical hypothyroidism without waiting for TSH to cross the lab threshold; blood donation every three to four months for iron-lowering; evening incandescent light for cytochrome-oxidase activation; [[progesterone|progesterone]] 5-10 mg evening in vitamin E for postmenopausal women; daily [[aspirin|aspirin]] 81-325 mg with vitamin K2 (aromatase inhibition, prostaglandin blockade, iron chelation). Biomarker-positive preclinical patients get the same protocol everyone over 50 gets, earlier and with tighter adherence.

**MCI.** Add low-dose [[methylene-blue|methylene blue]] 1-4 mg sublingually each morning — after washing out any SSRI, SNRI, tricyclic, MAOI, tramadol, dextromethorphan, or other serotonergic agent (the contraindication is hard; serotonin syndrome risk is real). Methylene blue acts as electron carrier in the mitochondrial chain, bypassing damaged Complex IV. Rodent literature consistent, small human cognitive trials positive (TauRx LMTX), pharmacology well-characterized. Peat in his 2015 *Politics and Science* interview referenced even smaller doses — 20-40 micrograms — as the optimal range "analogous to thyroid hormone."

**Mild AD.** Full-stack: T3 micro-doses through the day rather than a single morning dose; red-light array on the scalp 10-20 minutes evening; methylene blue 4-8 mg morning; progesterone (women) 5-15 mg evening; aspirin 325-650 mg with K2; coconut oil 1-3 tablespoons daily (MCT bypasses CPT-1 and produces ketones at glucose-stable doses without forcing systemic ketosis); niacinamide 50-100 mg with meals; frequent feeding to prevent overnight glycogen depletion. The protocol is run by a caregiver; that constraint dominates everything else.

**Moderate-to-severe AD.** The protocol simplifies. Warmth, food, sugar, gentle stimulation, family. Orthomolecular layer continues if the caregiver can maintain it. Full-spectrum interventions stop being marginal-improvement attempts and become palliative supports. At severe stage: glycine 1-3 g for excitotoxic protection, coconut oil in soft food, warm baths, daylight as tolerable.

What does *not* belong on the protocol: cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) push a damaged system harder by amplifying acetylcholine, which Peat read as an excitotoxin in the AD context. Memantine partially blocks NMDA glutamate receptors and is closer to protective-inhibition, but at the cost of further depressing already-underactive cortex. The anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) are not on the protocol; the framework predicts they will continue producing modest, expensive, ARIA-laden marginal benefits that do not bend the trajectory.

## What to AVOID

**Anticholinergics, long-term.** Diphenhydramine, oxybutynin, paroxetine, amitriptyline, and the dozens of other drugs with anticholinergic burden. Two-year-plus daily use is associated with roughly 50% increased dementia risk in the cleanest observational signal in the AD-prevention literature. Avoidable anticholinergics should be tapered in the 60+ population.

**Long-term benzodiazepines.** The 2014 Canadian study showed 32% increased AD risk; the French replication extended the finding. Decades of GABAergic suppression of cortical activity produce the hypofunction the framework predicts.

**Statins long-term in the elderly.** Contested ground. Statins lower cholesterol — substrate for pregnenolone and progesterone synthesis — at the time of life when neurosteroid production is already declining. Randomized evidence mixed; observational data lean negative on cognitive function in older patients. The framework predicts harm from long-term statin use over 70.

**Low-fat / Mediterranean / fish-rich diets, particularly ApoE4-targeted.** Standard ApoE4-targeted advice — heavy on PUFA-rich nuts, seeds, fish, olive oil — is, on the framework, the worst dietary recommendation possible for the most-at-risk genotype. The bioenergetic alternative is saturated fat from butter, ghee, coconut oil, dairy; protein from milk, cheese, gelatin, modest muscle meat balanced by glycine; carbohydrate from sucrose, fruit, modest starch.

**Prolonged fasting and ketogenic diets in the elderly.** The mainstream ketogenic intervention is built on "the dementing brain cannot use glucose." The Peatian counter: fasting mobilizes stored PUFA into the bloodstream where it is delivered to a brain already PUFA-loaded; ketosis produces less CO2 per oxygen than glucose oxidation; the underlying respiratory defect is not addressed. Intermittent fasting and time-restricted eating fall in the same category.

**Iron supplementation.** Iron-fortified flour and bread, multivitamins containing iron, supplemental iron without verified deficiency. Brain iron accumulates with age; adding more is straightforwardly harmful.

**Supplemental estrogen, particularly in postmenopausal women.** Seattle data plus the WHI Memory Study (increased dementia risk starting HRT after 65) plus the framework's reading of estrogen as the proximate driver of female AD predominance.

## Tracking signals

On top of metabolic basics ([[temperature-tracking|temperature and pulse]], fasting glucose, ferritin, TSH/T3, body weight):

- **MMSE / MoCA.** Quarterly. MoCA more sensitive at MCI; MMSE standard for tracking decline. A patient holding stable over 18-24 months on the Peatian stack is responding; continued decline despite adherence indicates substrate damage exceeds the framework's reach.
- **ADL function.** Family-reported tracking of dressing, bathing, toileting, feeding, transferring, continence. Often moves before the cognitive score.
- **Gait and balance.** Shuffling, narrow base, decreased arm swing predict accelerated decline.
- **Sleep continuity.** Nighttime awakenings, total sleep, subjective restedness. Framework predicts fixing the liver-glycogen-overnight pattern tightens continuity within weeks.
- **Mood and engagement.** Family-reported withdrawal, anhedonia, apathy track the energetic floor.
- **Family-reported recognition.** The single most morale-determinative signal: does the patient still recognize spouse, children, grandchildren by name. Deteriorates late; palliative-care decisions cluster here.

## Counter-arguments

**The amyloid hypothesis has not been fully refuted.** Familial early-onset AD, Down syndrome AD, and the lecanemab/donanemab Phase III results constitute a real signal that amyloid is causally involved in at least some AD pathology. The framework reads these signals as compatible — familial cases are an amyloid-primary minority within a category whose majority is energy-failure-primary; lecanemab's 27% slowing reflects modest plaque-extraction at modest cost. The framework is not amyloid-eliminative; it is amyloid-demoting.

**Genetic evidence is harder to dismiss.** APP, PSEN1, PSEN2 mutations cause early-onset AD with high penetrance; Down syndrome produces near-universal AD pathology by age 50. The framework concedes the genetic point for the 1% familial cases and argues that the 99% late-onset, sporadic, ApoE4-influenced majority — which drives the public-health burden — is a different disease whose genetic component operates as a vulnerability factor rather than a causal trigger.

**Maybe the metabolic abnormalities are downstream of an early, slow-accumulating amyloid signal below current detection thresholds.** A defender of the orthodoxy can argue early metabolic abnormalities reflect a slow amyloid burden current PET cannot see. The framework predicts that as detection improves, the data will continue showing hypometabolism first. As of 2026 the data lean toward hypometabolism first; the question is not settled.

**The Bredesen-protocol "ReCODE" approach is the most-cited alternative framework and it's not Peat.** Dale Bredesen's protocol — 36 lifestyle, nutritional, hormonal, and pharmaceutical interventions personalized to each patient's "subtype" — has produced anecdotal reversals in MCI and small published case series. Overlaps Peat on thyroid optimization, blood-sugar stability, sleep hygiene, hormone replacement; diverges on ketogenic diet, fish-oil emphasis, ApoE4-targeted Mediterranean diet. The framework reads Bredesen as partially right on the metabolic framing and wrong on the dietary fat axis; the empirical superiority of either has not been established by randomized trial.

**Lecanemab's 27% slowing is not nothing.** A defender can argue that 27% slowing over 18 months, accumulated over a five-year treatment course, is clinically meaningful — that an AD patient who reaches full institutionalization six months later is benefiting their family. The framework reads the same data as marginal, expensive, side-effect-laden intervention whose mechanism is downstream of the lesion. Both readings are coherent; long-term randomized data to settle them does not exist.

**Genetic determinism of ApoE4.** The "your ApoE4 status is your fate" narrative has hardened around 23andMe-era consumer genetics. The framework reads ApoE4 as a vulnerability factor whose meaning depends on metabolic environment; carriers who execute the bioenergetic stack from age 40 should converge with non-carrier outcomes. The empirical signal to confirm this does not exist; the framework rests on the mechanistic story.

## Data gaps

**The MCI-to-AD transition.** The fraction of MCI patients on full Peatian intervention who arrest progression versus continue declining is not known. The framework predicts aggressive intervention at MCI should reverse a substantial fraction; the systematic data does not exist.

**Methylene blue dosing.** Peat referenced doses from 20 micrograms to several milligrams; TauRx trials used 100-228 mg of LMTX. Optimal dose for AD prevention, MCI reversal, and mild-AD intervention is not established. Pharmacokinetics of sublingual versus oral, chronic-versus-acute dosing, and cytochrome polymorphism interactions are not worked out.

**The ApoE4-specific protocol.** Whether ApoE4 carriers should be on the same stack as non-carriers, or a modified version, is underdeveloped. The framework predicts the same anti-PUFA, saturated-fat, glucose-stable pattern with greater PUFA-exclusion stringency; the empirical case is not built out.

**The diagnostic CJD-AD overlap.** Peat cited a 1989 study showing 13% of "Alzheimer's" autopsies were actually CJD. Whether some fraction of current "AD" is a prionic disease driven by similar lipid-peroxidation-amplified protein misfolding has not been worked out.

**The aducanumab/lecanemab/donanemab era's long-term outcomes.** Three years of clinical experience with the new antibody class is not enough to know whether the modest cognitive deceleration sustains over 5-10 years, whether ARIA risk attenuates over time, or whether early-treatment cohorts diverge from controls enough to justify the cost. The framework predicts no; the data is still incoming.

**The dementia-as-misdiagnosis fraction.** Peat repeatedly observed that hypothyroidism, depression, and medication-induced cognitive impairment are routinely misdiagnosed as dementia. The fraction of current "AD" diagnoses that would resolve with simple thyroid optimization, anticholinergic taper, and benzodiazepine discontinuation is not known. The framework predicts it is substantial; the systematic data does not exist.

**Sleep architecture and glymphatic clearance.** Recent literature on the glymphatic system suggests sleep is when the brain clears amyloid through CSF exchange. Whether the Peatian sleep-stabilization recommendations (eating before bed, salt and sugar by the bed, evening incandescent light) translate into measurable amyloid clearance has not been studied.

## Safety note

Methylene blue carries a hard contraindication with all serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics, MAOIs, tramadol, meperidine, dextromethorphan, St. John's wort) — serotonin syndrome risk is real and has produced surgical fatalities. Fluoxetine washout is ~5 weeks; most other serotonergic drugs ~2 weeks. T3 dosing requires monitoring; iatrogenic hyperthyroidism is possible. Progesterone in men is more controversial than in women and warrants closer monitoring. None of this displaces clinical care; the Peatian stack is a complement to, not a replacement for, an attentive physician's assessment.
