# Mental Health Cluster — Peat's Framework

Index + router for any query about Peat's perspective on mood disorders, psychiatric medications, and metabolic alternatives. Doubles as a methodology demo for progressive disclosure: read the TLDRs, decide which sub-pages or source docs to pull next.

---

## The Unifying Thesis (read this first)

Peat collapses the psychiatric DSM categories into one **"brain stress syndrome"** — schizophrenia, depression, mania, manic-depression, ADHD, autism, Alzheimer's are not distinct diseases but variations of the same underlying state: **a failure of cellular respiration in the brain, mediated by excess serotonin, estrogen, cortisol, lactate, endotoxin, and PUFA, and a deficit of thyroid, progesterone, CO₂, and energy**.

> "The idea that schizophrenia is a disease in itself tends to distract attention from the things it has in common with Alzheimer's disease, autism, depression, mania, the manic-depressive syndrome, the hyperactivity-attention deficit syndrome." — *Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities* (2009)

Once you accept the unifying thesis, "alternatives to psychotropics" becomes a single question: how do you restore biological energy?

## On Psychotropic Medications (Peat's Critique)

| Drug class | Peat's take | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **SSRIs** | "Culturally conditioned promotional construction." Different antidepressants increase, decrease, or don't affect serotonin — the serotonin theory doesn't hold. SSRIs raised suicide rates 5× vs. tricyclics in Lilly's own studies; ~50K Prozac suicides estimated by Healy. | serotonin-depression-and-aggression |
| **MAO inhibitors (1st gen)** | Statistically *safer* than SSRIs. Doctors are indoctrinated to believe newer = safer because patents are still in force. | serotonin-depression-and-aggression |
| **Lithium** | Not rejected. Listed among effective therapies for "schizophrenia" alongside thyroid, penicillin, CO₂, hyperbaric oxygen, vitamins. When asked directly about low-dose lithium for mood volatility, Peat redirected: *"Good nutrition and thyroid function are the basic things. Keeping a record of waking and midday temperature and pulse rate for a few days can usually detect a thyroid metabolic problem, that could be destabilizing mineral metabolism."* | Email-Lithium |
| **Anti-serotonin agents** (cyproheptadine, methysergide, bromocriptine, reserpine) | Effective antidepressants. Reserpine (anti-serotonin) was a successful antidepressant before serotonin was even named a neurotransmitter — direct counter-evidence to the SSRI story. | serotonin-depression-and-aggression |
| **Cortisol supplement** | Counterintuitively antidepressant — suppresses CRH, which is more directly tied to depression than cortisol itself. | endotoxin-stress-depression |

## On Manic Episodes Specifically

Peat's framing differs from psychiatric: mania is one phase of an energy/mineral instability, not a disease in itself. Bipolar 1 patients spend 53% asymptomatic, 32% depressed, 15% manic/mixed (per Judd et al., cited in transcripts).

**Root-cause levers Peat would pull before a "chemical straightjacket":**

1. **Thyroid status** — track waking + midday temperature and pulse rate for a few days; hypothyroidism destabilizes mineral metabolism (the same axis lithium operates on)
2. **Progesterone** — natural anti-serotonin, neurosteroid via allopregnanolone. Allopregnanolone is the GABA-A modulator — the same pathway benzos and many mood stabilizers exploit, but produced endogenously
3. **Pregnenolone + DHEA** — neurosteroids; Peat recommended this combo directly in an email about a depression case
4. **Reduce endotoxin** — gut bacteria producing lactate from fermentable carbs cause anxiety + aggression in animal models; daily raw carrot, antibiotics where indicated, low-fiber easy-digest foods
5. **Reduce PUFA** — polyunsaturated fats raise tryptophan→serotonin uptake to brain and increase estrogen
6. **Saturated fat + sugar** — short/medium-chain saturated fatty acids are antiserotonin and antihistamine
7. **Calcium-rich, anti-fermentation diet** — fruit, milk, cheese; shifts balance away from lactic acid + serotonin toward CO₂ + progesterone
8. **CO₂** — bag breathing, altitude, acetazolamide; CO₂ inhibits serotonin release
9. **Aspirin** — anti-inflammatory, anti-prostaglandin, opposes estrogen
10. **Light, warmth, deep slow-wave sleep** — torpor (the broken-sleep state shared across hypothyroid/aged/depressed/schizophrenic patterns) reverses with thyroid + temperature

For mania specifically the lever order is: **thyroid + minerals → progesterone/pregnenolone → endotoxin reduction → PUFA reduction**. Lithium remains on the list of acceptable therapies but as a downstream tool, not the first lever.

## Counter-arguments

- **Mainstream psychiatry**: bipolar disorder has clear genetic loading, lithium has 70+ years of RCT data showing reduced suicide and hospitalization, and acute mania can be life-threatening. Replacing lithium with thyroid + progesterone in a stabilized patient is not casually defensible.
- **Lithium as essential mineral**: there's a separate body of literature treating lithium itself as a trace nutrient (e.g., Schrauzer's work on lithium in drinking water). Peat is permissive of low-dose lithium — the disagreement is whether high-dose lithium therapy is ever the *first* lever.
- **SSRI-suicide claims**: Healy's 50K estimate is contested. The FDA black-box warning is for ages 18–24 specifically.
- **Endogenous-progesterone-as-mood-stabilizer**: clinical evidence is mostly small trials, postpartum-specific (allopregnanolone/brexanolone is FDA-approved for postpartum depression — a partial vindication). Not yet validated for non-postpartum bipolar.
- **Hypothyroidism causes mood disorder**: well-established (subclinical hypothyroidism → depression). The reverse claim — that *all* mood disorders are downstream of metabolic failure — is Peat's extension, not consensus.

---

## Data gaps

- No RCT testing Peat's metabolic protocol against lithium for bipolar
- Allopregnanolone's role in mania (vs. depression) understudied
- Endotoxin → mood causation in humans is mostly inferential from animal models
- The "brain stress syndrome" unification is a frame, not an established diagnostic category
