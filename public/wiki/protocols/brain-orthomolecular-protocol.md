# Brain Orthomolecular Protocol

This is the action layer for restoring brain energy when the lesion is metabolic — a respiratory failure of cortical mitochondria producing too little ATP and too much excitatory drive. Framework at [[consciousness]] (mind as a state of energetic, CO2-producing oxidative metabolism in structured cytoplasmic water) and [[oxidative-metabolism]]. What is unique is that Peat published a *literal list* in **Chapter 12 of *Mind and Tissue: Russian Research Perspectives on the Human Brain* (1976)** — written when he was thirty, before he had become known for hormone-focused interventions, while translating the Luria-Anokhin-Bekhterev Soviet neuropsychology lineage into a chemistry he could act on. The list precedes his later hormone-centered work by a decade. It clarifies that niacinamide, vitamin E, progesterone, and androgens were never accidents in his vocabulary — they were a deliberately assembled stack engineered to refloat a sinking brain.

The 1976 list and its 2026 update do not replace [[dementia-protocol]], [[anxiety-depression-protocol]], or [[hypothyroid-recovery]] — they cross them. Read this when the energy-deficit reading dominates: cognitive decline of aging, depression with anhedonia and cold extremities, post-concussion fog persisting beyond three months, post-anesthesia delirium in an elderly patient, ADHD where the *fatigue* signature is loud, dementia where the cortex is still working at all. Skip this and run [[anxiety-depression-protocol]] when the dominant feature is acute panic, active suicidal ideation, mania, or psychosis — those need triage first.

## The 1976 protocol verbatim (Peat's named list)

Chapter 12 of *Mind and Tissue*, "Implied Therapeutic Approaches," compiles the substances Peat thought had the best case for restoring a brain whose oxidative metabolism had failed. Reproduced verbatim — the way he assembled it, grouped by what they restore.

**Energy substrate.** ATP itself (which Peat noted was used in Soviet clinics for cardiac and neurological cases); creatine phosphate (the phosphagen reservoir that buffers ATP); niacinamide (the non-flushing form of B3, supporting NAD/NADH redox); potassium (the intracellular cation whose retention is the signature of a healthy, structured cytoplasm — see [[consciousness]] on Ling's structured-water model, where bound potassium *is* the resting potential).

**Antioxidant and structural protection.** Vitamin E (mixed tocopherols, the lipid-phase antioxidant that protects mitochondrial membranes from [[polyunsaturated-fats|PUFA]] peroxidation); inositol (the membrane lipid Peat associated with structural support and second-messenger function); the broader B vitamins (B1, B2, B6, pantothenic acid, biotin — coenzymes for the dehydrogenases feeding the electron transport chain); vitamin C (cytosolic antioxidant, useful at modest doses, problematic in megadose for its iron-mobilizing effect).

**Hormonal restoration.** Androgens (testosterone, DHEA — supporting male brain function and, at small doses, female brain function); progesterone (the central neurosteroid, produced inside the brain at concentrations roughly ten times those in the bloodstream — full mechanism at [[progesterone]]).

**Adaptogenic load-shifting.** Ginseng (*Panax ginseng* — the classical adaptogen Peat read as thyroid-mimetic with mitochondrial-protective effects); eleutherococcus (*Eleutherococcus senticosus*, "Siberian ginseng" — the Soviet-favored adaptogen Brekhman published on through the 1960s, which Peat read while writing *Mind and Tissue*).

**Sulfur-redox chemistry.** Cysteine (the sulfur amino acid feeding glutathione synthesis); reduced glutathione (the cell's master thiol antioxidant).

**Dietary lithium.** The trace element Peat treated more sympathetically than any other in psychiatric chemistry — distinct from the 600-1200 mg/day pharmaceutical lithium carbonate of bipolar maintenance. Dietary lithium runs 0.5-2 mg/day from mineral water and orotate or aspartate compounds.

What is striking about the list at fifty years' distance is what is *not* on it. Thyroid hormone is implied through ginseng but not yet named as the master intervention it became. Methylene blue is present in the Soviet literature Peat was reading but not yet pulled forward. Aspirin is not yet the broad-spectrum tool of his later writing. The list is the seed crystal. Every item either supports oxidative phosphorylation, protects the lipid environment around it, or substitutes for steroid output when stress has suppressed it — the brain-energy framework before it had been fully named.

## Updated 2026 protocol (Peat's later additions)

Across the fifty years between *Mind and Tissue* (1976) and Peat's death (2022), five substances were added to the brain-energy stack. The 1976 framework anticipated them; the empirical case clarified.

**T3 (Cynomel / liothyronine).** The single most important addition. By the 1990s Peat was treating T3 as the master metabolic lever and the cleanest pharmacological route to the cytochrome-oxidase activation *Mind and Tissue* had argued the brain needs. From [[2014-ask-the-herb-doctor-memory-cognition|the 2014 memory and cognition episode]]: thyroid increases cytochrome-oxidase expression, the enzyme most sensitive to stress, estrogen, carbon monoxide, and "basically all of the toxins." Without thyroid the rest of the stack works against gravity. Full mechanism at [[thyroid]].

**Methylene blue.** The exogenous electron acceptor that bypasses damaged Complex I and Complex III. By the 2010s Peat referenced it at 1-5 mg microdose — sometimes 20-40 μg — as "analogous to thyroid hormone." Full pharmacology and the absolute SSRI contraindication at [[methylene-blue]]. The contraindication is broken out in its own section below.

**Aspirin.** The broad-spectrum brain protector. Aromatase inhibition, prostaglandin blockade, mitochondrial uncoupling, iron chelation — every mechanism aligns with the 1976 framework. Full mechanism at [[aspirin]].

**Coconut oil / MCT.** Medium-chain saturated fat repurposed in the Peat framework as *anti-PUFA replacement substrate* rather than ketogenic fuel. Cognitive benefit comes from displacing PUFA, raising metabolic rate via uncoupling-protein activity, and providing an immediately oxidizable fat. Full mechanism at [[coconut-oil]]. Dose: 1-3 tablespoons daily.

**B12 methylcobalamin.** The neurologically active form, distinct from cyanocobalamin. By the 2010s Peat emphasized methylcobalamin (and adenosylcobalamin) for brain-specific applications: peripheral neuropathy reversal, cognitive recovery in the elderly, post-anesthesia rehabilitation. Full mechanism at [[vitamin-b12]]. Dose: 1-5 mg sublingual daily.

The five additions sit on top of the 1976 list, not in place of it — the 1976 substrate set as the metabolic floor, the 2026 additions as high-leverage pharmacological multipliers.

## Dose ranges

Harvested from [[peat-applied-doses]] and the broader corpus. As with every Peat dose, these are *per person, per context, per moment* — titrate against feedback signals below.

- **Niacinamide.** 500-1500 mg/day divided into 3-4 doses with food. Maintenance: 100 mg three times daily. Brain-energy load: 500 mg three times daily. Avoid nicotinic acid (flush form) unless the lipid effect is desired. Above ~3 g/day risks hepatotoxicity.
- **Methylene blue.** 1-5 mg microdose, sublingual, with food. Peat referenced 20-40 μg as the most thyroid-analogous dose. Entry: 1 mg every morning, raised slowly. Urine turns blue-green within 2-4 hours when absorbed. **SSRI/MAOI contraindication is absolute** — see dedicated section.
- **Progesterone (Progest-E or compounded vitamin-E-based).** 25-100 mg/day topical, gums or skin, 2-4 doses. Starting unit: 1-2 drops on the gums. Postpartum/severe luteal: 100-200 mg/day. Men: 5-10 mg most days. Avoid oral cellulose capsules and creams.
- **Pregnenolone.** 50-200 mg/day, sublingual or in vitamin E oil. Peat's safety experiment ran 3,000-4,000 mg/day for a year without harm. Gentler than progesterone; expect days, not hours.
- **T3 (Cynomel / liothyronine).** 1-5 μg per dose, pulsed every 1-4 hours during waking, optional bedtime and nightstand micro-dose. Endogenous production is ~4 μg/hour; single doses above this are unphysiological. Titrate to morning oral temperature 98.0-98.4°F and resting pulse 75-85.
- **Magnesium.** 200-400 mg/day elemental, as glycinate, malate, or chloride. Glycinate at bedtime supports sleep; chloride topically bypasses gut tolerance. Avoid oxide.
- **Vitamin E.** 100-400 IU mixed tocopherols daily (alpha, beta, gamma, delta). Synthetic dl-alpha alone is incomplete.
- **CoQ10 / ubiquinol.** 100 mg ubiquinone or 50-100 mg ubiquinol. Ubiquinol preferred over 50. With fatty meal.
- **Vitamin C.** 250-1000 mg/day divided, food plus modest supplement. Megadose (10+ g/day) cautioned for iron-mobilizing effect in chronic use.
- **Cysteine / glutathione / NAC.** 600-1200 mg NAC daily, or 50-200 mg reduced glutathione sublingual. Useful at high oxidative load.
- **Vitamin B12 methylcobalamin.** 1-5 mg sublingual daily. Hydroxocobalamin injection preferred for established deficiency.
- **Inositol (myo-inositol).** 1-4 g/day in water before bed or divided.
- **B-complex.** B1 10-50 mg, B2 10-25 mg, B6 10-25 mg, pantothenic 50-200 mg, biotin 50-300 μg, with breakfast. Avoid heavy-folate forms without confirmed need; methyl-folate can be agitating.
- **Aspirin.** 81-650 mg/day with K2 (1-5 mg MK-4 per 325 mg aspirin), after largest meal. Bedtime dose for the 4 AM wake-up. Caveats at [[aspirin]].
- **Coconut oil / MCT.** 1-3 tablespoons daily in food or coffee.
- **Lithium (dietary).** 0.5-2 mg/day as orotate or aspartate, or via mineral water. *Not* the 600-1200 mg/day pharmaceutical lithium carbonate of bipolar maintenance.
- **Ginseng and eleutherococcus.** Standardized extracts at clinical dose (200-400 mg/day Panax at 4-8% ginsenosides, or 300-1200 mg/day eleutherococcus). Pulsed 5 days on, 2 off.
- **Creatine.** Monohydrate 3-5 g/day as the practical phosphocreatine proxy; oral ATP is largely gut-hydrolyzed.
- **DHEA.** 5-10 mg/day men over 50, 2.5-5 mg women, with breakfast and a little fat. Conservative versus anti-aging mainstream.

## When to use this protocol

The clearest indications are presentations where the energy-deficit reading dominates the picture — cognition has dropped from a previously higher baseline, somatic markers of low metabolism (cold extremities, morning temperature under 97.8°F, resting pulse under 75) accompany the cognitive complaint, and the timeline lines up with a depleting stressor (illness, surgery, head trauma, prolonged poor sleep, chronic dieting).

**Cognitive decline of aging.** The presentation *Mind and Tissue* was written for. Memory thinning, word-finding pauses, the loss of fifty-year-old sharpness. Run prevention/MCI of [[dementia-protocol]] in parallel.

**Depression with anhedonia, cold extremities, cortisol-dominant phenotype.** The flat, slow, cold presentation rather than the agitated-anxious one. T3, progesterone or pregnenolone, niacinamide, and substrate work do most of the lifting. The serotonin-load reduction of [[anxiety-depression-protocol]] is a less central layer here.

**Post-concussion syndrome lasting beyond 3 months.** TBI literature has converged on progesterone as the most consistently neuroprotective intervention. Add T3, magnesium, microdose methylene blue, B12 methylcobalamin, aggressive PUFA reduction. The high-leverage window is months 1-6 post-injury — after swelling resolves, before metabolic depression fixes.

**Post-anesthesia delirium in the elderly.** Anesthesia uncouples mitochondria; recovery is the reversal (see [[consciousness]]). Low-reserve elderly patients do not always recover spontaneously. Niacinamide, T3, progesterone (both sexes; pregnenolone for men preferring it), B12 methylcobalamin, magnesium, coconut oil. Runs alongside conventional postoperative care.

**Dementia where the cortex is still working.** Full stage-graded application at [[dementia-protocol]]. This protocol is the brain-energy-optimized layer, run alongside iron-lowering, PUFA reduction, warmth-and-sugar substrate work.

**ADHD where the energy-deficit angle dominates.** The *fatigued* ADHD phenotype — restless-because-starved, not disinhibited-by-structure. Niacinamide, T3 if hypothyroid markers present, magnesium, B-complex, coconut oil, bedtime-snack protocol. Stimulants can sit alongside; the protocol's job is to lower the stimulant dose required.

## Phased build

Loading the entire stack on day one is the most common error. Substrate and antioxidant layers first, hormonal second, high-leverage pharmacological multipliers last. Most patients underestimate how much benefit the first two layers produce.

### Week 1-4: stabilization

Stop ongoing injury and rebuild substrate. The orthomolecular list loads slowly so effects and tolerances can be observed.

- **Diet and substrate first** — [[anti-stress-diet]]: sugar and salt every 2-3 hours, bedtime carb snack, 80-100 g/day protein (not more — excess tryptophan/cysteine from muscle meat suppresses already-low thyroid), 2 qt milk + 1 qt OJ as micronutrient base, low PUFA. Without this the rest works against gravity.
- **Niacinamide 100 mg three times daily** with meals.
- **Magnesium 200-400 mg at bedtime** (glycinate or chloride).
- **Vitamin E 100 IU mixed tocopherols** with dinner.
- **B-complex with breakfast** (balanced; avoid heavy folate alone).
- **Vitamin C 250-500 mg/day** from food and modest supplement.
- **Coconut oil 1-2 tbsp daily.**
- **Bright morning light, dim evening, no fasting.**

Expected at week 4: morning temperature into 97.5-98°F, resting pulse into the 70s, sleep architecture improving, cognitive baseline modestly lifted. If none of this has moved, the substrate work is leaking.

### Months 1-3: hormonal build

Add once substrate is held. Order: T3 first, then progesterone (women) or pregnenolone (everyone), then remaining items.

- **T3 (Cynomel) 1-3 μg per dose, 2-4 times daily** with food. Titrate to morning temp 98.0-98.4°F and pulse 75-85. Backstop with 1/4 grain (15 mg) Armour AM if T3 alone causes afternoon dips. Full guidance at [[thyroid-dosing]].
- **Progesterone (women):** 1-2 drops Progest-E on gums, cyclical (days 14-28) or continuous for severe presentations. Build to 25-50 mg/day. Men: 5-10 mg most days.
- **Pregnenolone (men or women not tolerating progesterone):** 50-100 mg sublingual, build to 100-200 mg at 4 weeks if no effect.
- **CoQ10 100 mg or ubiquinol 50-100 mg** with dinner.
- **Inositol 1-2 g** before bed.
- **B12 methylcobalamin 1-5 mg sublingual daily.**
- **DHEA 5-10 mg men >50, 2.5-5 mg women** with breakfast.
- **Daily raw carrot with salt, coconut oil, vinegar** — endotoxin and estrogen binding.

Expected at month 3: cognitive substrate clearly lifted, mood and energy reliably higher, sleep restored, somatic markers reversed. Ready for high-leverage multipliers.

### Months 3-6: high-leverage layer

- **Methylene blue 1 mg sublingual mornings.** *Only after SSRI/MAOI contraindication cleared* — see next section. Raise to 2-5 mg over 4-8 weeks if no effect. Urine turns blue-green at effective dose.
- **Aspirin 81-325 mg daily** after largest meal with 1-5 mg K2 (MK-4). Bedtime 325-500 mg dose for the 4 AM wake-up. Skip if active GI bleeding, recent surgery, anticoagulant therapy, ulcer.
- **Ginseng or eleutherococcus, pulsed** (5 on, 2 off). Optional.
- **NAC 600 mg twice daily** if oxidative load is high.
- **Dietary lithium 0.5-2 mg/day** as orotate or aspartate. Optional; strongest case in mood-instability presentations.

### Ongoing maintenance

Once stable 2-3 months, simplify to the lowest sustainable set: substrate diet, T3 at lowest temp-holding dose, niacinamide, vitamin E, magnesium, cyclical progesterone for women, B-complex, B12 methylcobalamin, coconut oil, low-dose aspirin, low-dose methylene blue. Adaptogens and creatine on rotation. Re-baseline every 3-6 months. Winter doses quadruple summer doses on T3 and vitamin A — the protocol is not static.

## SSRI / MAOI absolute contraindication (methylene blue)

The methylene blue addition carries the single hardest safety constraint in the protocol. Methylene blue is a potent MAO-A inhibitor at any therapeutic dose, including 1 mg microdose. Co-administration with any serotonergic drug can produce serotonin syndrome — potentially fatal hyperthermia, tremor, clonus, autonomic instability. The constraint emerged in the literature after surgical fatalities in patients on SSRIs who received intraoperative methylene blue.

**Do not take methylene blue if the patient is on:** any SSRI (fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamine); any SNRI (venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine, levomilnacipran); any tricyclic (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine, doxepin); any MAOI (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, selegiline at antidepressant doses); any triptan (sumatriptan, rizatriptan, etc.); tramadol or meperidine; dextromethorphan; St. John's wort; linezolid; 5-HTP or high-dose L-tryptophan.

Fluoxetine has the longest washout — ~5 weeks for parent drug and norfluoxetine metabolite. Most others require ~2 weeks. **Taper of any of these is psychiatric-supervised work, the subject of [[anxiety-depression-protocol]].** Do not stop drugs to start methylene blue; coordinate the taper with the prescriber, complete washout, then start methylene blue.

Many "depressed" elderly patients with cognitive decline are on SSRIs not addressing substrate. Protocol sequence: substrate work → hormonal layer → SSRI taper coordinated with prescriber → washout complete → methylene blue added. Skipping the washout to "see if methylene blue helps" is the single most dangerous error available in this protocol.

Other 2026 additions (T3, aspirin, coconut oil, B12 methylcobalamin) do not carry this contraindication, though T3 + SSRI needs thyroid monitoring and aspirin + SSRI raises GI bleeding risk. The 1976 list does not carry it — every item is safe alongside SSRIs with the usual prescriber-coordination cautions on progesterone (hormone-sensitive cancer history) and androgens (endocrinologist supervision). The contraindication is a 2026-addition footnote, not an entry-protocol blocker.

## Tracking signals

Peat's insistence: *measure, do not assume*. The protocol has high degrees of freedom; titration depends on signals that the metabolism is rising.

- **Morning oral temperature on waking, before getting out of bed.** Target: 97.8°F at week 4, 98.0-98.4°F by month 3, 98.6°F long-run. Failure to rise signals substrate leakage — usually missed bedtime snacks, covert under-eating, or a thyroid dose raised too fast producing paradoxical adrenaline.
- **Resting pulse mid-morning, sitting.** Target: 75-85 bpm. Pulse over 90 with low temperature signals adrenaline-dominant compensation — drop T3 25% and deepen substrate work.
- **Achilles reflex relaxation phase.** A slow Hoffman sign on relaxation is a classical hypothyroid sign that resolves with recovery.
- **Finger and toe temperature.** Late but reliable signal that peripheral oxidative metabolism is online.
- **Sleep architecture.** Disappearance of the 3-4 AM cortisol-adrenaline wake is the most patient-noticeable signal.
- **Weekly mood and cognition diary, 1-10 scale** for energy, mood, word-finding, short-term memory, same time each week. Trajectory is more informative than absolute number.
- **Lab work periodically.** TSH is a marketing number here, but free T3, free T4, and reverse T3 give a clearer picture. Ferritin tracks iron status; aspirin tolerance via hemoglobin and BMP.
- **Family/caregiver observation.** In dementia and post-concussion cases, the people around the patient often see the change first. Weekly check-in with a spouse or caregiver is load-bearing.

## Counter-arguments

**Mainstream neurology — cholinesterase inhibitors and amyloid antibodies.** Donepezil-class drugs push acetylcholine signaling in a metabolically failing brain — asking damaged neurons to fire harder rather than restoring capacity. Lecanemab and donanemab clear amyloid (dropped cargo, not the respiratory defect). The trial results — modest slowing with substantial side-effect burden (ARIA edema, microhemorrhage at $26K/year) — are exactly what the framework predicts from a peripheral intervention. The protocol does not refuse pharmaceutical care; it predicts standard care underperforms the metabolic restoration this protocol produces.

**Nootropic-stack culture.** The contemporary scene (racetams, modafinil, noopept, longevity-influencer stacks) is a lossy descendant of *Mind and Tissue*'s list. Current emphasis is single-mechanism stimulants — adenosine antagonists, dopamine reuptake inhibitors, "focus enhancers" — over substrate-and-energy. These push a depleted brain harder rather than restoring its substrate, producing the brittle tolerance-and-rebound patterns that define the nootropic-user experience. The protocol is closer to traditional orthomolecular medicine (Pauling, Hoffer) than to modern nootropics — *raising the floor* rather than *raising the ceiling*.

**The SSRI/SNRI era.** The serotonin-deficit theory is, on the evidence, dead (Moncrieff and Horowitz 2022). The mainstream response defends the drugs on grounds that *something* helps even if the mechanism is misunderstood. The framework takes the *something* — allopregnanolone-elevating side effects of fluoxetine, dopaminergic and noradrenergic activity, placebo and clinical-attention components — and observes that substrate work, T3, progesterone, and methylene blue address the same downstream targets more directly. Not absolutely anti-SSRI; patients on stable SSRIs who don't want to taper can run the rest of the protocol alongside, but the lifting comes from elsewhere.

**Ketogenic and carnivore brain-health frameworks.** The keto-for-cognition argument relies on ketones bypassing impaired glucose oxidation. The framework reads it backward: ketosis requires fat oxidation (less CO2 per oxygen), relies on lipolysis (mobilizing stored PUFA into the brain), and suppresses thyroid within weeks. Frequent glucose, stable liver, no fasting is the opposite case. Short-term clarity on keto is adrenaline-of-glycogen-depletion, not recovery.

**The "exercise and sunlight is all you need" minimalism.** Substrate work is more important than supplements layered on top — the protocol's diet-first emphasis is exactly this. The case for the supplemental layer is that the patient with cognitive decline of aging, post-concussion fog, or post-anesthesia delirium is by definition a patient in whom diet alone has not been enough.

## Data gaps

**The 1976 list has not been trial-validated as a stack.** Peat assembled it from reading the Russian neuropsychology corpus and the orthomolecular tradition; he did not run a controlled trial of the combination. Strongest support comes from individual-substance trials (niacinamide for anxiety, progesterone for TBI, T3 for cognitive function in hypothyroid patients, methylene blue for memory in animal models) and clinical anecdote.

**Dose-response is not characterized in cognitive applications.** Niacinamide 500-1500 mg/day is bracketed by anxiety data and Peat's statements, not head-to-head cognitive-outcome data. Methylene blue 1-5 mg is bracketed by Peat's commentary and animal data, not human trials at this dose. Progesterone 25-100 mg/day is bracketed by TBI literature (much higher acute doses) and Peat's emails (much lower chronic doses).

**Interactions among items are mostly unstudied.** T3 + methylene blue is widely practiced and observationally benign but unstudied. The full stack's drug-drug interaction profile in elderly polypharmacy is unknown.

**Sex and age effects on the hormonal layer are partially characterized.** Progesterone dosing differs across premenopausal, postmenopausal, postpartum cognitive applications. Pediatric application is undercharacterized — niacinamide and B vitamins have pediatric data; methylene blue and T3 in children require pediatric specialist supervision.

**Long-term safety of low-dose methylene blue is uncharacterized.** Acute toxicity is well-characterized (serotonin syndrome risk, methemoglobinemia at high IV doses). Decade-scale safety at 1-5 mg/day daily is not.

**Effect in *severe* dementia is bounded.** Peat's reversal anecdotes concentrate in early-to-mid disease. A brain that has lost 40% of cortical volume will not regrow it. The inflection point is unspecified.

**ADHD adaptation is undercharacterized.** Plausible from the framework, untrialed in the modern ADHD literature.

**Current pharmacy reality varies item by item.** USP-grade methylene blue is hard to source; oral ATP is gut-hydrolyzed; reduced glutathione has limited oral bioavailability; ginseng quality is highly variable. Translation is non-trivial.
