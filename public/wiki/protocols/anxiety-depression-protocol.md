# Anxiety-Depression Protocol

This is the action layer on top of [[anxiety-depression]]. Read that article first — it explains *why* serotonin is the disease signature, *why* CRH sits above cortisol, and *why* the SSRI brand identity is mechanistically inverted. This page lays out the specific doses, timing, brands, and decision rules — and what to watch for when a patient is already on a long-term SSRI.

Four therapeutic phases run **in parallel**, not in sequence. The single most common implementation error is treating the protocol as a checklist — substrate first, then hormones, then inflammation. Substrate continues while the hormonal layer is added, and both continue while a slow taper happens. Nothing comes off the protocol until the patient has been stable for months.

## When to use this protocol

The protocol is the right tool for:

- **Mixed anxiety-depression** with somatic markers (cold extremities, 4 a.m. wake-up, post-meal crashes, premenstrual rage, postpartum mood crash, perimenopausal anxiety).
- **Treatment-resistant depression** where one or more SSRI / SNRI trials have failed or partially failed.
- **Patients on stable SSRIs** who want to taper but whose previous attempts triggered rebound symptoms.
- **Adjunct support** during SSRI use the patient is not currently trying to come off (the substrate and hormonal layers help even with the drug on board).

The protocol is **not** the right first move for:

- **Active suicidal crisis, mania, or psychosis.** See the escalation section at the bottom. Get emergency psychiatric care first.
- **Bipolar I in the manic or mixed phase.** Some Peat tools (T3, light) can accelerate mania in this window. Lithium and a psychiatrist before anything else.
- **Severe melancholic depression with catatonia, refusal of food, or persistent active suicidal ideation.** Inpatient care, then this protocol on the back end.

## Phase 1 — Stabilize the substrate

This is the single most important phase and the one most often skipped because it sounds prosaic. Without it the rest fails. The full background is on [[anti-stress-diet]] and [[energy-restoration]]; what follows is the depression-specific emphasis.

**Sugar with salt — every two to three hours during the day.** No gap longer than three waking hours without a meal or substantial snack. Orange juice with a pinch of salt, milk with honey, fruit with cheese, ice cream in the afternoon — every dose of sugar suppresses lipolysis, lowers free fatty acids, and stops the Randle block. The [[anxiety-depression|cortisol-CRH-serotonin]] cascade cannot run on a fed liver. From [[anti-depressants|Peat's emails on weaning off anti-depressants]]: "Keeping the metabolic rate and cholesterol up is important, so that repair and adaptation will be quick."

**The bedtime snack is non-negotiable.** The 4 a.m. wake-up with the heart pounding is glycogen running out. Ice cream, milk with salt and honey, or fruit with cheese before bed prevents the overnight cortisol surge. If waking at the same hour nightly, set an alarm thirty minutes before that time and eat the snack proactively for two weeks; the pattern usually unwinds.

**Salt to appetite, with a floor of ~2.5 teaspoons table salt daily** (adult, no kidney disease). In the anxiety-predominant variant, salt is anxiolytic on a same-day timescale; well-salted broth or salted orange juice can abort a panic episode.

**Bright light on waking, dim light after sunset.** Curtains open, outside, or a 10,000-lux SAD lamp for 20–30 minutes within an hour of waking. Light shuts off the morning cortisol-serotonin pulse the way breakfast shuts off the hypoglycemic surge. Seasonal-affective patients add the SAD lamp October through April.

**No fasting, no skipped meals, no low-carb, no intermittent fasting.** All four push the substrate the wrong direction. Patients coming off long IF practice usually feel worse before they feel better; warn them.

**Sleep window 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.** Sleep deprivation collapses thyroid output within days. Insomnia gets the [[sleep-restoration]] protocol — milk + honey + salt, magnesium glycinate, possibly low-dose [[cyproheptadine]] (Phase 4).

Expect first signs of effect within 72 hours. Morning pulse should drop from the 90s into the 70s; cold extremities should warm; the 4 a.m. wake-up should weaken or disappear. If none of that moves within two weeks, the substrate work is being broken somewhere — usually by missed bedtime snacks, under-salting, or covert caloric restriction.

## Phase 2 — Hormonal layer

When the substrate is held but symptoms persist, the second layer is hormonal. The order is thyroid first, then progesterone (women) or pregnenolone (men), then any further steroidal support.

### T3 — for hypothyroid presentations

Most chronic anxiety-depression patients are functionally hypothyroid even with TSH in the conventional reference range. Diagnostic argument on [[hypothyroidism]]; dosing on [[hypothyroid-recovery]].

- **Start low, go slow.** Typical entry: 1/4 grain natural desiccated thyroid (Armour, NP Thyroid, ERFA) twice daily, or 5 μg cytomel (T3) every 4–6 hours during waking. Cynomel/Cynoplus, Thyroid-S, or compounded 4:1 T4:T3 are alternatives. Peat dosed T3 in small frequent pulses rather than one large morning dose.
- **Feedback: morning oral temperature and resting pulse.** Target ≥97.8°F on waking, rising toward 98.6°F; resting pulse 75–85 bpm. TSH is a marketing number, not a feedback variable.
- **Anxiety can transiently increase in the first 1–2 weeks** as thyroid pulls cortisol off its floor. Hold dose; do not escalate. If severe, drop 25% and deepen substrate work.
- **Hashimoto's patients may need parallel antibody work** — selenium (200 μg/day), iodine moderation, gut work. See [[hypothyroid-recovery]].

### Progesterone — for women

Progesterone is the GABA-A allopregnanolone arm of the protocol and the highest-leverage hormonal intervention for women. Full mechanism on [[progesterone]].

- **Form: Progest-E (Kenogen) or compounded oil in vitamin E.** Avoid creams (low absorption) and oral micronized progesterone in cellulose capsules (heavy first-pass metabolism). Taken on the gums or swallowed.
- **Starting dose: 1–2 drops on the gums, repeated every 2–4 hours during a symptom episode.** From the [[progesterone|email exchange on progesterone]]: "It's usual to start with just a couple of drops of progesterone; depending on your symptoms, that amount could be repeated, as needed." Severe luteal-phase or postpartum cases scale to 100–200 mg/day across multiple doses.
- **Cycling: days 14–28 of a regular cycle,** or continuous for 2–3 months in severe perimenopausal/postpartum, then attempt cycling. From the email: "Some people take a little every day to prevent symptoms, but (especially when there's good thyroid function) its effectiveness is greatest when it's used cyclically."
- **In SSRI taper, dose-titrated to symptom suppression.** From [[anti-depressants|Peat on weaning anti-depressants]]: "People would use progesterone in amounts needed to stop the withdrawal symptoms."

### Pregnenolone — for men, precursor for everyone

For men and for women who don't tolerate progesterone, pregnenolone is the precursor steroid. Full mechanism on [[pregnenolone]].

- **Form: pure pregnenolone in vitamin E oil, or capsules with minimal excipients.** Capsule fillers cause more side effects than the molecule. Per [[pregnenolone|the email file]]: "It's possible to eliminate some of the impurities by mixing it with warm vitamin E, and after stirring it, allowing it to settle, and using only what dissolved in the vitamin E."
- **Starting dose: 50–100 mg sublingual daily, to 200–300 mg in divided doses if needed.** Peat ate 3,000–4,000 mg/day for a year without observed harm — wide safety margin. Effect is gentler than progesterone; expect days, not hours.
- **DHEA and testosterone in combination** preserve libido during the build-up. Per Peat: "Combining progesterone and DHEA or testosterone can produce the stabilizing effect without suppressing the libido." DHEA 5–25 mg/day for men, 5–10 mg/day for women; testosterone only under medical supervision.

## Phase 3 — Inflammation layer

Once substrate and hormones are in place, the third layer addresses the prostaglandin and free-fatty-acid amplifiers downstream of CRH and serotonin. The mechanism is on [[aspirin]].

**Aspirin 325–650 mg twice daily with food, paired with vitamin K2.** For active depressive episodes, 325 mg twice daily (after breakfast and with the bedtime snack) is the practical regimen. Bedtime dosing has special value — it interrupts the overnight inflammatory cycle that drives the 4 a.m. wake-up.

- **Vitamin K2 (MK-4): 1 mg per 325 mg of aspirin** as bleeding-risk offset.
- **Form: USP aspirin crystals dissolved in warm water,** or buffered tablets. Avoid enteric-coated.
- **Contraindications: active ulcer, bleeding disorder, recent surgery, anticoagulant therapy without supervision.**

**Niacinamide 100 mg three times daily** lowers free fatty acid release and pairs well with aspirin. Best taken with substrate meals (breakfast, lunch, bedtime snack). NR and NMN are not substitutes.

**Low-PUFA diet — strict.** Seed oils, fish oil, PUFA-heavy nuts and seeds out. Slowest-moving lever (tissue stores deplete over 2–4 years) but most structural. See [[pufa-depletion]].

**Gelatin daily — 10–20 g, from broth or unflavored powder in milk.** Glycine is itself an inhibitory neurotransmitter ("Salt and gelatin are both very helpful for good sleep" — Peat in the [[55-bioenergetic-nutrition-basics|bioenergetic nutrition basics episode]]) and offsets the tryptophan and cysteine load from muscle meat.

**Daily raw carrot.** Medium carrot, grated, with vinegar, coconut oil, and salt, with the first meal. Mechanism on [[carrot-salad]] — endotoxin and estrogen binding via the [[anxiety-depression|gut-CRH-serotonin loop]].

## Phase 4 — Serotonin-load reduction (severe and treatment-resistant cases)

When substrate, hormones, and inflammation are addressed but the patient is still in active severe depression — or in active acute anxiety with panic, racing thoughts, or insomnia that doesn't yield to the bedtime snack — the fourth layer directly attacks the serotonin load.

### Cyproheptadine — the most useful drug Peat ever named

Cyproheptadine (Periactin) is a serotonin and histamine antagonist with anticholinergic and calcium-channel-blocking effects — the single drug Peat most often recommended for serotonin-driven syndromes. 4 mg tablets:

- **Start ultra-low: 0.5 mg at bedtime** (quarter tablet, crushed, or compounded). From the [[cyproheptadine|email exchange on cyproheptadine]]: "It's good to start with about half a milligram, at bedtime, to judge its effects when sedation isn't risky."
- **Build to 2–4 mg at bedtime** over 1–2 weeks as tolerated. Sedation is the dose-limiting side effect; daytime dosing rarely needed.
- **Effect: deeper sleep, fewer nightmares, panic suppression, antihistamine relief, appetite stimulation.** From the same email: "Cyproheptadine, 2 to 4 mg at bedtime, would help with his sleep as well as the cancer. It also has calcium blocking action, aldosterone antagonism, and antagonizes serotonin's antidiuretic effect."
- **Treatment duration: 2–8 weeks** for an acute episode; longer-term acceptable with quarterly re-evaluation. Sedation usually fades after the first week.
- **Watch for:** appetite jump (therapeutic in cachexia, harmful in weight-loss), constipation, dry mouth, mild urinary hesitancy in older men.

### Other antihistamines and metergoline

If cyproheptadine is unavailable, the alternatives Peat named are **ketotifen** (1 mg at bedtime) and **diphenhydramine** (Benadryl, 25–50 mg at bedtime). Per the [[cyproheptadine|email file]]: "Have you tried other types of antihistamine, such as ketotifen, diphenhydramine, or cyproheptadine?" **Metergoline** is a more potent serotonin antagonist for refractory cases — compounding pharmacy required, psychiatric supervision territory.

### The LSD-precaution note

Peat repeatedly compared progesterone's serotonin-antagonist action to LSD's. From the [[anxiety-depression|2012 newsletter on Serotonin, Depression, and Aggression]]: "Progesterone, like LSD, can inhibit the firing of serotonergic nerves, but an overdose, unlike LSD, never produces hallucinations." Patients self-medicating with psychedelics for depression are tapping the same upstream serotonin-antagonist mechanism that progesterone, cyproheptadine, and metergoline target — but with more risk, less control, and worse interaction profiles with SSRIs (serotonin syndrome with psychedelics on board is a real risk). The protocol does not include psychedelics; it includes the safer endogenous and pharmacological tools that hit the same lever.

## The SSRI taper — the largest practical gap in the corpus, made operational

A direct implication of the [[anxiety-depression|serotonin-as-stress-signal thesis]] is that long-term SSRI use raises serotonin pharmacologically, and abrupt discontinuation crashes that pharmacologic ceiling — producing exactly the rebound serotonin pulse the protocol is built to suppress. This is why patients who try to stop SSRIs cold or on a 4-week "tapering schedule" so often crash worse than their original presentation.

The Peat-aligned approach, drawn together from [[anti-depressants|the emails on anti-depressants and weaning]] and the Moncrieff-Horowitz deprescribing literature:

### When to start a taper

- **Patient stable on the substrate and hormonal layers for at least 6–8 weeks.** Morning temperature and pulse in range; sleep restored; no active suicidal ideation; cyproheptadine on board if needed.
- **Patient wants to come off.** This is not a Peat-driven mandate. Many people do better long-term on a low dose of SSRI plus the metabolic substrate than off the SSRI on a chaotic substrate. Respect the patient's read.
- **Coordination with the prescribing physician.** This is not solo work. The taper schedule below is the framework; the prescribing physician owns the execution.

### How slowly

The deprescribing literature converges on **hyperbolic tapering** — geometric, not linear, dose reductions. The shape is reductions of 5–10% of the *current* dose every 4–6 weeks, not fixed milligram drops. Patients on long-term high-dose SSRIs (10+ years on 40 mg fluoxetine, e.g.) often need taper schedules of 1–3 years.

Practical guidance:

- **Use a liquid formulation or a compounding pharmacy for the small doses at the bottom end.** Most rebound symptoms occur in the final stretch from 5 mg → 0 mg of the parent SSRI; that stretch needs the finest titration.
- **Reduce no faster than the patient's symptoms allow.** If a step produces rebound anxiety, insomnia, "brain zaps," or returning depression, hold at the current dose for another 4–8 weeks, deepen the substrate work, and resume only when symptoms have re-resolved.
- **Cynomel (T3) at the start of a taper drop.** From the [[anti-depressants|email file]]: "Starting with a little, a sixth or fourth of a tablet, of cynoplus in the evening would be the best way to try it." A small T3 pulse with each taper step buffers the metabolic dip.

### Supportive supplements during the taper

- **Sugar and salt — non-negotiable.** Substrate work intensifies during taper.
- **Progesterone (women) or pregnenolone (everyone) to symptomatic effect.** Per Peat: "People would use progesterone in amounts needed to stop the withdrawal symptoms."
- **Cyproheptadine 0.5–4 mg at bedtime** for the duration of the taper, possibly continued 4–8 weeks past last dose.
- **Aspirin 325 mg twice daily** with K2.
- **Niacinamide 100 mg three times daily.**
- **B-complex with selenium 200 μg/day** to support liver detox. Per Peat: "cytomel (and/or sugar, selenium, B vitamins) allows the liver and other detoxifying systems to lower [estrogen-histamine-serotonin]."
- **Benadryl 25–50 mg at bedtime** if cyproheptadine is unavailable.

### What "withdrawal" feels like and what it is

The conventional frame calls SSRI discontinuation "discontinuation syndrome" — implying mild, time-limited, not real pharmacological dependence. The patient experience and the deprescribing literature (Horowitz, Framer, patient-led withdrawal communities) disagree. Symptoms include rebound anxiety, insomnia, "brain zaps," sensory sensitivity, derealization, returning depression often worse than baseline, suicidal ideation in some patients, and physical symptoms persisting weeks to months. Peat's lineage: "Withdrawal from morphine and SSRIs and migraine involve some similar processes." The protocol's role during withdrawal is to dampen the serotonin-histamine-estrogen rebound the falling SSRI dose is no longer ceiling-capping.

## Escalation criteria — when to seek emergency psychiatric care

The protocol is for chronic, sub-emergency management. It is not a substitute for emergency psychiatric evaluation. Escalate immediately if any of the following appear:

- **Explicit, active suicidal ideation with a plan or means.** Psychiatric ER, crisis line (988 in the U.S.), or mobile crisis team within hours.
- **Suicide attempt, recent self-injury, or stockpiling of medication.** Inpatient admission. Resume protocol only after acute crisis is resolved.
- **Mania or hypomania** — pressured speech, grandiose delusions, severe sleep reduction with elevated energy, impulsive behavior outside baseline. T3 and high-dose light can accelerate mania in bipolar patients. Psychiatric eval and likely mood-stabilizer (lithium has the deepest Peat endorsement) before further protocol work.
- **Psychotic symptoms** — hallucinations, paranoid delusions, severe disorganized thinking. Psychiatric eval first.
- **Catatonia, refusal of food and water, severe weight loss.** Inpatient care.
- **Serotonin syndrome** — hyperthermia, tremor, clonus, autonomic instability — especially when an SSRI is combined with another serotonergic agent (tramadol, MDMA, MAOIs, St. John's Wort). Emergency.
- **Severe SSRI withdrawal not responding to the supportive protocol.** Resume the SSRI at the last tolerated dose, stabilize, restart taper more slowly. Not a failure — a steep taper that needs to be flatter.
- **Postpartum depression with intrusive thoughts of harming the infant.** Postpartum psychiatric services, immediately.
- **Children and adolescents** — pediatric dosing and developmental risk profile differ. Pediatric psychiatric supervision required.

The escalation list is not a sign the protocol failed. These cases are above its weight class.

## Counter-arguments

**SSRIs help some patients acutely — and that benefit is real.** The serotonin-as-stress-signal thesis does not predict that SSRIs produce no benefit. The patient who reports "the fog lifted in two weeks on sertraline" is not lying, and the mechanism may be a combination of side actions (allopregnanolone elevation from fluoxetine, dopaminergic and noradrenergic activity from the broader SSRI class, placebo response, and the simple fact that a serious illness was finally named and treated). The protocol's stance is not "SSRIs never help" but "SSRIs are mechanistically not what their marketing claims, the substrate work is more durable, and the taper question deserves serious treatment when the patient wants to come off."

**Lithium.** Peat treated lithium more sympathetically than most psychiatric pharmacopoeia. Lithium has anti-inflammatory and pro-respiratory metabolic effects (the [[thyroid-insomnia-and-the-insanities-commonalities-in-disease|2009 newsletter on Thyroid, Insomnia, and the Insanities]] lists it among the early-twentieth-century therapeutic toolkit). For bipolar I and recurrent severe unipolar depression with mania history, lithium remains the most-evidence-based mood stabilizer, and the Peat protocol can sit alongside it. Lithium dosing is psychiatric-supervised work; the renal and thyroid side-effect profile demands monitoring.

**Mainstream first-line depression treatment.** The current standard — SSRI / SNRI titration, augmented with bupropion, atypical antipsychotics, ketamine, or ECT for treatment-resistant cases — has data behind it, especially for acute moderate-to-severe depression. The protocol does not replace this for the patient in acute crisis. Its strongest case is the long arc: the patient on three SSRIs for ten years still depressed, with hypothyroidism mainstream psychiatry never investigated.

**Psychotherapies.** CBT, IPT, ACT, and the psychodynamic therapies have evidence in mild-to-moderate depression and anxiety. The protocol sits alongside them. For the patient with broken substrate, CBT alone underperforms CBT + protocol. For the patient with intact substrate and primarily psychological drivers, the protocol is helpful but not central.

**The depression-as-spiritual frame.** A serious tradition — Christian, Buddhist, existentialist — reads depression as a meaning problem rather than a metabolic one. The frames overlap more than they conflict. Peat's own writing on the "choice-poverty" component of inescapable stress nods at the structural-meaning critique. The protocol is morally agnostic; it addresses the biology while the patient and their community address the rest. A protocol that treats the biology while ignoring the patient's life conditions will underperform, and so will a spiritual frame that ignores a thyroid running at 95.5°F.

**The neuroinflammation / probiotic frame.** Functional medicine has converged on similar gut-brain mechanisms via different language. Peat's reading is more skeptical of probiotics — many provide the very fermentative bacteria the carrot-and-low-fiber approach is trying to reduce. The protocol's gut work runs through reduced fermentable substrate plus the daily carrot, not through bacterial inoculation.

## Data gaps

- **Pediatric anxiety and depression.** Adult dosing does not extrapolate. Aspirin in children carries Reye syndrome considerations; pregnenolone and progesterone safety in children is underexplored. Pediatric psychiatric care required.
- **Pregnancy.** Aspirin in pregnancy has specific guidelines; supplemental progesterone decisions belong with the obstetrician. The substrate work (sugar, salt, light, low-PUFA, gelatin) is uniformly safe.
- **Bipolar protocol specifics.** Bipolar II depression — proceed with caution. Bipolar I in active or recent mania needs lithium and psychiatric supervision *before* T3 or high-dose light. The Peat-aligned mood-stabilization stack is not laid out in the corpus.
- **Long-term outcomes.** No prospective trial on the full protocol exists. Evidence is Peat clinical anecdote plus the deprescribing-community case series. The largest empirical gap.
- **Drug-protocol interactions.** Aspirin + SSRI raises GI bleeding risk; cyproheptadine + SSRI blunts SSRI effect (the mechanism the protocol exploits, but the prescriber needs to know); T3 + lithium needs thyroid monitoring.
- **Postpartum and perimenopausal protocols.** The progesterone-shift physiology aligns with the protocol, but specific dosing for postpartum versus perimenopausal versus PMDD is under-articulated.
- **Severe complex trauma.** PTSD with dissociative features and complex developmental trauma may not fully unwind from substrate work alone. Trauma-focused therapy in parallel is reasonable.
- **The specific SSRI being tapered matters.** Fluoxetine tapers more easily than paroxetine or venlafaxine. Actual taper math is drug-specific.
