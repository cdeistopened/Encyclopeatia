# Post-Finasteride Syndrome (PFS) Protocol

The action layer. Mechanism — why blockade of [[dht|5α-reductase]] cascades into a multi-system [[allopregnanolone|neurosteroid]]-deficiency syndrome that outlasts drug clearance by years — lives on [[post-finasteride-syndrome]]. This page is the order of operations, dose ranges, timeline expectations, failure modes, and the safety frame this particular protocol needs more than most.

Read the condition page first. PFS is not "an anxiety problem" or "a libido problem." It is the predictable downstream picture of someone whose morphogen-stabilizing chemistry has been pharmacologically suppressed long enough that regulatory feedback settled around a lower setpoint. Recovery is upstream substrate restoration on a months-to-years clock — not a prescription, not a stack, not a quick fix. The men who recover are the ones who treat the body to the [[anti-stress-diet]] for two years and let the chemistry resume its work, not the ones who run a six-week pregnenolone cycle and complain it "did nothing."

Five phases. Phase 1 stabilizes (weeks 1–4). Phase 2 rebuilds the neurosteroid cascade (months 1–6). Phase 3 quiets inflammation and supports cognition (months 3–12). Phase 4 addresses sexual function — the slowest domain (months 6–18). Phase 5 maintains (ongoing). The phases overlap; the order matters because a body still living on a PUFA-and-SSRI background does not have the substrate to use progesterone, and DHEA into an aromatase-elevated PFS patient makes the picture worse.

## Who This Protocol Is For

- A man who took finasteride (Propecia, Proscar) or dutasteride (Avodart) for any duration and developed the persistent symptom cluster on the [[post-finasteride-syndrome|condition page]] — sexual, mood, cognitive, sleep, skin, metabolic — that did not resolve on discontinuation.
- Symptoms persisting more than three months after the last dose. The acute side-effect window has closed; what remains is the syndrome.
- Realistic about timeline. Sexual function and sensation are the slowest to return; some men recover in six months, some take three years, some plateau. This is not Cialis-and-done.
- Willing to do the diet. Substrate restoration without [[anti-stress-diet]] compliance is reaching for hormones into a body that cannot use them.

Skip and route to clinician care immediately, before any protocol decisions, for: active suicidal ideation, severe major depression unresponsive to ordinary self-management, decompensated hypothyroidism, severe sleep deprivation producing impaired functioning, or worsening symptoms on any current SSRI / 5AR-inhibitor / aromatase-inhibitor combination. PFS depression is real, neurosteroid-mediated, and sometimes severe enough that the right first move is psychiatric stabilization while the protocol runs underneath.

## Phase 1: Stabilization (Weeks 1–4)

The first month is foundation, not heroics. The body has been running for months or years on a depleted neurosteroid cascade; throwing pregnenolone, progesterone, and DHEA at it on day one without a stable substrate base is the protocol equivalent of pouring fuel into an engine with a clogged intake. Stabilize first.

**[[anti-stress-diet|Anti-stress diet]] baseline, from week 1.** Two quarts of milk daily (calcium suppresses [[parathyroid-hormone|PTH]], which is anti-thyroid and pro-inflammatory in tissues already running hot). A quart of orange juice (fructose, potassium, naringenin against [[aromatase]]). Salted food to appetite. Coffee with milk and sugar, two to four cups, with meals. Four to six ounces of [[liver]] weekly. Two to three eggs daily for the cholesterol that progesterone is made from. One raw carrot daily (estrogen-binding fiber, [[endotoxin]] sweep). Cook in butter, ghee, or coconut oil. **No seed oils, no fish oil, no daily nuts.** A bedtime snack — milk and honey, ice cream, fruit — is mandatory; the 2 AM wakeup in PFS is an [[adrenaline|adrenaline]]-driven hypoglycemic event, and feeding the liver before bed softens it within the first week. Full diet detail on [[anti-stress-diet]]; the PFS-specific emphasis is the cholesterol and saturated-fat intake the steroidogenic cascade is downstream of.

**Thyroid evaluation, week 1.** Most PFS patients arrive functionally hypothyroid even if the TSH reads "in range" — finasteride disrupts liver and gut, both of which T4-to-T3 conversion depends on. Start [[temperature-tracking]] on day one: morning oral temperature on waking (target 97.8–98.2°F), again mid-afternoon (target 98.6°F+), and resting pulse (target 75–85 bpm). Run [[hypothyroid-recovery]] in parallel — the diet does most of the work. If by week 3 morning temperature is still under 97.8°F and pulse under 75 despite compliant diet, add T3 (Cytomel, Cynomel) at 5 µg with breakfast, titrating by 5 µg weekly to a second 5 µg dose mid-afternoon. Desiccated thyroid (Armour, Nature-Throid, NDT) at a quarter grain morning and afternoon is the alternative. T4-only therapy is the wrong tool — a PFS-impaired liver converts it badly.

**Remove the confounders.** This is the single most-skipped step.

- **Additional 5α-reductase inhibitors: stop immediately.** Saw palmetto, pumpkin seed oil at therapeutic doses, any continued finasteride or dutasteride. Topical-only finasteride is included; serum levels are non-zero.
- **SSRIs and SNRIs: clinician-supervised taper.** Do not stop cold — withdrawal is its own injury and may produce post-SSRI sexual dysfunction (PSSD) layered on PFS. But do plan the exit. SSRIs blunt the [[allopregnanolone]] benefit the rest of the protocol is built around — there is a competing pharmacology at the GABA-A receptor and at neurosteroid synthesis, and the data are now strong enough that a PFS protocol running on a maintained SSRI is fighting itself. Cross-taper if needed, with bupropion sometimes the most tolerable bridge.
- **Alcohol: stop, not "moderate."** PFS patients describe markedly worsened next-day mood and sleep on any alcohol. Mechanism is plausible — alcohol acutely raises allopregnanolone via [[gaba|GABA-A]] modulation, then collapses it via withdrawal, on a body that has no headroom for the swing.
- **Aromatase-driving foods at volume.** Soy, soy lecithin in chocolate and processed foods, beer (hop phytoestrogens), commercial dairy from soy-fed cows. Not paranoia about trace amounts; volume avoidance, especially for the first six months while the cascade is rebuilding.
- **High-PUFA foods absolutely.** Seed oils, fish oil, daily nuts, conventional pork and chicken. See [[pufa-depletion]] — this is a multi-year background, not a Phase 1 task, but it starts now.

**Core supplements, modest doses.** Vitamin D 5,000 IU daily with breakfast (target serum 50–80 ng/mL — retest at week 8). Vitamin K2 (menaquinone-4 or MK-7) 100–200 µg daily, partner to D. Magnesium glycinate 400–800 mg at bedtime (PFS sleep is GABA-poor; magnesium is a mild positive modulator and helps the constipation that often accompanies the syndrome). Zinc 15–30 mg daily with food (5α-reductase substrate cofactor, but more importantly a general androgen-axis supportive trace mineral). None of these is heroic; they're the floor.

**Markers of progress by week 4.** Morning temperature trending toward 97.8–98.2°F. Resting pulse above 75. The 2 AM wakeup softening. Hands and feet warmer. Mood floor lifting slightly (this is real and worth tracking — the diet alone moves mood in many PFS patients within ten days). Sexual function and sensation: typically no change yet. Cognitive: variable.

If temperature has not moved by week 4, the diet is incomplete — almost always a hidden seed oil, insufficient calories, or skipped milk. Audit before adding hormones.

## Phase 2: Neurosteroid Restoration (Months 1–6)

By week 4 the substrate base is in. Now rebuild the [[allopregnanolone|neurosteroid]] cascade — the central mood, sleep, and cognitive lever the [[dht|5α-reductase]] blockade depleted.

**[[Progesterone]] (Progest-E or equivalent), 25–100 mg/day, topical or transmucosal.** The single highest-leverage intervention in Phase 2. Progesterone is the direct precursor to [[allopregnanolone]]; from the [[allopregnanolone|allopregnanolone article]], a small oral dose of 20 mg tripled circulating allopregnanolone in published study. Start at 25 mg/day applied to inner cheek, gums, or thin-skinned areas (inner wrists, neck) in the evening — the timing matches the sleep mechanism the neurosteroid serves. Titrate by symptom: if mood floor rises, sleep deepens, anxiety softens within ten days, the dose is right. If response is muted, push to 50 mg/day, then 75–100 mg/day. From Peat in [[#61： Bodybuilding and Steroids ｜ Progesterone for Men？ ｜ Basic Bioenergetic Therapies with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* #61]]: "If you don't have symptoms, just a drop or two (maybe 5mg) has a protective effect" — but PFS men are symptomatic, and the symptomatic-male window is 25–50 mg/day for mood and sleep, sometimes higher in the early restoration phase.

The PFS-specific caveat from the condition page: progesterone alone cannot fully bypass impaired 5α-reductase, because the GABA-A action runs through allopregnanolone, which still requires the enzyme. The protocol's expectation is that *some* enzymatic activity has resumed (5α-reductase recovers gradually after finasteride clearance), and that high-substrate inflow (progesterone) shifts equilibrium toward allopregnanolone production at whatever enzymatic capacity exists. This is why mood, sleep, and anxiety often respond well before sexual function does — the brain has more enzymatic headroom than the prostate and genital tissue do.

**[[Pregnenolone]], 50–200 mg/day with breakfast.** The upstream substrate the entire steroidogenic cascade depends on. Pregnenolone converts to progesterone, [[dhea|DHEA]], allopregnanolone, and the rest. Start at 50 mg daily for the first two weeks; titrate to 100 mg if tolerated, up to 200 mg in patients with severe cognitive symptoms. From [[#51： Heat Shock Proteins ｜ Antibiotic Resistance ｜ DHT Safety ｜ Energy and Aging with Ray Peat, PhD|*Generative Energy* #51]]: "Generally, pregnenolone is the first thing, then DHEA." Pregnenolone is unusually safe — adverse effects above 200 mg are mild and uncommon, usually transient over-energization or mild anxiety. PFS-specific application: pregnenolone feeds the whole 5α-reduced steroid family, including allopregnanolone via the progesterone branch and 5α-THDOC via deoxycorticosterone. Take it with the breakfast fat (eggs, butter) — it absorbs better with dietary cholesterol.

**[[DHEA]], 5–15 mg/day, cautiously, titrated by symptom.** DHEA is the most aromatizable supplement in the protocol and the easiest to mishandle in PFS. The PFS endocrine picture often features upregulated aromatase (testosterone backing up into estrogen without the DHT outlet); a body in that state preferentially converts higher DHEA doses to estradiol, which is the opposite of the goal. Start at 5 mg/day with breakfast. Hold for two weeks. If mood and energy improve without breast tenderness, emotional reactivity, water retention, or worsened libido, titrate to 10 mg. Cap at 15 mg/day unless under endocrinology supervision. The mainstream "endocrinology" dose range of 25–50 mg/day is wrong for PFS — too much aromatization risk. Do not run DHEA without progesterone running underneath; the progesterone is the aromatization counterweight.

**Thyroid optimization continuing.** Maintain T3 (or NDT) at the dose that holds morning temperature at 98.0+°F and pulse 75–85. Re-titrate at week 8 and week 12 — restoration of the steroidogenic cascade often shifts thyroid demand. Some patients need more T3 as the body's metabolic rate rises; some need less as the syndrome resolves.

**Markers by month 3.** The most reliable Phase 2 signal is mood, sleep, and anxiety. Morning mood floor higher. Sleep more consolidated. Anxiety paradoxical-quality softening (the dysphoric edge that PMDD and PFS share, mediated by allopregnanolone). Cognitive symptoms (brain fog, word-finding) often start to lift around month 2–3. Sexual function: usually still no clear change, but morning erections may begin to return as an early signal.

**Markers by month 6.** Mood, sleep, and anxiety should be substantially improved if the protocol is working. Cognitive symptoms continuing to lift, sometimes with a stair-step pattern. Skin and mucous membrane symptoms (dry eyes, dry skin) softening. Sexual function: morning erections more reliable; libido beginning to recover; sensation lagging both.

## Phase 3: Anti-Inflammatory and Cognitive (Months 3–12)

By month 3 the substrate cascade is rebuilding and the gross symptoms have begun to move. Phase 3 layers in the anti-inflammatory and cognitive interventions that compound the neurosteroid work — particularly for patients whose cognitive component is severe.

**[[Aspirin]], 81–325 mg daily with food.** The daily workhorse anti-inflammatory. PFS involves persistent low-grade neuroinflammation downstream of estrogen-prostaglandin elevation (testosterone backed up into aromatase; tissue PUFA driving prostaglandin synthesis on a body without DHT's anti-inflammatory tone). Aspirin suppresses both. Start at 81 mg (one baby aspirin) with breakfast and titrate to 325 mg if tolerated. Take with food and a pinch of baking soda in water if there is gastric sensitivity. PFS-specific benefit: aspirin is also one of the cleaner [[aromatase]] inhibitors — it shifts the testosterone-to-estradiol conversion the syndrome accelerated. Stop three to five days before any planned surgery.

**[[Methylene blue]] microdose, if cognitive component is significant.** Reference [[methylene-blue-protocol]] for the full Phase 1 microdose detail. The PFS application: 0.5–1 mg sublingually, once daily mid-morning, for cognitive symptoms (brain fog, executive dysfunction, the "lights are on but no one is home" phenotype the registries describe). Methylene blue is a mitochondrial electron-transport-chain optimizer; it improves cerebral oxygen utilization in the same domains PFS cognitive symptoms manifest. Not for every PFS patient — if cognition is not a primary complaint, skip it. Hold steady at the microdose; do not chase higher doses. From the condition page, cognitive symptoms in PFS may also reflect [[dht|DHT]]'s direct anti-inflammatory action at the neuronal level — the protocol cannot replace it, but methylene blue provides a parallel cognitive-supportive mechanism that does not require 5α-reductase activity.

**[[Niacinamide]], 100–500 mg/day with meals.** Splits well as 100 mg three times daily with meals. The job is NAD+/NADH balance, reduced free-fatty-acid mobilization from adipose stores, and a mild anti-inflammatory effect at the central level. Niacinamide (not niacin — different molecule, different action) is one of the simplest, safest cognitive and metabolic supports in the [[anti-stress-diet]] backbone. PFS-specific benefit: by suppressing fatty-acid mobilization from PUFA-loaded stores, niacinamide reduces the circulating prostaglandin precursor pool while [[pufa-depletion]] runs in the background.

**Vitamin E and tocotrienols, 100–400 IU mixed tocopherols daily.** Antioxidant cover for the multi-year PUFA-depletion window. From [[pufa-depletion]]: stored linoleic acid takes about four years for complete turnover; vitamin E is the lipid-phase antioxidant that buffers the period. Take with the highest-PUFA meal of the day. PFS patients with significant residual tissue PUFA from years on a Western diet (which is most of them) benefit disproportionately.

**Red-light therapy and sunlight.** Bright incandescent light during the day; red-spectrum supplementation in the evening if no natural sunset is available. Sunlight supports steroidogenesis directly (skin cholesterol → vitamin D, and broader hormonal effects), and the red-spectrum component supports mitochondrial function in the same domain methylene blue does. Twenty to forty minutes of midday sun exposure on as much skin as feasible. Avoid sunscreen during this window — most modern sunscreens contain ingredients on the [[xenoestrogens|xenoestrogen]] watch list.

**Markers by month 9.** Cognitive symptoms substantially improved or resolved in protocol-responsive cases. Mood floor steady. Sleep architecture normalized or near-normalized. Sexual function still recovering — typically the slowest domain, with sensation often the last to return.

## Phase 4: Sexual Function (Months 6–18)

Sexual function and genital sensation are the slowest PFS domains to recover and the ones the early phases lay the groundwork for rather than directly target. By month 6, with the neurosteroid and anti-inflammatory layers in place, Phase 4 addresses sexual function specifically.

**Patience first.** The reason this is Phase 4 and not Phase 1 is that the underlying biology — peripheral [[dht|DHT]] action at genital tissue, androgen receptor responsiveness, possibly AR-promoter methylation outlasting drug clearance — recovers on a longer clock than central neurosteroid restoration. Trying to "fix" sexual function in month 1 is the protocol equivalent of repainting a house mid-construction. The framework expectation is that morning erections return first (months 2–6), libido second (months 4–12), erection quality on demand third (months 6–18), and sensation last (months 12–24+). A patient who has resolved mood, sleep, and cognition by month 6 but still has sexual symptoms is on the expected trajectory, not a failed protocol.

**PDE5 inhibitors short-term as a bridge.** Tadalafil 5 mg daily, or sildenafil as needed before intimacy, used as a *bridge* during months 6–12 when erection quality is improving but unreliable. From the condition page: PFS erectile dysfunction is often unresponsive to PDE5 inhibitors when the lesion is neurosteroid-and-androgen-receptor rather than vascular — but the subset whose function is partially recovered may find PDE5 inhibitors close the gap during the transition. Discuss with a clinician; do not run indefinitely. The goal is to *not need them* by month 18.

**Clomiphene under endocrinology coordination, if testosterone is low.** Some PFS men present with frankly low total or free testosterone, partly because the suppressed HPT axis has not normalized after chronic 5α-reductase inhibition. Clomiphene (typically 12.5–25 mg every other day, or low daily dose) restores endogenous testosterone production via pituitary stimulation, rather than substituting it externally as TRT does. This matters because exogenous testosterone in PFS is contraindicated by the framework (next paragraph). Clomiphene is not first-line — it goes in only after Phases 1–3 have run their course and serum testosterone remains low despite full substrate restoration. Endocrinology supervision is required because clomiphene has its own neurosteroid-affecting mechanism (it is a SERM, and case reports of clomiphene-induced mood and visual side effects exist).

**Testosterone replacement therapy: not first-line, often contraindicated.** From the condition page: exogenous testosterone in a PFS-injured body risks accelerating estrogen production via [[aromatase]] without restoring the DHT branch (which 5α-reductase has not yet recovered to do at full capacity). Result: high estradiol, mood worsening, breast tenderness, water retention, and a libido that is *worse* than baseline. TRT in PFS is a clinician decision after Phases 1–4 have not produced sufficient recovery, with concurrent aromatase management — and even then, the evidence is mixed. The framework default is: rebuild substrate, do not substitute hormones.

**Sensation recovery.** The slowest variable. Mechanism is the most contested (AR-promoter epigenetic silencing is the leading candidate — see the [[post-finasteride-syndrome|condition page]]'s mechanism section). The protocol position: maintained substrate restoration, continued anti-inflammatory work, [[pufa-depletion]] running in the background, and patience on a twelve-to-twenty-four-month clock. Some men recover full sensation; some recover partial; a recognized subset does not, on any timescale the registries have so far observed.

## Phase 5: Maintenance (Ongoing)

After twelve to twenty-four months of full protocol, most protocol-responsive patients enter a maintenance phase. The goal: minimum effective dose of the supplements that keep the syndrome from re-emerging, with diet and lifestyle holding most of the work.

**Progesterone taper.** Most patients can taper from 50–100 mg/day during active restoration to 5–25 mg/day for maintenance — sometimes only a few drops at bedtime for sleep support. Some men require indefinite low-dose progesterone; the framework treats this as normal physiology, not "dependence." Allopregnanolone has a baseline metabolic role independent of the syndrome it's being used to treat.

**Pregnenolone taper.** Many patients reduce from 100–200 mg/day to 25–50 mg/day for maintenance. Substrate restoration is largely a one-way improvement once the cascade is running; the maintenance dose is supplementing what aging would gradually erode anyway. Pregnenolone declines with age in everyone — 70-year-olds produce roughly half of what 30-year-olds do.

**Thyroid: indefinite if needed.** Most PFS patients arrive functionally hypothyroid and many never fully recover endogenous thyroid output to pre-syndrome levels. Maintenance T3 or NDT is normal. Monitor by symptom and morning temperature, not by TSH.

**DHEA: lowest dose that holds.** 5–10 mg/day for most maintenance patients; some can stop entirely once endogenous production has resumed.

**Diet: indefinite.** The [[anti-stress-diet]] is not a six-month protocol; it is the lifestyle backbone the entire restoration depends on. Patients who go back to seed oils, daily alcohol, and skipped breakfast see syndrome features return within months. This is the failure mode the registries describe most often in "I recovered then relapsed" reports.

**[[Pufa-depletion|PUFA depletion]]: two to four years.** Tissue stored linoleic acid takes about two years for half-turnover and four years for near-complete turnover. The Phase 1–4 work runs against this background and only finishes when the lipid environment has fully cleared.

## Tracking Signals

PFS is multi-domain; recovery is uneven; tracking is non-optional. The protocol assumes a 24-month minimum monitoring horizon for honest assessment.

**Daily, weeks 1–12:**
- Morning oral temperature on waking
- Resting pulse on waking
- Sleep duration and quality (1–10 scale)
- Mood (1–10) and notable shifts

**Weekly, months 1–24:**
- Libido (1–10) — averaged across the week
- Morning erection frequency (out of 7)
- Cognitive function (1–10) — concentration, word-finding, working memory
- Skin and mucous membrane status (dry eyes, dry skin)
- Energy through the day (1–10, plotted as morning/afternoon/evening)
- Anxiety floor (1–10)
- Anhedonia signal — emotional flatness vs ordinary mood

**Monthly, months 1–24:**
- Erection quality on demand
- Genital sensation (the slowest variable)
- Body composition signals (chest fat, abdominal fat, muscle tone)
- Hair status (loss continuing? regrowth?)

**Quarterly, ongoing:**
- Serum testosterone, free testosterone, SHBG, estradiol, LH, FSH, DHEA-S, prolactin, TSH, free T3, free T4, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel
- Vitamin D (target 50–80 ng/mL)
- The bloods are useful for direction of travel, not absolute targets — the PFS picture often features lab values "in range" while the patient is suffering, and the symptoms are the primary signal

A simple spreadsheet captures this. Many PFS forum users go further with tracking — that's fine, but the discipline matters more than the tooling. Looking at the curve at month 6 tells the story; the day-by-day noise is noise.

## Failure Modes

**SSRI continuation blocking allopregnanolone benefit.** The single most common cause of "the protocol isn't working" in PFS patients who were prescribed an SSRI for PFS depression. SSRIs and SNRIs interfere with allopregnanolone synthesis and GABA-A receptor responsiveness; running progesterone-into-allopregnanolone restoration on top of a maintained SSRI is fighting itself. Clinician-supervised taper is non-negotiable before Phase 2 produces its expected response. PSSD (post-SSRI sexual dysfunction) is the parallel condition for SSRIs — the protocol framework applies, but the picture compounds.

**Expecting overnight reversal.** PFS forums are full of "I tried pregnenolone for two weeks and it didn't work" reports. Phase 2 takes 60–90 days to produce reliable signal in mood and sleep, and 6–18 months for sexual function. Patients who quit at week 3 quit the moment before the protocol begins to compound. The condition page's timeline section spells this out — Months 6–24 is the protocol-responsive window, not Weeks 1–4.

**Testosterone monotherapy without progesterone (the aromatization trap).** A clinician sees low testosterone in a PFS patient, prescribes TRT, the patient's estradiol doubles within a month, mood collapses, libido worsens, breast tenderness appears, and the patient concludes "TRT made me worse." The framework: testosterone without the DHT branch functioning (which it isn't, until 5α-reductase has substantially recovered) backs up into [[aromatase]] preferentially. Progesterone running underneath is the counterweight. If TRT is used at all, it goes in after Phases 1–3, with progesterone in place, with estradiol monitored, and with an exit ramp planned.

**Aggressive DHEA in men → estrogen conversion.** The mainstream "supplement industry" DHEA dose is 25–50 mg/day, against Peat's clinical 5–15 mg range. In PFS, the aromatase pressure is already high; DHEA at 25+ mg/day in this population converts to estradiol at unusually high rates. Patients report breast tenderness, water retention, emotional reactivity, worsened libido. Start at 5 mg; titrate slowly; cap at 15 mg.

**Skipping the diet and "biohacking" with supplements alone.** A PFS patient running 100 mg pregnenolone, 50 mg progesterone, and 15 mg DHEA on a high-PUFA diet with skipped breakfast and daily alcohol is not running the protocol. The diet is the foundation; the hormones are layered on top of it.

**Chasing forum protocols promising "the cure."** PFS forums cycle through claimed cures — testosterone, anastrozole, clomiphene-only, methylene-blue megadose, peptide stacks, dutasteride paradoxical re-challenge. Most are noise, some are dangerous, and the durable framework remains substrate restoration on the [[anti-stress-diet]] backbone. The condition page's "what is *not* on the list" section is the discipline check.

**Hyperfocusing on the syndrome itself.** Peat's closing caution on the condition page: "People can get so interested in that particular problem, the finasteride syndrome or post-traumatic stress disorder, for example, that they forget what it is they're doing in their life." Recovery correlates with attention being on life-resumption (work, relationships, energy use) rather than on the syndrome. The protocol is permissive of this — the most successful patients run the protocol in the background while pursuing something they care about, not as the central project of their lives.

## What to AVOID

**Further 5α-reductase inhibitors, absolutely.** No finasteride, no dutasteride, no topical finasteride, no saw palmetto (which is a mild 5AR inhibitor), no pumpkin seed oil at therapeutic supplement doses, no high-dose green tea extract (EGCG has 5AR-inhibiting properties at supplement concentrations). Continued or repeat exposure is contraindicated; the system has had enough.

**SSRIs and SNRIs as PFS treatment.** Mainstream first-line for PFS depression is often an SSRI, which produces the worst possible outcome — blocking the allopregnanolone restoration the rest of the protocol is built on. Bupropion is a tolerable bridge if pharmacological support is necessary during clinician-supervised SSRI taper; benzodiazepines are not the answer (they bypass the substrate problem the protocol is trying to resolve).

**Aromatase inhibitors (anastrozole, exemestane, letrozole) outside endocrinology supervision.** Popular in PFS forums on the logic that "if estrogen is high, lower it." Aromatase inhibitors carry their own neurosteroid risks — they're the wrong tool when the upstream problem is substrate, not over-conversion. They go in only with specific endocrinology indication and lab monitoring.

**High-aromatase-driving foods at volume.** Soy in any concentrated form (soy protein, soy lecithin in chocolate and bars, soy milk). Beer (hop estrogens). Commercial dairy from soy-fed cows (modestly important — pasture-raised is meaningfully better here). Daily flax, sesame, or sunflower seeds.

**Alcohol excess, especially during Phase 1–2.** Allopregnanolone-modulating in a body that has no headroom for the swing. Moderate alcohol (2–3 drinks weekly with meals) may be tolerable in Phase 5 maintenance for some patients; daily drinking is contraindicated through restoration.

**Exogenous testosterone as monotherapy.** Covered above; the aromatization trap.

**Heroic supplement stacks "for libido."** Tongkat ali, fadogia agrestis, fenugreek, tribulus, and the rest of the "natural testosterone booster" category. The published evidence is thin, the safety profiles are not well-characterized, and they tend to act on the testosterone-pituitary axis without addressing the underlying neurosteroid depletion. The protocol's substrate-first frame is incompatible with this category.

**Microneedling and aggressive scalp interventions for the hair-loss subset.** PFS patients sometimes try to address residual hair loss with microneedling, derma rollers, or aggressive scalp treatments. Acute inflammation works against the restoration. If hair loss is present and bothersome, route to [[hair-loss-protocol]] — gentle mechanical, never aggressive.

## When to Escalate

**Active suicidal ideation: immediate.** PFS depression with suicidal ideation is a documented and serious feature of the syndrome — the FDA's 2022 label expansion reflects this. Crisis-line and clinician engagement come first; the protocol runs underneath stabilization, not as a substitute for it. The men whose suicide rate appears elevated in PFS registries are the ones who tried to manage severe depression alone.

**Severe sleep disruption (less than four hours nightly for more than two weeks).** Sleep collapse is both a Phase 2 target and a clinical-escalation threshold. Pharmacological sleep support (low-dose trazodone, mirtazapine, gabapentin — clinician choice) as a bridge during early Phase 2 is reasonable. Avoid benzodiazepines and Z-drugs (zolpidem, eszopiclone) — they hit the same GABA-A receptor the protocol is restoring and create dependence patterns.

**Persistent severe depression unresponsive to Phase 2 at month 4.** If progesterone, pregnenolone, and diet have produced no mood-floor improvement by month 4, psychiatric clinician engagement is warranted. Sometimes the right call is allopregnanolone-targeted pharmaceutical support (brexanolone IV is FDA-approved for postpartum depression on the same mechanism; zuranolone is the oral analog approved 2023). Both are clinician-administered and far outside the supplement category; they are mentioned here because PFS depression is one of the few indications where the pharmaceutical neurosteroid approach is mechanistically appropriate.

**Endocrine evaluation for low testosterone, high estradiol, or persistent thyroid abnormalities.** A reproductive endocrinologist who is open to the PFS framework (rare but findable through patient organizations) can coordinate thyroid optimization, manage clomiphene if needed, and run the lab workup the protocol benefits from. Cardiometabolic and lipid abnormalities also warrant clinician engagement — the metabolic domain of PFS includes glycemic instability and dyslipidemia that may need management.

**Symptoms worsening on protocol at month 3+.** Unusual but real. Possible causes: a missed confounder (continued 5AR inhibitor exposure, an undisclosed SSRI, an [[xenoestrogens|xenoestrogen]] exposure in personal care products), DHEA aromatization in a high-aromatase patient, progesterone application timing causing premature effects, or an unrelated condition emerging.
