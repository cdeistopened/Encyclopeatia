# Diabetes Protocol

This is the action layer for [[diabetes]]. The mechanism — diabetes as a metabolic-respiratory failure in which free fatty acids block glucose oxidation at pyruvate dehydrogenase while cortisol and adrenaline pour more glucose into the blood — lives upstream, along with the disease-naming history (Budd, Piorry, Kulz, the 1857 sugar-as-treatment lineage), the four-type taxonomy, the 1920–1944 iatrogenesis story, and the mainstream-treatment critique. What follows is what the Peat-frame patient runs, in three phases keyed to the three diabetic types, with the dose ranges Peat used in correspondence and the coordination tree for conventional treatment.

**This is not a cure, it is not a substitute for endocrinology, and the safety-critical line in the entire document is: insulin tapering NEVER without your endocrinologist.** The framework is adjunctive. Acute decompensation (ketoacidosis, severe hypoglycemia, hyperosmolar coma) is an emergency the protocol has nothing to add to; late-stage complications require specialist teams. The framework's value is the lanes mainstream endocrinology serves poorly: removing the upstream PUFA / estrogen / endotoxin / hypothyroid load, restoring oxidative respiration so tissues can use the glucose already in the blood, and addressing the iatrogenic component (FFA mobilization from insulin, B12 depletion from metformin, lean-mass loss from GLP-1) that produces a large fraction of what gets called "diabetic complications." Nothing here is individual medical advice.

## Diagnosis prerequisites: which protocol are you running

Name the patient before any intervention. The phases are not interchangeable.

1. **Type 2 (or pre-diabetes, metabolic syndrome).** Adult onset, no acute insulin dependence, HbA1c above 5.7%, often visceral adiposity. On metformin, GLP-1, SGLT2, sulfonylureas, or combinations. Run **Phase 1**.
2. **Type 1 (or LADA).** Insulin-dependent. GAD or islet-cell antibodies, low or absent C-peptide, ketosis-prone. Run **Phase 2** — read the safety section twice before adjusting anything. Goal: preserve residual beta cells, create conditions for regeneration. *Every insulin-dose change is the endocrinologist's call.*
3. **Gestational diabetes.** Screened-positive on the 24–28-week OGTT, with or without symptoms. Run **Phase 3** — rejects the carbohydrate-restriction paradigm Peat treated as physiologically harmful to the fetus, in coordination with the obstetric team.

Phase 1 is the substrate foundation under all three. **Thyroid restoration (the [[broda-barnes|Broda Barnes]] thyroid-prevents-every-complication observation) is the single most-protective intervention across all three phases** and gets its own section. Gut decontamination, neuropathy restoration, diabetic-foot prevention, conventional-treatment coordination, and the tracking panel sit on top of whichever phase applies.

## Phase 1 — Type 2 diabetes recovery (substrate restoration)

The framework's most-developed phase. Type 2 diabetes is the chronic clinical phenotype of PUFA-driven hyperinsulinemia plus estrogen, endotoxin, and hypothyroidism producing the Randle-cycle block on glucose oxidation. The intervention removes upstream insults (PUFA, estrogen, endotoxin), restores oxidative respiration (thyroid, niacinamide, sugar), and lets blood glucose normalize as tissues regain the ability to oxidize it. Multi-year project — stored linoleic acid has a two-to-three-year half-life — slow and durable rather than fast and rebound-prone.

**Diet.** Run [[core-diet-protocol]] and [[anti-stress-diet]] as the foundation. Diabetes-relevant priorities:

- **Sugar is therapy.** The Budd / Piorry / Kulz / 1993-Bantle lineage converges: adequate dietary sugar in the right matrix restores the substrate the body is starved of. One quart of fresh [[orange-juice]] daily (potassium, magnesium, naringenin, hesperidin). Ripe fruit through the day. Honey. Lactose from milk. Sucrose where appetite calls for it. Peat in [[2014-02-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-diabetes-i|*Ask the Herb Doctor: Diabetes I* (2014-02-21)]]: "The rising blood sugar shouldn't be fought in itself. The cause that interferes with the use of glucose should be concentrated on."
- **Two quarts of milk daily** as the calcium / protein / lactose floor. Calcium suppresses parathyroid hormone (pro-lipolytic and antithyroid).
- **[[gelatin|Gelatin]] 10–20 g/day** — broth, gelatin tea, or unflavored powder in juice. Shifts the protein mix toward glycine; lowers cortisol.
- **[[coconut-oil|Coconut oil]] 2–4 tbsp/day.** Medium-chain saturated fats bypass the Randle block — Houssay's 1947 zero-mortality coconut-oil-and-diabetes experiment is the historical anchor.
- **Salt to appetite.** Sodium restriction raises aldosterone and adrenaline, which mobilizes FFA. Iodized, generously.
- **No PUFA, absolute.** Eliminate corn, soy, safflower, sunflower, canola, grapeseed, peanut, cottonseed oils, fish oil, flax oil, "heart-healthy" margarines. Restaurant food is uncontrolled PUFA exposure. Replace cooking fats with [[butter]], ghee, [[tallow|beef tallow]], coconut oil. See [[pufa-depletion]] — adipose PUFA turnover takes years and is the slow rate-limiting axis.
- **Daily [[carrot-salad|raw carrot]]** — scraped or julienned, vinegar + salt + coconut or olive oil. Binds biliary estrogen, sweeps small-bowel overgrowth, lowers serum endotoxin.
- **No carbohydrate restriction, no prolonged fasting.** Carb restriction lowers HbA1c by inducing a cortisol-and-glucagon state that drives FFA mobilization — the surrogate improves, the underlying damage worsens.

**Thyroid restoration.** Phase 1 patients typically present hypothyroid by clinical signs even with "normal" labs. Run [[temperature-tracking]] day one: morning oral temp trending toward 97.8–98.2°F, afternoon temp 98.4–98.8°F, resting pulse 75–85. If after four to six weeks of compliant diet morning temp is still under 97.8°F and pulse under 75, enter [[hypothyroid-recovery]]. Start desiccated thyroid (NDT — Armour, Nature-Throid, NP Thyroid) at one-quarter to one-half grain (15–30 mg) with the largest meal, titrate by 15 mg every one to two weeks against temp-and-pulse. T4-only is wrong — the estrogen-and-PUFA picture impairs hepatic T4-to-T3 conversion and patients spend years on Synthroid with normal TSH and persistent symptoms. Most land at one to two grains at steady state, sometimes with a 5 µg T3 add-on at breakfast and lunch. See [[thyroid-dosing]].

**Aspirin 325–650 mg/day, divided, in solution** (water or seltzer, with food). Anti-inflammatory (prostaglandin cascade driving insulin resistance), FFA suppression (lipolysis-inhibiting at therapeutic doses), aromatase inhibition, anti-glycation. Co-supplement [[vitamin-k|vitamin K2]] MK-4 1 mg/day. Stop at any GI bleeding, melena, bruising, hematuria.

**[[niacinamide|Niacinamide]] 500–1,500 mg/day, divided.** The amide form, never niacin. Inhibits lipolysis directly (the central anti-FFA action), restores NAD+ supply, supports beta-cell function. From [[2014-03-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-diabetes-ii-and-how-to-restore-and-protect-nerves|*Diabetes II* (2014-03-21)]]: one of the most directly anti-diabetic single supplements because the lipolysis-inhibition goes to the exact lesion. Start 500 mg, titrate to 1,500 mg over two to four weeks. Liver labs every three to six months above 1 g/day.

**Vitamin E** 100–400 IU/day with fat, mixed tocopherols. Protects PUFA from peroxidation during depletion.

**Vitamin D3** 4,000–5,000 IU/day, target serum 25-OH-D 50–80 ng/mL. Peat in the [[diabetes|email exchange]]: "I think it's valuable to have a blood test for vitamin D to regulate the dose."

**Magnesium 300–500 mg elemental at bedtime.** Glycinate, taurate, or malate (oxide is poorly absorbed). Topical Epsom alternative.

**Coffee 2–4 cups/day with milk and sugar.** Anti-estrogenic, supports T4-to-T3 conversion, mild aromatase inhibition.

**[[progesterone|Progesterone]] in perimenopausal/postmenopausal women.** 10–30 mg/day topical or sublingual [[progest-e|Progest-E]]. Estrogen elevates FFA and worsens the Randle block; progesterone favors glucose oxidation. Synthetic progestins are the wrong molecule.

**Tracking.** Temperature and pulse daily. Fasting and two-hour postprandial glucose every one to two weeks early, less frequently as the picture stabilizes. HbA1c every three months. Qualitative signals (warm extremities, sleep continuity, absence of stress cravings) move first; HbA1c lags by months. A patient whose morning temp has risen from 97.0°F to 97.8°F and pulse from 65 to 78 is in the recovery curve even if HbA1c hasn't moved yet.

## Phase 2 — Type 1 diabetes / beta-cell regeneration

The most clinically delicate phase. Type 1 patients are insulin-dependent; the protocol cannot replace insulin and any dose reduction is the endocrinologist's call. What the protocol *can* do: preserve residual beta-cell mass, remove the PUFA / estrogen / endotoxin insults that continue to kill regenerating beta cells, and over months-to-years create conditions under which exogenous-insulin requirements fall. Peat's father's case — severe diabetes in the 1930s, recovered after a few weeks on brewer's yeast, never relapsed — is the lineage anchor. The 1995 rabbit DHEA studies and the 2008 Jetton glucose-infusion-doubles-beta-cell-mass paper are the modern mechanistic confirmation. The framework does not promise insulin freedom; it promises the conditions under which it becomes possible, over years, in coordination with an endocrinologist.

**Safety floor.** Every dose change is the endocrinologist's call. CGM strongly recommended — the protocol is actively restoring glucose oxidation, which lowers insulin demand, which means the standing dose is *over*-dosing the patient and producing hypoglycemia before it becomes symptomatic.

**Substrate, identical to Phase 1.** Two quarts of milk, one quart of OJ, ripe fruit, gelatin, coconut oil, salt to appetite, raw carrot daily, no PUFA. Insulin matching to carbohydrate counts is standard practice; the *quantity* of carbohydrate should be adequate. The Type 1 patient running carb restriction is doing the opposite of the framework's prescription.

**Brewer's yeast 1–3 tbsp daily** (food-grade, not the pharmaceutical tablet form). The lineage anchor. Multifactorial mechanism: high B-vitamin density (thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, B6, biotin), high chromium, high glutamine, trace-mineral profile, protein quality. Start one teaspoon mixed into juice or milk, titrate over one to two weeks (gas and bloating common at the start). Two heaping tablespoons is the typical maintenance dose. Use the de-bittered nutritional-yeast form (Lewis Labs or equivalent), not hydrogenated-oil-coated tablets.

**[[pregnenolone|Pregnenolone]] 100–200 mg/day, divided.** Upstream steroidogenic substrate supporting endogenous progesterone and DHEA. 50 mg breakfast, 50–150 mg lunch, with fat. Broadly safe at these doses.

**[[dhea|DHEA]] 5–25 mg/day.** 5–10 mg in women, 15–25 mg in older men. The 1995 Coleman / Schwartz rabbit studies and subsequent rodent work show modest-dose DHEA normalizes glucose tolerance and preserves beta-cell mass in chemically induced diabetic models. Lab-check DHEA-S, estradiol, and free testosterone at 6 weeks. Stop or reduce if estradiol rises or androgenic side effects appear in women.

**Raw carrot daily + cooked mushrooms 2–4 oz daily.** The gut-decontamination layer is non-negotiable in Phase 2 — endotoxin drives nitric oxide, which is directly beta-cell-toxic. Bengmark's well-cooked white button mushrooms (simmered an hour) supply chitin and mannan that suppress inflammatory bowel populations. Carrot does the conjugated-estrogen sweep. Full mechanism: [[endotoxin-reduction]].

**[[niacinamide|Niacinamide]] 1,000–3,000 mg/day, divided.** Pushed higher in Type 1 because the beta-cell-protective effect is the central action sought. The ENDIT trial (European Nicotinamide Diabetes Intervention Trial) at the turn of the century did not show onset-prevention in pre-diabetic relatives at the doses tested, but the mechanism (PARP inhibition, NAD+ replenishment, lipolysis inhibition) is intact and Peat's clinical recommendation was higher — 500 mg three to six times daily, paired with diet and thyroid. Liver labs every three months above 1.5 g/day. **Amide, never niacin.**

**Aspirin 650–1,300 mg/day, divided.** The FFA-lowering action stacks with niacinamide via a different mechanism — the combined niacinamide-plus-aspirin FFA suppression is the protocol's central beta-cell-protection mechanism. From [[2014-03-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-diabetes-ii-and-how-to-restore-and-protect-nerves|*Diabetes II*]]: "The cells are basically dying because they can't get the glucose they need. So they call on fatty acids from stores, and the fatty acids, in turn, kill the remaining or regenerating insulin-producing cells. So it starts a vicious circle." Niacinamide-plus-aspirin breaks the cycle. Co-supplement vitamin K2 1–2 mg/day MK-4.

**[[progesterone|Progesterone]]** 10–30 mg/day in women, 2–4 drops Progest-E (~6–12 mg) in men. Broadly anti-FFA, beta-cell-protective in animal models, neuroprotective for diabetic neuropathy.

**Thyroid restoration** — see the Thyroid section. The same B-vitamin and glucose-substrate deficiencies that drive Type 1 beta-cell injury also impair hepatic T4-to-T3 conversion.

**Vitamin D3 5,000–10,000 IU/day**, target 60–90 ng/mL. The autoimmune literature on Type 1 and vitamin D is one of the more robust supplement-and-diabetes data sets.

**Coordinating insulin tapering.** **NEVER without your endocrinologist.** As the protocol takes hold, requirements may fall gradually — a basal dose of 24 units may need 20, then 18, then 16 over months; a mealtime bolus of 8 may need 6, then 5. CGM is the safety net. The protocol does not cure Type 1 by replacing insulin with brewer's yeast; it creates the conditions under which insulin demand falls and the endocrinologist's titration reflects that. A subset of Type 1 patients, over years, return to minimal or no exogenous insulin — the optimistic case, not the standard expectation. Peat's father is the existence proof, not the typical outcome.

## Phase 3 — Gestational diabetes (rejecting the sugar-restriction paradigm)

The phase the framework treats most heterodoxly. Mainstream GDM management is carbohydrate restriction, finger-stick monitoring four to six times daily, and exogenous insulin if dietary targets fail. Peat treated GDM as substantially manufactured — the estrogen surge of pregnancy elevates maternal FFA, blocks maternal glucose oxidation, and raises maternal blood glucose so more glucose crosses the placenta to the fetal brain. The maternal hyperglycemia is a feature of normal pregnancy that the GDM threshold pathologizes. From [[diabetes-scleroderma-oils-and-hormones|*Diabetes, Scleroderma, Oils and Hormones* (2006)]]: "Normal pregnancy can be considered 'diabetic' by some definitions based on blood sugar... Old gynecologists told me that it was common knowledge that 'diabetic' women had intellectually precocious children."

Phase 3 rejects carb restriction as the central intervention and substitutes the Brewer–Peat pregnancy substrate. Aim: support fetal glucose supply, reduce maternal FFA mobilization, address the upstream estrogen-and-PUFA picture.

**Safety floor.** Coordinate with the obstetric team. A 2026 GDM diagnosis at fasting 92 mg/dL or 1-hour postprandial 130 mg/dL captures a large population whose hyperglycemia reflects normal pregnancy adaptation. Patients who *frankly* fail GDM criteria (fasting persistently above 105 mg/dL, postprandials above 180, polyhydramnios, macrosomia on ultrasound) are in a different clinical situation than patients who barely crossed a screening threshold. The framework does not endorse ignoring genuinely high readings; it endorses examining whether the threshold reflects real pathology.

**Brewer–Peat substrate.**

- **Protein 120–200 g/day.** Tom Brewer's pregnancy protein floor (1960s preeclampsia-prevention work). Milk (two to three quarts, ~75–100 g protein), eggs (two to four daily), gelatin (20–30 g), liver weekly, lean meat to appetite. Under-protein pregnancy is the field where GDM, preeclampsia, and IUGR cluster.
- **Salt 6–12 g/day, iodized, generously.** Brewer's clinical observation across thousands of pregnancies: liberal salt reduces preeclampsia and improves fetal outcomes. Sodium restriction is physiologically harmful in pregnancy.
- **Adequate sugar.** OJ, ripe fruit, honey, milk lactose, sucrose to appetite. Maternal blood glucose target is the fetal supply, not the GDM screening threshold. From the same 2006 newsletter: "I got interested in this when I talked to a healthy 'diabetic' woman who had a two year old child whose IQ must have been over 200, judging by his spontaneous precocious hobbies."
- **No PUFA, absolute.** Eliminate fish oil (the standard prenatal-vitamin PUFA load), seed oils, vegetable margarines. Replace with butter, ghee, coconut oil.
- **Coconut oil 2–4 tbsp/day.** Medium-chain bypass of the Randle block.
- **Thyroid screen and restoration if hypothyroid.** Free T3, free T4, TSH, reverse T3 against pregnancy-adjusted ranges (TSH below 2.5 first trimester). NDT or T3+T4 with obstetric coordination.
- **Daily raw carrot.** Pregnancy is an estrogen-loaded state by design; the gut-recirculation amplifies maternal FFA mobilization.
- **Magnesium 400 mg at bedtime. Vitamin D3 4,000–5,000 IU/day** in the second half.

**Reasonable carb adequacy, not carb counting.** Eat to appetite on the substrate above. If the obstetric team requires post-meal monitoring, supply it; readings within the pregnancy-adjusted range (fasting 70–95, 1-hour postprandial 110–140) require no further intervention. Persistently pathologic readings — exogenous insulin coordinated with the OB team as a bridge while the substrate protocol takes hold.

**Avoid in Phase 3.** Carb restriction below 150 g/day. Intermittent fasting (contraindicated in pregnancy). Anxious finger-stick monitoring (the cortisol from monitoring stress drives more FFA mobilization than a 10 mg/dL glucose excursion). Fish oil as a "prenatal omega-3." The Phase 1/2 high-dose aspirin and niacinamide are not pregnancy-applied without OB guidance — low-dose 81 mg aspirin is sometimes OB-prescribed for preeclampsia prevention, separate consideration.

## Thyroid restoration (the Broda Barnes intervention)

If a patient does only one thing from this protocol, it should be this. [[broda-barnes|Broda Barnes]]'s observation across 30+ years of practice, quoted by Peat in essentially every diabetes piece: his diabetic patients given thyroid never developed the expected complications — no retinopathy, no nephropathy, no peripheral vascular disease, no diabetic foot. The complications attributed to "the disease itself" were complications of chronic hypothyroidism layered on the diabetic state.

Mechanism is direct: thyroid hormone supports oxidative glucose metabolism at the cellular level, and hepatic T4-to-T3 conversion requires glucose. The diabetic liver — glycogen-depleted, fatty — cannot perform the conversion efficiently, so even normal-T4 patients run functionally hypothyroid at the tissue level. A hypothyroid diabetic is in compounded metabolic failure; restoring thyroid restores the substrate handling the entire protocol depends on.

**Diagnostic.** TSH alone is inadequate. Free T3, free T4, reverse T3, TSH — against the temperature-and-pulse signal ([[temperature-tracking]]). A "normal TSH" with morning temp 96.8°F and pulse 62 is functionally hypothyroid.

**Dose.** NDT at one-quarter to one-half grain (15–30 mg) with the largest meal, titrate 15 mg every one to two weeks against temp-and-pulse. Most diabetic patients land at one to two grains; a subset need a 5–10 µg T3 add-on at breakfast and lunch. Synthroid (T4-only) is the worst single choice in diabetes — impaired conversion, normal TSH, persistent symptoms.

**Endocrinology coordination.** Thyroid replacement in an insulin-using diabetic can *lower insulin requirements* as oxidative metabolism restores. Watch for hypoglycemia as the dose comes up; the endocrinologist titrates insulin downward in response. Aspirin-and-niacinamide can amplify T3 modestly — Peat in *Aspirin, Brain, and Cancer*: "by activating T3, aspirin can sometimes increase the temperature and pulse rate." Track daily through the titration.

## Gut decontamination

The endotoxin → nitric oxide → insulin resistance → beta-cell toxicity chain is one of the easier layers to address. Gut bacteria on high-PUFA, low-fiber, fermentable-starch diets produce LPS, which crosses the gut barrier, activates Kupffer cells, drives systemic inflammation, induces nitric oxide synthase, and damages beta cells via the peroxynitrite mechanism. Lowering the endotoxin load addresses every downstream lesion at once.

- **Daily raw carrot.** Peeled or scraped, julienned (not pulverized — Peat: "what doesn't work very well is to grind the carrot very fine"), with vinegar, salt, coconut or olive oil. Binds biliary estrogen, sweeps small-bowel overgrowth, lowers serum endotoxin within days.
- **Cooked white button mushrooms 2–4 oz/day.** Bengmark's protocol — sliced, simmered an hour. Chitin and mannan suppress inflammatory populations.
- **Low-fermentable diet.** Eliminate beans, lentils, raw alliums in volume, raw brassicas in volume, whole grains as a staple. Starch: well-cooked white rice, well-peeled potato. Sugar from fruit and lactose is the easier carb.
- **Activated charcoal, intermittent.** Peat citing Frolkis: "only intermittently" because "it does destroy some vitamins by oxidation." 1–3 g, away from meals and medications, once or twice weekly during inflammation flares.
- **Bowel transit.** Target one complete daily BM. Magnesium glycinate 400–800 mg at bedtime; cascara sagrada (low-dose) if magnesium alone doesn't move it.

Full detail at [[endotoxin-reduction]].

## Neuropathy and nerve restoration

The 2014-03-21 KMUD broadcast is the anchor for the neuropathy sub-protocol. Peat's framing: diabetic peripheral neuropathy is the slow result of chronic FFA exposure on nerve myelin, PUFA lipid-peroxidation damage in the same tissue, chronic hypothyroid-and-hypoglycemic injury to small fibers, and microvascular changes cutting off nerve substrate supply.

- **[[progesterone|Progesterone]] for its neurosteroid action.** Direct neurotrophic factor in peripheral nerve; Peat repeatedly cited progesterone-induced myelin regeneration. Women 10–30 mg/day; men with severe neuropathy 4–8 drops Progest-E (~12–24 mg).
- **Thyroid.** Cold extremities and slow capillary refill are hypothyroid signals; restoration improves peripheral perfusion and substrate supply.
- **B-vitamins.** Thiamine 100–500 mg/day (benfotiamine if absorption is an issue), B6 25–50 mg/day, methylcobalamin 1,000 µg/day sublingual (critical in metformin patients — metformin depletes B12 reliably). Brewer's yeast supplies most of the B-profile.
- **Magnesium 400–600 mg at bedtime.** Glycinate or topical Epsom.
- **Methylene blue 0.5–4 mg/day** in selected patients, away from any serotonergic medication. **MAOI interaction warning is non-negotiable** — off SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics, MAOIs at least two weeks (six for fluoxetine) before starting. See [[methylene-blue-protocol]].
- **Coconut oil 2–4 tbsp/day.** Medium-chain substrate the damaged nerve can use without the FFA block.
- **Aspirin** at Phase 1 dose for anti-glycation and microvascular protection.

Recovery is slow (months to years) and incomplete (advanced demyelination doesn't fully reverse), but trajectory is toward improvement rather than the standard "nerve damage is permanent" picture. Patients report reduced burning, returning sensation, improved sleep across the first six months.

## Diabetic-foot prevention

End-stage of microvascular-plus-neuropathic disease — arterial insufficiency, ulceration, infection, amputation. Prevention matters far more than treatment.

- **Neuropathy protocol above** as the foundation.
- **Daily foot inspection** (patient and partner). Cuts, blisters, callus changes, color, temperature asymmetry. Pathology starts small and silent because neuropathy has eliminated the pain signal.
- **Properly fitted shoes**, no pressure points, no rubbing seams. Most diabetic-foot ulcers are pressure ulcers from ill-fitting shoes a non-neuropathic patient would have corrected.
- **Warmth.** Cold feet are a microvascular danger sign. Wool socks indoors in winter; warm foot baths at bedtime (magnesium chloride or Epsom).
- **No prolonged sitting with feet down.** Venous stasis compounds arterial insufficiency.
- **Podiatry every three to six months** in any patient with neuropathy.

## Coordinating with conventional treatment

The framework is adjunctive. The endocrinologist, PCP, and (in pregnancy) OB are the dose-decision-makers; the framework's job is to disclose what's being added, ask the clinician to monitor for expected interactions, and support gradual reduction *as the clinician determines it's safe*.

**Insulin. Tapering NEVER without your endocrinologist.** As the protocol restores oxidative metabolism, insulin demand falls — gradually, with CGM as the safety net. The endocrinologist titrates downward in response to the new substrate environment. Self-tapering risks hyperglycemic decompensation in one direction and severe hypoglycemia in the other.

**Metformin.** Works by partial mitochondrial Complex I inhibition — it mimics the underlying disease rather than reversing it, and depletes B12 reliably. As Phase 1 takes hold and HbA1c improves, patients can ask their physician about a trial-off (half the dose for two to four weeks, then off, with weekly fasting-glucose monitoring). B12 1,000 µg sublingual methylcobalamin daily for any patient over a year on metformin.

**GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro).** Short-term blood-glucose-and-weight effects produced by inducing a low-intake state on a stress-mediated system; long-term costs (30–40% of weight loss as lean mass, gastroparesis, pancreatitis risk, emerging cardiovascular and bone data) are the predictable consequences. Transition off: 25% dose reduction every two to four weeks, with the substrate protocol becoming more important during the taper as appetite returns.

**SGLT2 inhibitors.** Kidney-glucose-dump mechanism. Tolerable but produces euglycemic-ketoacidosis risk (relevant with carb restriction) and UTI / Fournier's risk. As Phase 1 improves HbA1c, prescriber's call whether to maintain for the EMPA-REG cardiovascular signal or discontinue.

**Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glyburide).** Force beta-cell secretion — the worst single class for a restorative protocol because it exhausts the beta cells the protocol is trying to preserve. First class to transition off as Phase 1 takes hold.

## Tracking panel

Minimal-viable monitoring per Peat's [[diabetes|email exchange]]: "I think it's valuable to have a blood test for vitamin D to regulate the dose; TSH can be useful, too." Layered with the framework-standard markers:

- **Morning oral temperature** (daily, on waking, before getting up). Target trending toward 97.8–98.2°F.
- **Afternoon temperature** (one to three hours after lunch). Target 98.4–98.8°F.
- **Resting pulse** (first thing in the morning, sitting). Target 75–85 bpm.
- **Fasting glucose** (every one to two weeks early in the protocol, less frequent as stability returns). Interpret in context: a fasting glucose of 110 with morning temperature 97.0 and pulse 65 is the cortisol-and-adrenaline pattern; the intervention is bedtime snacking and adequate evening calories, not metformin.
- **Two-hour postprandial glucose** (more useful than fasting glucose as a substrate-handling marker). Should return to baseline within the normal range after a mixed meal of carbohydrate, protein, and saturated fat.
- **HbA1c** (every three months). Lags the metabolic-state markers by months; track but don't over-react.
- **Vitamin D 25-OH** (every six to twelve months). Target 50–80 ng/mL.
- **TSH, free T3, free T4, reverse T3** (every three to six months during titration; annually at steady state).
- **B12** (annually, especially on metformin). Target above 500 pg/mL.
- **Ferritin** (annually). Target 30–70 ng/mL; above 100 is the iron-overload warning.
- **CGM** (continuous glucose monitor) — strongly recommended in Type 1, optional in Type 2. Catches the hypoglycemic excursions that insulin-or-thyroid-restoration produces before they become symptomatic.
- **Qualitative**: warm hands and feet, sleep continuity, absence of stress cravings, regular bowel function, energy stability, mood stability. These are the daily clinical signals — more reliable than monthly lab numbers.

## Failure modes

Common patterns that produce a stalled protocol:

- **Continued PUFA exposure.** The single most consequential failure mode. The patient runs the rest of the protocol cleanly but continues to eat restaurant food cooked in seed oil, takes a "heart-healthy" fish-oil supplement, eats "healthy fats" like flaxseed-laced cereal. The Randle block doesn't release; the protocol stalls indefinitely. PUFA elimination must be absolute.
- **Carbohydrate restriction.** The patient hears "no PUFA, restore respiration" and pairs it with a low-carb or keto diet. The cortisol-and-adrenaline pattern intensifies, the morning temperature drops further, the protocol delivers the opposite of its intent. Carbohydrate adequacy is non-negotiable.
- **Inadequate thyroid dose.** The patient titrates to a half-grain of NDT, sees mild improvement, stops there. Most patients need one to two grains; under-dosing produces the persistent-symptom-with-normal-TSH pattern that is the central diagnostic complaint of the modern diabetic.
- **Sulfonylurea retention.** The patient stays on glipizide while running the substrate protocol. The drug forces continued beta-cell secretion the protocol is trying to relieve. Transition off in coordination with the prescriber.
- **Magnesium under-dosing.** Magnesium oxide tablets at 200 mg/day are the typical mainstream prescription; the protocol needs 400–600 mg of bioavailable magnesium (glycinate, taurate, malate, or transdermal Epsom).
- **HbA1c over-reaction.** The lab lags the metabolic state by months. A patient whose temperature has risen and whose pulse has come up but whose HbA1c hasn't moved yet is in the recovery curve, not a failed protocol. Recheck at three months, not at three weeks.
- **Fasting / IF.** The intermittent-fasting culture has produced a generation of diabetics who fast 16 hours daily and pair it with metformin. The cortisol-and-adrenaline mobilization across the fasting window drives FFA release that worsens the Randle block. Discontinue fasting; eat to appetite with adequate substrate; the picture improves over weeks.
- **Anxious glucose monitoring.** Checking the meter eight times a day in a stressed patient drives a cortisol response that itself raises glucose. CGM with target ranges, not finger-stick obsession.

## What to AVOID

- **Carbohydrate restriction** as the central intervention — worsens the underlying respiratory damage while improving the surrogate marker.
- **Ketogenic diets** in diabetes — the cortisol-and-glucagon state drives FFA release that compounds the Randle block.
- **Prolonged fasting** — same pattern in concentrated form.
- **High-PUFA "Mediterranean" diet variants** — heavy olive oil + fish + nuts is a high-PUFA dietary pattern that the popular literature has confused with a Peat-aligned diet. Olive oil in small amounts as a flavor fat is fine; the high-PUFA-Mediterranean pattern is not.
- **Fish oil and omega-3 supplementation** — the Hammes 1996 finding that fish oil accelerates diabetic retinopathy is the central data point. Concentrated PUFA is concentrated insult.
- **Iron-fortified foods and iron supplements** in adults without lab-documented deficiency. Iron overload drives the same oxidative damage in the diabetic capillary bed that PUFA does.
- **Statins** as routine therapy in metabolically healthy diabetics. The Mendelian-randomization case applies in selected high-risk patients; the routine statin depletes CoQ10 and worsens hepatic metabolism the protocol depends on. See [[heart-disease-protocol]] for the statin renegotiation conversation.
- **Self-tapering insulin** under any circumstances. Tapering NEVER without the endocrinologist.
- **Synthroid (T4-only) monotherapy** — the impaired-conversion picture leaves the patient functionally hypothyroid at tissue level.
- **High-dose niacin** (the flush form) at the doses Peat used for niacinamide. Niacinamide is the form; niacin is hepatotoxic at the gram-per-day range.
- **SSRIs and serotonergic antidepressants** — concurrent with the protocol's methylene blue layer, the MAOI interaction produces serotonin syndrome.
- **Bariatric surgery** as a first-line intervention. The framework would not dispute the empirical effect — the surgery does what it does — but treats it as the most aggressive possible form of carbohydrate restriction with proportional costs (B12, iron, calcium deficiencies; the eating-disorder-like patterns of chronic restriction).

## When to escalate

The protocol is for stable, ambulatory diabetic patients in coordinated care with their physicians. Escalate to emergency or specialist care for:

- **Diabetic ketoacidosis** (DKA) — fruity breath, deep rapid breathing, vomiting, abdominal pain, altered mental status, ketones on the meter, glucose >250 with bicarbonate <18. Emergency department, immediately.
- **Severe hypoglycemia** — confusion, seizure, loss of consciousness in an insulin- or sulfonylurea-using patient. 911 or ER; do not wait.
- **Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state** (HHS) — severe hyperglycemia (>600), severe dehydration, altered mental status, especially in elderly Type 2 patients. Emergency department.
- **New-onset chest pain, shortness of breath, focal neurological symptoms** — diabetic patients have elevated cardiovascular and stroke risk. ER, not waiting until morning.
- **Diabetic-foot ulceration with infection** — fever, spreading erythema, drainage, deep ulceration. Podiatry / vascular surgery / infectious-disease team urgently; outpatient management of an infected diabetic-foot ulcer outside specialist coordination is the most common single mistake leading to amputation.
- **Pregnancy with persistently elevated glucose** that does not respond to the Phase 3 substrate protocol within two to four weeks — obstetric team coordination, possible insulin bridging.
- **Pediatric Type 1 diabetes** — the framework's adult-Type-1 protocol does not translate directly. Pediatric Type 1 should be managed primarily by pediatric endocrinology; supportive measures (PUFA depletion, adequate sugar within carbohydrate counting, vitamin D and thyroid evaluation) can be added but never substituted.
- **Rapidly progressing diabetic retinopathy** — ophthalmologist; the framework's slower restorative interventions cannot reverse acute proliferative retinopathy.
- **Advanced nephropathy approaching dialysis** — nephrology team; framework's interventions are preventive and supportive, not reversal-of-stage-4-CKD.
