# Fertility

This protocol is for couples — never one partner alone. In Peat's framework, "infertility" almost always means a metabolic problem in one or both partners that is also a thyroid problem, a [[pufa]] problem, and a stress problem. The mainstream clinic frames it as a plumbing problem in the woman, solved by IVF. Peat's frame is the opposite: a healthy couple eating well for six months, even in their thirties or early forties, with normal body temperature and adequate luteal-phase progesterone, will conceive more often than not. When they do not, the bottleneck is upstream of the reproductive organs. Run [[core-diet-protocol]] first; this page is what you add on top.

## When to Use This Protocol

Run this as **pre-conception preparation** if any of the following are true:

- Planning to conceive within 6–12 months.
- Trying for six months or more without success; doctor has used "unexplained."
- One or both partners has [[hypothyroidism]], low body temperature, cold extremities, or estrogen-dominance symptoms (heavy periods, PMS, breast tenderness, fibroids, endometriosis).
- Male partner has borderline or low sperm count, motility, or morphology.
- Previous miscarriage, especially early or recurrent.
- Over 35 and the clinic is pushing [[ivf|IVF]] or AMH testing.
- Coming off [[hormonal-contraception|hormonal birth control]] and want endogenous cycling restored first.

**Skip and route to a clinician** if there is anatomical obstruction (blocked tubes, uterine septum, severe varicocele); azoospermia; primary ovarian insufficiency with FSH consistently above 25; or threatened miscarriage in an active pregnancy (jump to the implantation section).

## Phase 1: Foundation (Both Partners, 6 Months Pre-Conception)

The six-month window is not arbitrary. Sperm take ~74 days to mature; today's diet shows up in semen analysis in two and a half months. Ova are recruited from the resting pool ~100–120 days before ovulation; this month's egg was metabolically committed three to four months ago. Six months gives both gametes a full developmental cycle in a corrected environment.

### Foundation 1 — The diet, both partners

Run [[core-diet-protocol]] as the daily template for both partners. The four staples (milk, orange juice, salt, coffee with cream and sugar) deliver the floor; the optimization layers (gelatin, liver, oysters, coconut oil, eggs, daily carrot, aged cheese, ice cream) deliver trace nutrients and metabolic-rate support; the hard exclusions (vegetable oils, fish oil, soy, raw cruciferous in volume, beans, whole grains as staples, fasting, low-fat/low-salt dieting, plant milks) remove substrates that suppress thyroid and elevate estrogen.

Fertility-specific: oysters once a week carry zinc, copper, selenium, and iodine — direct cofactors for steroidogenesis, sperm production, and ovulation. Liver (4–8 oz weekly) carries vitamin A, the cofactor without which cholesterol cannot be converted to [[pregnenolone]], [[progesterone]], and [[testosterone]]. Gelatinous broth carries glycine. Coconut oil displaces PUFA from cellular lipids over time.

### Foundation 2 — Thyroid first, both partners

Hypothyroidism is the most common upstream cause of "unexplained" infertility in both sexes. Peat's [[menses|email exchanges]]: "Hypothyroidism is the basic cause of heavy menstruation." Heavy and painful periods are a thyroid signal long before a uterine signal. The [[thyroid|thyroid page]] gives the male side: "Hypothyroid men show decreased testosterone and increased estrogen, a pattern that correlates with low sperm count and poor sperm motility."

Mechanism runs through three places at once. Thyroid is required to convert carotene to retinol, and retinol is required to convert cholesterol to pregnenolone — without thyroid the steroidogenic cascade stalls. Low thyroid raises [[serotonin]] and [[prolactin]], both of which suppress reproductive function. Low metabolic rate means free fatty acids dominate over glucose, so the [[randle-cycle|Randle block]] suppresses glucose oxidation in ovary, testis, and uterus.

Use [[temperature-tracking]] as the diagnostic. Morning oral temperature should be at least 97.8°F and rise to 98.6°F by mid-morning. Resting pulse 75–85 bpm. If either partner is below those numbers for two weeks, run [[hypothyroid-recovery]] first. Peat's [[thyroid-misconceptions|"Thyroid: Misconceptions" (1993)]]: "Exercise lowers human (and some animals') fertility, and winter lowers animals' fertility. I think human fertility, as indicated by sperm count, for example, is likely to be lower in winter."

If diet alone has not restored temperature and pulse by month two, a low dose of thyroid (Cytomel/Cynomel, ~1 mcg T3 every 2–4 hours, titrated by symptom) is standard. T3 over T4-only because T3 is the hormone the gonads use directly.

### Foundation 3 — PUFA depletion, both partners

The dietary [[pufa]] load suppresses thyroid, elevates estrogen, and damages the membranes gametes are built from. Sperm in particular are mostly membrane. The Peat anecdote from [[oils-in-context|"Oils in Context"]]: an oil researcher spent 100 days on "the Eskimo diet" of seal blubber and mackerel paste. "His blood lipid peroxides reached a level 50 times higher than normal, and although MDA is teratogenic, he said he wasn't worried about fathering deformed children, because his sperm count had gone to zero." In [[immunodeficiency-dioxins-stress-and-the-hormones|"Immunodeficiency, Dioxins, Stress, and the Hormones"]] Peat grouped "decreased sperm counts, premature births and birth defects" with the postwar shift from saturated animal fats to industrial seed oils. The global sperm-count decline tracks PUFA more closely than any other dietary variable.

Six months is the minimum window for a low-PUFA diet to show up in tissue composition. Full [[pufa-depletion]] takes years, but six months of corrected phospholipids improves sperm parameters and egg quality measurably in most couples.

### Foundation 4 — Estrogen clearance, both partners

In men, excess estrogen suppresses testosterone, raises [[prolactin]], and produces the "low T" picture urologists treat with exogenous testosterone (which further suppresses sperm). The fix is removing substrates that drive [[aromatase]] activity — stress, PUFA, alcohol, adipose. Belly fat in men is a self-sustaining estrogen factory. The daily raw carrot binds intestinal endotoxin and unconjugated estrogen; [[orange-juice|orange juice]] supplies naringenin (a direct aromatase inhibitor); saturated fat plus coffee support hepatic clearance.

In women, tissue estrogen is typically 20–30 times serum; that pool suppresses ovulation, drives endometriosis, and prevents implantation. Peat's [[progesterone|progesterone summary]]: "Stress activates the estrogen receptor. Once estrogen is acting, that turns on the cell's production of the progesterone receptor. When progesterone binds to that receptor, it destroys the estrogen receptor." Lowering tissue estrogen is a precondition for the progesterone signal that sustains the luteal phase and implantation.

### Foundation 5 — Vitamin E, both partners

Vitamin E was originally called the "antisterility vitamin." Peat's [[vitamin-e-estrogen-antagonist-energy-promoter-and-anti-inflammatory|"Vitamin E"]] article: it "had been found to improve fertility of both male and female animals, and to prevent intrauterine death of the embryo or fetus." Peat's thesis advisor Soderwall "showed that providing hamsters with extra vitamin E postponed the onset of infertility in middle age." Peat's own experiments: "vitamin E increased the amount of oxygen in the uterus, correcting an oxygen deficiency produced either by supplemental estrogen or by old age."

The role is protection of polyunsaturated membranes from peroxidation. Sperm, oocytes, and endometrial lining are unusually vulnerable. Peat preferred mixed tocopherols at 100–400 IU/day in his [[vitamin-e|email exchanges]], with a caveat: "the products have been changing frequently in the last 15 years, so I think it's good to be cautious and use minimal doses."

## Phase 2: Cycle Tracking (Female Partner, Months 1–6)

The ovulation calendar app is not enough. Most apps assume a 28-day cycle and predict ovulation on day 14 — wrong for most women most of the time. Real cycles vary by five days or more. A fertility window placed on the wrong day is the most common reason "we're trying" produces nothing. Track the actual cycle, not the textbook average.

### Method 1 — Basal body temperature (BBT)

The same digital thermometer used for [[temperature-tracking]]. Take temperature on waking, before getting out of bed or drinking water. Plot on paper or an app (Kindara, Read Your Body).

Pattern: temperature runs lower in the follicular phase (97.2–97.8°F in a healthy woman; lower in hypothyroid women), rises 0.4–0.6°F at ovulation, stays elevated through the luteal phase, drops at menstruation. Elevated readings for at least 12 days post-ovulation indicate an adequate luteal phase.

Fertility signals:

- **Follicular phase below 97.0°F**: hypothyroidism is suppressing ovulation. Route to [[hypothyroid-recovery]] first.
- **No thermal shift mid-cycle**: anovulatory cycle. Common after coming off hormonal contraception, in perimenopause, or in chronic stress. Address upstream for 1–3 cycles.
- **Thermal shift present, luteal phase under 10 days**: short luteal phase. Classic indication for cyclic progesterone (Phase 4).
- **Thermal shift present, luteal phase 12+ days, no implantation over several cycles**: ovulation intact, implantation failing. Check male workup, thyroid antibodies, tissue estrogen.

### Method 2 — Cervical mucus

In the days before ovulation, cervical mucus shifts from sticky to clear, slippery, "egg white." This is the high-fertility window — intercourse here (especially the two days before the thermal shift) produces conception more often than any other timing. Mucus predicts ovulation; BBT confirms it. Toni Weschler's *Taking Charge of Your Fertility* is the standard reference (not Peat, but compatible).

### Method 3 — Intercourse pattern

Every other day during the fertile window (five days leading up to and including predicted ovulation) maximizes conception probability while preserving sperm density. Daily intercourse over a long window depletes density; abstaining over three days lets dead sperm accumulate. Sperm survive in cervical crypts up to five days; the egg is viable for 12–24 hours. Have living sperm present when the egg arrives — do not try to time ejaculation to the hour.

## Phase 3: Male Protocol

The male protocol is short because most of it is in Phase 1. Run [[core-diet-protocol]], get thyroid temperature into range, eliminate PUFA, and semen analysis follows. The additions:

### Male 1 — Oysters and shellfish, weekly

Six oysters a week deliver zinc at a dose no supplement matches for bioavailability. Zinc is required for testosterone synthesis, sperm chromatin condensation, and the acrosome reaction. Selenium (shellfish, plus one Brazil nut a day — not a handful) is the cofactor for the glutathione peroxidase that protects sperm DNA. Copper supports the mitochondrial chain on which motility depends.

### Male 2 — Gelatin and glycine, daily

Muscle meat is heavy in tryptophan, methionine, and cysteine — all three pro-inflammatory in excess, raising [[serotonin]] and suppressing thyroid. Gelatin from bone broth (10–20 g/day) or powdered glycine (3 g before bed) balances the profile and supports the GABA-ergic calm the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis needs.

### Male 3 — Not the bodybuilder "fertility diet"

The low-T-forum recommendation of broccoli, salmon, almonds, and lean chicken is wrong. Broccoli is goitrogenic in volume, salmon is a PUFA source, almonds are seed-oil in shell form, lean chicken without skin or gelatin is pro-inflammatory amino acids without protective glycine. Run the same plate the female partner runs. Coffee with cream and sugar after breakfast supports testosterone via lower [[cortisol]]; saturated fat from dairy and coconut oil supports the cholesterol pool.

### Male 4 — Avoid the saboteurs

Hot tubs and saunas above body temperature reduce sperm count for weeks. Laptop on lap, tight underwear, and frequent cycling do the same. Marijuana lowers testosterone and disrupts morphology in regular users. Alcohol in volume converts testosterone to estrogen via hepatic aromatase. Skip "T-booster" supplements.

### Male 5 — Testing

Baseline semen analysis at the start of the six-month window if there is any history of difficulty. Re-test at month three (one spermatogenic cycle into corrected diet) and month six. Targets: count >15 million/mL, motility >40% progressive, morphology >4% normal forms by strict criteria, volume >1.5 mL. If motility and morphology have not moved by month three, route to thyroid retesting and consider a low-dose T3 trial.

## Phase 4: Female Protocol

The additions Phase 1 alluded to: progesterone in the luteal phase, vitamin K from greens, and a salt-and-sugar emphasis the standard Core Diet supplies but the fertility application makes non-negotiable.

### Female 1 — Cyclic progesterone (luteal phase)

If BBT shows a luteal phase under 12 days, or there is recurrent early miscarriage, cyclic progesterone is the direct intervention. Natural USP progesterone dissolved in vitamin E ([[progest-e|Progest-E]], or bioidentical progesterone in mixed tocopherols) is applied to gums or skin from the thermal shift until menstruation. If pregnancy occurs, continue through the first trimester — the corpus luteum makes progesterone for the first 10 weeks; the placenta takes over at week 10. Tapering early is a common miscarriage cause.

Dose: Peat's [[progesterone|email exchanges]] start at "just a couple of drops" (~10 mg), titrated by symptom. Luteal-phase support: 20–40 mg/day, divided morning and evening, on gums. Threatened miscarriage: 100–200 mg/day under physician supervision.

Timing: starting before ovulation suppresses it via pituitary negative feedback: "symptoms of increased estrogen and cortisol are the result of either extraneous ingredients in the creams, or from using it at the wrong time, for example, too early, triggering premature ovulation." Wait for the thermal shift.

If cycles are anovulatory or too irregular for BBT, Peat's [[menses|calendar fallback]]: "go by the calendar, so 6 or 10 or 14 days later might coincide with the ovulation cycle... if there's no cycle you can detect, just starting the progesterone could renew the rhythm."

### Female 2 — Vitamin K from well-cooked greens

K1 (leafy greens) and K2 (grass-fed dairy, egg yolks, natto if tolerated) support endometrial development, oppose pathological clotting, and contribute to bone mineralization. Cook greens thoroughly — raw cruciferous in volume is goitrogenic. Chard, kale, collards, mustard greens, and beet tops give K1; K2 comes free from the [[core-diet-protocol]] dairy.

### Female 3 — Salt, sugar, and appetite

Override years of training to under-salt and under-sugar. Low blood volume and low blood sugar both raise [[cortisol]], and cortisol drives the estrogen-dominance pattern that suppresses fertility. Morning OJ with salt within five minutes of waking prevents the cortisol-adrenaline surge that would raise [[free-fatty-acids]]. The bedtime snack prevents nocturnal hypoglycemia. If menstruation is heavy, salt and sugar are doubly important during the bleed — [[menses|email exchanges]]: "Hypothyroidism is the basic cause of heavy menstruation. Things strong enough to kill an embryo can't be harmless to a person." Skip the contraceptive-pill fix.

Calorie restriction in the preconception window is the most common self-sabotage. Most fertility difficulty in women under 35 in well-fed populations is functional hypothalamic amenorrhea or sub-clinical undereating. Eat to appetite plus a margin. Pre-pregnancy weight gain of 5–10 lb is the protocol working, not failing.

### Female 4 — Aspirin in selected cases

[[Aspirin]] appears in Peat's [[eclampsia-in-the-real-organism-a-paradigm-of-general-distress-applicable-in-infants-adults-etc|eclampsia newsletter]] as a fertility-relevant agent: "it seems to protect the fetus against intrauterine growth retardation." For women with recurrent miscarriage, antiphospholipid antibodies, or signs of low-grade inflammation, low-dose aspirin (81 mg/day) from before conception through early pregnancy fits the framework. Peat's caveat: "aspirin can interfere with blood clotting, its use around the time of childbirth can be risky, and it is best to correct the problem early enough that aspirin isn't needed." Stop before delivery; coordinate with the obstetric provider.

## Implantation and Early Pregnancy

The hardest weeks are the two before the missed period and the eight after. Most miscarriages happen before the woman knows she is pregnant. The metabolic state in this window determines whether the embryo implants and whether the corpus luteum makes enough progesterone to sustain it.

Peat's [[aging-ovaries-not-the-eggs|"Aging Ovaries, Not the Eggs"]]: "Even in young individuals, when stress occurs around the time of ovulation, interference with progesterone production will prevent implantation. If progesterone becomes deficient after the embryo has become implanted, miscarriage occurs." On oxygen: "Old animals had too little oxygen in their uterus to keep the embryo alive at the time it would normally be ready to implant... they remain fertile if they are given a supplement of vitamin E and progesterone."

Practical additions:

- Continue cyclic [[progesterone]] from the thermal shift through the missed period; if pregnancy is confirmed, continue through week 12 minimum.
- Increase calories. First trimester ~150 extra/day, second ~300, third ~450. If anything is cut, cut from sugar or fat — not protein or salt.
- Hold salt at or above the [[core-diet-protocol]] floor. The [[eclampsia-in-the-real-organism-a-paradigm-of-general-distress-applicable-in-infants-adults-etc|eclampsia newsletter]] is explicit that low-salt pregnancy dieting raises stress hormones and contributes to toxemia.
- Avoid the standard pregnancy supplement stack: fish oil, "DHA for fetal brain development," prenatal vitamins with seed-oil-based vitamin E carriers. From the eclampsia newsletter: "Marian Diamond's work with rats clearly showed that increased exposure to estrogen during pregnancy reduced the size of the cerebral cortex and the animals' ability to learn, while progesterone increased the brain size and intelligence."
- Treat morning sickness as hypoglycemia plus salt depletion: "salty carbohydrate (soda crackers, typically) is the standard remedy for morning sickness." Crackers, salted juice, or milk-with-honey by the bed.
- For eclamptic warning signs (visual disturbance, persistent headache, edema, rising BP, proteinuria), the protocol is more food (protein and albumin), more salt, magnesium, and supplemental thyroid — under physician supervision. Broda Barnes "delivered more than 2,000 babies and never had a case of pre-eclampsia, though statistically 100 would have been expected."

Katharina Dalton's longitudinal data is the strongest argument for staying on progesterone through the first trimester in at-risk pregnancies. From [[progesterone]]: "Her patients' babies, who usually came from working-class backgrounds with average IQs of 90, tended to average 130 IQ after progesterone treatment. The intelligence of the babies corresponded to the amount of progesterone she gave the mothers."

## Counter-arguments

### Mainstream fertility medicine and IVF

The reproductive-endocrinology consensus is that fertility is age-bounded, that AMH is a reliable reserve marker, that "unexplained infertility" warrants IVF after a defined interval, and that male factor is best addressed by ICSI rather than diet. The honest counter: "unexplained" usually means "we did not look at thyroid or PUFA," and age-as-destiny ignores Peat's central point — age-related infertility is a uterine-oxygen and progesterone problem more than an egg-quality problem. From [[aging-ovaries-not-the-eggs|"Aging Ovaries, Not the Eggs"]]: "Many experimenters have demonstrated that old animals that have become infertile keep producing eggs. Several experimenters have removed eggs from the ovaries of old animals, and transplanted the fertilized eggs into young animals, where the embryos were able to implant and develop normally." The eggs are fine; the uterine environment is the bottleneck. IVF moves the embryo around the tubal-cervical part of the problem but cannot fix the uterine environment, which is why IVF success rates drop with maternal age in step with progesterone decline.

IVF is the right tool for anatomical obstruction (blocked tubes, azoospermia, surgical history). It is the wrong first tool for "unexplained infertility," "diminished ovarian reserve" by AMH alone, or recurrent first-trimester miscarriage.

### AMH testing

AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone) correlates loosely with the antral follicle pool but not with egg quality, natural-conception probability, or time-to-pregnancy in women still cycling. Low AMH in a 38-year-old with normal cycle, BBT, luteal phase, and partner sperm parameters means almost nothing. Clinics nonetheless use it as the threshold for immediate IVF recommendation.

### The age-as-destiny frame

The "fertility falls off a cliff at 35" claim is sourced largely from 18th-century French parish records and 20th-century IVF outcomes — neither represents modern women in their late 30s and 40s on a corrected protocol. Most "age-related infertility" is the accumulated effect of decades of PUFA exposure, hormonal contraception, chronic stress, hypothyroidism, and estrogen dominance — most of which are reversible in months. A 40-year-old with a corrected diet, normal temperature, adequate progesterone, and a metabolically healthy partner has a better prognosis than a 28-year-old on the standard American diet with a long history of birth-control pills.

### The "fertility is a women's problem" frame

Mainstream workups test the female partner exhaustively (HSG, AMH, FSH, ultrasound) before testing the male. Backwards. Male factor is 30–40% of fertility difficulty solo and 20–30% in combination. A $100 semen analysis tells you immediately whether the male partner needs a six-month correction. Do not let the workup proceed asymmetrically.

## Data gaps

**Progesterone dose for at-risk pregnancies.** Peat gave titration-by-symptom guidance but no definitive milligram protocol. Practitioner conventions: 100–200 mg/day for active threatened miscarriage, 20–40 mg/day for prophylactic luteal support. Coordinate with a physician.

**Vitamin E dose for fertility specifically.** Peat referenced Soderwall and his own uterine-oxygen experiments but published no human dosing protocol. The 100–400 IU mixed-tocopherols range from his [[vitamin-e|email exchanges]] is the working dose; higher-dose fertility benefit is undocumented.

**Male thyroid dosing for sperm parameters.** The female titration of T3 to morning temperature is documented. The male equivalent dose-response curve is not.

**Long-term outcomes.** Dalton's IQ findings come from a specific population (1950s–60s British women on high-dose progesterone for severe PMS and toxemia). No large-cohort follow-up exists for children conceived under a structured Peat preconception protocol.

**Endometriosis and PCOS.** General protocol applies; fertility-specific decisions (surgical timing in endometriosis; metformin / inositol / letrozole / clomid in PCOS) are not in Peat's writings.

**Seed-cycling, acupuncture, gua sha.** Peat did not address them. Neither endorsed nor contraindicated.
