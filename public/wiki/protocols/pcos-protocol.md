# PCOS Protocol

The action page. Mechanism — why PCOS is a downstream picture of hypothyroidism, gut endotoxin, and unopposed estrogen rather than a primary ovarian disease — lives on [[pcos]]. This document is the order of operations, dose ranges, timeline expectations, bridge-drug rules, and failure modes. It is for the woman who already understands her cysts are stalled ovulations and her androgens are adrenal, and who wants to know what to do on Monday morning.

Primary goal: restored ovulation. Cosmetic features (hirsutism, acne, hair loss) lag by six to twelve months. Weight is downstream of metabolic rate, not the lever. Fertility, if that is why you are here, is downstream of the same correction — link out to [[fertility]] when ovulation has returned.

Four phases in order. Phase 1: substrate (thyroid + liver fuel). Phase 2: gut. Phase 3: hormonal. Phase 4: fertility-specific. The metformin / spironolactone / OCP / GLP-1 question gets its own section — transitions are part of the protocol, not separate from it.

## Who This Protocol Is For

- Diagnosed PCOS by Rotterdam criteria, or a clinical picture that fits (irregular cycles, hirsutism, acne, scalp hair thinning, weight that won't move on caloric restriction, a luteal progesterone too low to read as ovulatory).
- Morning oral temperature below 98.0°F or resting pulse below 75 — markers that confirm the underlying hypothyroidism Rotterdam leaves out.
- Currently on metformin, spironolactone, an oral contraceptive, or a GLP-1 agonist, but wanting to transition off.
- Pre-conception, with cycle restoration as the staging goal before [[fertility]] add-ons.

Skip and route to a clinician for virilization advancing on a steep curve (deep voice change, clitoromegaly), serum testosterone above ~150 ng/dL (rule out adrenal or ovarian tumor), prolactin above 100 ng/mL (rule out prolactinoma), or decompensated hypothyroidism (heart failure signs, severe hyponatremia).

## Phase 1 — Substrate (Weeks 1 to 4)

Substrate before signal. The most common failure of every PCOS protocol is reaching for a hormone before the diet that fuels its production. A starved liver cannot run thyroid hormone, and supplemental progesterone applied to a tissue saturated with stored PUFA will be largely cleared before it reaches its receptor. Run the [[anti-stress-diet]] and [[hypothyroid-recovery]] baselines first; this section names what is PCOS-specific on top of them.

The four daily non-negotiables: two quarts of milk (~2,000 mg calcium, ~65 g protein — calcium suppresses parathyroid hormone, which is antithyroid and drives aromatase); one quart of fresh orange juice (fructose, potassium, and naringenin — a direct [[aromatase]] inhibitor giving the protocol a daily anti-estrogen drip); salt to appetite, iodized (PCOS patients have been told for years that salt restriction will fix the insulin picture — it will not; restriction raises aldosterone and adrenaline and worsens the adrenal-androgen cycle); coffee two to four cups daily with milk and sugar (suppresses estrogen, supports T4-to-T3 conversion, lowers [[prolactin]]). Add 4 to 6 ounces of [[liver]] weekly, two to three eggs daily for the cholesterol substrate that progesterone is made from, and one raw carrot daily (Phase 2 covers the carrot's mechanism). Cook in butter, coconut oil, or ghee. No seed oils, no fish oil — the PUFA exclusion matters more in PCOS than in almost any other condition because of the [[randle-cycle]] mechanism. The bedtime snack (milk and honey, fruit, ice cream) is non-optional: the PCOS 2 AM wakeup is a hypoglycemic cortisol-adrenaline surge, and feeding the liver before bed attenuates it within a week.

The thyroid layer: start [[temperature-tracking]] on day one. Three readings (waking, post-breakfast, mid-afternoon), targeting 98.0°F morning, 98.6°F afternoon, pulse 75 to 85. If after three weeks of compliant diet the morning temperature is still under 97.8 and the pulse under 75, add T3 — Cytomel or Cynomel, 4 to 8 mcg morning and again early afternoon, titrated weekly by symptom. T4-only therapy is the wrong tool for most PCOS patients because the same estrogen-and-PUFA picture that drives the syndrome also impairs hepatic T4-to-T3 conversion. Peat's preference, given in his [[thyroid-misconceptions|"Thyroid: Misconceptions"]] newsletter, was desiccated natural thyroid or combined T4+T3. Most women land between one and three grains daily at steady state.

Vitamin D is the supplement that goes in here, not later. From the [[2020-02-19-butter-living-podcast-butter-living-podcast-fertility-pregnancy-and-development|*Butter Living* podcast (February 2020)]]: "Vitamin D is helpful. Animal experiments show that removing the thyroid and giving gonadotropin to increase estrogen produces polycystic ovaries. This implies you want to normalize the thyroid and check the estrogen-to-progesterone balance." Dose 5,000 IU daily in the morning with food. Re-test at week 8 and titrate to a serum level of 50–80 ng/mL. Vitamin D is dampening the cytokine cascade endotoxin and high TSH drive, supporting local steroidogenesis in the granulosa cell, and improving beta-cell function in the pancreas at the same time.

Markers of progress by week 4: morning temperature trending toward 97.8 to 98.2, resting pulse above 75, the 2 AM wakeup softening, fewer afternoon crashes, hands and feet warmer. Cycle changes are not yet expected. If temperature has not moved, audit compliance — almost always the calorie floor, the salt, or a hidden PUFA source. If compliance is real, push T3 by 4 mcg and add a second weekly liver serving.

## Phase 2 — Gut (Weeks 2 to 8)

The gut layer runs in parallel with Phase 1, starting at week 2 once the diet is stable. This is the highest-leverage entry point in the syndrome and the one the standard workup ignores entirely. Mechanism — endotoxin → mitochondrial inhibition → apparent insulin resistance → ovarian estrogen elevation — sits on [[pcos]] and [[endotoxin]]. Actions are short.

**Raw carrot, daily, non-negotiable.** A medium carrot, scraped or julienned, with a teaspoon of vinegar, salt, and coconut or olive oil. The non-fermentable fiber binds conjugated estrogen the liver excretes in bile before it can be deconjugated and reabsorbed, and it physically sweeps bacterial overgrowth from the small intestine. From [[natural-estrogens|"Natural Estrogens"]]: "Because of my own experience in finding that eating a raw carrot daily prevented my migraines, I began to suspect that the carrot fiber was having both a bowel-protective and an antiestrogen effect. Several women who suffered from premenstrual symptoms, including migraine, had their serum estrogen measured before and after the 'carrot diet,' and they found that the carrot lowered their estrogen within a few days." Cooking destroys the fiber structure that does the work. Peat's clarification in his [[carrot|carrot email]]: "Just chewing a carrot is best, any saturated fat around the same time is o.k. What doesn't work very well is to grind the carrot very fine." Skip the food processor.

**Activated charcoal, cycled — not daily.** Peat was specific in his [[activated-charcoal|activated charcoal email]] that V. V. Frolkis used charcoal "only intermittently" because "it does destroy some vitamins by oxidation." The PCOS use case is acute endotoxin episodes — a bloating flare, a slow-transit week, a dietary slip with clear inflammatory rebound. Dose 1 to 3 grams, away from meals and medications by at least two hours, once or twice weekly. The carrot is the daily anchor; charcoal is episodic reinforcement. From [[bowel-toxins-accelerate-aging-process|"Bowel Toxins Accelerate Aging Process"]]: Frolkis's mice "lived 43% longer than animals on the standard diet when they periodically had activated charcoal added to their food. Besides endotoxin, I think the charcoal might protect against microbial estrogen and glucocorticoids, carbon monoxide, cyanide, and unsaturated oils."

**Low-fermentable, well-cooked.** Eliminate beans, lentils, raw alliums in volume, raw brassicas in volume, whole grains as a staple class — all fermentable substrate that feeds the LPS-producing populations the protocol is suppressing. Starch options are cooked white rice and well-peeled cooked potato. Ripe tropical fruit and dairy carbohydrate are the easier sugars. The rule is well-cooked, well-spaced meals, no fermentable bulk sitting in the small intestine for hours.

**Glycine.** A heaping tablespoon of [[glycine]] powder or dissolved gelatin once or twice daily. Glycine balances the tryptophan and cysteine load from any muscle meat in the diet, suppresses serotonin synthesis, and provides bile-acid conjugation substrate the liver needs to actually excrete estrogen. Dose is generous in PCOS because tissue tryptophan load is typically high (high cortisol, low thyroid, slow tryptophan oxidation).

**Betaine HCl, if indicated.** Low stomach acid is common in hypothyroid women and seeds bacterial overgrowth — bacteria that should be killed by gastric pH walk through into the duodenum. If there is heavy post-meal bloating, a sensation of food sitting for hours, or low ferritin despite adequate dietary iron, trial 500 to 1,500 mg with the largest meal. Stop if there is heartburn. Not a permanent supplement — three to six weeks usually clears the picture as thyroid normalizes and gastric secretion returns.

**Bowel transit.** Target one complete bowel movement daily. Constipated transit gives bacterial beta-glucuronidase more time to deconjugate biliary estrogen — the entire problem the carrot is solving. Magnesium glycinate 400 to 800 mg at bedtime is the first move; cascara sagrada (the gentle anthraquinone Peat recommended in [[estrogen-detox]]) is the second.

Markers of progress by week 8: less bloating, regular daily transit, the post-meal crash softening, less acne, occasionally a first signal of cycle change — a luteal phase that registered seven days now registering ten, or a period arriving on day 35 instead of day 60.

## Phase 3 — Hormonal (Weeks 4 to 12)

By week 4 the hormonal layer goes in. Where mainstream endocrinology adds metformin and spironolactone here, the Peat-aligned protocol adds [[progesterone]] in the luteal phase with [[aspirin]] and [[vitamin-k]] as supporting agents.

**Cyclic progesterone, luteal phase — the single highest-leverage intervention.** Natural progesterone dissolved in vitamin E (Progest-E is the original formulation), applied to gums, inner cheeks, or thin-skinned areas (inner wrists, neck) from approximately cycle day 14 through cycle day 28, or from the BBT thermal shift until menstruation. Start at 10 to 20 mg per day, divided morning and evening. Titrate by symptom — if breast tenderness and water retention soften, the dose is right; if symptoms barely respond, push to 30 to 50 mg per day. Peat's [[progesterone|progesterone email]]: "It's usual to start with just a couple of drops of progesterone; depending on your symptoms, that amount could be repeated, as needed." For severely irregular cycles or none at all, the [[menses|calendar fallback]]: "If you know the date of the last menstruation, you could go by the calendar, so 6 or 10 or 14 days later might coincide with the ovulation cycle... if there's no cycle you can detect, just starting the progesterone could renew the rhythm." Start on calendar days 14 through 28 of month one and expect the cycle to re-organize around it.

Timing rule: do not start progesterone before ovulation. From the same [[progesterone|email]]: "Symptoms of increased estrogen and cortisol are the result of either extraneous ingredients in the creams, or from using it at the wrong time, for example, too early, triggering premature ovulation." If BBT is unclear, wait for the thermal shift. What progesterone is doing in PCOS: destroying the estrogen receptor, suppressing the chronically elevated LH that drives adrenal androgens, inhibiting ovarian aromatase, and providing the GABA-ergic anti-stress signal that lowers cortisol.

**Aspirin, daily.** 325 mg with food in the morning, with a pinch of baking soda in water if there is stomach sensitivity. Aspirin directly suppresses aromatase (Peat called it "a very effective aromatase inhibitor") and blocks the prostaglandin E synthesis pathway that high tissue PUFA drives upward. The second job is more important during the multi-year tissue PUFA depletion window. From [[estrogen-detox]]: daily aspirin "compensates for a large fraction of the PUFA-driven aromatase drive during the transition period." Stop three to five days before any planned surgery and during heavy menstrual flow.

**Vitamin K, from food.** Well-cooked dark leafy greens for K1; grass-fed butter, hard aged cheese, and egg yolks for K2. Vitamin K supports the bile-acid pathway the liver uses to excrete estrogen and opposes the pathological clotting elevated estrogen drives.

**Pregnenolone, optional.** For women whose progesterone production is slow to return on thyroid plus diet alone, [[pregnenolone]] 50 to 200 mg daily with food provides upstream substrate with an excellent safety profile. Cycle it like progesterone if preferred. PCOS women generally do not need supplemental [[dhea|DHEA]] — adrenals are already over-producing it.

Markers by week 12: a cycle starting to organize even if irregular; clearer skin; steadier mood through the would-be luteal phase; less water retention; cosmetic features softening. Not all will be present. The first three months show metabolic markers (temperature, pulse, energy) more reliably than reproductive ones.

## Phase 4 — When Fertility Is the Goal

If pregnancy is the reason for running this, do not collapse PCOS and fertility into one phase. Stage them. Run Phases 1 to 3 until ovulation has returned for at least three consecutive cycles, then layer the [[fertility]] protocol on top. The fertility add-ons (vitamin E at higher doses, cyclic progesterone extended into early pregnancy, the male-partner six-month preconception preparation) assume an ovulating cycle to work with.

The male side is non-optional. From [[fertility]]: "Male factor is 30–40% of fertility difficulty solo and 20–30% in combination." Order a semen analysis at the start of Phase 4 and run the male partner on the [[core-diet-protocol]] for at least one full spermatogenic cycle (74 days) before drawing conclusions.

Once ovulation is established and [[fertility]] additions are running, the progesterone protocol continues through the luteal phase and, if conception occurs, through at least week 12 of pregnancy. Tapering progesterone before the placental handoff (week 10 to 12) is a common avoidable cause of first-trimester miscarriage in PCOS pregnancies.

## The Metformin, Spironolactone, OCP, and GLP-1 Question

Most women arriving here are already on at least one of these. None are the enemy; all are downstream bridges that became chronic. Do not stop cold — transitions are part of the protocol.

**Metformin.** Compatible with Phases 1 and 2. Continue through substrate and gut work. Once morning temperature is at or above 98.0 for four weeks and the cycle is organizing, taper by 250 mg weekly (1,500–2,000 mg/day to 1,000 to 500 to off, over two months). Re-test fasting glucose and HbA1c at six weeks post-taper. If markers worsen, the upstream cascade has not corrected — usually the gut work or PUFA — and the drug goes back on temporarily while you audit.

**Spironolactone.** Largely redundant with progesterone. Hold steady through Phases 1 to 3. Once progesterone is in steady use and cycles are organizing, taper by 50 mg weekly. Hirsutism and acne sometimes flare briefly in the transition as the androgen blockade washes out before progesterone's effect has fully taken over. Hold the protocol; do not chase the flare with retinoids or antibiotics. Resolution usually arrives by month four off.

**Oral contraceptives.** The most disruptive transition and the most worth doing. The pill suppresses the HPO axis entirely; the "regular bleeding" is a placebo-week withdrawal bleed, not a period. Run Phase 1 for at least eight weeks before stopping — thyroid and liver need that time. Stop at the end of a pack and expect one to three months before the first endogenous cycle (some women cycle back immediately, others take six months). Run progesterone cyclically using the calendar fallback starting month two if nothing has appeared. Do not restart the pill to "regulate" recovery — the pill does not regulate anything; it overrides the system entirely.

**GLP-1 agonists.** Newest bridge, weakest long-term track record. Results since 2022 are striking — fifteen to twenty-five percent weight loss with ovulation restoration in months. The Peat-side concern is that the mechanism is appetite suppression rather than restored oxidative metabolism, and the multi-decade cost in muscle, gallbladder, and pancreas is unmeasured. If already on one, run Phases 1 and 2 alongside it and re-evaluate at month six. If weight has moved into a healthier range, taper over two to three months while holding dietary and gut work tight. If weight has not moved, the GLP-1 is not the right tool and the upstream cascade was the real lever.

## Timeline

The protocol works on three nested clocks: the metabolic cycle (weeks), the menstrual cycle (months), and the tissue PUFA cycle (years).

**Weeks 1 to 4 — metabolic.** Temperature and pulse climb. The 2 AM wakeup softens. Daytime energy stabilizes. Hands and feet warm. Sleep deepens. No cycle change yet.

**Months 2 to 6 — cycle restoration.** First ovulation typically returns within three to six months: a clear BBT thermal shift, a luteal phase of ten to fourteen days, a period that arrives in the predicted window rather than spontaneously. Cycles are still often irregular — a 28-day cycle followed by a 42-day cycle is normal. Hair shedding may briefly increase as the hair cycle reorganizes; this is not the protocol failing.

**Months 6 to 12 — ovulatory normalization.** Cycles settle. Luteal progesterone rises. Hirsutism and acne meaningfully soften. Adrenal androgens fall. The string of pearls on ultrasound begins to resolve as ovulations complete rather than arrest. Cyclic progesterone often still helps but at a lower dose than months one through six.

**Months 12 to 24 — cosmetic catch-up.** Scalp hair returns toward baseline. Hirsutism softens further but rarely resolves completely without supplemental anti-androgen support — follicles laid down under high-androgen exposure during puberty are histologically committed, and only new growth comes back differently. Body composition recovers.

**Years 2 to 4 — tissue PUFA depletion.** The slowest variable. Stored linoleic acid has a tissue half-life of about two years, near-complete turnover at four. Until then, the body continues to release PUFA from fat stores under stress, driving aromatase activity at a baseline higher than it will eventually be. Aspirin and vitamin E during this window are not optional. See [[pufa-depletion]].

Failure modes by phase: if temperature has not moved by week 4, the diet is not actually complete. If no cycle change by month 4, audit progesterone timing (most common error: starting too early) and the PUFA exclusion. If cosmetic features have not softened by month 12, run an estrogen audit per [[estrogen-detox]] — almost always a hidden xenoestrogen source (lavender or tea tree oil in personal care, soy lecithin in chocolate, a supplemental vitamin E in soy oil).

## Counter-arguments

**Rotterdam, not Peat, is the diagnostic standard.** The 2003 Rotterdam consensus excludes thyroid dysfunction from the diagnostic picture and treats it as a separate condition to rule out. Contemporary endocrinology will not validate the upstream-thyroid framing this protocol assumes. The animal model — thyroidectomy plus gonadotropin producing polycystic ovaries in three species — is real and reproducible but not in any current human guideline.

**Modern PCOS treatment is more nuanced than this article implies.** Standard-of-care has moved past metformin-and-pill monotherapy. Inositol has RCT support for cycle regularity in some subpopulations. Letrozole has replaced clomiphene as first-line ovulation induction with better pregnancy rates. This protocol is not a substitute for a competent reproductive endocrinologist — it runs alongside one.

**GLP-1 agonists are working.** A woman whose fertility window is closing may correctly choose semaglutide, restore ovulation in months, and conceive. Long-term Peat-side concerns are real but unproven. The protocol's position is to run alongside, not against, and re-evaluate at month six.

**Inositol is absent here.** Meaningful PCOS evidence has accumulated, particularly with insulin resistance. Peat did not address it. Same downstream-insulin category as metformin. No reason to stop if a reader is using it; the protocol does not need it either.

**OCPs and spironolactone have defined niches.** A teenager with severe acne, dysmenorrhea, and emotional volatility may use a low-dose progestin-only contraceptive as a bridge for one to two years while running Phases 1 and 2 underneath. Spironolactone is a reasonable bridge for severe hirsutism until the protocol's slower anti-androgen mechanisms take effect. The article's emphasis is on transitioning off, not refusing on principle.

**The three-to-six-month ovulation timeline is optimistic for severe cases.** Women anovulatory for ten or fifteen years, arriving with morning temperatures of 96.5°F and BMI over 40, often need twelve to eighteen months before the first true ovulation. The window in this article is for a mid-severity case; the framework still applies on a slower clock.

## Data gaps

**No Peat-authored full-length article on PCOS treatment.** Dose ranges are reconstructed from the [[progesterone]] and [[menses]] emails, the [[2020-02-19-butter-living-podcast-butter-living-podcast-fertility-pregnancy-and-development|*Butter Living* podcast]], and inferential extension from [[anti-stress-diet]] and [[estrogen-detox]]. The framework is consistent across the corpus; the doses require individual titration.

**Pediatric and adolescent PCOS.** Increasingly common, not addressed by Peat in dosing form. The framework applies; adult doses do not transfer directly. Adolescents should run the diet and gut work alone for three to six months before any hormonal layer, with pediatric endocrinology supervision.

**Lean vs high-BMI phenotypes.** Two recognized phenotypes with potentially distinct mechanistic roots. Peat did not differentiate. Lean PCOS often responds faster to thyroid alone; high-BMI PCOS often needs more aggressive gut and PUFA work before the hormonal layer has anywhere to land.

**Inositol, NAC, berberine.** Modern supplements with reasonable PCOS-specific evidence Peat did not address. Same downstream category as metformin.

**Letrozole and clomiphene.** Pharmacological estrogen-axis interventions Peat did not address directly. Symptomatic but effective for some women; not contraindicated during a defined fertility window.

**The PCOS microbiome literature.** Recent work showing distinctive gut microbiome signatures fits the endotoxin framing. Peat did not engage this research that has emerged since 2015. Pre- and probiotic specifics are speculative.

**Endometrial cancer screening.** Long-standing anovulation is the highest-risk reproductive endocrine state for endometrial cancer because of decades of unopposed estrogen. Women with five or more years of anovulation should have transvaginal ultrasound for endometrial thickness at intake and at month twelve; the >11 mm threshold in a non-cycling woman is the standard trigger for biopsy referral.

**Surgical interventions.** Wedge resection is rarely offered today; laparoscopic ovarian drilling has largely replaced it. Both can restore ovulation short term but neither corrects the systemic environment. Third- or fourth-line option after eighteen months of dietary, gut, and hormonal layers.
