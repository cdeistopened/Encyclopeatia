# Estrogen Detox

## The Four-Lever Thesis

"Estrogen detox" is a misleading phrase if you treat it the way people treat a juice cleanse. Estrogen is not an isolated toxin sitting in a single organ waiting to be flushed out. It is a hormone produced continuously by your own tissues, recirculated by your gut, amplified by environmental chemicals that mimic it, and opposed (or not) by the hormone progesterone at the receptor level. Any protocol that targets only one of these mechanisms will fail, because the other three will keep the estrogen burden high.

Ray Peat's framework treats the problem as a system with four leverage points that must be worked simultaneously:

1. **Reduce estrogen production** at the source by inhibiting [[aromatase]], the enzyme that converts androgens to estrogen.
2. **Block estrogen recirculation** through the gut and liver so that conjugated [[estrogen]] actually gets excreted instead of reabsorbed.
3. **Reduce external estrogen load** by eliminating [[xenoestrogens]] from food, water, packaging, and personal care.
4. **Oppose estrogen at the cell level** with [[progesterone]], the only hormone that directly destroys the estrogen receptor.

Think of it like draining a bathtub with the tap still running. Lever 1 turns down the tap. Lever 2 unclogs the drain. Lever 3 stops strangers from pouring buckets in. Lever 4 shrinks the tub itself. Skip any one of these and the water level stays high, no matter how hard you pull on the others.

This protocol assumes you are also running a Peat-style metabolic baseline: adequate protein, ripe fruit and well-cooked starch, dairy, saturated fats, thyroid support where needed. Estrogen clearance is a job the liver does, and the liver cannot do it without sugar, protein, and thyroid hormone. Without that baseline, every intervention below becomes less effective. Start with [[anti-stress-diet]] and build from there.

## Who This Protocol Is For

High estrogen burden does not always show up on a lab panel. Tissue estrogen can be elevated while serum estradiol looks normal, especially in men and in postmenopausal women. Peat's approach is to read the symptoms first and confirm with labs second.

**Men.** Gynecomastia (breast tissue), low libido, soft or watery muscles, stubborn belly fat, fatigue, loss of morning erections, elevated prolactin, gynoid fat patterns (hips and thighs), emotional fragility, brain fog. Rising aromatase activity with age means most men over 40 are drifting into elevated estrogen regardless of total testosterone.

**Women.** PMS, heavy or clotting periods, fibroids, endometriosis, cyclical breast tenderness, fibrocystic breasts, water retention and cyclical weight swings, mood swings in the luteal phase, mid-cycle spotting, migraines tied to the cycle, and heavy bleeding around perimenopause. Women typically have estrogen dominance not because estrogen is high in absolute terms but because progesterone is low.

**Both sexes.** Fatigue, hair loss (note that both estrogen excess and androgen excess can cause hair thinning, and they respond to different protocols), cold hands and feet, cold intolerance, anxiety, insomnia, migraines, varicose veins, spider veins, rosacea, thinning skin, and slow wound healing.

**Age 40 and up.** Aromatase activity rises progressively with age regardless of sex. Most people over 40 benefit from at least some portion of this protocol, and most people over 50 benefit from all four levers.

For the full symptom map and mechanism, see [[estrogen]] and [[aromatase]].

## Lever 1: Reduce Aromatase Activity

Aromatase is the enzyme that converts testosterone and androstenedione into estrogens. It is expressed in fat tissue, breast tissue, the brain, bone, and the gonads. More body fat means more aromatase. Higher prostaglandin E means more aromatase. Inflammation, stress, and PUFA all push it up. Peat identified a short list of practical inhibitors:

**Aspirin.** Peat called aspirin "a very effective aromatase inhibitor." It works on two fronts: it directly suppresses aromatase expression, and it blocks the prostaglandin E synthesis pathway that drives aromatase upward. Daily low-dose aspirin (typically 325 mg with food, with a pinch of baking soda if stomach sensitivity is an issue) is the cheapest and most reliable intervention in this protocol. See [[aspirin]] for dosing and cautions.

**Progesterone.** Progesterone does something no other hormone does: it destroys the estrogen receptor while also inhibiting aromatase. For women, topical or oral progesterone is the single highest-leverage intervention. For men, small doses are occasionally useful for acute estrogen dominance. See [[progesterone]].

**Thyroid hormone.** Hypothyroidism raises aromatase activity directly. A sluggish thyroid means a slower liver, colder tissues, lower progesterone, and a systematic drift toward estrogen dominance. Restoring thyroid function (temperature, pulse, T3) is often enough to drop estrogen on its own. See [[hypothyroid-recovery]].

**Coffee and caffeine.** Peat's short list of aromatase inhibitors was "coffee, progesterone, flavonoids, and aspirin." Coffee supports liver function, raises metabolic rate, lowers prolactin, and directly suppresses aromatase. Drink it with cream and sugar, not black. See [[coffee]].

**Naringenin.** Found in oranges and particularly concentrated in orange peel. Marmalade made from whole oranges is one of Peat's favorite delivery mechanisms. Daily orange juice with some pulp or a small amount of peel extract contributes. See [[oranges]].

**Apigenin.** A flavonoid found in guavas, celery, and parsley. Not as potent as the above but a useful daily food-level contribution.

**White button mushrooms.** Contain compounds that inhibit aromatase, particularly relevant for breast tissue. Must be cooked for at least an hour to destroy the agaritine and hydrazine content. Raw or lightly cooked mushrooms are not safe for regular consumption.

**Pregnenolone.** Does not inhibit aromatase directly, but lowers cortisol, and high cortisol drives aromatase. Useful for people under chronic stress.

Full mechanism detail at [[aromatase]].

## Lever 2: Block Estrogen Recirculation Through the Gut and Liver

The liver conjugates estrogen (attaches a glucuronic acid molecule to it), packages it into bile, and dumps it into the small intestine for elimination. This is the designed pathway. The problem is the gut. Bacterial beta-glucuronidase, produced in excess under conditions of dysbiosis and [[endotoxin]] exposure, cleaves that glucuronic acid back off. The estrogen is then reabsorbed through the intestinal wall and sent right back into circulation. You end up metabolizing the same estrogen molecules over and over.

This is why people who "eat clean" still have estrogen problems. Their production may be low, but their recirculation is high.

**Raw carrot salad, daily.** This is Peat's signature intervention. A medium raw carrot, scraped or julienned, tossed with a small amount of vinegar, coconut oil, and salt, eaten once a day. The non-fermentable fiber in raw carrot binds to conjugated estrogen in the gut and drags it out in the stool. It also has mild antimicrobial effects on the small intestine. Peat wrote that "a daily raw carrot often helps to balance progesterone, cortisol, and estrogen." This single habit has more leverage than most people expect. Cook the carrot and it loses the fiber structure that does the work. It must be raw.

**Support liver function.** The liver needs adequate protein (100 grams or more daily for most adults), B vitamins (especially B1 and B2), and thyroid hormone to conjugate estrogen efficiently. A hypothyroid liver cannot keep up with estrogen clearance, and the backlog becomes tissue estrogen. See [[liver]] for dietary sources of the relevant nutrients, and consider weekly liver consumption for vitamin A and B-complex.

**Reduce endotoxin load.** Less endotoxin means less beta-glucuronidase activity in the gut, which means less deconjugation and less recirculation. The anti-endotoxin diet overlaps heavily with the anti-estrogen diet: reduce fermentable fiber, avoid resistant starches that feed lower-bowel fermentation, eat well-cooked food, keep the small intestine moving. See [[endotoxin]].

**Regular bowel movements.** Constipation increases the time that conjugated estrogen sits in the gut, which increases the amount that gets deconjugated and reabsorbed. Target at least one complete bowel movement daily. Cascara sagrada (a gentle laxative Peat recommended) or supplemental magnesium can help if transit is slow.

## Lever 3: Eliminate External Sources of Xenoestrogens

Even if you turn off production and unclog the drain, you can still be loading estrogen in from outside. Xenoestrogens are compounds that are not structurally identical to estradiol but bind the estrogen receptor and produce estrogenic effects. The modern environment is saturated with them. The highest-yield eliminations:

**Plastics.** BPA, BPS, phthalates, and the general class of microplastic particles. The worst exposures come from drinking from plastic water bottles (especially ones that have been warmed in a car or frozen and thawed), eating food from plastic containers, and canned foods with plastic-lined interiors. Switch to glass and stainless steel for food and water storage. Do not microwave in plastic.

**Soy.** Every form except small amounts of traditionally fermented soy (miso, natto). Eliminate soy milk, tofu, tempeh, soy protein isolate, edamame, soy lecithin (which sneaks into chocolate, dressings, and baked goods), textured vegetable protein, and soy-based meat substitutes. Soy isoflavones are potent estrogen-receptor agonists.

**Beer and wine.** Beer is particularly bad. Brewer's yeast produces actual estradiol, and hops are one of the most potent phytoestrogens known. Wine has a lower but still meaningful load of plant estrogens and polyphenolic compounds that can act estrogenically. If you drink alcohol during the protocol, a small glass of spirits is the least-bad option.

**Pesticides.** Organophosphates and glyphosate have endocrine-disrupting effects. Buy organic where the budget allows, prioritizing the highest-exposure produce (the "dirty dozen" categories).

**Tap water.** Municipal water in many cities contains measurable levels of pharmaceutical estrogens (from oral contraceptive excretion) and other hormone-disrupting residues. A carbon block filter handles most of it. Reverse osmosis handles nearly all of it.

**Polyphenolic antioxidant supplements.** This catches people by surprise. Green tea extract, resveratrol, quercetin supplements, and similar concentrated polyphenol products are frequently estrogenic at supplement doses. The compounds are less concerning in whole food form. Stop the supplements.

**Flaxseed.** The single highest source of lignans (phytoestrogens) in the Western diet. Often marketed as a "hormone balancer" because it is said to block estrogen, but in practice the lignans act as weak estrogens themselves and add to the burden. Eliminate.

**Industrial seed oils.** Soybean, corn, canola, sunflower, safflower, grapeseed, and cottonseed oils. These contain linoleic acid (PUFA), which through prostaglandin E signaling drives aromatase upward. They also contain residual estrogenic impurities from the refining process. Cook with butter, coconut oil, beef tallow, or ghee instead.

For the full list and mechanism, see [[xenoestrogens]].

## Lever 4: Oppose Estrogen at the Cell Level with Progesterone

Progesterone is the only hormone in the body that directly destroys the estrogen receptor. When progesterone binds its receptor, the cell downregulates the nearby estrogen receptors and becomes physically less responsive to estrogen signaling. No other intervention does this. Estrogen-blocking drugs (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors) attack one step in the pathway. Progesterone attacks the whole cellular response.

**Topical progesterone.** Peat's preferred delivery form is a vitamin E based oil (Progest-E is the original formulation). For women, typical dosing is 30 to 100 mg daily, applied to thin-skinned areas (inner wrists, inner thighs, neck, face). Premenopausal women usually cycle with their natural cycle, using progesterone on days 14 through 28. Postmenopausal women can use it continuously or cycle three weeks on, one week off. The vitamin E base matters: progesterone dissolved in water or alcohol is poorly absorbed and largely wasted.

**Oral progesterone.** Less bioavailable than topical but still useful, particularly for sleep, since the first-pass liver metabolism produces sedating metabolites. Useful for women with insomnia.

**Dietary support for endogenous production.** Progesterone is made from cholesterol, which is made from saturated fat. Low-fat diets starve the progesterone pathway. Vitamin A (from liver, butter, egg yolks), adequate thyroid hormone, magnesium, and sunlight all support endogenous progesterone synthesis. A woman with healthy thyroid function and a cholesterol-adequate diet makes significantly more of her own progesterone than one on a low-fat regimen.

**Men.** Men need smaller amounts, primarily for the aromatase-inhibiting and cortisol-lowering effects. Peat himself used small amounts of progesterone occasionally for acute stress or to oppose estrogen. A drop or two of Progest-E dissolved in coffee is a typical male dose.

See [[progesterone]] for the full pharmacology.

## The 6-Week Baseline Protocol

The protocol is staged, not all at once. Eliminations come first because they cost nothing and start working immediately. Additions come second, after the baseline is stable. Targeted interventions come last, once the system has had time to respond.

**Week 1 to 2.** Eliminate xenoestrogens. Clear the house of plastic water bottles, soy products, flaxseed, industrial seed oils, beer, and wine. Replace with glass or stainless steel, butter and coconut oil, and coffee or orange juice. Begin the daily raw carrot salad. Begin daily aspirin, 325 mg with food.

**Week 2 to 4.** Optimize the [[anti-stress-diet]] baseline. Morning orange juice (fresh, or bottled pulp-free if fresh is not possible) for the naringenin and the easy sugar. Shift all cooking fats to grass-fed butter, coconut oil, or beef tallow. Add coffee with cream and sugar two to three times daily. Raise protein intake to 100 grams or more for most adults. Target three meals plus at least one snack to keep blood sugar stable, because dropping blood sugar raises cortisol which drives aromatase.

**Week 4 to 6.** Add targeted aromatase inhibitors. Cooked white button mushrooms (cooked at least one hour) two to three times weekly. Liver once a week, 4 to 6 ounces, for vitamin A and B vitamins. Cheese and dairy daily for calcium (calcium suppresses parathyroid hormone, which otherwise drives aromatase upward). If thyroid is suspected low, begin addressing it per [[hypothyroid-recovery]].

**Week 6 and beyond.** Re-evaluate. If symptoms are resolving, maintain. If symptoms persist, consider progesterone supplementation (especially for women), check thyroid markers (TSH, free T3, temperature and pulse), and look harder for hidden xenoestrogen sources. Track morning temperature and resting pulse weekly to monitor metabolic progress.

## The PUFA Connection

This is the caveat that determines whether the protocol succeeds or stalls. High tissue PUFA continuously drives aromatase activity via prostaglandin E synthesis. The PUFA stored in your fat cells from years of seed oil consumption does not go away quickly. The tissue half-life of linoleic acid is estimated at about two years, with near-complete turnover taking roughly four years. This means you cannot fully finish detoxing from estrogen while you are still PUFA-loaded.

The good news is that you do not have to wait four years to see results. Aspirin blocks the prostaglandin E synthesis pathway downstream of PUFA, which is exactly why Peat emphasized it so heavily. Daily aspirin compensates for a large fraction of the PUFA-driven aromatase drive during the transition period. Vitamin E (natural mixed tocopherols, not the synthetic form) protects stored PUFA from oxidation while it waits to be replaced. Niacinamide can slow the release of free fatty acids from fat stores, which reduces the acute PUFA burden on the liver during weight loss.

See [[linoleic-acid]] and [[aspirin]].

## Signs the Protocol Is Working

**Men.** Morning erections return (often the first sign, within two to four weeks). Libido rises. Waist measurement shrinks even before total weight changes. Chest tissue firms up as water and soft fat leave. Mood becomes steadier, anxiety drops, motivation returns. Sleep deepens.

**Women.** PMS shortens and softens. Periods become lighter, shorter, and more regular. Breast tenderness resolves. Water retention drops (often a quick 3 to 5 pound loss in the first two weeks that is pure fluid). Mood stabilizes through the luteal phase. Libido rises. For perimenopausal women, hot flashes often decrease.

**Both sexes.** Warmer hands and feet (measure finger temperature if you want a marker). Better sleep. More stable daytime energy without crashes. Less cyclical weight fluctuation. Clearer skin.

**Lab markers, if you test.** Lower estradiol, higher progesterone, higher free testosterone (men), lower SHBG, lower prolactin, lower TSH, higher free T3, lower reverse T3. A single lab snapshot matters less than the symptom picture, but tracking trends over months is useful.

## When Symptoms Persist

If six weeks of the full protocol has produced little improvement, look for one of these problems:

**Hidden xenoestrogen source.** The most common culprits are workplace exposures (plastics industry, printing, dental work), personal care products (lavender and tea tree oil are both estrogenic, as are many "natural" sunscreens and lotions), and supplements you forgot to consider (multivitamins with soy-derived vitamin E, for instance).

**Unaddressed hypothyroidism.** A hypothyroid liver cannot clear estrogen at the rate the protocol assumes. If temperature and pulse are low, treat thyroid before expecting the estrogen protocol to finish the job. See [[hypothyroid-recovery]].

**Underlying tumor.** Rare but serious. Prolactinomas raise prolactin which dysregulates the whole hormonal axis. If prolactin is elevated on labs and does not respond to protocol adjustments, get imaging.

**PUFA saturation too high.** Some people need more than six weeks, especially those coming off decades of a seed oil diet. Add niacinamide (100 to 250 mg with meals), increase vitamin E (400 IU natural mixed tocopherols), and extend the timeline to six months or a year.

**Progesterone dose too low.** Women using topical progesterone sometimes need higher doses than the starting range suggests, especially in perimenopause. Titrate upward cautiously.

## Common Failure Modes

Most protocols fail in one of these predictable ways:

- Avoiding soy but eating flax. Flax is worse. It is a swap in the wrong direction.
- Avoiding plastic water bottles but drinking unfiltered tap water. The tap water contains actual pharmaceutical estrogens.
- Avoiding seed oils but buying olive oil stored in clear glass bottles. Light degrades the oil and the PUFA fraction oxidizes. Buy olive oil in dark glass or tins.
- Taking green tea extract or resveratrol as "antioxidants." Both are estrogenic at supplement doses.
- Using calcium d-glucarate as the main gut intervention without addressing the actual gut flora. It helps at the margin but does not replace the raw carrot or the endotoxin work.
- Forgetting the raw carrot. It sounds too simple to matter, which is why people skip it. It matters.
- Going low-fat in parallel with this protocol. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are all required for estrogen clearance, and cholesterol is required for progesterone synthesis. Low-fat dieting kneecaps the whole effort.
- Over-exercising. Chronic cortisol elevation from excessive cardio drives aromatase upward and suppresses progesterone. Walk, lift moderately, do not chase endurance during the protocol.
