# Weight Loss Protocol

This is the canonical clinical-reference page for weight loss in the Peat frame. It is the actionable companion to [[obesity|*Obesity*]] (the conceptual hub) and the structured complement to [[peatmaxxing-cut|*The Peatmaxxing Cut*]] (the voice-y, narrative version of the same protocol). Where the obesity hub explains why fat accumulation is a symptom of metabolic-respiratory failure and the peatmaxxing-cut page tells the story in Peat's own register, this page lays out the five-pillar architecture as a reference clinicians and disciplined readers can apply.

*This page documents Peat's bioenergetic framework. It is not medical advice. It assumes coordination with a clinician for any pre-existing condition — particularly diabetes, cardiovascular disease, eating-disorder history, thyroid medication management, pregnancy, postpartum lactation, or current use of GLP-1 agonists, antipsychotics, corticosteroids, beta-blockers, or other weight-affecting drugs. Nothing here should be read as instruction to discontinue any prescribed medication; the question is always how to work alongside conventional care, not whether to replace it. Rapid unexplained weight change, severe symptoms, or any history of disordered eating warrants medical evaluation before any of the levers below are pulled.*

## The Peat self-experiment

The personal anchor for the entire protocol is Peat's own published experiment with himself, told most directly in the [[coconut-oil|*Coconut Oil*]] article and recapitulated in the [[weight-loss|email exchange on weight loss]]. He had been steady at 185 pounds for twenty-five years. One evening he added a tablespoon of coconut oil to a serving of rice and noticed something he had not expected: thirty minutes later his pulse had climbed to roughly 98, his breathing had deepened, his skin had gone pink, and the sensation was indistinguishable from having just exercised. The response replayed every day for two weeks before tapering — a sign, he reasoned, that the antithyroid load on his system was being relieved each time the saturated medium-chain triglycerides occupied his oxidative machinery. He kept his diet otherwise unchanged (including a daily quart of ice cream) and added roughly 200 to 250 calories per day as coconut oil. Over six months his weight dropped from 185 to 175 pounds. More coconut oil dropped it further. Less raised it again.

The experiment is diagnostic in three ways. It violates the calorie-balance prediction by adding calories and losing weight. It exposes the metabolic-rate mechanism in real time through the transient hyperthyroid surges and visible peripheral perfusion. And it inverts the conventional dose-response: more of the right substrate produced more fat loss, not less. In the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]] he gave the slower, more conservative variant: "Yes, it's best to lose it slowly. When I tried adding about a tablespoon of coconut oil once a day I lost about two pounds a week, for several weeks, without eating less."

The population-scale anchors come from the same article. G. W. Crile and his wife, surveying basal metabolic rates across populations in the 1930s, found that "the metabolic rate of people in Yucatan, where coconut is a staple food, averaged 25% higher than that of people in the United States." The adaptive prediction would have been the opposite, since populations in hot climates typically run cooler to reduce heat load. The Yucatan ran hotter despite the climate because the dietary signature was saturated tropical fat, fruit sugars, and lime-processed maize — exactly the substrate the framework predicts will keep the thyroid axis active. Albert Schweitzer, operating his clinic in tropical Africa across the early twentieth century, reported that he saw almost no obesity, diabetes, cancer, or cardiovascular disease until the European diet arrived. Both observations are population-level confirmations of the personal experiment: a body running on saturated fat, active thyroid, and adequate sugar runs warmer and stays lean almost regardless of caloric arithmetic. See [[obesity]] for the full historical and mechanistic treatment.

## The five-pillar framework

Every actionable lever in this protocol falls into one of five pillars. The pillars are ordered roughly by upstream priority — thyroid restoration is the master variable, PUFA depletion is the slowest and most decisive, substrate adequacy is the daily floor, walking is the only exercise, and stress-and-sleep restoration runs in parallel with all four.

1. **Thyroid optimization** — restore the metabolic rate that determines how every other variable responds. Linked protocol: [[hypothyroid-recovery]].
2. **PUFA depletion** — clear the stored linoleic and arachidonic acid that suppresses thyroid signaling at the cellular level. Linked protocol: [[pufa-depletion]].
3. **Substrate adequacy** — eat enough of the right foods to keep cortisol quiet and glycogen full. Linked protocol: [[anti-stress-diet]].
4. **Walking only** — the single exercise modality consistent with the framework. Endurance running, HIIT, and chronic cardio are explicitly opposed.
5. **Stress reduction and sleep** — cortisol drives fat storage and dysregulates appetite hormones. Linked protocol: [[sleep-restoration]].

The pillars are not parallel. The first three are causally upstream of the fourth and fifth — without thyroid restoration, PUFA depletion, and substrate adequacy, no amount of walking or sleep hygiene will produce sustained fat loss. Without walking and sleep, the first three will be running into a stress-physiology headwind they cannot fully overcome. The pillars compound when they run together and stall when any one is neglected.

## Pillar 1: Thyroid optimization

Thyroid is the master variable. If thyroid output is low, every other intervention is fighting a metabolic ceiling that does not move. This is the lesson of the practice Peat described in the [[2013-02-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-weight-gain|2013 weight-gain episode]] — women on 700 calories per day who were not lying about their intake and not losing weight because the thyroid had collapsed first and the muscle had been catabolized to defend the fat stores. The intervention layer for them was never "eat less." It was to feed the thyroid back into function.

The diagnostic markers for hypothyroidism in this frame are documented in [[hypothyroid-recovery]] in detail; the short list is morning oral temperature below 97.8°F (target 98.0–98.6°F after breakfast), resting pulse below 75 (target 75–85 bpm in a well-fed warm adult), cold fingers and toes through the day, slow Achilles reflex, dry skin, constipation, brain fog, and the characteristic flat-or-descending temperature curve that does not rise after breakfast. Standard lab markers (TSH, free T3, free T4, reverse T3) are useful as supporting evidence, but the temperature-and-pulse signature is the primary instrument in this framework.

Restoration is, in order: dietary first (the substrate-adequacy pillar will raise the rate in most readers within three weeks), then supplemental thyroid if dietary alone does not move the markers. Peat's standard starting dose from the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]] was "5 mcg of T3 twice a day, while watching for changes in your pulse rate, temperature, and ability to sleep. Half a grain of Armour, or about 30 mcg of T4 and 7.5 mcg of T3, is traditionally a common starting dose; it should be taken with a meal, so that it absorbs slowly." The treatment goal is never a particular TSH number — it is the recovered temperature-and-pulse signature and the qualitative recovery markers (warm extremities, regular cycles in women, morning erections in men, vivid dreams returning, calmer baseline mood).

The thyroid bridge ends when endogenous signaling is no longer being suppressed by circulating PUFA. From the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]]: "Taking a thyroid supplement is reasonable until the ratio of saturated fats to PUFA is about 2 to 1." That is the four-year horizon of the PUFA-depletion pillar showing up here as the endpoint of the thyroid bridge. Most readers will be on supplemental thyroid for two to four years; some indefinitely. The dose typically falls over time as the substrate state of the body shifts toward saturation and endogenous thyroid function returns. See [[thyroid-dosing]] for protocols on titration, splitting, and combination with food.

The single most common mistake at this pillar is starting thyroid before the diet is in place. The diet alone will raise the metabolic rate in most readers, and stacking exogenous thyroid on top of rising endogenous function will overshoot — tachycardia, insomnia, anxiety, and the predictable abandonment of the protocol. Diet first for three weeks; thyroid second only if the markers have not moved.

## Pillar 2: PUFA depletion (the four-year tissue-turnover protocol)

Stored polyunsaturated fat is the slowest lever in the human body and the most decisive variable in this protocol's long-term success. Linoleic acid in adipose tissue has a half-life of roughly two to three years; a heavily seed-oil-loaded adult takes about four years on a saturated-fat-dominant diet to substantially clear the stored load. Peat named this horizon explicitly in the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]]: "When the polyunsaturated fats in the diet are reduced, the amount of them stored in the tissues decreases for about four years, making it progressively easier to keep the metabolic rate up, and stress hormones down."

The mechanism is documented in [[pufa]] and [[pufa-depletion]]. The short form is that linoleic acid suppresses T4-to-T3 conversion, suppresses tissue response to thyroid hormone, substitutes into mitochondrial cardiolipin and weakens cytochrome c oxidase, generates inflammatory lipid-peroxidation products, and — when mobilized from fat stores under stress or hunger — recirculates as free fatty acids that block glucose oxidation through the [[randle-cycle|Randle cycle]]. Rapid weight loss in a PUFA-loaded body is not a clean win; it is the mobilization of an inflammatory lipid pool into the bloodstream.

The intervention layer is the substitution of saturated fats for polyunsaturated fats in the diet, sustained over years. Specifically:

- **Replace seed oils completely.** No corn, soy, safflower, sunflower, canola, peanut, grapeseed, cottonseed, or vegetable oil — in cooking, in dressings, in restaurant food where avoidable, in baked goods, in protein powders, in nut butters, in margarine and "plant butter" products.
- **Replace fish oil and oily fish.** Fish oil is among the most concentrated stored-PUFA forms a person can eat; fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines, tuna) carry a substantial PUFA load. Shellfish (oysters, shrimp, scallops, crab) are the seafood-category exception — low in PUFA, rich in trace minerals.
- **Use butter, coconut oil, and beef tallow as the working fats.** Cold-pressed olive oil in small quantities is tolerated but is not a featured fat (its oleic acid content is metabolically neutral, but it oxidizes under heat and substitutes poorly for the saturates).
- **Cut PUFA-heavy meats.** Pork and conventional chicken carry the dietary PUFA of their feed; beef, lamb, and bison from ruminants are the preferred muscle meats. Eggs cap at two per day because of their PUFA content (Sarah Johanneson Murray's standing rule from the [[2016-12-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-food|2016 food episode]]).
- **Cut nuts and seeds as a category.** All are PUFA-dense; coconut is the saturated-fat exception.
- **Add daily coconut oil.** One tablespoon to three tablespoons across the day, divided across meals. The dose tiers are: a teaspoon three times a day for hypometabolic readers, one tablespoon a day for readers with intact metabolisms (Peat's own n=1 dose), and three tablespoons a day divided across meals for the upper-tier maintenance dose described in [[peatmaxxing-cut]]. The mechanism is the medium-chain saturated fats occupying the oxidative machinery and momentarily displacing the antithyroid PUFA load. See [[coconut-oil]] for the full substance-level case.

The four-year horizon is the reason the protocol can feel modest in the first ninety days. The first three months mostly restore water balance, repair muscle, and raise the metabolic rate. The fat loss that people associate with Peat's approach — slow steady leaning out with no crash and no rebound — is the four-year turnover playing out as the dietary ratio shifts the stored fat composition of the body from PUFA-dominant toward saturation. The coconut oil accelerates this. Vitamin E (mixed tocopherols, see [[vitamin-e]]) protects against the lipid-peroxidation products generated during fat mobilization. Aspirin and niacinamide buffer the free-fatty-acid release during the transit. But the horizon itself is fixed by physiology. Understand that going in and the protocol becomes a direction rather than a deadline.

## Pillar 3: Substrate adequacy

The single most counterintuitive pillar for a reader trained on mainstream weight-loss advice is that you must eat enough. Undereating drives cortisol, suppresses thyroid further, cannibalizes lean tissue, and produces the binge-restrict cycle that crashes most weight-loss attempts. Peat was unsparing on this point in nearly every email and transcript that touched weight: the weight-loss diet is not a low-calorie diet. It is a metabolically supportive diet with the fat calories pulled back enough to open a small and sustainable deficit while the rate does the actual work.

The working targets, drawn from the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]], the [[2016-12-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-food|2016 food episode]], and the broader [[anti-stress-diet]]:

- **Protein: 80–120 grams per day.** The floor for an average adult is around 80 grams from the 2016 food episode; the working range for active or larger adults is 100–120 grams. Below 80 grams, protein synthesis falters and lean tissue is lost. Above ~150 grams, particularly if eaten without carbohydrate, the cortisol surge Peat described becomes a problem. Sources: two quarts of low-fat milk daily (~64 grams), one to two eggs, cheese, gelatinous broths (ox-tail, lamb shank, chicken-back), occasional shellfish, occasional beef or lamb. Pair every protein meal with carbohydrate; never eat protein alone.
- **Carbohydrate: 180–250 grams per day minimum, frequently higher.** Sources: orange juice (a quart a day is the working target, more if tolerated), ripe fruit, milk sugars, a daily raw carrot, well-cooked potato or masa, honey or table sugar in moderation. The carbohydrate floor is what keeps cortisol quiet and glycogen full. Cutting carbohydrate "for weight loss" is the fastest way to crash this protocol.
- **Salt: 6–12 grams of salt per day (roughly 2.4–4.8 grams of elemental sodium), salted to taste with appetite as the guide.** Peat's floor in the [[55-bioenergetic-nutrition-basics|bioenergetic-nutrition-basics episode]] was 5 grams of elemental sodium endorsed with "oh, for sure"; the Mongolia population eats 30 grams of salt per day without hypertension. Salt is anxiolytic, anti-inflammatory, raises CO₂ production, lowers cortisol, and quiets adrenaline. Under-salting is the hidden failure mode of most weight-loss attempts. See [[salt]] and the [[salt-energy-metabolic-rate-and-longevity|salt newsletter]].
- **Calcium: ~1,200–2,000 mg per day.** Two quarts of milk delivers ~2,400 mg; cheese and a calcium-citrate supplement (if dairy is not tolerated) cover gaps. Calcium is anti-stress, anti-inflammatory, supports thyroid responsiveness, and is one of Peat's named weight-loss variables in its own right.
- **Total energy: enough to sustain the metabolic rate, not a fixed calorie target.** A typical Peat-frame weight-loss day for an adult woman lands at 1,800–2,200 calories; a typical day for an adult man lands at 2,200–2,800 calories. These are working ranges, not prescriptions. Reduce only by the amount of fat calories — never by cutting protein or carbohydrate. The temperature-and-pulse markers tell you whether intake is adequate. A morning temperature that drops below 97.5°F or a resting pulse that falls below 70 means intake is too low.

The literal weight-loss plate from the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]]: "When a person wants to lose excess fat, limiting the diet to low fat milk, eggs, orange juice, and a daily carrot or two, will provide the essential nutrients without excess calories." Two quarts of 1% or skim milk, one to two eggs cooked in butter, a quart of orange juice, one or two raw carrots, gelatinous soup, occasional shellfish or beef, salt to taste, coconut oil with meals. That is the entire core. Everything else in [[anti-stress-diet]] is consistent with this pillar; the variant for weight loss is the lower fat content of the milk and the addition of coconut oil.

The single most important rule at this pillar is *never eat protein without carbohydrate.* Peat repeated this in the [[2010-09-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-sugar-i|2010 sugar episode]], the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]], and the protein email: "Preceding the protein with some carbohydrate makes the protein go farther, otherwise under the influence of cortisol a lot of protein is used just for energy." Orange juice before eggs. Milk with cheese. Fruit before steak. The glycogen pre-load gives the liver something to release when insulin drops after the protein meal and forestalls the cortisol surge that would otherwise burn the protein for fuel and drive the metabolic rate down.

## Pillar 4: Walking only

Peat opposed endurance exercise as a weight-loss tool with the same vigor as he opposed caloric restriction, and for the same reason: it raises cortisol, catabolizes lean tissue, and runs the metabolic rate down at the same time it appears to be producing leanness. From the [[2013-02-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-weight-gain|2013 weight-gain episode]]: "There was a study using radioactive testosterone. They compared runners to weightlifters who had developed huge skeletal muscles. Using isotopes, they imaged the heart, lungs, and brain. The runners' tissues had atrophied; the lean long-distance runners had lean, flabby, very thin hearts." The endurance runner looks lean because tissue is catabolized, not because the metabolism is high.

The framework's exercise prescription is short:

- **Walking is the working modality.** Thirty to ninety minutes a day, broken across the day or in one or two blocks. Walk after meals when possible — the post-meal walk improves glucose handling and supports lymphatic flow without driving cortisol. Walk warm and fed; if it is cold out, dress warmer than feels necessary. See [[walking|walking practice page (reserved)]] in the practices/ category.
- **Brief concentric resistance training is permitted and useful.** Peat's specific prescription from the exercise email: "Ten pound dumbbells, lifted quickly for 30 to 60 seconds, for example, is usually good for increasing the anabolic and protective hormones." A single set of ten to thirty bodyweight squats once or twice a day, light dumbbell curls, kettlebell swings, a few brief hill sprints of ten to fifteen seconds. The goal is to signal the muscle to grow and to briefly raise the metabolic rate — not to burn calories through volume.
- **Endurance running, long cycling, "chronic cardio," CrossFit-style high-volume metcons, and most HIIT protocols are explicitly opposed.** These produce sustained lactic-acid elevation, drive cortisol chronically, suppress thyroid (T3 falls and stress signals rise after roughly 20–30 seconds of intense exertion), and cannibalize lean tissue. Eccentric loading under fatigue (running downhill, descending stairs as exercise) is the worst of the worst.
- **Never train fasted.** Eat something with sugar and salt fifteen to thirty minutes before any exertion. The fasted workout uses cortisol for fuel, catabolizes muscle, and accelerates lipolysis of stored PUFA in a way the liver cannot keep up with.
- **Sauna is permitted and arguably useful.** The warmth itself supports the metabolic rate; replace fluid and salt afterward.

For the reader coming from a chronic-cardio addiction, the transition is psychological as much as physiological. The first month of cutting endurance training will often produce scale-weight gain as the catabolized tissue rebuilds and as stress hormones drop and water rebalances. This is the predicted body-recomposition pattern — muscle returning, water rebalancing, fat loss following. Read the waist measurement, not the scale, through this transition.

## Pillar 5: Stress reduction and sleep

Cortisol drives visceral fat storage. Sleep deprivation dysregulates ghrelin and leptin and produces appetite distortions that no amount of dietary discipline will fully overcome. The two run together: a stressed body sleeps poorly; a poorly-slept body produces more cortisol the next day. The intervention layer is broader than any single skill or supplement and overlaps with the other four pillars at multiple points.

The actionable layer:

- **Sleep target: seven to nine hours per night, in a cool dark room, with a bedtime snack of milk and salty crackers, a small bowl of fruit, or a similar carbohydrate-and-salt combination.** The bedtime snack is not optional in this protocol. It is the mechanism that keeps liver glycogen replenished through the night and prevents the 3 AM cortisol spike that fragments sleep, drives the next morning's adrenaline, and crashes the next day's temperature. See [[sleep-restoration]] for the full sleep protocol.
- **Morning light, evening dim.** Get bright natural light within the first hour of waking; dim lights and avoid screens in the last hour before bed. This is not Peat-specific advice but is consistent with the framework's broader emphasis on circadian alignment and thyroid responsiveness.
- **Stress management as a daily practice, not a crisis response.** Peat's broader writing emphasized warmth, social connection, light, music, prayer, and the active management of the environments that drive sustained cortisol — chronic-overwork patterns, hostile workplaces, sleep debt, financial stress, isolation. The framework does not prescribe a particular modality; it does insist that chronic stress is metabolically obesogenic and that the weight-loss protocol cannot fully succeed in a body running on sustained allostatic load.
- **Salt and sugar as adrenaline-lowering tools.** The substrate-adequacy pillar overlaps here. A well-salted, well-fed body produces less adrenaline; an under-salted, under-fed body climbs into the stress physiology even at rest. Sleep often improves dramatically within a week of restoring adequate salt and adding a bedtime snack.
- **Avoid stimulants as stress masks.** Caffeine in moderate quantities with milk and sugar is permitted and arguably useful (Peat drank coffee regularly), but caffeine used to mask chronic under-fueling drives the same stress physiology the rest of the protocol is meant to quiet. If coffee is being used to skip breakfast, the coffee is the problem.

## Women-specific layer

The female reader has variables not present for the male reader, and the protocol has cycle-aware and life-stage-aware refinements.

**Estrogen reduction is central.** Estrogen elevates free fatty acids via growth hormone, suppresses oxidative metabolism, increases water retention, and drives the gynoid (hip-and-thigh) fat distribution characteristic of premenopausal women. The estrogen-progesterone balance is the dominant female-obesity variable in the Peat frame. Interventions include:

- **Progest-E (Peat's vitamin-E-vehicle natural progesterone) in the luteal phase.** Typical dose ranges are documented in [[progesterone]]; the cycle-aware pattern is to use it from ovulation through the start of menstruation, at a dose calibrated to the severity of premenstrual symptoms. Postmenopausal use is continuous at a lower dose.
- **Daily raw [[carrot-salad|carrot salad]].** The carrot fiber binds weakly-conjugated estrogens moving through enterohepatic circulation and reduces the gut-liver estrogen recirculation that keeps levels elevated. Peat said in the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]]: "Yes, I know people who have lost weight just by eating a raw carrot every day, reducing endotoxin stress." Daily, eaten raw, with a little vinegar, olive oil, and salt.
- **See [[estrogen-detox]] for the full estrogen-reduction protocol.**

**Cycle-aware diet.** The late luteal and premenstrual phases impose a roughly four-pound water-weight oscillation that mainstream weight-loss tracking misreads as fat regain. Track temperature, not scale, across the cycle; weigh at the same point in the cycle from month to month if weighing at all. Progesterone and salt support are most needed in the late luteal phase.

**Pregnancy and postpartum require their own treatment.** Pregnancy is, by design, a state of insulin resistance, elevated free fatty acids, and substrate redirection toward the fetus; the postpartum weight-retention pattern is typically a thyroid and prolactin issue rather than a calorie-balance failure. Lactation is a fat-mobilization opportunity if maternal substrate is adequate and PUFA load is low. See [[pregnancy-nutrition]] and [[child-feeding]].

**PCOS overlaps.** The PCOS phenotype (insulin resistance, androgen excess, weight gain, irregular cycles) is in the Peat frame a hypothyroid-and-estrogen-excess pattern rather than a primary androgen-overproduction disorder. The full protocol is in [[pcos-protocol]] and overlaps substantially with this page.

## Men-specific layer

The male reader has a different set of risks and a different default pattern. The central concerns are testosterone preservation and avoidance of the visceral-fat / portal-FFA / metabolic-syndrome cascade.

**Adequate calorie, protein, and fat intake protects testosterone.** Chronically restricting any of these — particularly the combination of low calorie, low fat, and high training volume — crashes testosterone within weeks. The young male reader experimenting with low-carb and high training volume to "get lean" is running the most reliably testosterone-suppressing protocol in modern fitness culture.

**Avoid the heavy-training-plus-low-carb combination specifically.** Each one alone is sub-optimal in this frame; together they produce the chronic-cortisol-elevated, low-thyroid, low-testosterone phenotype the framework is built to reverse.

**Visceral (apple-shape) obesity is the central male presentation.** It is the portal-FFA-pipeline phenotype that drives the [[diabetes|metabolic-syndrome]] cluster (fatty liver, elevated triglycerides, low HDL, hyperinsulinemia, hypertension). The intervention layer is the same five pillars; the response is often faster than female fat loss because gynoid fat is not driving the picture.

**Aromatase activity in adipose tissue is the male estrogen-elevation route.** Adipose tissue converts testosterone to estrogen via aromatase, and adipose mass itself elevates aromatase activity in a self-reinforcing loop. Aspirin is a mild aromatase inhibitor (Peat noted this in the [[weight-loss|weight-loss email]]); see [[aspirin]] for dose ranges.

## Gut focus

Lipopolysaccharide leaking from the colon reaches the liver via the portal vein, where it provokes Kupffer-cell activation, serotonin release, cortisol elevation, and free-fatty-acid mobilization. The chronic systemic inflammation that mainstream literature calls "inflammaging" is, in Peat's reading, largely an endotoxin signal. Endotoxin drives insulin resistance and fat storage; reducing endotoxin reduces both.

The endotoxin-reduction layer (see [[endotoxin-reduction]] for the full protocol):

- **Daily raw [[carrot-salad|carrot salad]].** The single highest-leverage gut intervention. Peat: "Yes, I know people who have lost weight just by eating a raw carrot every day."
- **Well-cooked white mushrooms.** Their chitin component absorbs gut endotoxin; cook thoroughly to denature the antinutrients.
- **Limit fermentable starches.** Beans, lentils, large servings of cooked grains, raw onions, raw cabbage — all increase fermentation in the small bowel.
- **Well-cooked potato or masa rather than raw or undercooked starches.** Cooking destroys the antinutrients that drive gut irritation.
- **Cascara sagrada in moderate doses, occasionally.** A natural laxative Peat used to accelerate gut transit when needed. Not a daily intervention.

## What this protocol REJECTS

The Peat-frame weight-loss protocol stands in explicit opposition to the dominant mainstream and counterculture weight-loss prescriptions of the last fifty years. The rejections are not stylistic; they are mechanistic.

- **Caloric restriction.** Produces brief weight loss followed by adapted-low-metabolism rebound. The Biggest-Loser data and broader adaptive-thermogenesis literature (Hall and Kerns; Rosenbaum and Leibel) confirm the prediction.
- **Intermittent fasting and time-restricted eating.** Caloric restriction concentrated into windows; the metabolic-adaptation cost is the same, with the added cost of acute cortisol elevation and lean-tissue catabolism during fasting windows. See [[intermittent-fasting]] (research brief at [[intermittent-fasting-research]]) for the full critique.
- **Extended fasting and "fasting heals."** Peat described what happens after glycogen runs out in the [[2010-09-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-sugar-i|2010 sugar episode]]: thymus dissolves within hours; skin atrophies; skeletal muscle is converted to sugar to feed the brain. "People who fast for a week or two will usually lose more muscle weight than fat because of the very rapid conversion of protein into fuel under the influence of cortisol."
- **Ketogenic diet.** Largely water loss followed by adapted-low-metabolism stabilization on chronically elevated cortisol and growth hormone, with worsened thyroid output and accelerated PUFA mobilization in PUFA-loaded bodies.
- **Low-carb diet generally.** Forces the body to run on free fatty acids — exactly the substrate state the framework identifies as the proximate driver of insulin resistance and chronic stress.
- **Low-fat diet (1970s–2000s orthodoxy).** Replaces saturated fat with antithyroid PUFAs; the net effect is metabolic suppression that the dietary advice was meant to prevent.
- **Chronic cardio (distance running, long cycling, marathon training).** Catabolizes lean tissue, drives cortisol, suppresses thyroid, and produces the lean-but-flabby phenotype Peat described.
- **HIIT for fat loss.** Sustained lactic-acid elevation, T3 drops, stress signals rise. Short concentric resistance training is acceptable; sustained high-intensity intervals are not.
- **Weight-loss pharmaceuticals across the categories.** Phentermine and stimulant suppressants (chronic adrenergic activation, cardiovascular risk). The 1990s fen-phen and 2000s sibutramine class (predictable cardiovascular signal). HRT and growth hormone for cosmetic fat loss (cancer-and-cardiac-risk costs). Bariatric surgery (forced caloric restriction with substantial long-term sarcopenia and skeletal cost). See [[iatrogenic-obesity]] for the full ledger.
- **GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide) with caution rather than blanket rejection.** The framework's prediction is that drugs lowering caloric intake without addressing the upstream metabolic state will reproduce the caloric-restriction adaptation pattern at higher pharmacologic intensity; the substantial lean-mass loss reported in trials (a quarter to a third of weight lost is lean tissue) and the predictable rebound on discontinuation are the practical concerns. The position is not "never use." It is that any patient on GLP-1 therapy needs the five-pillar substrate-adequacy and thyroid-restoration layer running alongside to mitigate the predictable iatrogenic costs. **Coordinate with the prescribing clinician; never discontinue abruptly.**

## Tracking signals

The instrument determines the behavior. Use the wrong instrument and the protocol gets abandoned in week three when the scale moves in a direction the framework predicts but the reader does not.

The Peat-preferred markers, in order of priority:

- **Morning oral temperature, taken on waking before getting out of bed.** Target 97.8–98.2°F on waking, rising to 98.6°F after breakfast. A flat or descending curve through the day is the stress-and-hypothyroid pattern.
- **Mid-morning and afternoon temperature.** The shape of the curve carries more signal than any single reading. Peak should be in the afternoon, not the morning.
- **Resting pulse, taken at the same time each day.** Target 75–85 bpm in a well-fed warm adult. Below 75 means intake is too low or thyroid output is suppressed; above 90 at rest suggests adrenaline is running the metabolism instead of thyroid.
- **Waist circumference, mid-thigh circumference, hip circumference.** Weekly, with a tape measure, at the same point in the cycle for women. The body composition tells the truth the scale obscures.
- **Photos from the front, side, and back.** Weekly or biweekly, in the same lighting and posture. The eye sees changes the tape measure does not.
- **Finger and toe warmth.** A direct read of peripheral perfusion and thyroid response. Cold extremities with warm core is the hypothyroid pattern; warm extremities throughout the day is the recovered pattern.
- **Sleep quality and dream vividness.** Returning vivid dreams signal recovering thyroid function. Fragmented sleep is the early-warning sign of failing substrate adequacy or rising cortisol.
- **Mood, energy, libido, motivation.** The systemic signs of metabolic recovery, often visible weeks before any visible body-composition change.
- **Achilles-reflex relaxation speed.** The classic Barnes-era hypothyroid test. A slow, lazy relaxation is hypothyroid; a brisk return is the recovered pattern.
- **The scale belongs in this panel only as a fifth or sixth indicator,** weighed weekly at the same time of day under the same conditions, with the expectation that it will move non-linearly (down for water in week one, up for muscle through weeks two through five, then slowly down for fat from week six onward).

## Realistic expectations

The protocol is slow and sustained. Expectations the reader brings from mainstream weight-loss culture will sabotage adherence; expectations calibrated to the framework's actual timeline will sustain it.

- **Weeks one through three:** Water rebalancing as the stress tissue releases fluid. Scale weight typically drops two to five pounds; circumferences move modestly. Temperature and pulse start to rise. Sleep often improves within the first week as salt and substrate adequacy quiet adrenaline.
- **Weeks four through eight:** Muscle regrowth as the catabolized lean tissue rebuilds under improved cortisol-and-thyroid conditions. Scale weight may stabilize, rise slightly, or move slowly. Circumferences continue to shrink. Most readers abandon the protocol here if they are weighing daily and panicking. Don't.
- **Weeks nine and beyond:** Sustained fat loss begins, typically one to two pounds per month for a moderately overweight reader, faster for a heavily overweight reader, slower for a near-target-weight reader. Circumferences continue to shrink. The body-composition pattern is now visibly leaner with stable or rising muscle mass.
- **Months six through twenty-four:** Continued slow steady fat loss as the PUFA-depletion clock runs. Thyroid support typically reduces or comes off as endogenous function returns. The recovered metabolic-rate signature (warm hands and feet, vivid dreams, regular cycles, calmer mood, more energy at lower intake than the pre-protocol baseline) is now stable.
- **Years two through four:** The four-year PUFA-depletion horizon completes for most readers. The metabolic ceiling has substantially shifted upward; the body now defends a lower fat mass on the same dietary pattern. Maintenance is the same protocol minus the supplemental thyroid bridge.

Rapid loss is not the goal. Two pounds per week of true fat loss is the upper edge of what is physiologically possible without lean-tissue cannibalization; one to two pounds per month after the first phase is the sustainable target. Anything faster is water, muscle, or both, and is being paid for from the body's stores against later interest.

## Failure modes

The framework has predictable ways of failing in practice. Naming them in advance is the difference between a protocol that holds and one that collapses in month three.

- **Under-eating and the binge cycle.** The reader carries the calorie-restriction reflex into the protocol, eats half of what the framework calls for, drives cortisol, breaks down under the resulting hunger and stress, binges, and blames the protocol. The fix is to actually eat the substrate-adequacy floor; the warm hands and the steady mood will follow.
- **Under-salting.** The hidden killer. Mainstream advice still drills sodium restriction; the framework asks for the opposite. Under-salted readers run insomnia, anxiety, low morning temperature, and the slow collapse of every other lever.
- **Cutting carbs because they feel "fattening."** The orange juice is the engine of the cut. Cutting it is the fastest way to crash the protocol.
- **Cutting fat too aggressively.** The fat reduction is for weight-loss-specific calorie arithmetic, not a fat-phobic position. Coconut oil and butter remain present; the fat that gets pulled back is the whole-milk fat and the cream and ice cream. Cutting all fat collapses hormone production within weeks.
- **Ignoring the PUFA layer.** Reader keeps eating restaurant food cooked in seed oils, keeps taking the fish-oil supplement, keeps the protein bars. The other pillars work against the PUFA headwind they refuse to remove and the protocol's full effect never materializes.
- **Fasting attempts as "tests" or "resets."** Even a single 24-hour fast pulls cortisol up sharply and undoes a week of the substrate-adequacy work.
- **Chronic-cardio addiction.** The reader cannot quit the long runs because of the psychological identity the running represents. The chronic cortisol headwind stalls every other pillar.
- **Daily-scale-weighing panic.** The reader weighs daily, sees the muscle-regrowth phase, panics, reverts to restriction, and the protocol dies in week four. Weekly weighing at most; daily measurements should be temperature and pulse instead.
- **Adding thyroid before the diet is in place.** Stacks exogenous thyroid on top of recovering endogenous thyroid; produces tachycardia and insomnia; the reader concludes thyroid "doesn't work for me" and abandons the supplement.
- **Stopping thyroid abruptly when feeling better.** Thyroid is a bridge; bridges come off slowly. Sudden discontinuation produces a hypothyroid rebound.
- **Treating PUFA depletion as a 90-day project.** The four-year horizon is not negotiable. Readers who quit at month three because the rate of fat loss has not matched a crash diet's never reach the year-two reward.
- **Eating-disorder pattern carried into the protocol.** The framework explicitly does not work in a body running active eating-disorder physiology. Clinician coordination is mandatory in any reader with a history of anorexia, bulimia, binge-eating disorder, orthorexia, or chronic dieting since adolescence.

## When to escalate

Most readers can apply the five pillars without clinical complication. Specific situations require clinician coordination before any lever is pulled:

- **Pre-existing diabetes (Type 1, Type 2, or LADA).** The substrate-adequacy pillar interacts directly with insulin dosing and oral hypoglycemic regimens. Glucose monitoring becomes mandatory; insulin and medication doses will need to be adjusted as the metabolic rate recovers. See [[diabetes-protocol]] for the diabetes-specific overlay and coordinate with an endocrinologist throughout.
- **Cardiovascular comorbidity.** Hypertension, prior MI, stent placement, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, severe valvular disease, or current cardiac medication regimens all warrant cardiology coordination. The salt-and-thyroid layer in particular requires medical oversight in any patient on antihypertensive, anticoagulant, or antiarrhythmic medication.
- **Active or recent eating-disorder history.** The framework's emphasis on adequate eating is therapeutically aligned with eating-disorder recovery, but the body-composition focus can re-activate disordered patterns. Coordinate with a treating clinician or eating-disorder specialist throughout.
- **Pregnancy and lactation.** The protocol is broadly safe and aligned with [[pregnancy-nutrition]], but caloric needs and thyroid dynamics shift substantially during pregnancy and postpartum. Obstetric coordination throughout.
- **Pediatric obesity.** The framework's child-feeding layer in [[child-feeding]] is the canonical reference. Children need substrate; caloric restriction in children is contraindicated.
- **Current GLP-1 agonist, antipsychotic, corticosteroid, beta-blocker, SSRI, lithium, or other weight-affecting medication.** Do not discontinue without prescriber coordination. The framework runs alongside conventional treatment, never instead of it.
- **Rapid unexplained weight change in either direction during the protocol.** Loss above two pounds per week sustained, or gain above three pounds per week sustained, warrants medical evaluation to rule out thyroid storm, decompensated diabetes, fluid retention from cardiac or renal pathology, occult malignancy, or other clinical processes that have nothing to do with the diet.
- **Severe symptoms during the protocol.** New chest pain, severe dyspnea, syncope, sustained tachycardia above 110 at rest, sustained temperatures above 99.5°F, severe edema, jaundice, or any neurologic change warrants prompt medical evaluation. The protocol's expected discomfort is mild and transient (a few days of breathlessness or lightheadedness as PUFA mobilizes); anything beyond that is not a protocol effect to be pushed through.

## Counter-arguments

The protocol inverts mainstream and counterculture weight-loss orthodoxy at multiple points; the counter-arguments deserve explicit acknowledgment.

The **CICO** orthodoxy holds that caloric arithmetic is the only relevant variable and that any intervention must close the energy gap. The framework's response is that CICO is true as a thermodynamic identity and underdetermined as a regulatory model — substrate quality, hormonal context, and mitochondrial state together determine how a given caloric load is partitioned. The full case is in [[obesity]].

The **carb-insulin / low-carb camp** (Lustig, Taubes, Ludwig) holds that hyperinsulinemia from carbohydrate is the proximate cause of obesity. The framework's response is that hyperinsulinemia is the secondary response to PUFA-blocked glucose oxidation, not the primary driver, and that low-carb success is largely water loss followed by adapted-low-metabolism stabilization. The full critique is in [[obesity]] and [[insulin-resistance-and-metabolic-syndrome]].

The **intermittent-fasting camp** (Fung, Longo) holds that fasting heals through autophagy and metabolic flexibility. The framework's response is that fasting cannibalizes tissue in a predictable order (water, glycogen, lean mass, fat last), reproduces the caloric-restriction adapted-low-metabolism pattern, and is uniformly opposed across the Peat corpus. See [[intermittent-fasting]].

The **chronic-cardio camp** (running culture, endurance fitness) holds that aerobic exercise is the cornerstone of fat loss. The framework's response is the radioactive-testosterone study cited in the 2013 weight-gain episode — runners' hearts and tissues atrophied; weightlifters' did not.

The **mainstream endocrinology / set-point camp** (Friedman, Leibel, Rosenbaum) holds that body weight is regulated around a defended set-point that resists intervention. The framework's response, documented in [[leptin-resistance-and-set-point]], is that the set-point is environmental and dietary in origin and shifts as the substrate state of the body shifts; the four-year PUFA-depletion horizon is the timescale on which it shifts.

The **GLP-1 era** position is that semaglutide-class drugs have permanently solved the obesity problem. The framework's response is that the trials are short, lean-mass loss is substantial and underreported, rebound on discontinuation is severe, and the long-term sarcopenia and gastroparesis costs will clarify with longer follow-up. The position is not blanket rejection — it is that GLP-1 therapy without the substrate-and-thyroid layer running alongside reproduces the caloric-restriction failure pattern at higher pharmacologic intensity.

## Data gaps

- **Exact gelatin gram targets.** Peat never specified a daily target; the rule is structural (presence in the diet balancing muscle-meat amino acids) rather than numeric. The 5–15 g/day range that appears in some practitioner protocols is extrapolated, not Peat-sourced.
- **Precise coconut oil titration in PUFA-heavy starting populations.** The three-tier dose framework is well documented; the specific mobilization rate at each tier and the rate at which it should be advanced is not.
- **Niacinamide and aspirin dosing for FFA suppression during the cut.** Peat used 100 mg niacinamide and named aspirin as useful without specifying weight-loss-specific milligram doses. See the substance pages.
- **Behavior-change scaffolding.** The framework is rich on physiology and thin on the psychological and habit-architecture layer that determines whether a reader actually executes the protocol consistently across years. Adjacent literature (Charles Duhigg, BJ Fogg, Wendy Wood) may provide the missing layer; integration with the Peat frame is not yet done.
- **Outcomes data at population scale.** The framework's evidentiary base is a combination of physiologic mechanism, Peat's clinical practice across forty years, and population-level natural experiments (Yucatan, Tokelau, traditional Africa). Randomized controlled trial data on the full five-pillar protocol does not exist.
- **Microbiome integration.** The endotoxin-reduction pillar implicates the gut microbiome directly but predates most of the 2010s-2020s microbiome literature. The framework's emphasis on carrot, mushrooms, and antibiotic-where-warranted is consistent with reducing endotoxin-producing taxa, but specific microbiome metrics are not part of the standard tracking layer.
- **Wearable-device and CGM integration.** The temperature-and-pulse signature is the framework's primary instrument; continuous glucose monitors, DEXA scans, and metabolic-chamber data postdate most of Peat's writing. Integration with the modern wearable layer is a forward-looking gap.
