# Arthritis Protocol

The mechanism is in [[arthritis]] — chronic [[inflammation]] driven by [[estrogen]] excess, [[serotonin]] and prostaglandin amplification, [[pufa]]-loaded membranes, [[hypothyroidism|hypothyroid]] suppression of oxidative metabolism, gut-derived [[endotoxin]], and a depleted progesterone-cortisol-thyroid axis, with joint tissue as the local expression. This page is the action layer. What to do on day one. Which version of the protocol applies to OA versus RA versus lupus versus the HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathies versus psoriatic arthritis. The dose ranges Peat actually quoted in the 1986 *Hormone Balancing* newsletter, the 1986 patent case series, the 2007 *Blocking Tissue Destruction* article, and the 2016 *Ask the Herb Doctor* RA episode. The phasing across a four-week acute flare, a three-month stabilization, and indefinite maintenance. The places where the protocol must coordinate with rheumatology — biologics, methotrexate, long-term steroids — rather than replace it.

**What this protocol is not.** It is not a rejection of rheumatology. A patient with active RA on a biologic, a lupus patient on hydroxychloroquine, an AS patient who has lost spinal mobility on a TNF inhibitor — none of these stop their drugs to read a wiki. The Peat framework is additive: it addresses the upstream metabolic state the biologics suppress downstream. Drug taper is a conversation with the rheumatologist after the metabolic field has demonstrably stabilized — months, not weeks. The protocol is also not a substitute for orthopedic surgery when joint destruction is anatomically advanced; the patent's Case 3 (60-year-old woman) had already received two artificial joints before topical DHEA-progesterone produced "complete relief" of her remaining symptoms. Structural damage gets structural fixes; the bioenergetic protocol addresses the upstream state that produced the damage.

## Diagnosis prerequisites: which arthritis are you running

Peat refused the partition between arthritides — "I don't make a clear distinction between any kind of arthritis," he told Andrew Murray in the [[2016-10-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-rheumatoid-arthritis|2016 RA episode]]. The metabolic core is shared. What changes between diagnoses is the emphasis layer, the failure-mode profile, and the rate at which the protocol will or will not work without rheumatology coordination.

1. **Osteoarthritis (OA).** "Wear-and-tear" cartilage degradation, often presenting in finger DIP joints (Heberden's nodes), knees, hips, and lumbar spine. Postmenopausal female predominance for hand OA. No systemic autoimmune markers. Imaging shows joint-space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis. The Peat reading: chronic inflammation driving fragile connective tissue with mechanical failure as the visible endpoint. Emphasis layer below: gelatin/glycine for matrix support, topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol gel (the 1986 patent formulation), preserve mobility via walking not impact exercise.
2. **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA).** Symmetrical synovitis, morning stiffness lasting more than an hour, rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP antibodies, joint destruction visible on radiograph or MRI within months to years. Three-times-more-common in women. Systemic comorbidity: cardiovascular acceleration, anemia of chronic disease, interstitial lung disease, vasculitis. Emphasis layer: estrogen reduction (the two cases in the 1986 newsletter that cleared on stopping HRT), progesterone in women, [[cyproheptadine]] when serotonin signs dominate, thyroid optimization.
3. **Lupus arthritis.** Joint inflammation as one criterion of SLE; the rest of the SLE picture (malar rash, photosensitivity, serositis, renal involvement, ANA positivity) dominates the clinical decision tree. Female-of-reproductive-age preponderance. Emphasis layer: same as RA but with kidney protection as the load-bearing concern. Peat noted in the [[2009-08-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-you-are-what-you-eat-2009|2009 *You Are What You Eat* episode]]: "I have seen lupus patients over the last 30 years recover completely, or at least not die of kidney failure, by using thyroid, progesterone, and avoiding polyunsaturated fat (PUFA)."
4. **Ankylosing spondylitis (AS) and HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathies.** Sacroiliitis, inflammatory back pain (better with movement, worse with rest), enthesitis, uveitis, HLA-B27 positive in ~90%. Male-predominant. Emphasis layer: gut focus, because the HLA-B27 association tracks gut [[endotoxin]] and Klebsiella overgrowth in the mainstream literature as well as the Peat framework. Daily carrot salad is non-negotiable, antibiotic trial (minocycline) considered, sometimes formal SIBO workup.
5. **Psoriatic arthritis.** Joint inflammation accompanying psoriasis; characteristic DIP involvement, dactylitis (sausage digit), nail pitting. HLA-B27 association in axial disease. Emphasis layer: same gut focus as AS, with the skin biology of psoriasis as adjunct signal — [[serotonin]] and [[prolactin]] dominance, often histamine load.
6. **Unclassified / overlapping / "non-specific joint pain."** The 1986 patent explicitly covered "non-specific joint pain associated with stress or incidental to another ailment." Most patients arriving without a clean serology fit one of the patterns above weakly; the protocol's daily core applies regardless and the emphasis layer follows the dominant clinical signal.

A first-time-arthritis patient without a diagnosis runs the daily core for four to six weeks while a rheumatology workup proceeds in parallel. Anti-CCP, RF, ANA, HLA-B27, CRP, ESR, and imaging are useful diagnostic data; the protocol does not depend on them but the emphasis layer does.

## The daily core

This is what every arthritis variant runs from day one. The doses below are the lower-to-mid ranges Peat quoted in the corpus; titration upward is appropriate during a flare and downward during maintenance.

**Aspirin, 81 to 650 mg daily with food, paired with vitamin K1.** The single highest-leverage pharmaceutical in the protocol. Aspirin works on every arm of the arthritis cascade simultaneously: it inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 prostaglandin generation from arachidonic acid, suppresses aromatase (lowering endogenous estrogen), shifts mitochondrial metabolism toward glucose oxidation, opposes serotonin's metabolic effects, and protects against [[endotoxin]]. For mild OA or maintenance, 81 mg daily with breakfast. For active RA, lupus arthritis, or flares, 325 mg twice daily (after breakfast and with the evening meal) and titrating to 650 mg twice daily where pain remains active. Full pharmacology on [[aspirin]].

The K1 pairing matters. Chronic aspirin depletes vitamin K and prolongs bleeding times; the conventional fear of long-term aspirin use is largely a fear of unsupplemented chronic aspirin use. Murray's rule of thumb in the 2016 episode: 1 mg of vitamin K1 per 325 mg of aspirin. Vitamin K2 (MK-4) at 1–5 mg daily covers both the aspirin offset and the bone-density piece, and is the more useful form long-term. Avoid enteric-coated aspirin (the coating interferes with the food-buffered absorption Peat preferred); USP aspirin crystals dissolved in warm water or buffered tablets are the canonical forms.

**Gelatin, 10 to 20 g daily.** The amino-acid rebalancing intervention. Hydrolyzed gelatin powder mixed into orange juice or milk, or bone broth from joint-bearing bones (oxtail, knuckle, chicken feet — not marrow, which is iron-rich). Gelatin is approximately 32% glycine, which functions as the biochemical mirror image of serotonin and a direct anti-inflammatory at the cellular level. The case is on [[gelatin]]; the arthritis-specific note is that Peat reported friends' relatives "recovered from long-standing pain problems (arthritic and rheumatic and possibly neurological) in just a few days after taking 10 or 15 grams of gelatin each day." The amino-acid dilution effect — gelatin contains essentially no tryptophan and minimal cysteine and methionine — is what shifts the stress-protein profile that drives the serotonin-cortisol-inflammation cascade.

The form choices: Great Lakes hydrolyzed collagen, NOW Foods beef gelatin, or homemade broth simmered 6–12 hours from joint-bearing bones with a splash of vinegar to extract collagen. For OA specifically, the 10–15 g daily dose is the load-bearing intervention; for RA the gelatin is one piece in a larger cascade.

**Magnesium glycinate, 200 to 400 mg elemental daily, with the evening meal.** Magnesium relaxes vascular and skeletal smooth muscle, supports oxidative metabolism, reduces the cortisol-adrenaline excitation that drives flares, and protects mitochondrial function. The glycinate form double-doses the glycine pathway. Magnesium chloride applied topically (Ancient Minerals magnesium oil on the calves and shoulders, 200–600 mg elemental equivalent) is the absorption-bypass when GI tolerance is limiting. Magnesium oxide is poorly absorbed; citrate works but the laxative threshold caps the dose. Most arthritis patients are functionally magnesium-deficient because the stress-cortisol cycle excretes it through the kidneys; supplementing addresses the depletion rather than building beyond physiologic need.

**Vitamin D3 (2,000 to 5,000 IU daily) and vitamin K2 (MK-4 1 to 5 mg, or MK-7 100 to 200 mcg) daily.** Vitamin D suppresses parathyroid hormone (itself antithyroid and pro-inflammatory) and supports immune modulation; serum 25(OH)D target 50–70 ng/mL. K2 directs calcium to bone rather than vasculature and joint capsule, offsets the aspirin-bleeding concern, and protects bone density during the protocol's long arc. Take both with a fat-containing meal.

**Vitamin E, mixed tocopherols, 100 to 400 IU daily with a meal.** Protects stored PUFA from peroxidation during the years-long tissue PUFA turnover. The mixed-tocopherol form (alpha + gamma + delta) is the goal; pure synthetic dl-alpha-tocopherol is the form to avoid. Unique Mixed E, NOW Foods Gamma E Complex, or comparable. Vitamin E was the carrier in Peat's 1986 patent formulation — the topical DHEA-progesterone gel used 80% mixed tocopherols by mass — because tocopherol is itself anti-inflammatory in joint tissue and because its absorption through skin into the lymphatic system bypasses hepatic first-pass.

**Coconut oil and butter for cooking; eliminate all seed oils.** The highest-leverage dietary change. Soybean, corn, canola, safflower, sunflower, cottonseed, peanut, grapeseed — out of the pantry, off the cooking line, scrutinized in every restaurant order. Replace with butter, ghee, beef tallow, coconut oil, and ([[olive-oil|olive oil]]) for low-heat dressings. The PUFA-elimination move is the long-arc background of the protocol; adipose half-life for PUFA is roughly four years, so visible benefit accumulates over months and the full clearance over years. Restaurant food is the hidden source; fish oil supplements are the concentrated version of the wrong molecule and should stop on day one. Full case at [[pufa]] and [[seed-oils]].

**Daily raw carrot salad.** One large carrot, peeled, grated, dressed with a tablespoon of coconut oil or olive oil, a teaspoon of vinegar, and salt. The insoluble fiber binds biliary estrogen, bacterial fragments, and [[endotoxin]] in the gut, lowering the systemic inflammation load. Daily, ideally with the largest meal. Particularly important for AS and psoriatic arthritis where the gut piece is load-bearing. Full case at [[carrot-salad]].

**Two quarts of milk, 1–2% fat, daily.** Delivers ~2,000 mg of calcium (the cardiovascular floor and the joint-matrix substrate), provides whey and casein in a context that the calcium-magnesium-vitamin balance supports, and supplies the sugar (lactose) for liver glycogen support. Cheese, yogurt, and ice cream substitute where milk volume is limiting. Calcium supplements are not the equivalent; pulsed bolus dosing of calcium without the fat-soluble vitamin context drives vascular calcification rather than bone deposition.

**A quart of orange juice daily.** Potassium (~2,000 mg/quart), magnesium, naringenin and hesperidin (mild aromatase inhibition, relevant in RA and lupus), and the sucrose to support liver T4-to-T3 conversion. Splits across the day with meals.

**Salt to appetite, 3–6 g sodium daily.** Salt restriction raises aldosterone and adrenaline, both of which sensitize the inflammatory cascade. Salt-to-taste at every meal. The PURE-study J-curve sits at 4–5 g sodium per day; the DASH 1,500 mg target is below the bottom of the safe range for an arthritis patient running on inflammatory substrate.

This core is the same for OA, RA, lupus, AS, psoriatic, and unclassified arthritis. What follows is the emphasis layer.

## RA and lupus emphasis layer (estrogen reduction, progesterone, antiserotonin)

The female preponderance of RA (3:1) and lupus (9:1) and the dramatic remission of RA during pregnancy point at the estrogen-progesterone axis as load-bearing. The two opening cases in the [[hormone-balancing-natural-treatment-and-cure-for-arthritis|1986 *Hormone Balancing* newsletter]] — both women with disabling RA, both achieving "total relief and clearing of the swellings" within a week or two of *stopping estrogen* (one had been told her dose was protective and needed *raising*) — are the clinical pointers. Pregnancy resolves RA in roughly 70% of cases and the resolution tracks the placental progesterone surge; postpartum flare tracks the abrupt withdrawal.

**Stop or taper exogenous estrogen.** Oral contraceptives, HRT, compounded estradiol creams. The taper is a conversation with the prescribing physician — abrupt cessation can produce its own symptom cascade — but the direction is clear. The 1986 newsletter cases recovered within one to two weeks of cessation; expect a similar timeline if the case is genuinely estrogen-driven.

**Progesterone (Progest-E or equivalent).** The dose Peat quoted in the [[progesterone-summaries-progesterone-supplementation-dosage-of-progesterone|*Progesterone Summaries*]] correspondence: "Three to four drops, providing ten or twelve milligrams of progesterone, is typical for a day, but some women prefer to repeat that amount." For RA or lupus arthritis flare in a woman:

- **Cycling women (premenopausal):** 3–4 drops Progest-E (≈9–12 mg) on the gums or inner lip, twice or three times daily across days 14–28 of the cycle. Pregnancy-mimicking continuous high-dose protocols (30–100 mg daily across multiple doses) for severe flares, then return to cyclical.
- **Perimenopausal or postmenopausal women:** Continuous low-dose (3–4 drops, 1–3 times daily) for the protective antistress effect, escalating during flares to 30–100 mg daily across multiple doses.

The form matters. Progest-E delivers progesterone in vitamin E oil for trans-mucosal absorption that bypasses hepatic first-pass. Oral micronized progesterone (Prometrium) is sedating from first-pass conversion to allopregnanolone and is useful for insomnia-predominant cases; transdermal creams absorb variably and are second-best. Synthetic progestins (medroxyprogesterone) are *not* the same molecule — they antagonize natural progesterone's anti-inflammatory effects and were the active arm of the Women's Health Initiative cardiovascular harm signal. The pharmacy distinction is essential.

**Cyproheptadine when serotonin signs dominate.** RA flares concentrated at certain times of day (4 a.m. wake-up with pain, evening flare, premenstrual exacerbation), IBS comorbidity, migraine comorbidity, anxiety-insomnia-RA cluster — all signal the [[serotonin]] amplifier piece. The dose Peat quoted in the [[cyproheptadine|cyproheptadine correspondence]]: start with half a milligram (one-eighth of a 4 mg tablet) at bedtime, escalating to 1–2 mg as tolerated. Pediatric syrup formulations make fractional dosing easy. Sedation is the limiting factor for daytime dosing but the desired effect for evening. The drug's antiserotonin action persists longer than the sedating action, so intermittent dosing often produces extended symptom relief — Peat noted "when symptoms stop the effects can last for days without using it." Full protocol on [[cyproheptadine]]; the arthritis-specific note is that cyproheptadine is the rescue tool for acute flares rather than chronic maintenance.

**Cycle pregnancy-mimicking progesterone in flares.** When an RA or lupus patient enters a severe flare, scale the progesterone toward the pregnancy-physiology range (50–200 mg daily across multiple oral or sublingual doses) for two to four weeks, then taper back to cyclical or low-dose continuous. The rationale is direct: pregnancy reliably remits RA, and the remitting agent is the placental progesterone load. Reproducing the hormonal context reproduces part of the clinical effect.

**Thyroid optimization.** Most RA and lupus patients are functionally hypothyroid. The diagnostic apparatus (morning oral temperature, resting pulse, Achilles reflex relaxation time) is on [[hypothyroidism]]; the protocol is [[hypothyroid-recovery]] and [[thyroid-dosing]]. The arthritis-specific note: the 71-year-old man in Peat's opening 1986 case had arthritis resolve "entirely" in less than two weeks on thyroid extract alone — no progesterone, no DHEA, no aspirin. Thyroid is upstream of the rest of the protocol.

**DHEA, 10–25 mg daily oral, for women (5 mg start), or 25–50 mg for men.** Adrenal androgen depletion is a near-universal finding in chronic RA; restoring physiological DHEA improves the precursor pool for all downstream steroids. Start low — the androgenic effects in women (acne, scalp-hair-thinning, facial hair) appear above the physiological dose range. Avoid in patients with significant body fat, where peripheral aromatase conversion to estrogen can paradoxically worsen the inflammatory state.

## OA emphasis layer (gelatin, topical 1986 patent gel, mobility)

Osteoarthritis runs the same daily core but the emphasis differs. Estrogen reduction matters less (most OA is postmenopausal with already-low endogenous estrogen); progesterone matters in women but the dose is lower; the load-bearing interventions are gelatin/glycine for the matrix, topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol gel for direct joint application, mechanical mobility preservation, and weight management where excess load is contributing.

**Gelatin, escalate to 15–25 g daily.** OA-specific dose is upward of the daily core because the connective-tissue matrix support is doing more clinical work here. Hydrolyzed collagen in two daily doses (one with breakfast, one with the evening meal), or bone broth from joint-bearing bones as the daily warming beverage in cold months. Glycine's anti-inflammatory and ATP-sparing actions, combined with the amino-acid rebalancing, produce the "fewer days to several weeks" timeline Peat reported in *Gelatin, Stress, Longevity*.

**Topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol gel (the 1986 patent formulation).** This is the arthritis-specific intervention that distinguishes Peat's protocol from any conventional treatment. The patent (US 4,628,052, December 1986) specifies the composition: **5% DHEA, 80% mixed tocopherols, up to 10% olive oil, up to 3% progesterone, optionally up to 0.5% testosterone for male patients.**

The patent case series records dramatic same-day responses:
- **Case 1 (Patent):** 78-year-old man with mild knee OA — applied 2.5% DHEA + 2.5% progesterone in tocopherol/olive oil, rose easily and walked without pain within two hours. Remained symptom-free for eight months with continued use.
- **Case 3:** 60-year-old woman with severe RA, two artificial joints, post-cartilage repair — applied 7% DHEA + 3% progesterone solution; experienced "complete relief" and walked sightseeing for two days without after-effects.
- **Case 5:** 62-year-old man with arthroscopically confirmed cartilage damage refusing surgery — applied 20 mL of the solution, walked normally within 45 minutes, slept through the night for the first time in years.

The compounding pathway: a compounding pharmacy mixes DHEA powder (5 g per 100 mL) and progesterone powder (3 g per 100 mL) into a base of 80% mixed tocopherols and 10–20% olive oil, applied 0.05–0.2 mL per square inch of skin over the affected joint, rubbed in thoroughly, and covered to prevent rub-off during absorption. Warmth (heating pad, hot bath) accelerates absorption. The patent dose range is "400 mg to 1000 mg active material topically, and may be repeated 2 times per day" — that is, total active steroid mass per application, not per square inch.

For patients without compounding-pharmacy access, the consumer-grade approximation is Progest-E applied directly over the painful joint (3–4 drops, ≈9–12 mg progesterone), combined with a separately-applied DHEA oil (compounding pharmacies or careful DIY using DHEA powder dissolved warm in vitamin E oil at 5% concentration). The mixed-tocopherol vehicle is non-negotiable; the patent specifies it because tocopherol both carries the steroids through skin and provides the anti-inflammatory action locally.

**Weight management as needed.** The OA-load-bearing question is honest: in knee and hip OA, excess body weight does multiply the mechanical load on already-fragile cartilage. The protocol's metabolic moves (PUFA elimination, milk, fruit, salt-to-taste, thyroid restoration) tend to produce gradual weight loss without explicit caloric restriction because the metabolic rate climbs and lipolysis subsides. Aggressive caloric restriction is counterproductive — it raises cortisol and worsens the inflammatory substrate.

**Preserve joint mobility via walking, not impact exercise.** Walking, swimming, cycling, gentle stretching, and resistance work with controlled load preserve cartilage and synovial circulation. Running, high-impact aerobics, plyometric work, and heavy weight-bearing exercise on already-damaged joints accelerate the destruction. The 1986 patent Case 3 walked sightseeing for two days after topical application — walking is therapeutic; running on the same joints would not have been.

## AS and psoriatic emphasis layer (gut focus, HLA-B27, minocycline)

Ankylosing spondylitis and psoriatic arthritis emphasize the gut-derived [[endotoxin]] chain Peat placed at the center of his inflammation framework. The HLA-B27 association tracks Klebsiella and other Gram-negative overgrowth in the mainstream literature; the Peat framework absorbs this picture as a specific case of the general endotoxin-driven inflammation cascade.

**Gut decontamination intensified.** Daily raw carrot salad becomes twice-daily. Cooked white mushrooms (200 g daily, sautéed in butter or coconut oil) bind endotoxin via mannan polysaccharides. Bamboo shoots (canned, rinsed, 100–200 g) provide additional antibacterial fiber. Charcoal (activated, 1–2 capsules) once or twice weekly between meals for a deeper gut sweep, never daily and never with thyroid or other medications. Full case at [[endotoxin]] and [[carrot-salad]].

**Minocycline trial (with rheumatology coordination).** The MIRA trial and longer-running observational data support minocycline as a partial-response intervention for RA, AS, and reactive arthritis. The framework's interpretation: minocycline reduces gut bacterial load and modulates the matrix metalloproteinase pathways that drive joint destruction. Standard dose 100 mg twice daily for two to six months; coordinate with rheumatology because the response timeline overlaps with biologic-decision windows. Doxycycline is the second choice. Not for daily indefinite use.

**The bowel-rest piece.** Peat noted in multiple interviews that fiber from raw vegetables (carrot, bamboo, mushroom) was useful while fiber from grains, beans, and legumes was often inflammatory in this patient population. AS and psoriatic patients frequently improve dramatically when grains and beans drop out of the diet. Not because grains are universally toxic but because the gut substrate driving the spondyloarthropathy is sensitive to the particular fermentation profile grains and beans produce.

**Eliminate alcohol.** Beer (phytoestrogens + endotoxin from yeast metabolism) is the worst offender, but spirits and wine all stress the liver and increase intestinal permeability. AS and psoriatic patients are the subgroup that responds most visibly to alcohol elimination.

## Topical interventions (DMSO + aspirin, DMSO + progesterone)

DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide) is Peat's most ambivalent topical recommendation. He experimented with it from 1965 onward and found it useful in specific scenarios, but warned repeatedly about quality control, breakdown products, histamine release, and skin reactions. The protocol places DMSO interventions in the "selective use, with quality controls" category — never the daily core, always with clear rationale, and always with the right carrier.

**DMSO + aspirin solution for localized flares.** USP-grade aspirin (crushed from a 325 mg tablet, or USP aspirin powder) dissolved in pharmaceutical-grade DMSO (Jacob Laboratories, DMSO Store, or equivalent — the impurity profile is what makes or breaks this intervention) at approximately 10% concentration, applied with a glass dropper to the joint, rubbed in, and covered. The aspirin penetrates trans-dermally into the joint capsule at concentrations far above what oral dosing achieves locally. Useful for a single inflamed knee, wrist, or finger joint where systemic aspirin is already at ceiling.

**DMSO + progesterone for refractory joint pain.** Progest-E (3–4 drops) mixed with a few drops of pharmaceutical-grade DMSO and rubbed directly over the joint. The DMSO functions as a penetration enhancer for the progesterone. The 1986 patent's tocopherol/olive oil vehicle is gentler and the canonical first choice; DMSO is the second-line vehicle when penetration through thicker skin (knees, hips, lumbar spine) is limiting.

**DMSO cautions.** Skin reactions (redness, itching, mild burning) are common and usually resolve with reduced concentration or carrier dilution. The garlic-onion taste in the mouth within minutes of skin application is universal and harmless. The serious concern is impurity: industrial-grade DMSO carries breakdown products and contaminants that produce histamine release and occasionally serious reactions. Pharmaceutical-grade only, single-bottle source, never decanted into non-glass containers (DMSO leaches plasticizers from polyethylene and polypropylene). Never combined with chemical sunscreens, fragrances, dyes, or topical pharmaceuticals on the same skin area — DMSO carries whatever is on the skin into the bloodstream. The clean-skin rule is absolute.

Peat's published caution in the [[blocking-tissue-destruction|2007 *Blocking Tissue Destruction* article]] reads: experiment carefully, use the cleanest material available, and recognize that systemic aspirin is more reliable than topical DMSO-aspirin for most patients most of the time.

## Phase 1: Acute flare (weeks 1–4)

The protocol's load-bearing first phase. The patient is in active inflammation: pain disrupting sleep, morning stiffness lasting more than an hour, swollen joints, fatigue, possibly active systemic symptoms (RA cardiovascular comorbidity, lupus serositis, AS uveitis). The goal is rapid suppression of the inflammatory cascade while the upstream interventions begin their longer-arc work.

**Aspirin daily at the upper range.** 325 mg twice daily for OA, 325–650 mg twice or three times daily for active RA / lupus arthritis. Always with food. K1 1 mg per 325 mg aspirin.

**Gelatin daily, 15–25 g.** Split into two or three doses across the day. Hydrolyzed collagen mixed into orange juice for breakfast; bone broth as a warming evening beverage; a third dose in the afternoon if symptom-driven.

**Daily raw carrot salad, two if AS or psoriatic.**

**Cyproheptadine evening dose if serotonin signs.** Start at 0.5 mg (one-eighth tablet) at bedtime, escalating to 1–2 mg after the first three to seven days if tolerated. Particularly important for the RA patient with 4 a.m. pain-driven wake-ups.

**Thyroid check and (likely) initiation.** Morning oral temperature, resting pulse, basal metabolic signs. If temperature is below 97.8°F and pulse is below 75, initiate desiccated thyroid at 15 mg (one-quarter grain) with breakfast or T3 at 5 mcg twice daily, building per the [[hypothyroid-recovery]] protocol. Magnesium loading (400–600 mg/day) precedes thyroid initiation by one week to prevent the transient cardiac symptoms Peat warned about in the [[2019-08-20-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-vitamin-d-thyroid-evolving-consciously|2019 vitamin D / thyroid episode]].

**Minimize all triggers.** No PUFA (zero seed oil, zero fish oil), no undigested protein crossing the gut (cooked meals, no raw protein, possibly minocycline trial if AS/psoriatic), no cold exposure (warm clothing, avoid air conditioning, hot baths for joint warming), no acute psychological stressors (workload reduction, sleep prioritization), no alcohol, no fasting, no skipped meals.

**Topical interventions as needed.** Topical DHEA-progesterone-tocopherol gel for OA flares; Progest-E directly over inflamed joints; DMSO+aspirin for refractory localized RA flares with quality controls in place.

**Tracking daily.** Morning stiffness duration (the most sensitive single sign), pulse and oral temperature on waking, sleep quality, mood and cognition. The expected change over four weeks: stiffness compressing from hours to minutes; sleep improving; cold extremities warming; pain backing off without further escalation in dose.

The drug-coordination piece. If the patient enters this phase on a biologic, methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine, or oral steroid, those continue under rheumatology supervision. The protocol's job in Phase 1 is to demonstrate metabolic-state movement that creates the conversational basis for taper in Phase 2 or 3.

## Phase 2: Stabilization (months 1–3)

The protocol becomes routine. Doses titrate to the lowest effective level. The rheumatology conversation about drug taper begins.

**Aspirin titrates down to the lowest dose holding the symptom signal.** Many OA patients move from 325 mg twice daily to 81 mg daily; many RA patients hold at 325 mg twice daily; lupus arthritis patients may need 650 mg twice daily for the duration. The decision rule: drop one dose level every two weeks while monitoring morning stiffness; if stiffness lengthens, return to the prior dose.

**Thyroid dose titrates upward to physiological signs.** Morning temperature 98.0°F, afternoon 98.6°F, resting pulse 75–85 bpm. Most patients land at one to two grains of desiccated thyroid per day, or T3 at 5–15 mcg two to three times daily, by the end of Phase 2.

**Progesterone shifts from acute-flare dose to cyclical (premenopausal) or continuous-low (peri/postmenopausal).** RA patients in remission can often drop to 3–4 drops Progest-E twice daily and hold there; lupus patients may need to stay closer to the upper end.

**Cyproheptadine reduces to intermittent or stops.** Most patients find the serotonin signs resolving on the broader protocol and the drug becomes occasional rather than nightly.

**Topical interventions reduce in frequency.** The 1986 patent Case 1 (78-year-old man with knee OA) "remained free of symptoms for eight months with continued treatment" — but the application became every-few-days rather than multiple times daily. OA patients on the topical gel often move to twice-weekly application by week 8–12.

**Drug taper conversation with rheumatology.** With three months of stable signs — morning stiffness under 30 minutes, normal pulse and temperature, no flare symptoms, declining CRP and ESR — the conversation about reducing methotrexate dose, extending biologic dosing intervals, or tapering off a chronic oral steroid becomes substantively grounded. The patient is not advocating drug cessation; they are presenting metabolic-state data and asking the rheumatologist to consider whether the drug dose remains necessary at current intensity. Most rheumatologists will engage with this conversation when the data is concrete (reproducible temperature/pulse trends, declining inflammatory markers, no flare events).

**Tracking weekly.** Morning stiffness duration, sleep quality, mood, joint count if RA, inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR, fibrinogen) every 4–6 weeks. The expected pattern: stiffness under 30 minutes by week 8; CRP halving by week 12; subjective energy and cognition improving before joint signs fully resolve.

## Phase 3: Maintenance (ongoing)

The protocol becomes background. Daily core continues. Emphasis layer adjusts seasonally and as life events introduce stressors.

**Daily aspirin + gelatin + diet + tracking.** The four non-negotiables. Aspirin at the maintenance dose (81 mg daily for most OA, 325 mg twice daily for RA in long-term remission). Gelatin daily at 10–15 g (the matrix support is permanent). Diet (no PUFA, milk and fruit, salt to taste, daily carrot) is the lifestyle background. Tracking compresses to monthly check-ins: morning temperature one day a week, stiffness on waking, sleep quality, pulse.

**Seasonal adjustment.** Winter often calls for higher progesterone (for women), more bone-broth gelatin, more sunlight (or red-light therapy) to maintain skin vitamin D production, and warmer clothing to avoid the cold-stress-induced flares. Summer often allows lower thyroid doses, less aspirin, more raw fruit displacing cooked carbohydrate.

**Stressor responses.** Surgery, illness, bereavement, major life events — the protocol re-intensifies temporarily. Progesterone scales toward the pregnancy-physiology range. Aspirin returns to twice-daily dosing. Cyproheptadine re-enters for two to four weeks. The acute flare response is the same as Phase 1 in compressed form.

**Drug taper completion.** If the rheumatology conversation in Phase 2 produced drug-reduction agreements, Phase 3 sees those completed. Methotrexate often comes off entirely within 6–12 months of stable metabolic-state signs. Biologic dosing intervals lengthen (every-other-week becomes monthly, then bi-monthly, then discontinuation) over 12–24 months. Oral steroid taper completes within 3–6 months of Phase 2 entry. Hydroxychloroquine for lupus is the slowest to taper — coordinate with rheumatology and watch ANA, complement levels, and clinical signs.

**Annual recalibration.** Once yearly, run a fresh diagnostic: full thyroid panel (TSH, free T4, free T3, reverse T3 for the conversion piece), 25(OH)D, magnesium, fasting glucose, lipid panel (read as a thyroid panel), inflammatory markers, and clinical reassessment of joint count and function. The protocol adjusts based on what the year produced.

## What to AVOID

**Long-term opioids.** Chronic opioid use for arthritis pain produces dependence, tolerance, constipation, hypothalamic suppression, and the iatrogenic cluster that overlaps with depression. Acute use for a specific flare (post-surgical, severe acute injury) is reasonable; chronic daily use is the wrong intervention for the chronic inflammatory state. The protocol's job is to reduce the pain at its source, not to manage the perception with neuro-active drugs that depress the metabolic state further.

**Long-term high-dose oral steroids.** Prednisone above 5 mg daily for more than a few months produces the iatrogenic Cushing syndrome Peat described in the 1986 newsletter: osteoporosis, immune suppression, skin atrophy, cataracts, glaucoma, hyperglycemia, mood derangement. The exception is the patient already on chronic steroids when the protocol starts — taper, do not stop abruptly. Adrenal suppression from chronic steroid use takes weeks to months to recover; abrupt cessation can precipitate an adrenal crisis. Coordinate the taper with rheumatology, support with pregnenolone and progesterone during the taper, and run the full protocol simultaneously.

**Biologics if the Peat protocol reduces need, but coordinate with rheumatologist.** TNF inhibitors (etanercept, infliximab, adalimumab), JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib), IL-6 inhibitors (tocilizumab), and B-cell depleters (rituximab) produce real symptom suppression at a real cost (recurrent infection, malignancy risk, latent tuberculosis reactivation). The protocol's framework treats these as downstream interventions on a cascade with upstream causes. Most patients on the full protocol find their flares becoming less frequent and less severe over six to twelve months; the biologic-dosing interval can extend, then taper, then discontinue, under rheumatology supervision. The mistake is unilateral discontinuation, which produces predictable flare and damages the trust that makes future protocol negotiation possible.

**Methotrexate folate-disruption without clinician balance.** Methotrexate is a folate antagonist; chronic use depletes folate, B12, and the methyl-donor pool. Standard rheumatology protocol includes folic acid 1 mg daily, but the literature varies on dose and timing. If a methotrexate-using patient runs this protocol, ensure folate supplementation is adequate (often 5 mg weekly is more reliable than 1 mg daily), monitor B12, and watch for the methotrexate-specific side effects (transaminitis, marrow suppression, pulmonary toxicity). The protocol's goal is to reduce the methotrexate dose over time, but the timing is a clinical decision.

**Statins, if avoidable.** RA patients are at elevated cardiovascular risk and are often started on statins prophylactically. The framework reads the elevated lipid panel as a downstream signal of hypothyroidism and PUFA-driven oxidation, not as a causal driver to be suppressed pharmacologically. The full statin discussion is on [[heart-disease-protocol]]; the arthritis-specific note is that statins deplete CoQ10 and worsen muscle pain, which is the symptom many arthritis patients are already trying to manage.

**Estrogen in any form for women with RA, lupus, or estrogen-driven OA.** Oral contraceptives, HRT, bioidentical estradiol creams, soy isoflavones, flax-seed phytoestrogens. The 1986 newsletter's two opening cases are the load-bearing clinical data: stopping the estrogen resolved the RA.

**SSRIs prescribed for "RA-associated depression."** The depression and anxiety in chronic arthritis are downstream of the same serotonin-cortisol-inflammation cascade driving the joint disease; raising serotonin further is the wrong direction. The protocol's approach to mood is on [[anxiety-depression-protocol]] — substrate, hormones, inflammation, antiserotonin — which addresses both the mood and the arthritis simultaneously.

## Tracking signals

Peat preferred clinical signals to laboratory markers. The protocol's tracking is structured around what changes early and what changes late.

**Morning stiffness duration.** The single most sensitive sign. Time the interval between waking and the joints feeling "operational." Untreated RA produces hours; effective treatment compresses this to minutes within weeks. OA stiffness is typically shorter at baseline but follows the same pattern. The metric is patient-reported but reproducible across days when the patient is engaged.

**Pulse and basal temperature.** Resting pulse on waking (target 75–90), oral temperature on waking (target ≈98.0°F) and mid-afternoon (target 98.6°F). The composite indicates thyroid status. Persistent low temperature despite "normal" TSH means under-treatment. Improvement in temperature and pulse typically precedes improvement in joint signs by two to four weeks; the metabolic-state move is upstream of the joint-state move.

**Sleep quality.** Patent Case 5 used "ability to sleep through the night without analgesic" as the binary outcome. Pain-driven waking, 4 a.m. anxious waking, and inability to fall asleep are the three pre-protocol patterns; all three resolve in characteristic order — falling asleep first, then sleep continuity, then absence of pain-driven waking — over four to eight weeks.

**Mood and cognition.** Recovery typically improves these *before* the joint signs change. The patient who reports "I'm thinking more clearly and feeling more capable" in week three but whose joint count hasn't moved is on the right track; the metabolic-state movement is happening and the joint movement will follow.

**Cold tolerance.** Warming of hands and feet on adequate thyroid replacement is the patient's most reliable subjective signal. Cold extremities at baseline that warm by week four or six is the clinical correlate of the temperature signal.

**Inflammatory marker drop.** CRP, ESR, fibrinogen. Useful but slow; morning stiffness and pulse-temperature signals lead them by weeks. Order every 4–6 weeks during Phase 1 and 2; less frequently in Phase 3.

**Joint count if RA.** DAS28 or equivalent if a rheumatologist is following the case. Useful objective marker but slower than morning stiffness as a tracking signal.

**Imaging if applicable.** MRI synovitis scoring, X-ray progression of joint-space narrowing, ultrasound power-Doppler activity. These are slow-changing and useful for the annual recalibration rather than month-to-month tracking.

The trap is judging the protocol by single-symptom improvement. Aspirin alone can reduce joint pain while the underlying state worsens. A full-protocol patient should feel different across multiple axes — energy, sleep, temperature, mood, digestion — within weeks. If only the pain has moved, the cascade has not been intercepted; it has been suppressed.

## Failure modes

**Incomplete PUFA elimination.** The single most common failure. The patient cuts seed oils at home but continues restaurant meals, salad dressings, baked goods, "heart-healthy" margarines, and fish oil supplements. The protocol stalls. The adipose half-life for PUFA is roughly four years; visible improvement requires near-total elimination, not partial reduction. Audit every fat source. Restaurant questioning ("what oil do you cook in?") is non-negotiable for the first six months. The PUFA load is what makes every other intervention work less well.

**Ignoring gut dysbiosis.** Particularly common in AS, psoriatic, and refractory-RA presentations. The patient runs the daily core but the gut [[endotoxin]] load continues feeding the inflammatory cascade. Daily carrot salad becomes twice-daily; cooked mushrooms enter the rotation; activated charcoal weekly between meals; minocycline trial if rheumatology coordinates; grain-and-bean elimination if the gut signs are pronounced. Without the gut piece, AS and psoriatic arthritis often plateau at partial response.

**Stopping aspirin abruptly mid-protocol.** A patient who reads about aspirin's bleeding risk and stops cold often experiences a flare within days. The rebound is real — aspirin's prostaglandin suppression is removed, vitamin K stores are still low from chronic use, and the inflammatory cascade re-amplifies on substrate the protocol has been holding under threshold. The taper rule: reduce by 50% for two weeks, then to 50% of that for two more weeks, then to maintenance dose or discontinuation. The K2 supplementation continues for at least four weeks after aspirin discontinuation.

**Thyroid initiation without magnesium loading.** Peat warned about this directly. Two patients in his correspondence developed chest pain on 15 mg of desiccated thyroid; the fix was not a smaller dose but a larger magnesium intake before starting. The protocol: load magnesium at 400–600 mg/day for one week before the first thyroid dose, continue at the same level during the titration, and recognize that transient cardiac symptoms during thyroid initiation usually mean magnesium-deficient tissue rather than thyroid intolerance.

**Estrogen supplementation continuing.** A woman on the protocol who continues oral contraceptives, HRT, or bioidentical estradiol cream is fighting the protocol from inside the body. The estrogen-RA-lupus-finger-OA link is direct; supplemental estrogen extends the disease that the rest of the protocol is trying to resolve. The two opening cases in the 1986 newsletter are the demonstration.

**Synthetic progestin substituted for progesterone.** Medroxyprogesterone, levonorgestrel, drospirenone — these are not progesterone. They antagonize natural progesterone's anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective actions and were the active arm of the WHI cardiovascular harm signal. If a prescription reads "Provera" or "MPA" or any synthetic progestin, the prescription is the wrong molecule. Progesterone is Progest-E, Prometrium (oral micronized), or a compounded bioidentical preparation.

**Insufficient dose for severity.** The protocol's dose ranges are wide because the disease severity is wide. A patient with mild OA may run on 81 mg aspirin and 10 g gelatin and resolve completely. A patient with active RA needs 325 mg aspirin twice daily, 20 g gelatin, full thyroid replacement, continuous progesterone, and possibly cyproheptadine. Starting at the bottom of the range for a severe presentation produces "the protocol doesn't work" before the protocol has been tried. Match the dose to the severity.

**Skipping the topical intervention for OA.** The 1986 patent's case series is the strongest single clinical signal in Peat's arthritis writing — same-day relief, durable response, disabled patients walking and sleeping. Patients with localized OA (knee, hip, hand, lumbar facet joint) who skip the topical gel and try to run the protocol on diet and oral interventions alone often miss the most direct intervention available. Compound the gel or buy the consumer-grade approximation. Apply consistently for the first four weeks; the response is usually visible within hours.

**Trying to do this without rheumatology coordination in severe RA/lupus.** The protocol works best when the rheumatologist is engaged. A patient who stops methotrexate unilaterally, drops a biologic without taper, or discontinues hydroxychloroquine without supervision generates a flare that confounds the protocol's signal and damages the relationship that future drug-taper conversations depend on. The protocol is additive in Phase 1 and 2; subtractive moves wait for Phase 3 and happen with the rheumatologist's agreement.

## When to escalate

The protocol coordinates with rheumatology and orthopedics; it does not replace them. Specific escalation criteria:

**Joint destruction progressing despite full protocol implementation.** A patient running the full protocol for six months with declining inflammatory markers but radiographic progression of joint destruction needs rheumatology re-engagement. The metabolic state may be moving but the local joint damage is outrunning the systemic improvement. Biologic intensification, joint-specific intervention (intra-articular steroid, hyaluronate, PRP), or surgical consultation enters the decision tree.

**Biologic-required cases.** Some patients have RA severe enough at presentation that biologic therapy is the right initial intervention regardless of metabolic state. The protocol runs alongside the biologic from day one rather than instead of it. The conversation about taper happens in Phase 2 or 3 once the protocol has demonstrably moved the substrate.

**Severe systemic involvement.** Lupus nephritis with rising creatinine, RA-associated interstitial lung disease, lupus cerebritis, vasculitis with end-organ ischemia, AS with cardiac involvement — these require immediate rheumatology and subspecialty intervention. The protocol can run in parallel but the acute care drives the immediate decisions.

**Acute joint event.** Septic arthritis (red, hot, swollen single joint with fever) is an emergency requiring joint aspiration, culture, and IV antibiotics. The protocol does not address acute infectious arthritis. Crystal arthritis (gout, pseudogout) overlaps the protocol's pathway (uric acid, kidney function, fructose handling) but acute episodes need specific intervention.

**Symptoms outside the rheumatology box.** A patient developing new neurological signs, unexplained weight loss, fever of unknown origin, or systemic symptoms that don't fit the rheumatology diagnosis needs broader workup. Don't assume everything maps to the arthritis frame.

**Drug-taper complications.** Adrenal insufficiency during steroid taper, methotrexate-withdrawal flare, biologic-discontinuation flare — these need rheumatology partnership. The protocol's framework is correct that long-term high-dose steroids and indefinite biologics are not the goal, but the taper is a clinical event with risks that need supervision.
