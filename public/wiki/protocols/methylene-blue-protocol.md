# Methylene Blue Protocol

The mechanism is at [[methylene-blue]]; the argument that a 19th-century thiazine dye can shuttle electrons across damaged Complex I and Complex III to keep a mitochondrion respiring, that the same molecule scavenges nitric oxide and reoxidizes lactate to pyruvate, and that its dose-response curve inverts above the low-micromolar range, is not re-walked here. What follows is the action layer: who takes what, in what amount, in what order, with which substances paired, and which patients must not touch the molecule under any circumstances.

This is a late-corpus Peat protocol. Methylene blue is not in the 1980s newsletters or in *From PMS to Menopause*. It enters his vocabulary around 2014, in the [[2014-11-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-nitric-oxide|*Ask the Herb Doctor* nitric oxide episode]] and the [[2014-12-19-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-you-are-what-you-eat-2014|December "You Are What You Eat" episode]], and stays through the [[generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] episodes 35, 36, and 41 (2020–2022). The full evolution timeline is at [[where-peat-evolved]]: the mechanistic frame (electron transport, redox cycling, cytochrome oxidase) was already in place — what changed is that Hani Atamna's 2008 cytochrome c oxidase induction paper and the TauRx Alzheimer's program gave Peat reason to engage with a molecule he had previously ignored. The protocol that follows is built from that late material and from Georgi Dinkov's elaborations in the *Generative Energy* episodes. Anyone reading "Peat recommended methylene blue" should know that he recommended it cautiously, late, in microgram-to-low-milligram doses, and with a specific dose ceiling that the contemporary biohacker market routinely violates.

## Absolute Contraindication — SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, and the Serotonergic Drug List

This section comes before the pharmacology because patients have died from skipping it. **Methylene blue is a potent monoamine oxidase inhibitor — specifically MAO-A — at any therapeutic dose, including the 1–4 mg sublingual range Peat referenced.** Combined with any drug that raises serotonin, the result is serotonin syndrome: autonomic instability, hyperthermia, neuromuscular hyperactivity (clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity), altered mental status, and a mortality rate high enough that the FDA issued a black-box warning in 2011 after multiple fatalities in surgical patients given IV methylene blue while on SSRIs. Onset is within hours of co-administration. The full clinical picture is at [[serotonin-syndrome]].

The hard rule: **do not take methylene blue if you are taking, or have recently taken, any of the following:**

- **SSRIs**: fluoxetine (Prozac), sertraline (Zoloft), paroxetine (Paxil), citalopram (Celexa), escitalopram (Lexapro), fluvoxamine (Luvox)
- **SNRIs**: venlafaxine (Effexor), duloxetine (Cymbalta), desvenlafaxine (Pristiq)
- **Tricyclics**: amitriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine, nortriptyline, desipramine
- **MAOIs**: phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, selegiline (above 10 mg)
- **Triptans**: sumatriptan and the rest of the migraine class
- **Opioids with serotonergic activity**: tramadol, meperidine (Demerol), fentanyl, methadone
- **Dextromethorphan** (cough syrups including Robitussin DM and Delsym)
- **St. John's wort**
- **MDMA and recreational serotonergic drugs**
- **Lithium** (relative contraindication; coordinate with prescriber)

Peat warned about this interaction repeatedly across the late corpus. He framed methylene blue as the cleaner pharmacology — useful as the antidepressant strategy *after* an SSRI has been tapered, not alongside it. **Minimum washout windows:** fluoxetine 5 weeks (norfluoxetine has a multi-week half-life), other SSRIs/SNRIs and MAOIs 2 weeks, triptans/tramadol/dextromethorphan 24 hours, St. John's wort 1–2 weeks.

Tapers must be coordinated with the prescriber — cold-turkey SSRI discontinuation produces its own syndrome. Many patients on long-term SSRIs are hypothyroid patients in unrecognized metabolic decline; the cleaner sequence is to begin [[thyroid]] support and the [[core-diet-protocol|core diet]] first, confirm the response over several weeks, *then* taper, observe the washout, *then* introduce methylene blue. The [[hypothyroid-recovery|hypothyroid recovery protocol]] is often sufficient without ever reaching for the dye.

## Pharmacology

The dye chemistry, redox cycle, mechanism, and history are at [[methylene-blue]]. The practical pharmacology relevant to dosing:

**Grade.** USP pharmaceutical-grade methylene blue is the only acceptable source. The methemoglobinemia injectable standard is the reference; a 1% aqueous solution (10 mg/mL) compounded by a pharmacy is the practical preparation. **Industrial-grade and aquarium-grade methylene blue is contaminated with mercury, arsenic, aluminum, lead, and other heavy metals** at concentrations acceptable for staining a fish tank and unacceptable for human ingestion. A 1 mg dose of USP is a different substance from a 1 mg dose of aquarium-grade, and most side effects wrongly attributed to "methylene blue" in the popular literature come from the contamination, not the molecule. Reputable consumer sources include MitoZen, Troscriptions (pharmacist-compounded sublingual troches), and a handful of compounding pharmacies that will fill a prescription on physician request. Verify the certificate of analysis. If a vendor cannot produce one, the product is not USP grade regardless of marketing claims.

**Dose ranges (oral or sublingual, USP grade):**

- **Microdose / "low dose":** 1–5 mg/day. The range Peat referenced most often. He referenced even smaller doses — 20–40 micrograms — as the optimal "analogous to thyroid hormone" window in the [[politics-and-science|2015 *Politics and Science* episode]], though commercial preparations below 1 mg are difficult to source.
- **Moderate / therapeutic:** 10–50 mg/day. The range of Naylor's 1987 depression trial (15 mg/day) and most contemporary chronic-fatigue and mild-cognitive protocols.
- **High:** 100–200 mg/day. The Ehrlich 1891 malaria dose and the upper edge of contemporary cancer-adjunct protocols. Requires clinician coordination.
- **Hospital / IV:** 1–2 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes for methemoglobinemia and vasoplegic shock. Not a self-administered range.

**Route.** Sublingual under the tongue or against the cheek is the preferred route — bypasses first-pass hepatic metabolism that demethylates oral methylene blue into less active metabolites. Hold the liquid for 60–90 seconds before swallowing. Plain oral capsules work but require ~2× the dose for equivalent systemic exposure. Transdermal is the route Peat used in his own self-experimentation; absorption is slow and steady but inconsistent across skin sites.

**Timing.** Morning dosing for most indications. The molecule has mild stimulant effects (raised oxygen consumption, raised heat production) that can interfere with sleep when taken in the evening. Twice-daily (morning + early afternoon) is acceptable for therapeutic ranges; avoid dosing within six hours of bed.

**With food or fasted.** Sublingual is route-independent of meals. Oral methylene blue is better absorbed with food. Some practitioners pair the dose with a small fat-containing meal (cheese, milk, ice cream) to slow absorption and reduce the brief peak.

**Staining.** Blue-green urine appears within 2–4 hours of an adequate dose and persists 24–48 hours after the dose is cleared. This is the reliable signal that the molecule reached systemic circulation. Saliva, tongue, and oral mucosa stain transiently with sublingual dosing. Sweat acquires a faint blue tint at higher doses. None of these are pathological. **If the urine is not blue after a few mg orally or 1–2 mg sublingually, absorption was poor and the dose probably did not produce systemic effects** — recheck the source (USP grade?), the route (sublingual held long enough?), and the timing (within the 2–4 hour window?).

## Phase 1: Conservative Microdose

Two to four weeks at the lowest dose that produces a verifiable systemic effect (blue urine). The purpose is tolerance assessment, not therapy. Most adverse responses to methylene blue surface in the first week.

**Dose:** 1 mg sublingual USP methylene blue, mornings, on or after breakfast. A 1% aqueous solution from a compounding pharmacy at 10 mg/mL means 0.1 mL — measured with a calibrated oral syringe, not a household dropper. Troscriptions makes a 4 mg lozenge that can be cut into quarters; Just Blue (BlueCannatine without the nicotine) is at 16 mg per troche and is too high for Phase 1.

**Duration:** 14–28 days. Some people respond clearly within the first week (lifted mood, sharper word retrieval, less afternoon fog). Others see no effect at 1 mg and need to move to Phase 2 to assess.

**Tracking signals:**

- Morning mood and energy — a journal entry within 30 minutes of waking
- Word retrieval, mental clarity, focus during the workday
- Resting pulse and oral temperature (morning) — the same metrics used for [[thyroid]]
- Urine color confirmation (should be blue-green by mid-morning)
- Sleep quality — if methylene blue interferes with sleep, move the dose earlier (waking only, or within an hour of waking) rather than dropping it
- Any unusual symptoms — headache, dizziness, mild nausea are common and usually self-limiting; clonus, hyperreflexia, autonomic instability are not (see escalation below)

**What Phase 1 is not.** It is not a therapeutic trial of methylene blue against a specific condition. Two weeks of 1 mg/day will not reverse early dementia or resolve chronic fatigue. The purpose is to confirm the molecule reaches systemic circulation, to surface any tolerance issue, and to give the patient experience with the staining and the mild stimulant effect before raising the dose.

**Pair with:** A baseline of [[core-diet-protocol|the core diet]] — adequate protein, sugar, salt, calcium; PUFA suppressed; orange juice and milk in rotation. The methylene-blue response is much sharper in a metabolically supported patient than in a fasted, undernourished, hypothyroid one. Many people are disappointed by methylene blue at 1 mg because they tried it on top of a low-carb diet, two-meal-a-day intermittent fasting, and a low-grade thyroid suppression — the substrate isn't there for the dye to support.

## Phase 2: Therapeutic Dose

The working dose range for the conditions where Peat referenced methylene blue most often: chronic fatigue and brain fog, mild cognitive impairment, mild-to-moderate depression, mitochondrial support during recovery from a viral or septic illness, and as the antibacterial adjunct in chronic infection.

**Dose:** 5–50 mg/day. Most patients land in the 10–25 mg range. Titration is slow:

- Weeks 1–2: 5 mg/day morning
- Weeks 3–4: 10 mg/day morning (or split 5 mg morning + 5 mg early afternoon)
- Weeks 5–8: 15–25 mg/day if symptoms continue to improve
- Maintenance: the lowest dose at which subjective and objective improvement is maintained

The Naylor 1987 trial used 15 mg/day for severe depression and is the best-documented therapeutic range. Above 30 mg orally the tongue stains visibly enough to be socially awkward; many patients find 15–25 mg sublingual the practical ceiling for daytime use.

**Indication patterns at Phase 2:**

- **Chronic fatigue and brain fog:** 10–25 mg/day with [[thyroid]] (5–10 mcg T3 twice daily titrated to morning temp 98.0–98.4°F, pulse 75–85). The methylene blue substitutes for the electron-carrier function of cytochrome oxidase the moment thyroid is restoring it. The two work additively. Add red light to the chest and head ([[red-light]]) 10–20 minutes daily.
- **Mild cognitive impairment / early dementia adjunct:** 5–15 mg/day with [[progesterone]] (20–40 mg evening for postmenopausal women, 5–10 mg most evenings for men), aspirin 81–325 mg with K2 after the largest meal, and the [[dementia-protocol|full dementia protocol]] stack. Methylene blue is one node in a multi-substance protocol at this stage, not a monotherapy.
- **Mild-to-moderate depression** (after SSRI washout): 10–20 mg/day morning. Often paired with [[niacinamide]] 50–100 mg three times daily, [[thyroid]] support, and dietary adequacy. The mechanism is at [[anxiety-depression-protocol]]; methylene blue is the addition once the substrate work is done.
- **Recovery from viral or septic illness:** 10–25 mg/day for 2–4 weeks. Mechanism is nitric-oxide synthase inhibition and electron-carrier rescue of mitochondria damaged by inflammatory cytokines. Dinkov referenced four COVID studies in the [[2015-10-16-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-current-trends-on-nitric-oxide|2015 nitric oxide trends episode]] showing methylene blue both prevented and treated COVID-19.
- **Antibacterial / chronic infection adjunct:** 25–50 mg/day short course (1–2 weeks) for the same indication Ehrlich and Guttmann used in 1891 — methylene blue is the original antimalarial and remains an antiseptic across a broad spectrum. Pair with the rest of the [[immune-protocol|immune support stack]].

**Tracking:** Same as Phase 1 — morning temperature and pulse, mood and energy journal, urine confirmation. Add cognitive testing if available (MoCA, simple word-list recall, computerized reaction-time tasks) for the cognitive indications. Re-baseline every 4 weeks.

**Pair with:** [[thyroid]] (the deeper intervention; methylene blue substitutes for what T3 normally enables), [[red-light]] (the photobiological partner — methylene blue absorbs red wavelengths and the combination drives cytochrome oxidase activity even when each alone is insufficient), [[niacinamide]] (the NAD+ precursor that supports the upstream half of the respiratory chain methylene blue patches downstream), [[vitamin-c|vitamin C]] (parallel electron carrier; the two are often paired in chronic-fatigue protocols), morning sun and warmth.

## Phase 3: High Dose

The acute-recovery and cancer-adjunct range. Clinician coordination is required at this dose. Self-administration above 50 mg/day is the boundary where the protocol exits the safe-at-home category.

**Dose:** 50–200 mg/day. The Ehrlich malaria dose was 100 mg five times daily for five days. Contemporary cancer-adjunct protocols use 50–200 mg/day divided into 2–4 doses. The Naylor depression trial did not extend above 15 mg/day, so the depression evidence does not support Phase 3 dosing.

**Indications at Phase 3:**

- **Acute cognitive recovery** in mid-stage dementia where the patient is non-verbal or severely impaired and the family is coordinating round-the-clock care under medical supervision: 4–10 mg sublingual twice daily as part of the full [[dementia-protocol]] stack (T3 micro-dosed hourly, red light to the scalp twice daily, aspirin 325 mg, progesterone 30–50 mg evening, pregnenolone 50–100 mg morning). This is the only intermittent Phase-3 use case Peat referenced; the dose is at the lower edge of "high" but the integration is full-stack.
- **Cancer adjunct, chemotherapy-coordinated:** 50–150 mg/day with the oncologist's knowledge, alongside chemotherapy and ideally pulsed red-light therapy for the photodynamic effect on superficial or accessible tumors. The mechanism is at [[warburg-effect]] — methylene blue reverses the fermentation shift by scavenging nitric oxide, reoxidizing lactate to pyruvate, and supporting Complex IV electron flow. Tumor tissue preferentially retains methylene blue because the reductive intracellular environment traps the dye; red light at 660 nm then drives the localized cytotoxic effect Pfizer holds patents on. This is not a monotherapy and not an alternative to oncologic care — it is an adjunct that addresses the metabolic substrate Warburg, Szent-Györgyi, Barnes, and Selye independently identified.
- **Severe mitochondrial dysfunction** (post-sepsis, post-cardiac-arrest cognitive decline, refractory chronic fatigue that failed Phase 2, mitochondrial myopathies): 50–100 mg/day under specialist supervision.
- **Acute methemoglobinemia and vasoplegic shock:** clinician-only IV at 1–2 mg/kg over 5 minutes. Not a self-administered indication. The hospital ICU is the right setting.

**Coordination requirements at Phase 3:**

- Physician aware of the dosing and prescribing the USP-grade product
- Confirmed SSRI/serotonergic-drug washout (the contraindication tightens at higher dose; serotonin syndrome can occur at 1 mg/kg in a sensitized patient)
- G6PD status checked (see contraindications below)
- Renal function checked (CrCl >30 mL/min; the molecule is renally cleared)
- Pregnancy excluded
- Baseline cognitive testing if the indication is cognitive
- Tumor markers, imaging, and oncology coordination if the indication is cancer

**Pair with:** The same companion-substance stack as Phase 2, scaled to indication. Cancer-adjunct adds red-light photodynamic sessions (specifically the 660 nm and 810 nm wavelengths) directly to accessible tumor sites with the oncologist's knowledge. Mid-stage dementia adds the full schedule of T3 micro-doses, red light to the scalp, aspirin, progesterone, pregnenolone, and frequent feeding from the [[dementia-protocol]].

## Indication Patterns — How to Choose a Phase

Three rules apply across all indications. **First**, substrate before pharmacology — methylene blue is the workaround for a damaged respiratory chain; the deeper fix is [[thyroid]], adequate sugar and protein, PUFA suppression, and the [[core-diet-protocol]]. A patient on a low-carb diet and a hypothyroid pulse will get less from methylene blue than from breakfast. **Second**, the SSRI rule is non-negotiable at every phase. **Third**, the dose-response curve is asymmetric — the protective window is on the low side, the pro-oxidant window is on the high side, and "more" is not "better" past the Phase-2 range.

## Synergies

Methylene blue is one node in a metabolic-restoration stack. **[[thyroid]]** is the deeper intervention — Peat was explicit in the [[2020-02-17-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-menopause-estrogen-thyroid-coronavirus-and-glaucoma|2020 One Radio Network interview]] that methylene blue is "a chemical substitute for the active thyroid hormone." Run thyroid first; add methylene blue as adjunct. **[[niacinamide]]** supplies the NAD+ that methylene blue uses as its electron donor; the two are upstream/downstream of each other. **[[red-light]]** and morning sun drive cytochrome oxidase in the same band methylene blue absorbs and are the basis of the photodynamic pairing in tumor and infected tissue. **[[vitamin-c]]** is the natural parallel electron carrier; both can deliver electrons past damaged Complex I/II. **[[progesterone]]** suppresses nitric oxide synthase in parallel and is additive in Warburg-shift tumor metabolism and neuro-inflammation. **[[aspirin]]** uncouples mitochondria mildly, blocks prostaglandin synthesis, and chelates iron — frequently in the same stack at [[dementia]] and chronic-inflammation indications. PUFA reduction ([[pufa]]) and adequate saturated fat are the substrate precondition for the dye working at all.

## Other Contraindications

The serotonergic-drug interaction is the dominant contraindication; these are the others:

**G6PD deficiency.** Affects ~400 million people worldwide, most common in Mediterranean, African, and Southeast Asian ancestry. Methylene blue in a G6PD-deficient patient triggers oxidative hemolysis because the reduction cycle requires NADPH that G6PD provides. Symptoms: dark urine (hemoglobin, not dye), back pain, jaundice, weakness. **Test G6PD status before Phase 2 or higher dosing.**

**Pregnancy.** Methylene blue crosses the placenta and has been associated with kernicterus, intestinal atresia, and fetal hemolysis. **Avoid at all doses during pregnancy.** Lactation: pump-and-dump after a dose or avoid.

**Renal failure.** Renally cleared; reduced CrCl concentrates the dose. CrCl <30 mL/min: avoid. CrCl 30–60: halve the dose and coordinate with the prescriber.

**Severe hepatic dysfunction**, **children under 6 months**, and **PKU** (some preparations contain phenylalanine-derived stabilizers) are additional cautions. Concurrent **ifosfamide** chemotherapy requires oncology coordination since methylene blue is also the treatment for ifosfamide encephalopathy.

## Tracking Signals

The minimum tracking set for any phase:

- **Morning oral temperature** — should rise to 98.0–98.4°F if thyroid is co-supported
- **Resting pulse** — should be 75–85 in an adequately metabolically supported patient
- **Mental clarity and energy** — a daily journal entry within 30 minutes of waking and at end of workday
- **Mood** — same journal; track depression-specific items if the indication is depression
- **Cognitive testing** — for cognitive indications: MoCA every 4–6 weeks, word-list recall, simple reaction-time tasks (free apps exist)
- **Urine color** — confirms absorption; absence of blue-green tint after an adequate dose means a sourcing or route problem
- **Sleep quality** — methylene blue can interfere with sleep; track and adjust timing
- **Oxygen saturation** — pulse oximeter reading; should remain 96%+. A drop suggests methemoglobinemia (the pulse oximeter reads falsely low at high methylene blue concentrations, but a real drop below 92% needs medical evaluation)
- **For cancer-adjunct use:** tumor markers and imaging per oncology schedule
- **For chronic-fatigue use:** baseline and 8-week follow-up on energy and capacity (the [[chronic-fatigue|chronic fatigue assessment]] applies here)

## Failure Modes

The protocol fails in five characteristic ways:

**Industrial-grade contamination.** A patient buys "methylene blue" from an aquarium-supply website at 5% concentration for $15, takes 4 mg/day, develops headache and metallic taste, and concludes the molecule is not for them. They were taking 4 mg of methylene blue plus an uncalibrated dose of mercury, arsenic, and aluminum. The fix is the same as the prevention: USP grade only, certificate of analysis verified.

**High dose without titration.** A patient reads a biohacker blog claiming 50 mg/day is the "real" therapeutic dose, takes 50 mg on day one with no prior exposure, develops severe headache, nausea, and the unsettling experience of bright blue saliva, and concludes the molecule is dangerous. They skipped Phase 1 and went directly to the high end of Phase 2. The fix is the schedule above — 1–5 mg for 2–4 weeks first.

**SSRI co-administration.** The mechanism of every methylene-blue-related ICU admission. The clinical picture (autonomic instability, hyperthermia, hyperreflexia, clonus, altered mental status) emerges within hours. Untreated mortality is high. The fix is the contraindication list above and a documented washout before any dose.

**Expecting overnight reversal of chronic conditions.** A patient with two decades of chronic fatigue, undiagnosed hypothyroidism, and an SSRI-treated depression starts 1 mg of methylene blue and expects the morning fatigue to lift in three days. It doesn't. They conclude methylene blue is hype. They were correct that methylene blue alone won't reverse the condition; the protocol is methylene blue *plus* thyroid *plus* dietary adequacy *plus* PUFA suppression *plus* light exposure, and the time frame is months, not days.

**Absorption failure.** Patient takes 5 mg of pharmaceutical-grade USP methylene blue orally, urine doesn't turn blue, no effect is felt. The molecule passed through the gut, was metabolized by first-pass demethylation, and did not reach systemic circulation. The fix is sublingual route, hold under tongue 60–90 seconds, lower starting dose, or compounded troche.

## When to Escalate to Medical Care

**Immediately** (ER, do not wait):

- Any neurological symptoms after a dose: clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity, severe agitation, hyperthermia (>101°F), altered mental status — these are the serotonin-syndrome picture and are life-threatening
- Dark urine separate from the dye color (red, brown, "cola-colored") with back pain or jaundice — hemolytic anemia, possible G6PD-deficiency reaction
- Severe abdominal pain
- Severe shortness of breath, oxygen saturation persistently below 92%
- Chest pain
- Severe allergic reaction (hives, swelling, throat closing)
- Confusion, hallucinations, or seizure

**Within 24–48 hours** (call physician):

- Persistent nausea or vomiting that prevents oral intake
- Headache that is not relieved by reducing the dose
- Skin photosensitivity reaction
- Dizziness on standing that does not resolve
- Pre-syncope or syncope
- Significant change in mental status that is not the expected improvement

**At next scheduled visit:**

- Mild ongoing headache or dizziness
- Sleep disturbance
- Mild metallic dysgeusia
- Lack of expected response after 4–8 weeks at Phase 2 dose

This protocol is documentation of a framework, not individual medical advice. The framework's claim is that mitochondrial dysfunction is upstream of much chronic disease and that methylene blue is one of the molecules that can bypass damaged respiratory machinery while the deeper substrate is restored. The implementation in any particular patient is a clinical decision that involves prescribing, monitoring, adjusting, and coordinating with other care. Anyone reaching for methylene blue on the strength of this page should also reach for a physician who will take the substrate work seriously and coordinate the pharmacology.
