# Hair Loss Protocol

This is the action layer for [[hair-loss]]. The condition page argues the case: hair loss is a downstream marker of [[thyroid]] failure, [[prolactin]] and [[cortisol]] elevation, [[estrogen]]-driven prostaglandin damage, and a follicle starved of oxidative energy. What follows is the work.

Sequencing matters. Doing Phase 3 (topicals) before Phase 1 (metabolic foundation) is the most common failure pattern — topicals into a hypothyroid scalp produce a months-long stall or an irritation-driven regression. Substrate first. Hormones second. Topicals third. Three to six months for slowed shed rate; twelve months for new growth in receptive zones; twenty-four months for a long-bald scalp to re-populate. The people who quit at month four are quitting the moment before the regenerative work begins.

Sex matters. Men with vertex-and-frontal recession and women with diffuse crown thinning share the same upstream physiology but need different Phase 4 proportions. Women get cyclic [[progesterone]]; men need progesterone plus [[dhea|DHEA]] plus [[aspirin]], none as monotherapy.

## Phase 1: Metabolic Foundation (Months 1–3)

Execute the full [[anti-stress-diet]] and, in parallel, the [[hypothyroid-recovery]] staging. From [[#80： World War 3 ｜ Heavy Metals ｜ Progesterone for Hair Loss ｜ T3 to T4 Thyroid Ratio with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* #80]]: "The hair follicle has to produce energy vigorously. If your systemic energy is being blocked, the skin is one of the first places to experience atrophy." No topical, peptide, or hormone compensates for a follicle that cannot respire.

The non-negotiable items for the hair-loss case:

1. **Daily morning oral temperature and resting pulse.** Target: morning over 98.0°F, afternoon 98.6°F+, resting pulse 75–85. See [[temperature-tracking]].
2. **Two quarts of milk daily, minimum.** Two quarts delivers ~2,000 mg of calcium, the PTH-suppression floor. From [[2019-07-18-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-milk|*One Radio Network*, July 2019]]: "High parathyroid hormone (PTH) is very destructive to the hair follicle. Lowering PTH with Vitamin D and calcium is probably the most protective thing for both heart disease and hair follicles."
3. **A quart of orange juice daily.** Anti-aromatase (naringenin, hesperidin), potassium, and the sugar that interrupts the cortisol-fat-mobilization loop driving stress-pattern shedding.
4. **PUFA elimination.** No seed oil. No fish oil. No daily nuts. The inflammatory prostaglandins driving follicular atrophy (see the [[hair|hair email]]: "prostaglandins are involved in atrophy of hair follicles") come from circulating and stored PUFA. Circulating free fatty acids drop within two weeks of strict avoidance; tissue stores take four years — see [[pufa-depletion]].
5. **Bedtime snack, mandatory.** A glass of milk with honey or a small bowl of ice cream before sleep, and a glass of orange juice on the bedside table for the overnight wake-up that may still happen in the first weeks.

If after three weeks the morning temperature is still under 97.8°F, add T3. The dosing for someone in active hair loss: **5 µg of T3 (Cytomel or Cynomel) two to three times per day with meals**, starting from a single 5 µg dose at breakfast and incrementing weekly until morning temperature reaches 98.0°F. Desiccated thyroid (Armour, Nature-Throid, NDT) at a quarter grain at breakfast and a quarter grain at lunch is the alternative. T4-only therapy is the worst choice — [[estrogen]]-impaired livers convert it poorly. The first visible hair response (slowed shed, thicker emerging shafts) appears six to ten weeks after the temperature normalizes.

Do not skip to Phase 3 until temperature and pulse have stabilized in the target range for two weeks.

## Phase 2: Anti-PUFA, Anti-Endotoxin, Anti-Estrogen (Months 1–6, Concurrent)

Runs in parallel with Phase 1 and continues through the entire protocol. The follicle is downstream of three loops to quiet independently: PUFA-prostaglandin, gut-endotoxin, estrogen-prolactin.

**Gelatin daily.** 15–25 g protein from a heaping tablespoon of powdered gelatin in a warm drink, or — preferable — a daily bowl of bone broth (oxtails, lamb shanks, chicken backs). The glycine offsets the cysteine and methionine load from muscle meat that would otherwise suppress thyroid.

**The daily raw carrot.** A medium raw carrot, peeled, with a little olive oil and vinegar or salt. The indigestible fiber binds [[endotoxin]] and excess estrogen in the gut. Alopecia areata specifically, per the [[hair|hair email]], is driven by "endotoxin and nitric oxide from bacterial overgrowth in the small intestine"; pattern and diffuse loss share the pathway at lower intensity.

**Vitamin E with the highest-PUFA meal.** 100–200 IU of mixed tocopherols at the meal with the highest residual PUFA. A holding action while tissue stores deplete. See [[pufa-depletion]].

**Coffee with iron-containing meals.** The polyphenols blunt iron absorption. From the [[2011-07-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-hair-loss-inflammation-osteoporosis|2011 *Ask the Herb Doctor*]]: "Iron attacks polyunsaturated fats and tends to increase serotonin."

**Shellfish weekly.** Four to six ounces of oysters, shrimp, or crab — the dietary copper that competes with iron, plus trace zinc and selenium. Get copper from food, not a copper-bisglycinate megadose.

**Liver once per week, four to six ounces.** Vitamin A, trace minerals, anti-prolactin. Avoid daily liver — past a threshold, vitamin A inhibits thyroid.

## Phase 3: Topical Work (Starting Month 3, Not Before)

Topical interventions delivered into a hypothyroid scalp produce frustration. Delivered into a scalp downstream of a normalizing metabolism, they accelerate the regrowth that Phase 1 has made possible. The order matters.

### Mechanical scalp stimulation — gentle, daily, anti-inflammatory

The mechanical lever exists because long-dormant follicles are quiescent, not dead. The published case in [[stem-cells-cell-culture-and-culture-issues-in-regeneration|*Stem Cells, Cell Culture, and Culture Issues in Regeneration*]] is the foundation: an elderly bald man fell into his fireplace and his burned scalp regrew hair as it healed.

The mechanical practice Peat endorsed is the *opposite* of microneedling. From [[#85： Protein Restriction ｜ Lidocaine for Hair Loss？ ｜ Brain Size, Intelligence & Symptom Recognition|*Generative Energy* #85]]: "Just a very gentle massage, such as gently rubbing oil into it. You don't want to cause inflammation... If you increase blood flow by irritation, that's because you're doing damage that causes lactic acid production. A very soft, stimulating, pleasure-giving massage is the opposite of a rough, irritating massage."

Actionable specifics:

- **Duration:** 5–10 minutes per session.
- **Frequency:** once daily, evening (parasympathetic window).
- **Pressure:** firm enough to move the scalp over the skull, gentle enough that the skin never reddens. The hand glides on oil, doesn't drag.
- **Technique:** fingertips (not nails), slow circular sweeps from front hairline to crown, temples to occiput. Move the galea aponeurotica over the skull. Don't scrub the skin.
- **Vehicle:** a small amount of olive or coconut oil, optionally carrying the topicals below.

Microneedling, derma-rollers, metal-point massagers, and hard-bristle brushes are **contraindicated**. Acute inflammation creates short-term hair-cycle perturbation that looks like regrowth at three months and collapses later. The fireplace anecdote was a single event followed by months of healing — not a repeated practice.

### Topical formula one: caffeine + aspirin + niacinamide

The daily workhorse, applied once or twice per day in alcohol-water solution.

**Recipe (~100 mL, one week's supply):**

- 4 plain uncoated aspirin tablets, 325 mg each (1.3 g acetylsalicylic acid)
- 4 caffeine tablets, 100–200 mg each, or equivalent instant coffee
- 1 g [[niacinamide]] powder (one #0 capsule emptied) — start without this and add at week three if tolerance is good
- 100 mL of 50/50 vodka and water

Crush the aspirin and caffeine, dissolve in the alcohol-water mix, decant the clear liquid into a glass dropper bottle. Apply 1–2 mL (half a dropper) to receding areas once or twice daily. Massage gently. Let dry. The faint vinegar smell of fresh aspirin is normal; a sharp vinegar smell means the batch has hydrolyzed and should be remade.

Supporting Peat: from the [[2011-07-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-hair-loss-inflammation-osteoporosis|2011 *Ask the Herb Doctor*]]: "Caffeine, which has some overlapping effects with thyroid hormone, is now being added to lotions to renew hair growth." From the [[hair|hair email]]: "topical aspirin and caffeine stimulate hair growth... since prostaglandins are involved in atrophy of hair follicles, inhibiting prostaglandins locally could help."

### Topical formula two: progesterone + DHEA in olive oil (Sarah Murray formula)

The hormonal topical — for women always, for men on the receding hairline only.

**Recipe (~30 mL):**

- 50 mg progesterone (from [[progest-e|Progest-E]], ~25 drops at 2 mg/drop)
- 25 mg DHEA (one 25 mg capsule emptied)
- 30 mL olive oil, warmed briefly to dissolve the DHEA, then cooled

From the [[2011-07-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-hair-loss-inflammation-osteoporosis|2011 *Ask the Herb Doctor*]] — Sarah Murray's formula: "a little olive oil, 25mg of DHEA, and 50mg of natural progesterone mixed together... Yes, those regenerate many skin functions, including hair growth."

Apply a half-dropper to the receding hairline or crown periphery, once daily, evening. Massage two to three minutes. Allow to absorb 30 minutes before lying down on a clean cotton pillowcase. For men, limit to 1–2 mL per application, only on the most receding areas, no more than once daily, rotated with the caffeine-aspirin solution rather than stacked. Transdermal DHEA over months risks peripheral aromatization to estrogen if thyroid status is poor — another reason Phase 1 precedes Phase 3.

### Topical formula three: copper, used sparingly

The most powerful pigment-restoration tool Peat described, and the one with the clearest cautions. From the [[hair|hair email]]: "applying a weak solution of copper just once would restore color immediately to eyebrows, or to about 10% of sideburn hairs... a slightly too strong solution could cause a mole to develop almost instantly, with an invasion of pigment cells."

The home recipe (see [[copper|copper email]]): soak a small piece of pure copper with aspirin in distilled water until a pale blue color appears. Test on a single eyebrow with one drop. Wait 48 hours. If no mole, irritation, or pigment cluster appears, a single drop may be applied to the receding hairline once per week — not more. Any pigment-cluster reaction: stop entirely. Most people in this protocol don't need topical copper; DHEA produces gentler pigment effects without the mole risk.

### Topical thyroid hormone — defer

Compounded T3 in DMSO-ethanol exists in the Peatian community but lacks safety-window characterization. From the [[hair|hair email]] on NDT: "Desiccated thyroid gland doesn't contain any free hormone... when digested releases the hormones" — NDT topically does not deliver active hormone. Synthetic T3 topically delivers it but with high, unpredictable systemic absorption. If oral T3 in Phase 1 worked, defer topical T3 pending practitioner supervision.

## Phase 4: Internal Hormonal Layering (Starting Month 3–6)

The systemic hormonal layer is what makes the topical work durable. The proportions split by sex.

### For women

[[Progesterone]] is the central tool. Cleanest delivery is [[progest-e|Progest-E]] taken on the gums or with food. Peat dose for hair loss in a cycling woman: **50–100 mg/day, cyclically** — luteal phase only (days 14–28), two weeks on, two weeks off. Menopausal women: the same cyclic pattern ignoring the menstrual reference.

From the [[progesterone|progesterone email]]: "I have seen some people start recovering immediately with just 10 mg of progesterone." Start at 10–20 mg/day; titrate up based on temperature, pulse, sleep, and shedding rate. The same email: "Since progesterone helps the thyroid to secrete, and helps the liver to regulate glucose and convert T4 to T3..." — adding progesterone may temporarily raise the apparent thyroid demand. Reduce the T3 dose by 25% if heart-pounding or restlessness appears in the first cycle.

DHEA orally, 5 mg with breakfast (taken with olive oil or butter), is the secondary tool. Larger doses risk masculinization — the [[dhea|dhea email]]: "DHEA and testosterone at those doses are likely to grow whiskers" (at 15 mg DHEA + 5 mg testosterone).

### For men

Progesterone-only therapy is rarely sufficient. From [[#80： World War 3 ｜ Heavy Metals ｜ Progesterone for Hair Loss ｜ T3 to T4 Thyroid Ratio with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* #80]] (Peat to Roddy): "Regarding hair loss, it has to go with general health, specifically the elimination of factors blocking the hair follicle. That includes good thyroid function, Vitamin D, and adequate calcium. Progesterone combined with Vitamin D, calcium, and thyroid can optimize natural resistance and restore hair growth." The system is the protocol.

The practical stack for an adult man:

- **Progesterone:** 10–20 mg/day from Progest-E on the gums, mid-day or evening. Five days on, two off (or three weeks on, one off) to avoid receptor desensitization. Stop entirely if libido drops.
- **DHEA:** 5–15 mg/day with breakfast and olive oil/butter. The [[dhea|email]]: "Ten milligrams of DHEA is pretty safe for men, the most common side effects are pimples, oily skin, and sex dreams. If your thyroid is very low, you should be cautious with the DHEA, because stress hormones can cause it to turn to estrogen."
- **Pregnenolone:** 25–100 mg/day, especially in the alopecia areata case where the [[hair|hair email]] specifies: "a pregnenolone supplement can improve response to thyroid supplements." Pregnenolone partitions to either progesterone or DHEA as needed — see [[5-alpha-dihydroprogesterone-5a-dhp|the 5a-DHP email]] on Peat's precursor preference.
- **Aspirin:** 325 mg with the largest meal, plus 1 mg vitamin K2 (MK-4) to offset prothrombin. Anti-prostaglandin, anti-aromatase, anti-prolactin — all three converge on the follicle. See [[aspirin]].
- **Vitamin D:** 5,000 IU/day, targeting 50–60 ng/mL serum 25-OH-D. The PTH-suppression couple with calcium from [[2019-07-18-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-milk|*One Radio Network*, July 2019]].
- **Vitamin A:** from diet (liver weekly, daily egg yolks, daily milk fat), roughly 10,000–20,000 IU/day equivalent. Retinyl palmitate at 5,000–10,000 IU/day when dietary intake falls short.
- **Niacinamide:** 100–500 mg with the two largest meals. The [[niacinamide|niacinamide email]] confirms standalone use.
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-4):** 1–5 mg/day with the aspirin and fat-containing meals.
- **Biotin:** dietary intake from eggs and liver is enough. High-dose biotin (5–10 mg) produces cosmetic nail/surface-hair effects without addressing the upstream variable and confounds thyroid assays.

### When stuck after six months

If month-six shows no shed-rate improvement, no temperature lift, no hairline pause:

1. Re-audit Phase 1. The most common failures are under-eating sugar and under-salting food.
2. Increase calcium to 3,000 mg/day from milk and cheese.
3. Test 25-OH vitamin D and bring it to 50–60 ng/mL.
4. Add 5 µg T3 in the late afternoon to address the 3 PM thyroid dip.
5. Consider altitude exposure. Peat in [[#80： World War 3 ｜ Heavy Metals ｜ Progesterone for Hair Loss ｜ T3 to T4 Thyroid Ratio with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* #80]] referenced Central Asian regrowth at 14,000 feet — a six-week mountain trip is not casual, but it is a lever Peat named.

## The DON'T list

**Finasteride / dutasteride.** Inhibit the enzyme that produces both DHT and allopregnanolone. The allopregnanolone loss is what creates post-finasteride syndrome — anxiety, depression, anhedonia, sexual dysfunction. From [[#27： Thyroid Function & Pulse Rate ｜ Weaponized Culture ｜ Finasteride as an ＂Insane Decision＂|*Generative Energy* #27]]: "It's an irrational decision and probably means you're already abjectly obedient to authority. It's a very powerful intervention in your steroid chemistry, especially affecting your brain." When finasteride does produce regrowth, it does so by deranging the steroid system; the price is paid in the brain, gut, and bile-acid system. Recovery from finasteride damage is harder than recovery from hair loss itself. Dutasteride is the same argument at higher intensity.

**Minoxidil.** From [[the-dark-side-of-stress-learned-helplessness|*The Dark Side of Stress*]]: "drugs that are intended to increase the effects of nitric oxide (asparagine, sildenafil/Viagra, minoxidil/Rogaine) and acetylcholine... should be avoided." Nitric-oxide vasodilation buys hair retention at the cost of activating one of the most destructive inflammatory signals in the body. The post-discontinuation shed is the visible dependency the drug created.

**Saw palmetto, beta-sitosterol, pygeum, pumpkin seed oil, stinging nettle.** All weak 5-alpha reductase inhibitors. Same mechanism as finasteride, lower dose, same direction.

**Spironolactone (in women).** Androgen-receptor blocker. The hair-protective action attributed to androgens (see [[hair-loss]] on DHT-as-bodyguard) is suppressed in proportion. Electrolyte and menstrual side effects are real.

**Topical ketoconazole.** Useful for seborrheic dermatitis. Not a hair-loss treatment.

**Microneedling, derma-rollers, scalp-injury devices.** See Phase 3 mechanical-stimulation. Acute injury produces lactic acid and prostaglandins — the very signals suppressing the follicle.

**Hair transplants.** The procedure doesn't address the upstream variable. A transplanted scalp in a hypothyroid, PUFA-loaded body produces a thin border of transplanted hair around a continuing pattern of loss. Address the upstream variable for one year first; many who would have booked the procedure at year zero do not at year two.

**JAK inhibitors (baricitinib, ritlecitinib).** Effective for alopecia areata specifically — pharmacologic immune suppression. The Peat-consistent reading is that the IFN-γ cascade these drugs block is the downstream expression of an endotoxin-and-stress signature. For severe, rapidly progressing alopecia areata, run the JAK inhibitor decision in parallel with this protocol, not as a substitute.

**Megadose biotin (5–10 mg).** Cosmetic surface effect only. Confounds thyroid blood tests.

## Timeline expectations

- **Weeks 1–3:** temperature and pulse rise. Sleep deepens. Cold extremities warm. No visible hair change.
- **Weeks 4–10:** shed rate declines (100+/day → 50–80/day). Emerging shafts at the part-line and temples thicken. No regrowth yet.
- **Months 3–6:** the first short, soft, lighter-pigmented hairs appear in the receptive zones — hairline corners in men, crown periphery in women. Vellus-to-terminal transitions. Don't pull on them; don't over-massage.
- **Months 6–12:** terminal hairs in the front and crown thicken and lengthen. Month-12 vs. month-0 photographs show the first unambiguous change.
- **Months 12–24:** dormant follicles in zones bald for 5+ years begin to push hair. Sideburns, temple recession, and the front hairline are most receptive. The vertex is least receptive — and is the zone in which finasteride regrowth is most often claimed.
- **Months 24+:** the protocol becomes maintenance. Stopping the diet returns the original pattern within 6–18 months.

The metric that matters: shed rate at four-week granularity. Photograph the part-line and hairline in identical lighting every four weeks. The change at month 12 is invisible at month 1 and obvious at month 12; the photographs are how you know.

## Counter-arguments

**Finasteride trial efficacy.** Propecia trials show real cosmetic effect — roughly 65% of men experience visible retention or modest regrowth at two years vs. 7% on placebo. The Peat reading is that finasteride produces a hair-cycle perturbation favoring the follicle short-term while the systemic price is paid in brain, sexual function, and bile-acid system. The cosmetic effect is real; the trade is poor. Post-finasteride syndrome is not contested in the literature — only its prevalence (manufacturer minimizes; patient registries report 5–20% depending on definition).

**Minoxidil's vasodilator mechanism.** Topical 5% minoxidil produces measurable terminal-hair conversion in a fraction of users. The Peat objection is to the means (chronic NO activation in scalp tissue), not the end (better circulation). The caffeine-aspirin topical produces a milder circulatory effect through a non-NO pathway.

**5-alpha reductase Type 2 deficiency.** Men with congenital absence of the enzyme do not develop pattern baldness. This is the cleanest natural experiment available for the DHT theory and is not fully resolved by Peat's framework, which argues (without strong empirical support) that these men's atypical steroid profile protects them globally.

**Spironolactone in women, finasteride in men** both reduce androgen signaling and both produce measurable hair retention. The DHT-as-bodyguard inversion in [[hair-loss]] is the most contested claim in the Peat framework and the most in need of rigorous testing.

**Twin studies and GWAS.** Identical-twin concordance is high; GWAS hits cluster around the androgen-receptor locus. The Peat reading is that the modern environment (PUFA-saturation, xenoestrogen exposure, chronic stress) expresses genetic susceptibility in a way the same genome would not in a different metabolic environment. This protocol is, in part, an environmental intervention against a genetic susceptibility.

**JAK inhibitors in alopecia areata.** Baricitinib and ritlecitinib produce dramatic regrowth by suppressing the IFN-γ cascade. The mechanism aligns with the Peat reading of alopecia areata as endotoxin-driven inflammation; the protocol above attacks the same upstream variables more slowly. For severe, rapidly progressing AA, the JAK inhibitor decision deserves serious consideration in parallel with this protocol.

**Topical melatonin trials.** Several small trials show retention effects. Mechanism is consistent with reduced follicular oxidative stress. A reasonable add-on for the determined experimenter after foundational phases are in place.

## Data gaps

- **Caffeine-aspirin topical concentration-response.** Peat described the principle but never published precise dosing. The formula above is Peatian community consensus, not Peat-published.
- **Topical T3 absorption and safety window.** Compounded T3 in DMSO-ethanol is in use but systemic absorption is uncharacterized. Defer pending practitioner supervision.
- **Male progesterone dosing for hair loss.** Peat in [[#80： World War 3 ｜ Heavy Metals ｜ Progesterone for Hair Loss ｜ T3 to T4 Thyroid Ratio with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* #80]] established that progesterone alone is insufficient and system-level intervention is required, but the precise male dose remains under-specified compared to the female protocol.
- **Four-year PUFA depletion timeline.** Plausible from adipose biopsy data; individual variation (initial load, ongoing intake, body composition, metabolic rate) is large.
- **Altitude exposure as a hair-loss intervention.** The Central Asian regrowth anecdote is uncontrolled. The specific hair-loss effect needs cleaner testing.
- **Mechanical-stimulation dose-response.** Peat endorsed gentle massage qualitatively. The 5–10-minute daily specification is consensus, not Peat-published.
- **Transdermal DHEA to estrogen conversion over 12+ months.** Oral 5–15 mg/day is characterized; topical olive-oil delivery to the scalp is not.
