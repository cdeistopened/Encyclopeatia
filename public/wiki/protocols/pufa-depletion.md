# PUFA Depletion

Most of the metabolic problems Ray Peat spent his career addressing are downstream of stored tissue polyunsaturated fat. The average adult walking around in 2026 has between two and four decades of accumulated seed oil sitting in their fat tissue, cell membranes, and mitochondrial inner walls. That fat does not just sit there inertly. It gets released slowly by normal adipose turnover, and it gets released suddenly during stress, fasting, illness, cold exposure, over-exercise, or weight loss. Every time it enters circulation it generates inflammatory prostaglandins, lipid peroxides, age pigment, and the [[randle-cycle]] block that keeps cells from burning glucose properly.

This is the hidden layer underneath almost every Peat protocol. Hypothyroidism, estrogen dominance, poor sleep, bad skin, gray hair, slow wound healing, autoimmunity, premature aging, stubborn body fat, the whole catalog of "just getting older" complaints: the thread that runs through them is stored [[linoleic-acid]] and its metabolites. The fix, the real fix, is slow elimination over a 4-year horizon, supported by substances that reduce the damage of the PUFA that is already there while the tissue pool turns over.

This is not a 90-day protocol. It is not a challenge. It is not something you do until you feel better. It is a decade-shaping commitment that changes what food means to you and how you move through the world. The people who actually execute it do not think of themselves as being on a diet. They think of themselves as people who no longer eat seed oil, the way a Catholic does not think of themselves as "on a meat-free diet" on Fridays in Lent. It is an identity shift.

Before you start, you should understand the mechanism ([[linoleic-acid]], [[fish-oil]], [[age-pigment]]), you should understand what you are replacing it with ([[coconut-oil]], [[anti-stress-diet]]), and you should understand that the first year is the hardest because it is the year in which you are doing all the discipline and getting almost none of the visible reward. The payoff is backloaded. That is the nature of the half-life.

## The 4-Year Horizon Explained

The reason the horizon is four years and not four months has to do with how adipose tissue actually turns over in a living human being.

Adult adipose tissue has a turnover half-life of roughly eight to ten years for the fat cells themselves, but the lipid content within those cells cycles faster. Tracer studies put the half-life of linoleic acid in human subcutaneous fat at about 600 days, which is to say that if you stop eating linoleic acid entirely today, in 600 days your stored linoleic acid will be half of what it is now. In another 600 days it will be a quarter. Four years gets you to roughly one sixteenth of your starting load, which is the neighborhood where tissue ratios begin to approach what human beings looked like before the industrial seed oil era.

The half-life of DHA in tissue is shorter, but the exposure continues from daily food unless you are rigorous about it, and once stored, the damage compounds. Cell membranes turn over faster than bulk adipose (weeks to months) but membranes are constantly rebuilt from the tissue pool, so the composition of your membranes tracks the composition of your fat stores with a lag. You cannot have clean membranes sitting on top of a dirty tissue reservoir.

Eliminating new PUFA is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. The stored PUFA continues to circulate for years, released every time you lose weight, skip a meal, get cold, get stressed, fight an infection, exercise hard, or sleep poorly. True improvement in the tissue saturated to PUFA ratio starts showing meaningful changes at 12 to 18 months. Full normalization, the kind where your plasma mead acid starts climbing (see [[mead-acid]]) and your arachidonic acid starts dropping, arrives at roughly four years.

If someone tells you they fixed their PUFA problem in six months, they did not fix their PUFA problem. They fixed their intake. That is step one of ten.

## Stage 1: Stop Adding PUFA (Day 1 Forever)

The non-negotiable foundation. There is no clever workaround, no "80/20" version, no "moderation is key" compromise that works here. Linoleic acid is cumulative. Every gram you add extends the timeline. Complete elimination means complete:

- All industrial seed oils: soybean, corn, canola (rapeseed), safflower, sunflower, cottonseed, rice bran, grapeseed, peanut, sesame as a cooking oil
- All products fried in seed oils: chips, crackers, tortilla chips, french fries, fried chicken, donuts, most fast food, most restaurant food
- All packaged foods listing "vegetable oil" in the ingredients (this is nearly the entire middle aisle of a grocery store)
- All salad dressings except homemade versions using olive oil, butter, or dairy
- All mayonnaise made with soybean or canola oil (which is almost all of it)
- All nuts and seeds as daily staples; small occasional amounts are fine but daily is a problem
- All "healthy fats" that are PUFA: flax, chia, hemp, walnuts, almonds, cashews, pecans, pistachios, pumpkin seeds, sunflower seeds
- All fish oil supplements without exception
- All cod liver oil
- All omega-3 supplements in any form, including algae-based versions sold to vegetarians
- All krill oil
- Most restaurant food, because you cannot see the cooking fat
- Chicken and pork from grain-fed animals, because the tissue PUFA of the animal reflects the PUFA of its feed
- Eggs from corn and soy fed chickens (most commercial eggs)
- Farmed fish (salmon, tilapia, trout, catfish), which are fed grain and concentrate PUFA in their flesh

The practical rules that follow from this list:

1. Read every label. If "vegetable oil," "canola oil," "soybean oil," "sunflower oil," or "rapeseed oil" appears, put it back.
2. Cook at home by default. Eating out is for occasions, not routines.
3. When you do eat out: plain grilled or roasted meats (ask about the cooking oil, accept that the answer will sometimes be a lie), plain rice, potatoes, fruit, coffee with cream, ice cream if it is made with real cream.
4. Build a travel kit: butter in a small sealed container, a jar of coconut oil, gelatin packets, salt, maybe a tin of cocoa powder. Hotels and airports are seed oil territory.
5. If you slip because someone served you something you could not refuse without insulting them, do not panic. One exposure is not catastrophic. Make it rare and recover with coconut oil and aspirin the next day.

The diet this leaves you with is smaller than the American supermarket would suggest but it is not small: milk, cheese, butter, cream, ice cream, eggs from pastured chickens, beef, lamb, gelatin, shellfish, white fish, tropical fruit, orange juice, potatoes, white rice, masa, honey, maple syrup, sugar, coffee, tea, salt, coconut oil, tallow, cocoa butter, dark chocolate made with cocoa butter and not vegetable oil, and most root vegetables. This is enough.

## Stage 2: Substitute with Saturated Fat (Day 1 Forever)

Elimination without replacement is a recipe for failure. If you just subtract PUFA, your body will be hungry for fat, your meals will feel incomplete, and you will cave within weeks. The move is to replace the slot that seed oil used to fill with fats that are metabolically clean.

- **[[coconut-oil]]** is the primary therapeutic fat. One to three tablespoons daily. It is roughly 90% saturated, the medium-chain triglycerides in it are oxidized preferentially in the mitochondria, and it actively supports thyroid function rather than suppressing it. Use it for sauteing, in coffee, stirred into oatmeal, melted over potatoes, in baked goods.
- **Butter** from grass-fed cows. K2, CLA, butyrate, and a fatty acid profile that human beings have been eating for thousands of years. Salted butter on vegetables, potatoes, bread, fruit. Unsalted for baking.
- **Beef tallow** for high-heat cooking, roasting, and frying. Stable, flavorful, cheap if you render it yourself from suet.
- **Cocoa butter** for dessert and flavor applications. It melts at body temperature which makes it useful for homemade chocolates and for adding richness to coffee.
- **Cream** in coffee, on fruit, on ice, whipped with sugar as dessert, stirred into sauces.
- **Ghee** for those who react to dairy proteins but tolerate pure butterfat.
- **Full-fat dairy** generally: whole milk, cheese, ice cream made with real cream and no vegetable oil.

Eating saturated fat does not "compete" with PUFA in a bad way. It displaces PUFA substrate in the same metabolic slots, it provides mitochondria with clean fuel, and it gives you enough satiety that you are not reaching for a handful of almonds at 3 pm out of hunger.

## Stage 3: Supporting Substances That Reduce PUFA Damage

Because stored PUFA continues circulating for years while the tissue pool turns over, a handful of supporting substances are worth running continuously during the depletion window. They do not speed up turnover, but they reduce the damage that the released PUFA does on its way through the bloodstream.

**[[aspirin]] (daily)** is the single most valuable intervention in the whole protocol outside of the diet itself. It blocks prostaglandin formation from arachidonic acid, which is the PUFA derivative that drives most of what we call "chronic inflammation." It inhibits aromatase. It reduces stress-related free fatty acid elevation, which means less PUFA getting liberated from storage in the first place. Peat described it as "another thing that reduces the stress-related increase of free fatty acids, stimulating metabolism." Typical dose for depletion support is 81 to 325 mg daily with food, with vitamin K on board to offset the clotting effect. The exact dose is calibrated to the individual and the tolerance of the stomach lining. See [[aspirin]] for the nuance around bleeding risk, Reye considerations, and buffered versus plain.

**[[vitamin-e]]** is the chain-breaking antioxidant that belongs in any PUFA-loaded person's regimen. Lipid peroxidation is a propagating radical cascade. Vitamin E stops the propagation by giving up a hydrogen to the radical, which is why a small dose of the natural form is more protective than megadoses of random antioxidants. The natural form is d-alpha-tocopherol, not dl-alpha, and ideally mixed tocopherols including gamma. 100 to 400 IU daily with food. More is not better and the synthetic form is worse than useless.

**Niacinamide** (not niacin, which causes flushing and lipid mobilization) at 50 to 500 mg daily. It slows lipolysis, which means stored PUFA stays in the fat cell instead of circulating. This is especially important during any weight loss phase, when fat mobilization would otherwise flood the blood with stored linoleic acid. Peat: "Niacinamide helps to lower free fatty acids." Start low and work up; 100 mg three times a day with meals is a common landing place.

**Vitamin K (K2 as MK-4)** at 1 to 5 mg daily. Supports mitochondrial function, directs calcium to bone rather than soft tissue, opposes some of the calcification that lipid peroxides drive, and works alongside aspirin by balancing the effect on clotting factors. See [[vitamin-k]].

**Thyroid support.** Active thyroid hormone (T3) increases the rate at which tissues oxidize fatty acids, including PUFA. A low-functioning thyroid allows PUFA to accumulate faster than it clears, which is one reason hypothyroid people seem to age faster and carry more inflammation than their euthyroid peers. Fixing the thyroid is its own protocol; see [[hypothyroid-recovery]]. Without adequate thyroid function, the depletion timeline stretches from four years to something much longer.

**Coconut oil at every meal.** Beyond being the replacement fat in Stage 2, coconut oil actively helps with PUFA clearance. Peat: "A small amount of coconut oil with each meal will increase the ability to oxidize fat, by momentarily stopping the antithyroid effect of the PUFA." The mechanism is partly that medium-chain triglycerides bypass the carnitine shuttle and get burned directly, and partly that they occupy the enzymatic machinery that PUFA would otherwise use to suppress thyroid signaling.

These substances together are not a replacement for the dietary change. They are supports for the long timeline. Taking them without cleaning up the diet is like taking aspirin for a headache while continuing to bang your head on the wall.

## Stage 4: Behavioral Supports for the Long Haul

The diet is half the protocol. The other half is how you live, because your lifestyle decides how fast the stored PUFA gets released into circulation.

- **Do not fast.** Fasting is one of the worst things a PUFA-loaded person can do, because it mobilizes stored fat and dumps it into the bloodstream where the PUFA generates lipid peroxides on the way to whatever tissue finally burns it. The trendy "autophagy" narrative ignores the fact that you are autophaging your way through a decade of seed oil. Three meals and a bedtime snack, every day.
- **Do not lose weight too fast.** Aggressive weight loss liberates stored PUFA faster than the body can clear it. Peat observed that obese people "age 10 years" when they drop 30 pounds in a hurry. One pound per week is a sane ceiling during the depletion window. If you need to drop more, see [[peatmaxxing-cut]] for a protocol that is compatible with depletion.
- **Eat carbs.** Carbohydrate intake suppresses lipolysis via insulin, which keeps stored PUFA where it belongs. Fruit, juice, potatoes, rice, honey, sugar in coffee and milk. The fear of sugar that dominates mainstream advice is precisely backward for a PUFA-loaded person.
- **Eat every two to three hours.** Same mechanism. Long gaps between meals allow stress hormones to rise and pull fatty acids out of storage.
- **Always have a bedtime snack.** Nocturnal lipolysis, driven by the drop in glucose during the long overnight fast, is one of the major pathways by which stored PUFA enters circulation. A small carb and protein snack before bed (milk and honey, ice cream, orange juice with gelatin) blunts this. The side effect is better sleep.
- **Salt liberally.** Sodium lowers the stress hormones (adrenaline, aldosterone) that drive lipolysis. Salt your food to taste and then some.
- **Avoid over-exercise.** Cortisol-driven fat mobilization is PUFA release. Walking is fine. Gentle lifting is fine. Long-distance endurance training during depletion is actively counterproductive because you are burning your dirtiest stored fuel under maximum stress conditions.

None of this is extreme. It is the same advice that most traditional cultures gave to people who were recovering from illness: rest, eat, do not skip meals, keep warm, take it easy.

## Weight Loss While PUFA-Depleting

The tension is obvious. If you are PUFA-loaded and you want to lose body fat, the fat you are mobilizing to burn is PUFA-loaded, and the stress of rapid loss compounds the toxicity of what is being released. Doing an aggressive cut on top of a depletion protocol is asking to feel worse before you feel better.

The solution is slow. Target one pound of loss per week, maximum. Run niacinamide at the upper end of the range (300 to 500 mg daily) to suppress lipolysis during the phase when you are actively in a deficit. Run aspirin daily. Run coconut oil at every meal to keep thyroid function supported under the caloric stress. Run vitamin E to mop up the lipid peroxides that will form anyway. Keep protein high, keep carbs adequate (do not go low-carb, which accelerates lipolysis), and keep the deficit small.

[[peatmaxxing-cut]] covers the full weight-loss protocol and is designed to be compatible with depletion. The short version is that you can lose fat on this framework but you should not be in a hurry about it. Four years to clean up the tissue pool, maybe two to reach your goal weight, running in parallel. Not competing.

## Signs Depletion Is Progressing

Because the timeline is long and the day-to-day feedback is minimal, it helps to have milestones to watch for. These are approximate and individual, but the pattern is consistent across people who actually stick to the protocol:

- **Month 3**: less afternoon fatigue after meals. The post-lunch energy crash that almost everyone treats as normal starts to disappear because insulin is working better and the Randle block is loosening.
- **Month 6**: better sleep quality. Deeper sleep, more vivid dreams, waking up less often in the middle of the night. Body temperature during sleep stabilizes.
- **Month 12**: skin texture changes. Fewer new "age spots" forming (see [[age-pigment]]). Wounds heal faster. The skin under the eyes looks less tired.
- **Month 18**: noticeable improvement in exercise recovery. Soreness clears faster, workouts do not wreck the following day.
- **Year 2**: body composition changes possible. Fat tends to redistribute before it decreases. Cold tolerance improves. Hair and nails grow faster.
- **Year 3**: many symptoms tied to chronic inflammation resolve or significantly improve. Joint stiffness, low-grade allergies, skin conditions.
- **Year 4**: the tissue ratio approaches normal. You are essentially a different metabolic animal from the one who started.

The lab marker that tracks real progress is the plasma fatty acid profile. Arachidonic acid should decrease and mead acid (20:3 n-9) should increase over time (see [[mead-acid]]). The appearance of mead acid is a good sign; it means the tissues are finally drawing down the omega-6 pool enough that endogenous synthesis of the n-9 pathway fatty acids is kicking in. This is what a human being looked like before the industrial food system.

## Common Failure Modes

- Expecting 90-day results and quitting when you do not get them.
- Taking fish oil "for omega-3 balance." Fish oil is more PUFA, not less, and the long-chain highly unsaturated omega-3s are if anything more prone to peroxidation than linoleic acid. This is the single worst thing in the Peat framework that gets marketed as a health food.
- Eating "organic" seed oils as if the source matters. Canola is canola. Organic canola is still a 25% linoleic acid oil that was invented in a laboratory in the 1970s. Organic soybean oil is still soybean oil.
- Eating "pasture-raised" chicken as if it solves the PUFA problem. It reduces the problem compared to conventional but does not eliminate it. Chicken fat is inherently high in linoleic acid relative to beef or lamb fat.
- Fasting during the depletion phase. Every fast floods the bloodstream with whatever you have in storage, and for most people that is decades of seed oil.
- Aggressive calorie deficits. Same mechanism as fasting, stretched out.
- Over-exercising, especially endurance cardio. Cortisol pulls fat out of storage; you are then burning it under maximum oxidative stress.
- Taking the supplements without fixing the diet. Aspirin and vitamin E cannot outrun a daily chipotle bowl cooked in soybean oil.
- Giving up in month three when dramatic results have not appeared. Month three is exactly when the discipline feels hardest and the payoff feels thinnest. This is also when most people quit.
- Taking "antioxidant" supplements other than vitamin E. Most of them are worse than useless and some (high-dose beta-carotene, high-dose vitamin C as ascorbic acid by itself, selenium without context) are actively harmful in the wrong dose.
- Eating nuts daily "for healthy fat." An ounce of almonds has seven grams of linoleic acid. Every day. That is a depletion-protocol killer hiding in a health food.
- Using flax oil as a "neutral" oil or for "plant omega-3s." Flax oil is roughly 55% alpha-linolenic acid and it oxidizes on contact with air. It is plausibly the single worst oil in the grocery store.

## The Psychological Commitment

This is the hardest Peat protocol to stick to because:

- Results are slow and non-obvious. You cannot feel your tissue linoleic acid dropping from 20% to 18%.
- The diet is socially awkward. Restaurants are a minefield. Travel is a puzzle. Dinner parties require either eating what is served or eating beforehand and pretending you are not hungry.
- Well-meaning people will tell you your diet is wrong. Your doctor will tell you to eat more fish. Your friends will tell you to eat more nuts and olive oil. The government food pyramid contradicts every element of this protocol.
- The mainstream narrative contradicts every element. This is not a paranoia. It is a reality. The scientific consensus on dietary fat has been wrong for 70 years and there is a lot of institutional inertia behind the error.
- Weight changes are slow and sometimes non-monotonic. You may go up before you come down. Your body is reorganizing itself.
- Energy and sleep improvements happen gradually. There is no dramatic "before and after" week.

The discipline this requires is less like a diet and more like a religious practice. The people who succeed at it are the people who stop framing it as a project with an end date and start framing it as a way of being. You are not doing PUFA depletion the way you might do a Whole30. You are becoming a person who does not eat seed oil. The question "how long are you doing this?" stops making sense, the same way "how long are you going to keep brushing your teeth?" does not make sense.

If that reframe feels like too much to ask, this protocol is not the right one for you yet. Come back when the reasons feel strong enough that the commitment feels like a relief rather than a sacrifice.
