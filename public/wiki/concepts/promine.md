# Promine

Promine is the name Albert Szent-Györgyi gave, in the 1950s and 1960s, to a hypothetical class of small molecules whose function in the cell is the opposite of [[retine|retine's]]: they donate electrons to the cell's protein-water lattice, push the lattice toward the reduced state, and license the proliferative, undifferentiated, dividing behavior that complex multicellular organisms normally restrain. Cancer, in this framework, is the state of a tissue in which retine has been lost or promine has been gained — or both. The lattice melts toward the dividing default. Mainstream cancer biology read this proposal as the late hobby of an old Nobel laureate and let it die with him in 1986. Peat, who spent half a century reading [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] more closely than any other twentieth-century scientist, treated the promine-retine framework as the most accurate available account of what cell division actually is, and used it to read mainstream medicine's "growth-supportive" pharmacology against itself. The article exists to explain what a promine is, why Szent-Györgyi proposed the class, who the candidate molecules are, and which of medicine's everyday molecules — estrogen, prolactin, serotonin, the unsaturated fats — behave, in Peat's reading, as promines.

The inversion is direct. Where the textbook story names "growth factors" as the engines of healthy development and "tumor suppressors" as the brakes, the [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]]–Peat framework reads the cell's basic state as already-dividing — proliferation is the *primordial* unicellular default — and reads the apparatus of complex multicellular life as a system of retine-class brakes built on top of that default. To stimulate growth is not to add an engine; it is to lift a brake. To accumulate promines is not to gain a useful nutritional cofactor; it is to fail to oxidize, fail to differentiate, and fail to restrain the primordial state. The same molecule that is sold as a hormone of reproductive vitality reads, in this lens, as a chemical license to revert.

## Szent-Györgyi's proposal: what a promine is

[[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]]'s framework grew out of his late "submolecular biology" — the claim that biology is not best understood at the level of molecules but at the level of the electrons that move between molecules, through the cooperative protein-water lattice of the cytoplasm. In that picture the cell's protein-water-ion system is a doped semiconductor whose macroscopic functions — contraction, secretion, conduction, division — are the electronic states the doping produces. Cell division, in particular, was supposed to be the state the lattice expresses when it has too many electrons; cell differentiation, the state it expresses when those electrons are pulled out by appropriate electron-acceptor (dicarbonyl) molecules. Retine was the name for the inhibitory class; *promine* (from *promovere*, to push forward) was the name for the stimulatory class.

Peat compressed the framework in [[100-years-of-cancer-metabolism|*100 Years of Cancer Metabolism*]] (July 2016): "In the 1950s and 1960s, Szent-Gyorgyi began talking openly about life processes in terms of a very precise balance between oxidation and reduction which kept systems of electrons in cells in a special free-radical-like state, oscillating between 'donor' and 'acceptor' molecules. He believed that a deficiency of the naturally occurring oxidative dicarbonyl molecule [[methylglyoxal]] might be a cause of cancer." Methylglyoxal — a small dicarbonyl produced as a side-stream of glycolysis — was the proposed candidate for retine. Promine remained underdetermined. Szent-Györgyi believed it existed because the framework required it: an electron-donating molecule (or class of molecules) whose accumulation would shift the lattice toward division as surely as methylglyoxal's withdrawal would. He extracted candidate fractions from rapidly dividing tissue — embryonic tissue, regenerating liver, tumor tissue — but never reduced the active fraction to a single named compound the way he had reduced his anti-cancer fraction to methylglyoxal.

This is the first thing to say honestly about promine: in Szent-Györgyi's own work it stayed at the level of an extracted fraction, not a single identified molecule. *Electronic Biology and Cancer* (1976) developed the framework as a balance, with retine on one side and "the various growth-promoting tendencies" on the other, and Szent-Györgyi was content to leave the donor side as a class rather than as a substance. The reason matters: the donors he was looking for were probably not one substance. They were a system. Anything that could donate electrons to the protein lattice in the right place, at the right time, in a cell whose redox state was already reduced — anything that could perturb the lattice toward division — would belong to the class.

The mechanistic prediction was specific. A promine-class molecule, when bound to a cellular protein, would push electrons *into* the backbone rather than pulling them out, lowering the protein's affinity for the structuring ions (K+, Mg++) that stabilize its differentiated configuration, melting the structured water that depends on the structured protein, releasing previously bound calcium and sodium, and shifting the cell into the swollen, electrically excited, division-permissive state. This is the same physical picture [[gilbert-ling|Ling]] developed in his Association-Induction Hypothesis under different vocabulary: the cardinal-adsorbant control of cell state, with electron-donor adsorbants destabilizing the resting configuration and electron-withdrawing adsorbants stabilizing it. Promine, on this account, is a cardinal de-adsorbant — a molecule that releases the cell from its bound, structured, differentiated state.

## The candidate-molecule problem

What is a promine? The honest answer is that no single molecule has been identified as the promine. Szent-Györgyi's program ended without one. Peat's reading does not name a unique molecule either — and the article should not pretend otherwise. What Peat *did* do, repeatedly, was identify a list of substances whose behavior matches the promine prediction in a way that mainstream physiology fails to acknowledge: substances that donate electrons (directly or through their metabolic descendants), that accumulate in rapidly dividing tissue, that interfere with [[oxidative-metabolism|oxidative metabolism]], that push cells from differentiation toward proliferation, and whose pharmacology — when read against the framework — looks like a license for the cell to revert to its unicellular default. The list is short and consequential.

### Estrogen as the prototype promine

The substance whose pharmacology most cleanly matches the promine prediction is [[estrogen]]. Peat documented its mechanisms across forty years of newsletters and across vault pages including [[estrogen|estrogen]], [[estrogen-and-cancer]], and [[aromatase]]. In summary form: estrogen interferes with mitochondrial respiration, increases free fatty acid release, stimulates [[nitric-oxide|nitric oxide]] and carbon-monoxide production (both of which "quickly and seriously interfere with mitochondrial respiration," as Peat wrote in *Estrogen and Brain Aging in Men and Women*), increases intracellular calcium, raises [[prolactin]] and [[cortisol]] (which further suppress [[thyroid]] and increase [[aromatase]] activity in a self-amplifying loop), and is mitogenic to almost every tissue it reaches. Cells in the cell cycle are estrogen-permissive; cells in the resting state are not. The clinical record from the Women's Health Initiative (2002) and its successors makes estrogen replacement therapy's cancer, stroke, and dementia signal robust — exactly the spectrum a promine would predict.

Peat made the framework move explicit in [[adaptive-substance-creative-regeneration|*Adaptive Substance, Creative Regeneration*]]: "Warburg and Szent-Gyorgyi, in thinking about cancer, emphasized that growth is the primordial function of all cells, and that the differentiated functions of complex organisms involve restraints of that primitive function, imposed by a system that has developed through time." Estrogen, in Peat's reading, is one of the molecules that lifts those restraints. It is sold by reproductive endocrinology as a hormone of female vitality. Read through the [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] lens, it is a chemical license for the cell to revert. The reproductive function of estrogen in the female cycle is the structured deployment of a small amount of a promine-class molecule for a specific dedifferentiating purpose — endometrial proliferation, mammary duct development — in a tissue whose oxidative competence the cycle's other hormones (progesterone, thyroid) immediately restore. The pathological state is the *unopposed* exposure of tissues to that license, year after year, in a body whose [[progesterone|progesterone]] and [[thyroid|thyroid]] have failed to oppose it.

### Polyunsaturated fats as the structural promine

The slow-moving, decisive promine in Peat's framework is the polyunsaturated fats — see [[pufa]] for the foundational chain. Linoleic and arachidonic acids, accumulated over years in cell membranes and stored adipose tissue, function as a long-half-life pool of electron-donating, oxidation-sensitive material whose mobilization under any stress floods the cell with material that interferes with mitochondrial respiration, generates inflammatory prostaglandins, drives lipid peroxidation, and supplies the carbonyl-fragment substrates ([[arachidonic-acid|arachidonic acid]] breakdown products, MDA) that Peat's *Generative Energy* #35 exchange with Danny Roddy traced to the so-called "glycation" damage of diabetes.

The PUFA-as-promine reading has direct experimental support. Peat documented in [[fats-and-degeneration|*Fats and Degeneration*]] (1996) that "polyunsaturated fats both initiate and promote cancer" — the standard rodent carcinogenesis literature finding which the seed-oil industry has spent fifty years explaining away. The mechanism is the framework's: PUFA-loaded cells are more reduced, more electrically excitable, more permissive of division, more dependent on glycolysis, more responsive to estrogen (because aromatase is upregulated and because PUFA breakdown products signal through the same inflammatory pathways estrogen activates), and slower to oxidize fuel. Every prediction the promine model would make for an accumulated electron-donor pool is what the PUFA literature reports.

The "essential fatty acid" doctrine that occupies the textbooks, in this reading, is the most successful single act of inversion in twentieth-century nutrition: the pharmacological promotion of a class of slow-acting promines as if they were vitamins. The 1929 Burr paper — see [[pufa|PUFA]] — was thin evidence; the seed-oil industry's post-1945 reframing of these compounds as "drugs that lower cholesterol" and then as "good fats" sealed the inversion. The framework reads the entire shift as the rebranding of a tissue-level dedifferentiating insult as a nutritional necessity.

### Serotonin, prolactin, and the inflammatory mediators

[[serotonin]] and [[prolactin]] are two further substances whose pharmacology fits the promine pattern. Both rise in stress; both promote proliferation in their target tissues; both interfere with [[oxidative-metabolism|oxidative metabolism]]; both are associated experimentally with tumor growth and metastasis. Peat made the cancer-promoting role of serotonin explicit in *Tryptophan, Serotonin, and Aging*: "Histamine and serotonin are other metabolic cancer promoters for which there are safe antagonists" (the antiserotonin drugs [[cyproheptadine]], lisuride, methysergide). Prolactin is documented in the [[prolactin]] vault page as an estrogen-dependent dedifferentiating hormone whose elevation is mechanistically linked to bone loss, immune suppression, and tumor promotion. Neither is structurally a dicarbonyl's opposite — neither is a simple electron donor — but both behave functionally as promines in the [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] sense: they license dedifferentiation and proliferation, oppose oxidation, and accumulate in the cells that have lost their oxidative competence.

The prostaglandins, which are produced by oxidizing the polyunsaturated fats, occupy a related position. "Since some of the prostaglandins suppress immunity, cause inflammation and promote cancer growth," Peat wrote in [[fats-and-degeneration|*Fats and Degeneration*]], "some people have divided them into the 'good prostaglandins' and the 'bad prostaglandins.'" Read as promines — small fragments of the PUFA pool, deployed locally, that license inflammation and proliferation — the prostaglandins are a downstream layer of the same electron-donating insult.

### The amino-acid candidates

[[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] and other electron-donor-acceptor theorists in the 1960s also looked at certain free amino acids — particularly the aromatic amino acids ([[tryptophan]], phenylalanine) and the sulfur-containing ones — as candidate donors. Tryptophan is the precursor of [[serotonin]] and of the neurotoxic metabolite kynurenine; both behave promine-fashion in stressed tissue. The mechanistic case is weaker than for estrogen or PUFA: a free amino acid in solution is not obviously a cardinal adsorbant. But Peat noted across the corpus that excess free tryptophan, particularly in the context of cortisol-driven [[proteolysis|protein breakdown]] and the lactate-flooded cell, behaves as a promoter rather than a structural unit. The amino-acid candidate-pool is the least developed branch of the promine question and is one of the article's data gaps.

## Peat's reframing: mainstream "growth support" as promine excess

The mechanism above is the Szent-Györgyi proposal. The reading Peat layered on top of it is what makes the framework politically consequential. In Peat's hands, the promine class is the lens through which to read most of twentieth-century pharmacology against itself.

A short list of the substances mainstream medicine markets, prescribes, or recommends as growth- or vitality-supporting that read, through the lens, as promines or promine-generators:

- **Estrogen replacement therapy** (oral, transdermal, implant). Marketed as bone-protective and cognition-preserving. Confirmed by the WHI as a cause of breast cancer, stroke, dementia, and clotting disorder — the predicted phenotype of chronic promine exposure in a tissue whose differentiating apparatus is failing with age.
- **The polyunsaturated "essential fatty acids"** (corn, soy, safflower, sunflower, canola; fish-oil supplements). Marketed as cardioprotective, anti-inflammatory, and brain-supportive. The animal-carcinogenesis, mitochondrial-suppression, and lipid-peroxidation literatures are consistent with their promine reading.
- **The selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)**, which raise extracellular serotonin. Marketed as antidepressants. Peat's reading: serotonin is a promine; raising it persistently is functionally pro-proliferative and anti-oxidative.
- **Growth hormone supplementation** in adults. Marketed as anti-aging. Growth hormone increases free fatty acid release (mobilizing the PUFA pool), decreases glucose oxidation, and is mechanistically linked to age-related cancer rates — a derived promine effect.
- **Estrogenic plant compounds** (soy isoflavones, "phytoestrogens") in the food supply. Marketed as natural alternatives to hormone therapy. Their estrogen-receptor binding makes them promine-class.
- **Some "anabolic" interventions in nutrition** (high-leucine supplementation, certain amino-acid mixtures). Less developed in Peat's corpus, but flagged across his discussions of aging muscle as candidates where the framework's careful reading would intervene.

The framework does not say that all growth is bad. Embryonic development *is* the deployed, structured, time-limited operation of the proliferative state. Wound healing is its local re-deployment. Endometrial cycling is its monthly re-deployment. The complaint is not against the existence of the promine class. It is against the chronic, unopposed, pharmacological deployment of promine-class molecules in adult tissues whose differentiation depends on their absence — and against the cultural reframing of those molecules as nutritional necessities or therapeutic interventions.

## The retine/promine balance as the unified model

The deepest move in the framework is to read cancer, aging, and the chronic degenerative diseases as one phenotype: the failure of the retine-promine balance toward the reduced, dividing, undifferentiated state. The lens reorganizes mainstream cardiology, oncology, endocrinology, and gerontology into a single picture in which the relevant intervention is to restore [[oxidative-metabolism|oxidative metabolism]] — the metabolic state that *makes* retines (methylglyoxal, [[carbon-dioxide|CO₂]], the dehydroascorbate form of [[vitamin-c|vitamin C]], the active dicarbonyl forms of the flavonoids) and *clears* promines (estrogen via the liver, PUFA via slow depletion, serotonin via MAO, prolactin via dopamine).

Peat made the consolidation explicit in [[100-years-of-cancer-metabolism|*100 Years of Cancer Metabolism*]]: "Aging itself involves a metabolic shift in the direction of cancer metabolism, with a relative inability to reduce energy expenditure in the basal, fasting state, and with increased fat oxidation, decreased glucose oxidation." Aging *is* the slow shift toward promine excess and retine depletion. Cancer is the local crystallization of that shift in a tissue whose differentiating restraints have collapsed completely. The clinical interventions Peat recommended — thyroid, sugar, CO₂, gelatin, saturated fats, low PUFA, low estrogen, low tryptophan, low serotonin, aspirin, progesterone — are uniformly retine-supporting and promine-clearing. They are the framework's pharmacology.

This is the unifying claim of Peat's late writing, and the reason the [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] framework matters. It produces one therapeutic program from one mechanistic picture. The picture's central object — the redox state of the cell's protein-water lattice — is not measurable by routine clinical biochemistry, but its surrogates (temperature, pulse, CO₂, [[lactic-acid|lactate]], the oxidized/reduced ratios of vitamin C and glutathione, the estrogen/progesterone ratio, the prolactin level, the [[thyroid|thyroid]] axis) are. The framework gives the practitioner a coherent map of what to push and what to pull.

## Counter-arguments

- **No molecule has been identified as the promine.** This is the load-bearing counter-argument. [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] himself never reduced the donor class to a named compound the way he reduced the acceptor class to [[methylglyoxal]]. The class remains a hypothesis with candidate members. Critics can reasonably say that a class whose members cannot be enumerated is not falsifiable in the form a strict experimentalist requires. Peat's reading — that the class is real but plural, with estrogen, PUFA, serotonin, prolactin, and the prostaglandins as functionally equivalent members — is plausible but is not the same as having extracted, sequenced, and characterized a single endogenous promine molecule.

- **The "growth factor" framework of mainstream cell biology is empirically successful.** Growth factors are real molecules with real receptors and reproducible mitogenic effects in defined tissue-culture systems. The framework that calls IGF-1, EGF, VEGF, and TGF-β "growth factors" has produced functioning oncology drugs (bevacizumab, cetuximab, trastuzumab) that bind these targets and slow tumor growth in the clinic. The [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] framework needs to absorb these molecules — perhaps as downstream consequences of the protein-lattice state — rather than dismiss them. Peat's writing engages this only intermittently.

- **Mainstream oncology has not adopted any promine-clearing strategy.** Despite five decades during which the [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] framework has been available, no major oncology center treats cancer as a redox-balance problem to be addressed by lowering estrogen, lowering serotonin, lowering PUFA, and raising thyroid + CO₂. The framework's clinical track record outside Peat's own clinical practice and a small community of bioenergetically informed practitioners is thin. Defenders can reasonably say the framework is suppressed; critics can reasonably say that absence of clinical traction over five decades is itself evidence against.

- **Some "promine" candidates have proven therapeutic value.** Fish oil reduces triglycerides and may reduce certain inflammatory endpoints in trials; SSRIs do treat clinical depression in some patients; estrogen does maintain bone mineral density. The framework's claim that these molecules are net-harmful requires it to dismiss or reinterpret the studies showing benefit. The reinterpretations are often plausible (the cholesterol-lowering effect of PUFA is real but is not the same as cardioprotection; SSRI antidepressant effects are real but the relevant target may not be serotonin itself) but they are not always conclusive.

- **The unicellular-default reading of cell behavior is contested.** [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] and Peat treat division as the primordial state and differentiation as the imposed restraint. Modern cell biology often inverts this — treating the differentiated state as the default that requires only the maintenance of normal physiology, and treating proliferation as a state that must be specifically induced. The two framings make different predictions about what "stress" does to a quiescent cell. The Szent-Györgyi picture says stress reverts the cell toward its primordial program; the mainstream picture says stress activates specific pro-proliferative signaling. The empirical content of the disagreement is more about emphasis than about facts, but the difference matters for therapeutic strategy.

- **The receptor model of estrogen action is empirically supported.** Estrogen receptors α and β are well characterized; their occupancy by estradiol drives transcription of specific genes; estrogen-receptor-negative tumors behave differently from estrogen-receptor-positive ones; tamoxifen and the aromatase inhibitors target this system therapeutically. The [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]]–Peat reading of estrogen as a promine-class electron donor is consistent with the receptor literature only in part. The framework needs the receptor effects to be downstream of the electron-perturbation effect, which is not where the molecular endocrinology literature has placed them.

- **The PUFA-as-promine reading is contested even within the bioenergetic community.** Some sympathetic readers accept Peat's claim that PUFA in excess is harmful but stop short of accepting that the EFAs are entirely non-essential or that they function structurally as promines. The more defensible reading is that physiological PUFA requirements are met at extremely low intake and that the modern diet's 10%+-of-calories intake is pharmacological rather than nutritional — but this is a quantitative dispute about a substance class that does play *some* essential role, not a categorical promine assignment.

- **The promine framework was never developed clinically by [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] himself.** *Electronic Biology and Cancer* (1976) is a theoretical synthesis, not a clinical playbook. The leap from framework to "lower estrogen, lower PUFA, raise thyroid" is Peat's. The leap is internally consistent with the framework, but [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]] did not himself prescribe it.

## Data gaps

- **No single endogenous molecule has been identified as the promine.** This is the central scientific gap. The class is defined functionally — "electron-donating to the cell's protein-water lattice, push toward division" — but no compound has been isolated from rapidly dividing tissue, characterized, and shown to produce the predicted dedifferentiating effect in a clean reductionist preparation. The class may turn out to be plural in a way that defies single-molecule identification, but the absence of a named promine is the framework's biggest unfinished business.

- **The boundary between physiological and pathological promine signaling has not been quantified.** Estrogen is necessary for the female cycle; PUFA at trace levels is plausibly essential; serotonin has gut-motility and platelet roles; prolactin has lactational function. The framework needs a careful account of *how much* of each, *in what tissue context*, marks the transition from physiological promine signaling (deployed in differentiation) to pathological promine excess (sustained dedifferentiation). Peat's writing operates at the qualitative level; the quantitative bridge has not been built.

- **The mechanistic chain from "electron donation to the protein lattice" to "transition into the cell cycle" has gaps.** The framework predicts that promine-class molecules shift the cell's redox state, melt structured water, release bound calcium, and activate the proliferative machinery. The molecular-biology literature on the cell cycle is well developed, but the mapping between the electron-flow language and the cyclin/CDK/p53/Rb language has not been done in detail. Reconciling the two pictures is an outstanding theoretical project.

- **The role of microbial promines is unaddressed.** The gut microbiota produce serotonin, tryptophan metabolites, lipopolysaccharide-derived signaling molecules, and others that fit the promine pattern. The framework's account of how endogenous promines integrate with microbially-derived ones is thin in Peat's writing, even though the gut-to-liver-to-systemic-circulation pathway is one Peat developed in detail elsewhere.

- **The amino-acid candidate-pool is underdeveloped.** [[tryptophan]] and the aromatic amino acids appear in some Szent-Györgyi-influenced literature as candidate donors. Whether free amino acids in solution can act as cardinal adsorbants on the protein-water lattice is unclear. The mechanistic case has not been built.

- **Pediatric and developmental contexts.** The framework treats embryonic and developmental growth as the structured deployment of the promine-permissive state. But pediatric cancers — which arise in cells with no plausible accumulated adult-life promine exposure — do not fit cleanly. Whether the framework explains pediatric malignancy as germline retine deficiency, as developmental dedifferentiation gone awry, or as something else is not worked out.

- **The dose-response curve for PUFA as a structural promine is missing.** Peat's writing distinguishes "low intake" from "high intake" but does not specify the dose at which PUFA crosses from essentially-trace-fatty-acid to functional promine. Practitioners working from the framework operate by elimination (avoid seed oils, lower-PUFA diet) rather than from a quantitative threshold.

- **The promine framework has not been integrated with modern receptor biology.** Modern cell biology has localized the action of estrogen, prolactin, serotonin, and the eicosanoids to specific receptor systems with defined downstream signaling cascades. The [[albert-szent-gyorgyi|Szent-Györgyi]]–Peat framework needs to specify where, in those cascades, the electron-perturbation acts: at the level of receptor binding, at the level of membrane structure, at the level of downstream kinase activity, or at the level of nuclear chromatin organization. The integration has not been performed.

- **Hungarian-language primary literature.** Szent-Györgyi's early work was published partly in Hungarian and German. Whether material in those sources elaborates the promine candidate-molecule question more concretely than the English-language Woods Hole books is not known.
