# Dreaming

Mainstream sleep science treats dreaming as a peculiarity of REM — the rapid-eye-movement stage where the cortex confabulates a narrative out of brainstem-generated noise. The dominant accounts since the 1970s (Hobson's activation-synthesis, later predictive-coding refinements, the memory-consolidation literature on hippocampal replay) all rest on the same picture: the dream is downstream of stochastic activation, the brain is doing maintenance, and the experiential content is the cortex's after-the-fact storytelling. The clinical implication runs in the same direction. Lost dream recall is treated as benign; nightmares are a psychiatric symptom; nothing about the metabolic-physiological state of the dreaming person is taken to be load-bearing for what the dream is or means.

Ray Peat inherited a completely different account from the post-Pavlovian Russian tradition. In Chapter 17 of [[book-mind-and-tissue|*Mind and Tissue*]] (1976) the dream is described as the lawful reversal of the day's focus-of-excitation into a focus-of-inhibition, surrounded by re-excitation that works over the material the waking focus had purposefully ignored. The dream is not noise; it is the *recruitment-of-image* — the assembly of a new dominant from the background material the daytime task had pushed aside. The 90-minute cycle that structures a night of sleep is the architecture in which this reversal happens four to six times. Each cycle is an opportunity for the day's residue to be re-evaluated against the dreamer's accumulated dominants — what Ukhtomskii called the centers of organized attention through which we know the world.

The framework's distinctive predictions follow directly. Dreaming is metabolically expensive — a high-energy *obraz*-building mode running without external sensory input — and it requires the same substrate that supports waking integration: adequate [[thyroid]], abundant [[glucose]] oxidation, [[progesterone]]-supported reticular activating system, structured cytoplasm. A brain that cannot afford waking integration cannot afford dreaming either. The chronic un-dreamer is, more often than not, the chronically hypothyroid person whose 90-minute cycles never reach the high-energy phase REM requires. Dream content is downstream of metabolism; read it that way. For the broader sleep frame, see [[sleep]]; for the consciousness substrate, see [[consciousness]]; this page covers what is unique to the dreaming phase itself.

## Pavlov's law of focus-reversal

The mechanism Peat builds the framework on is one of Pavlov's later observations, made in the course of the long conditioned-reflex experiments and not widely circulated in the West. From [[book-mind-and-tissue|*Mind and Tissue*]] (Ch. 17):

> We can think of the active part of the cycle as being a focus of excitation, as described by Pavlov, which is surrounded and defined by areas of inhibition. Discrimination is achieved by this process of selective suppression. The focus of excitation and discrimination exists in the brain as an actual center, analogous to the "centers" or "analyzers" for various abilities, which become more sharply refined in evolution.

Daytime attention, in this picture, is a focus of excitation maintained against a surrounding field of inhibition — the brain's way of holding one thing in awareness while refusing the rest. The pattern is the same one the [[inhibition]] entry treats from the side of selective inhibition. *Attention is mostly a pattern of refusals.* The center is sharp because the surround is suppressed. Discrimination — knowing this from that, this object from that ground — is achieved by the inhibitory field that defines the excitatory center.

Pavlov's contribution past this familiar picture was the observation that, *with prolonged discriminative learning*, the center reverses:

> With prolonged discriminative learning, Pavlov noticed that the point of excitation becomes fatigued, and shifts to inhibition — this can lead to a hypnotic state, or to normal sleep. This center of inhibition then, according to a law described by Pavlov, becomes surrounded by excitation in a reversal of the previous pattern. Since the discriminative inhibition was structured by the learning experience, the excitation which replaces it must have a similar structure.

The reversal is the framework's load-bearing claim. The same anatomical-functional center that was the focus of excitation during the day becomes the focus of inhibition during sleep. The same surrounding tissue that was inhibited during the day becomes excited. The pattern of the inhibition was *carved by the day's discrimination* — it has the structure of what was ignored — so the excitation that replaces it inherits that structure. The dream that results is not a random tour through the cortex; it is a tour through what waking attention had refused, taking the shape that refusal had cut.

This is the part of the framework most easily checked against subjective experience. The dream is rarely about the day's central task. It is about everything the central task pushed aside — the conversation half-heard, the face glimpsed in the crowd, the worry shoved down at the meeting. Pavlov's law of focus-reversal explains why: the inhibitory field that *was* the background during the day becomes the excitatory field at night, and that field has structure because the day's discrimination cut it.

## The 90-minute sleep cycle

The architecture in which the reversal happens is the 90-minute cycle. Peat treats this not as a curiosity of sleep physiology but as the temporal grain of normal nervous function — present in waking and in sleep both. From the same chapter:

> We normally have, throughout the day and the night, regular cycles of alternating dreaminess and "motor" activity. These cycles usually last about 90 minutes each.

The waking version is the familiar ultradian rhythm — the alertness wave that peaks and troughs across the day, the "second wind" that arrives roughly 90 minutes after fatigue, the natural rhythm of focused work giving way to looser, more associative thinking and back again. The sleep version is the four-to-six-cycle architecture: NREM descents into deeper slow-wave sleep, REM ascents back toward the surface, the period lengthening toward morning. The two are the same rhythm; sleep is not a separate state with its own clock but the nighttime expression of an alternation the body runs all day.

The reversal Pavlov described happens at each turn of the cycle. The daytime focus, having accumulated discrimination-load, shifts toward inhibition at its center; the surround re-excites in the inherited pattern; the dream phase organizes the recruited material; the cycle resolves; the next cycle begins from a slightly altered state because the integration just completed has updated the dominants. A full night is not one dream but four to six recruitment passes over the accumulated daytime residue, each starting from where the previous one left off.

The link to [[sleep]] is direct. The 90-minute cycle's high-energy REM ascents become available only when the metabolic substrate can afford them — when liver glycogen is adequate, when [[thyroid]] supports temperature and respiration, when [[progesterone]] is present at the reticular formation, when [[carbon-dioxide]] is buffering protein amines in the structured-cytoplasm phase. A hypothyroid, glycogen-depleted, PUFA-loaded brain runs the 90-minute cycle at a lower amplitude. The NREM goes too deep (toward torpor); the REM never quite rises; the recruitment-of-image misfires into fragmented anxious imagery or fails to register at all.

## Dream as recruitment-of-image and resolution of waking focus

Peat's most distinctive contribution to dream theory is the framing of dreaming as *recruitment*. From [[book-mind-and-tissue|*Mind and Tissue*]] (Ch. 17):

> This seems to explain the indirect way in which dreams relate to the preceding experiences. What was purposefully ignored is now attended to; awareness becomes expansive, and works over the background material, unifying it, strengthening the focus which was established in the preceding phase. Distant and seemingly trivial aspects of the background can now be re-evaluated, and put into a new structure, which may reveal the importance of certain images. Each image, of course, is itself a center of excitation, a "dominant," and as such exists in our memory as an organizing influence. Working from a certain strong center — the core of the dream — we approach and test other image centers for their relevance. Thus, the insight with which we began to dream may be small compared to the discovery that is established during dreaming. This is a kind of recruitment — of image, of dominant, of excitation, of information.

Three features of the dream phase are load-bearing. **Awareness becomes expansive** — the narrowing daytime discrimination requires is released, and the brain admits material the day's focus had excluded, not as noise but as content with potential structure. **The background gets unified** — the fragmentary residue of the day, the half-thoughts and glimpsed faces and unresolved tensions, is the surround the daytime focus inhibited, and in the reversal that surround is excited and worked over for the structure the inhibition had carved. **Recruitment-of-image** — the image, Peat's central organizing unit and the Soviet *obraz* treated at length in [[image-and-perception]], is itself a center of excitation, a dominant, an organizing influence in memory; dreaming recruits *new images* by working from a strong central one outward to other image-centers, testing them for relevance.

This makes the framework's account of dreaming a theory of *integration* rather than of consolidation. The contemporary memory-consolidation literature treats sleep as the brain filing the day's experiences into long-term storage. Peat's framework treats sleep as the brain integrating the day's residue into the organism's accumulated dominants — building new dominants, strengthening old ones, modifying the integral image of the world through which subsequent waking will perceive. The dream is not a transcript of consolidation; it is the recruitment in process, the *obraz*-building apparatus running on internal content rather than on perceptual input. REM is the orienting-reflex mode running internally, the waking *obraz*-builder operating on the day's unfinished material. Compare [[memory]]: memory in Peat's framework is the current state of the organism, the integral of every metabolic event that built it; dreaming is one of the operations by which the day's events become incorporated into that integral.

## Ukhtomskii's "dominant"

Peat's reading of Pavlov is filtered through Ukhtomskii, the Soviet brain physiologist whose concept of the *dominant* gives the framework much of its analytic power. The dominant is "a physiological way of expressing the idea that the organism works as a whole in organizing its own functions, and in organizing knowledge about the world" — a center of organized attention that holds together the perception of an object, a person, a problem, or a situation. Each image is a dominant; each strong attentional focus is a dominant; the integral image of the world a person carries is the assembly of all their active dominants.

The dominant is not a stored representation; it is an *operation* — a way the brain works as a whole around a center, integrating sensory input, memory, metabolic state, and the field of possibilities into a unified perceptual gesture. The waking person works from dominant to dominant; the depressed-helpless person is locked into a single foreclosed dominant; the creative person can hold loose dominants that admit re-organization. The cross-link to [[inhibition]] is direct: forming a dominant is the same operation as the selective inhibition that defines an excitatory center — they are the same process described from two sides.

Dreaming, in the framework, is the operation by which dominants get re-worked. The day's focus reverses; the surround excites; the recruitment finds new candidate dominants from the background; the integration tests them against the existing structure; the successful candidates are incorporated. The strong waking dominant carries more material into the dream phase, organizes more of the surround under itself, and emerges on waking with more material attached. The dream is not a presentation to an interior viewer; there is no homunculus watching it. There is a metabolism doing recruitment, and the recruitment *is* the dreaming.

## Dostoyevsky and Blake as proto-scientists of dreams

Peat's most distinctive editorial move in the dreaming chapter is the treatment of Dostoyevsky and Blake as proto-scientists of the dream-state, not as literary curiosities. Ukhtomskii himself took Dostoyevsky's novels as scientific data — Peat quotes him: "Long ago, since I was 19 or 20, I began to wonder wherein lay the essence of the problem that tormented Dostoyevsky." The "problem of the double," the central torment of Dostoyevsky's protagonists, is read by Ukhtomskii as a literary depiction of the failure to form dominants for other persons — the protagonist sees only his own faults projected outward, never gets to a dominant for the actual other, and the failure to integrate the surround prevents the building of new images. The novelist working close to his own dream-states had, in Peat's reading, mapped the same territory the physiologist would map decades later.

Blake gets the parallel treatment. Peat treats him as "the west's foremost psychologist" — the visionary who described several degrees of consciousness which Peat reads as "degrees of cortical isolation from or integration with the rest of the body — ways of being which admit energy and desire to varying degrees." Blake's "single vision" is the abstracted Newtonian consciousness that admits only weight, measure, and number; "twofold vision" is ordinary practical consciousness with colors experienced passively; *threefold vision* is dream vision, in which the intrinsic beauty of being is enjoyed as images are constructed; and *fourfold vision* is the integrated state in which "the person recognizes his own active perceptual process, and also sees this active perceptual 'humanness' as an objective property of the world."

The ascending sequence is precisely the framework's account of recruitment. Threefold vision is the dreaming-state — the recruitment-of-image, the construction of images from recruited material, with the intrinsic beauty of being available because the daytime narrowing has released. The defect of threefold vision is that *the dreamer does not recognize their own role in the construction* — the state evaporates as we try to participate actively in it. Fourfold vision is the integrated state in which the recruitment is recognized as the dreamer's own act, and the dream-vision is sustained into waking. *Dreaming into the world*, in Colin Wilson's phrase (cited at [[consciousness]]), is fourfold vision; it is the recruitment-of-image apparatus running with full metabolic support and applied to the world rather than only to internal content. Both writers, on Peat's reading, were doing what the framework calls science — close observation of the operation of their own *obraz*-building apparatus, recorded with sufficient precision that later physiology could recognize the territory.

## Practical protocols

A brain that cannot afford the high-energy REM ascents of the 90-minute cycle cannot do recruitment, and the dream-loss it produces is downstream of metabolic deficit. Restore the substrate and dreaming returns.

**Eat before bed for dream recall.** Liver glycogen runs the brain through the night; when it depletes, the body shifts to free-fatty-acid mobilization, [[adrenaline]] surges, and the high-energy REM phases collapse into fragmented anxious imagery or are lost entirely. Restoring nighttime liver glycogen — orange juice with a pinch of salt, warm [[milk]], a small bowl of ice cream, honey-and-salt water — returns the metabolic substrate the dream phase requires. Dreamers report vivid recall returning within nights of the bedtime carb-and-salt change; full protocol at [[eat-before-bed]] and the rescue-version at [[sleep]]. Dream recall as a tracking signal for whether the bedtime fuel was adequate.

**Progesterone for women with low dream recall.** [[Progesterone]] supports the reticular activating system that runs both the orienting reflex and REM. From [[consciousness-nootropics-and-progesterone|*Consciousness, Nootropics, and Progesterone*]] (January 2018): "When progesterone is given during sleep, it increases the amount of REM." The clinical pattern is consistent: women in the luteal phase report richer dream-life; postmenopausal women losing dream recall often recover it on progesterone; the perimenopausal pattern of fragmented dreams and 3 AM waking responds to the same intervention.

**Thyroid for the chronically un-dreaming.** Hypothyroid sleep is long, heavy, unrefreshing, and dream-poor — the 90-minute cycle running at amplitude too low for REM to engage. Restoring [[thyroid]] raises the temperature setpoint, refills liver glycogen capacity, and brings the REM phases back online. Patients commonly describe dream-recall returning before any other cognitive change is noticed; it is among the cleanest tracking signals for adequate thyroid replacement.

**Light and CO2 for the "buzzing half-sleep" state.** The deficient-inhibition state Peat described in *Mind and Tissue* — the buzzing confusion, the unsatisfying half-sleep, the inability to enter the upper-zone restorative inhibition — produces an analogous failure of dreaming. The dreamer never settles into clean REM cycles; the recruitment-of-image misfires; the imagery fragments into rumination rather than resolving into dominants. The route out is the metabolic chain that supports upper-zone inhibition: bright daytime light to suppress nighttime melatonin overproduction, warm evening light to support cytochrome oxidase, bag-breathing or breath-holding to raise arterial [[carbon-dioxide]] (which binds to neuronal protein amines and supports the structured-cytoplasm state). Full apparatus at [[inhibition]] and [[carbon-dioxide]].

The reading principle: vivid dream recall is a tracking signal, not a goal. Read it as a check on the metabolic substrate. Vivid recall with warm hands and feet, stable mood, and morning energy is one signal; vivid recall with the 3 AM wake, sweat, and an anxious morning is a different one.

## Counter-arguments

**Activation-synthesis hypothesis (Hobson, McCarley).** The dominant late-twentieth-century theory holds that REM dreams are the cortex's after-the-fact synthesis of stochastic activation from the pontine brainstem. The framework's reply is partial. The brainstem activation pattern (the PGO waves) is real; the dispute is whether the waves are random (Hobson) or organized by the dominants the day's experience laid down (Peat). The empirical test: dream content should be predictable from daytime attentional focus on Peat's account, random on Hobson's. The clinical literature on day-residue effects, noted since Freud, consistently favors the prediction-from-attention reading.

**Memory consolidation theories.** The contemporary consensus treats dreaming as the experiential correlate of memory consolidation — the brain filing the day's experiences into long-term storage. The framework's reply, building on the substrate-content critique developed at [[memory]]: there is no consolidation in the storage-and-retrieval sense the model assumes, because there is no storage. The recruitment-of-image account treats dreaming as the integration of the day's residue into the organism's accumulated dominants — a real and substantive operation, but one that does not involve writing files to synaptic addresses.

**"Dreams are random brain noise."** Crick and Mitchison's 1983 reverse-learning proposal — that REM serves to *unlearn* spurious associations — predicts dreams should be uniformly unsalient and forgettable, which fails as an account of the lived phenomenology. The recruitment-of-image account predicts dreams will frequently surface material that subsequent waking reflection finds salient, which is what most dreamers report.

**Freudian backlash.** Twentieth-century psychoanalytic dream theory collapsed under empirical scrutiny, and the cognitive-neuroscience community has largely repudiated it. The framework agrees with Freud that dreams have content with structure related to waking life; it disagrees with the specific mechanism (wish-fulfillment via censorship and disguise) and substitutes the Pavlov-Ukhtomskii recruitment-of-image account. The backlash against Freud threw out the genuine observation along with the specific failed mechanism.

## Data gaps

**The focus-reversal claim has not been operationalized.** The Pavlov-Ukhtomskii law that the daytime center of excitation reverses to a center of inhibition during sleep is, in principle, testable with modern imaging techniques. The prediction is that brain regions showing the highest activity during daytime focused attention should show reduced activity (relative to surrounding regions) during the dream phase that follows. Modern fMRI and PET work on REM has not been designed to test this.

**The 90-minute cycle's metabolic correlates have not been systematically mapped.** The framework predicts the ultradian rhythm corresponds to a measurable oscillation in cerebral metabolic rate, CO2 production, body temperature, and structured-water phase. The clinical literature on BRAC (Basic Rest-Activity Cycle, Kleitman's original framing) is suggestive but not developed into the metabolic detail the framework requires.

**The progesterone-REM dose-response curve.** The framework predicts a clean dose-response curve between physiological progesterone levels and REM-stage duration. The clinical literature is consistent in direction but has not been developed into the quantitative detail needed for clinical dosing.

**The chronic-un-dreamer-as-hypothyroid prediction.** The clinical observation that hypothyroid patients commonly report loss of dream recall is consistent with the framework but has not been systematically studied. Dream-recall frequency should correlate with thyroid status across a mixed-thyroid-status population, and recovery of dream recall should follow thyroid replacement on a similar time course to other cognitive recoveries.

**Recruitment-of-image as a measurable operation.** The claim that dreams work from a strong central image outward, testing other image-centers for relevance, is testable in principle through dream-report analysis paired with daytime attentional measurement. The operationalization is methodologically demanding and has not been done.
