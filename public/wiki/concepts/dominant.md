# The Dominant

The dominant is Aleksei [[ukhtomskii|*Ukhtomskii*]]'s central concept and the analytic backbone of Peat's psychology. Peat cited it across [[book-mind-and-tissue|*Mind and Tissue*]] more than he cited any Soviet figure except Pavlov, returned to it in [[book-generative-energy|*Generative Energy*]] and the 1995 [[adaptive-substance-creative-regeneration-mainstream-science-repression-and-creativity|*Adaptive Substance, Creative Regeneration*]] essay, and treated it as the level of organization at which behavior, learning, memory, dream content, and personality actually happen.

A *dominant* is a stable functional system that has captured the organism's metabolic and attentional resources and is redirecting all incoming stimuli — relevant and irrelevant alike — toward its own ends. The hungry rat searching a maze has formed a dominant for food. The infatuated adolescent has formed one for the beloved. The scientist who reads while traffic fades to nothing has formed one for the problem. The dominant is the physiology of focus — but much more than focus, because focus names only what the dominant looks like from the outside.

Peat needed the concept for a specific reason. His clinical framework joins a molecular account of energy metabolism ([[thyroid]], [[glucose]] oxidation, [[carbon-dioxide|CO2]], [[oxidative-metabolism]], [[gilbert-ling|structured cytoplasm]]) to a behavioral account of how patients feel and behave. The gap between micrograms of hormone and a life's character is what most metabolic-medicine frameworks never close. The dominant is the missing link — the level at which substrate becomes behavior. For Ukhtomskii the man (Orthodox training, Soviet survival, 1942 starvation death in the Leningrad siege, the lineage from Sechenov through Vedensky and Pavlov forward into [[anokhin|Anokhin]]'s functional-systems theory), see [[ukhtomskii]].

## Ukhtomskii's definition: the four properties

Ukhtomskii's 1911 founding observation was deceptively simple. A spinal frog given electrical stimulation of a motor-cortical area that would normally produce hindlimb flexion would instead — if the animal was already doing something else — *intensify that other behavior*. A defecating frog stimulated where its leg should have kicked defecated more vigorously. The stimulus had been *captured* by the ongoing functional system and rerouted into its support. The nervous system at any moment contains functional systems in elevated activity that do not coexist with other neural processes; they *recruit* them.

A dominant has four defining properties.

**1. Heightened excitability.** The system is already partially activated. Its cells sit in the high-readiness upper-zone state Peat treated at [[inhibition]] — primed to respond to weak stimulation that would not move a resting cell. The substrate has been pre-positioned.

**2. Inertia.** The dominant persists once formed. It continues for seconds, minutes, or hours depending on cellular state, and reasserts itself when a partial version of its initial stimulus returns — the cue, the smell, the half-glimpse, the throwaway phrase. This is the property memory works on: a recallable memory is a dominant reactivable by weak partial cues (see [[memory]]).

**3. Summation of incidental stimuli.** The deepest feature, and the one that distinguishes the dominant most sharply from any Western concept of attention. Stimuli with no intrinsic relevance feed into and *intensify* the active dominant. A hungry organism is more startled by noise. An anxious organism reads ambiguous social cues as threats. An obsessive ruminator finds confirmation of the rumination everywhere — the dominant is summating ordinary stimuli into itself, and the world genuinely does look that way from inside the dominant.

**4. Inhibition of competing systems.** A formed dominant suppresses other potential dominants. Its metabolic and structural priority drains resources from competing systems, and the inability of those competing systems to form coherent dominants of their own *is what attention feels like from the inside*. Forming a dominant is the same operation as the selective inhibition that defines an excitatory center — they are the same process described from two sides.

The dominant is not located in any single anatomical structure. It is a *configuration* spanning cortex, hypothalamus, autonomic centers, hormonal axes, and skeletal musculature — the organism-level unit Western neuroscience could not generate from synapse-and-circuit reductionism, the answer to *what is doing the integrating* when an organism behaves as one creature rather than a federation of reflexes.

## How the dominant differs from "attention"

The English vocabulary that comes closest names only the visible surface. Cognitive neuroscience treats attention as a frontal-parietal network's allocation of processing capacity — a cortical process that *selects* from a pre-existing perceptual field. The dominant inverts both moves.

It is *not* a cortical process. The cortical pattern is one symptom of an organism-wide state that also includes autonomic shift, postural set, metabolic readiness in target muscles, digestive and respiratory adjustments, hormonal recalibration. When the hungry rat's food dominant forms, salivary glands are recruited, stomach motility increases, heart rate adjusts, olfactory bulbs sensitize, skeletal musculature primes for locomotion. *All of this is the dominant.* Cortical attention is the part visible to brain imaging.

And it is *not selective* but *constructive*. Attention-as-spotlight implies a pre-existing perceptual field from which the system selects. The dominant *creates* the perceptual field appropriate to itself: the lover does not attend to the beloved's face out of an array of equally valid alternatives; the beloved's face *is* the perceptual world for as long as the dominant holds. Peat saw the Gestalt affinity explicitly: "Some forms of Gestalt theory are practically identical to Ukhtomskii's theory; Goldstein and Merleau-Ponty are in this group" (*Mind and Tissue*, Ch. 1).

A patient whose attentional capacity is degraded is not a patient with a localized attentional-circuit failure. He is a patient whose substrate can no longer support the formation of coherent dominants. The intervention follows: strengthening attention is not attention-training but restoring the metabolic conditions under which dominants stabilize.

## How the dominant differs from "focus"

*Focus* names something narrower — voluntary, conscious, effortful direction of attention. The dominant operates in a different register on three counts.

It is not primarily conscious. The dominant for hunger forms whether or not the rat decides to be hungry; the depressive dominant forms in spite of the patient's strenuous decision not to be depressed. Consciousness is a feature of some dominants — the ones that include cortical attention as part of their configuration — but the dominant itself is whole-organism and includes autonomic and visceral components no act of will reaches.

It operates across the unconscious. The dream phase of sleep is the rearrangement of the day's dominants under inhibited cortical attention (see [[dreaming]]). The hypnagogic state, the daydream, the sudden return of an unresolved problem in the shower — these are dominants reasserting themselves outside conscious control.

It operates across the autonomic system. A formed sexual dominant includes specific changes in pelvic blood flow, breathing, skin conductance, pupil dilation that no amount of conscious focus produces. The voluntary-conscious vocabulary of focus has no purchase on these levels. The dominant is the larger category; focus is one feature of certain dominants.

## Where dominants come from

Dominants are not designed. They emerge from three converging streams.

**Metabolic state determines which dominants are even available.** A replete cell in the upper zone of [[inhibition|protective inhibition]] — adequate [[thyroid]], [[glucose]] oxidation, structured cytoplasm, low [[free-fatty-acids|free fatty acids]], adequate [[carbon-dioxide|CO2]], the protective steroid pool intact — can participate in stable, complex dominants. A depleted cell in the lower zone (hypothyroid, glycogen-empty, PUFA-loaded, [[serotonin]]-elevated, [[stress]]-cascade dominated) cannot. The same person on adequate substrate sustains a research-problem dominant across hours; on depleted substrate the dominant collapses within minutes and is replaced by primitive defensive dominants — anxiety, escape, irritation, rumination.

**Chronic patterns set defaults.** A nervous system that has run a dominant repeatedly — the rumination loop, the alcohol-craving sequence, the morning-anxiety cascade — has lowered the threshold for that dominant to re-form. Vedensky's parabiotic-state mechanics (see [[inhibition]]) describe the cellular substrate: cells that repeatedly enter a pattern of activation acquire structural changes that make the pattern easier to re-enter. This is what people mean by *habit*.

**Cumulative experience shapes the repertoire.** Ukhtomskii's *integral image of the world* — the assembly of all the dominants a person can form — is the integral of their formative history. "Whatever an individual's dominants, such too is his integral image of the world; and whatever his integral image of the world — whatever his behavior — such is his happiness and misfortune." Dominants are not designable on the time-scale of a session. They are grown over weeks to years by changes to substrate and input. A session can only *set the conditions* under which new dominants form themselves.

## Pathological dominants

Some dominants are stable in ways the organism cannot easily reverse. Rumination is the textbook case — the depressed patient cannot stop thinking the thought; the same dominant re-forms across hours, days, and years; ordinary stimuli are summated into it; competing dominants cannot get traction. American psychiatry treats this as a chemical imbalance to be corrected pharmacologically. The Ukhtomskii frame treats it as a stable functional system that has captured the substrate and resists replacement because no alternative dominant of sufficient strength is available to compete with it.

Addiction shows the same structure. The dominant for the substance — its smell, sequence, entire approach-and-consumption pattern — captures every incidental stimulus into itself. The recovering addict who passes a familiar street finds the dominant reasserting itself in spite of his decision not to use. The decision operates on a level (conscious will) the dominant does not occupy. Depression's full form is a *narrowing* of available dominants; chronic anxiety is a dominant for threat that summates every incidental stimulus into evidence of threat. OCD, eating disorders, post-traumatic re-experiencing are pathological dominants of varying composition. The late-stage failure pattern Peat treated at [[learned-helplessness]] is the collapse of dominant-formation capacity itself.

The framework's most useful clinical move follows. *You cannot fix a pathological dominant by attacking it.* Suppression, argument, pharmacological silencing — these strengthen the dominant by recruiting it as the object of attention, which summates the suppression effort into the dominant itself. The patient who tries hardest not to think the thought thinks it most. What works is the *installation of a competing dominant of greater strength*. Therapy at its best supplies the conditions — relational presence (see [[effect-of-person]]), metabolic restoration, environmental enrichment, novel meaningful work — under which a competing dominant can form, capture some of the substrate the pathological dominant had been using, and progressively displace it.

## Healthy dominants

Peat's positive frame is consistently structured around the dominants a healthy organism can form: curiosity, learning-orientation, absorbed work, generative play, the dominant for another person who is genuinely seen. The healthy dominant is *open* (admitting new material rather than rejecting all input as confirmation or threat), *generative* (producing new dominants downstream rather than collapsing into a stable loop), of *appropriate duration* (released when the work is done), and *makes room for other dominants*. It draws on the upper-zone cellular high-readiness state that adequate thyroid, sugar, [[carbon-dioxide|CO2]], and the protective steroids support. A person whose substrate is intact forms healthy dominants spontaneously when the environment supplies appropriate input. The clinical task is rarely to design them directly; it is to restore the substrate and the input and let the dominants form themselves.

## How to shift a dominant

Three operations.

**Do not attack the pathological dominant directly.** Direct attack strengthens the dominant by feeding the suppression effort back into it through stimulus summation. The patient is recruited into a contest with the dominant in which the dominant has the metabolic advantage.

**Lower the dominant's substrate.** The pathological dominant runs on the same metabolic resources every other dominant runs on. Hypothyroidism, [[adrenaline]] elevation, glycogen depletion, [[serotonin]] elevation, PUFA loading, CO2 deficiency, [[stress]]-axis activation — these conditions select for the primitive defensive dominants. Restoring the substrate (adequate thyroid, salt-and-sugar feeding through the night via [[eat-before-bed]], [[progesterone]] for women, sun exposure, low PUFA) does not directly remove the pathological dominant but removes its preferential substrate access. It raises the floor under which alternative dominants can form.

**Install a competing dominant of greater strength.** The [[orienting-reflex]] is the gateway. Genuine novelty — a new environment, problem, person, physical activity, or domain of skill — provokes the orienting reflex, which is the precondition for new dominant formation. If the substrate supports the high-energy resting state, the orienting reflex resolves into a new dominant; if the substrate is degraded, it fires without resolving (the cognitive signature of chronic stress). Hence the importance of treating the substrate before expecting environmental enrichment to produce gains. The relational extension matters here: the presence of another person who can sustain a dominant for the patient — Ukhtomskii's *interlocutor*, treated at [[effect-of-person]] — supplies the conditions under which the patient's own dominant-formation capacity reorganizes.

## Practical protocols

The interventions are the same chain that runs through Peat's larger framework. Three emphases matter for the dominant specifically.

**Environmental enrichment.** The 1960s Berkeley experiments (Rosenzweig, Krech, Diamond) established that rats in varied environments versus isolated controls developed thicker cortices, greater cholinesterase activity, better learning, stronger stress resistance. The dominant frame explains why: enriched environments supply the input conditions under which dominant-formation capacity is exercised. Choice-rich daily life — varied work, conversation, movement, study, music — is treatment, in the bioenergetic sense, for the conditions that suppress dominant formation.

**Novel-experience exposure with substrate adequacy.** Novelty without substrate produces only orienting without consolidation — the brain that startles at every new stimulus but cannot incorporate any of them into a stable functional system. The combined protocol is novelty *plus* metabolic support: a new problem worked at over weeks while the patient eats well, sleeps long, supplements [[thyroid]] if needed, walks in daylight, keeps warm hands and feet.

**Sleep and dreaming as dominant-reorganization periods.** Sleep architecture is largely about dominant reorganization (see [[dreaming]]). Pavlov's law of focus-reversal — the daytime focus of excitation becoming the focus of inhibition during sleep, with the surround re-exciting — is the apparatus through which the day's dominants are tested, recombined, and either incorporated or released. Chronic insufficient sleep produces a brittle, repetitive dominant repertoire that cannot accommodate new input. The bedtime protocol — adequate fuel through the night, warm room, dark room, low cortisol load — supports both restorative upper-zone inhibition and the dominant-reorganization work sleep does.

For stuck dominants while metabolic work proceeds: cold-water face immersion (mobilizes the orienting reflex), [[bag-breathing]] to raise CO2, bright morning light, walking in varied environments, in-person conversation with a worthy interlocutor.

## Connection to Anokhin's functional systems

[[anokhin|Pyotr Anokhin]] (1898–1974), Ukhtomskii's intellectual heir, extended dominant theory into *functional systems theory*. An Anokhinian functional system is essentially Ukhtomskii's dominant equipped with two additional components: an *afferent synthesis* (integration of motivational state, environmental input, memory, and trigger stimulus into initial activation) and an *acceptor of action* (an anticipatory image of the expected result against which feedback is compared, with mismatches initiating correction or system collapse).

Peat cited Anokhin frequently — especially the framing of the orienting reflex as having four properties (central integration, exclusion, adaptational effect, feedback). The acceptor-of-action concept is the closest Soviet equivalent to what Western cognitive science has developed under predictive-coding and active-inference frameworks — but built outward from the dominant's metabolic substrate rather than from information-theoretic priors. Peat treated Ukhtomskii and Anokhin as one cumulative tradition, with Anokhin carrying the dominant concept forward into Bernstein's movement theory, Luria's frontal-lobe work, Sokolov's orienting-reflex research, and Leontyev's activity theory.

## Why this matters for the Peat framework

Peat's clinical writing repeatedly makes a move that without the dominant concept reads as a non-sequitur. He describes a metabolic intervention — adequate thyroid, sugar, salt, progesterone, sleep, sun, low PUFA, supportive social contact — and then predicts changes in attention, mood, dream content, capacity for love, relationship to work, quality of presence. *How can sugar restore the capacity for love?* The frame appears to skip the mechanism.

The dominant supplies the mechanism. Sugar (and the rest of the substrate chain) supplies the metabolic conditions under which the cellular high-readiness state of [[inhibition]] becomes accessible. That state is the precondition for any dominant of complexity beyond the primitive defensive ones. A patient whose substrate has been restored can form the kind of complex, sustained, relational, generative dominants that mainstream psychiatry treats as personality traits or developmental achievements.

The clinical signature — warm hands and feet, temperature returning to 98.6°F, pulse rising into the 80s at rest, morning dreams returning, sustained work re-emerging, relational presence becoming available — tracks both the substrate variables and the dominant-formation capacity simultaneously because they are the same chain measured from two ends. The substrate determines which dominants the organism can form; the dominants the organism forms are what the organism is.

## Counter-arguments

**Attention-as-circuit-process.** The dominant has no obvious mapping onto the dorsal- and ventral-attention-network architecture (Corbetta and Shulman), the global-neuronal-workspace theory (Dehaene), or the cholinergic-noradrenergic-dopaminergic vocabulary of attention pharmacology. The Ukhtomskii-Peat reading does not contradict the circuit findings but treats them as one downstream description of an organism-wide state. Whether the circuit account is *sufficient* (as mainstream neuroscience assumes) or only *partial* (as the dominant framework implies) cannot be decided by current data.

**"The dominant is mystical."** The dominant's holistic character, combined with Ukhtomskii's Orthodox orientation and the *interlocutor* concept's spiritual coloration, has made it readable to some Western philosophers as a residue of pre-scientific organismic vitalism. The defense, which Peat adopted implicitly, is that the empirical core — the four properties, the cellular substrate, the relationship to inhibition and the orienting reflex — is independent of the spiritual extensions. The Orthodox monk and the empirical physiologist were the same person; the work survives the separation.

**Computational-neuroscience reductionism.** The contemporary alternative is to formalize attention, motivation, and behavior in Bayesian-inference, predictive-coding, active-inference, or value-function vocabulary. The affinities are obvious — a dominant is something like a high-prior generative model that captures incoming evidence and biases action selection — but no serious translation has been done. Whether the dominant could be reconstructed in active-inference terms while retaining its distinctive bioenergetic component is an open question.

## Data gaps

**English-language scholarship is thin.** A handful of cross-cultural psychology articles, scattered chapter-references, almost nothing in clinical or empirical neuroscience. The Russian primary literature — Ukhtomskii's *Collected Works*, the 1923 *Dominanta* monograph, the 1950 consolidation, Anokhin's elaborations — is largely inaccessible to non-Russian-reading scholars.

**Clinical operationalization is weak.** The framework predicts that substrate restoration should improve dominant-formation capacity, that chronic stress should narrow the available repertoire, and that specific metabolic interventions should shift dominant inertia in measurable ways. The empirical work has not been done; the dominant has not been formalized in a clinical-research instrument.

**The dominant-displacement therapeutic principle has not been clinically tested.** The framework predicts that interventions designed to *install competing dominants* (environmental enrichment, novelty exposure, relational presence, metabolic restoration) outperform interventions designed to *suppress the pathological dominant* (cognitive-behavioral suppression, pharmacological silencing) in long-term outcomes for ruminative depression, addiction, OCD, and chronic anxiety. Case-series data are consistent with this; the head-to-head trial has not been run.

**The neuroimaging signature has not been mapped.** The framework predicts a coherent pattern of activation and inhibition spanning cortex, hypothalamus, autonomic centers, and brainstem during a stable dominant — measurable in principle with simultaneous fMRI, autonomic recording, and behavioral assessment. The relevant literatures exist; they have not been organized around the dominant's four-property claim.

**The Anokhin extension has not been systematically related back.** Which features Anokhin retained from Ukhtomskii's original framing, which he modified, what was lost in the elaboration — the Soviet primary literature exists; the English scholarly synthesis has not been done.
