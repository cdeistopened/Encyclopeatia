# Temperature & Pulse Tracking

**Stub.** Queued for full expansion in the `practices/` wave. See [[temperature-tracking|protocol-side write-up]] for the current canonical reference while this page is thin.

## What it is

Peat's primary measurement framework for metabolic status — a Broda Barnes inheritance, applied as daily biofeedback rather than lab-confirmed diagnosis.

- **Morning waking temperature:** taken before getting out of bed, axillary or oral. Target: ~97.8–98.2°F on rising, climbing to 98.6°F by mid-morning after breakfast.
- **Resting pulse rate:** taken sitting, after a few minutes of stillness. Target: 75–90 bpm at rest in a healthy adult; higher (85–95) acceptable in pregnant or post-cold-thyroid recovery.
- **Mid-afternoon re-check:** the diagnostic complement — if temperature drops after lunch instead of rising, that's the stress-substrate gap (low glycogen, adrenaline-driven thermogenesis collapsing).

## Why Peat used it

Lab thyroid panels (TSH, free T3, free T4) routinely lie because of pulsatility, diurnal rhythm, reverse-T3 conversion, and the AM-draw artifact. See [[hypothyroidism]] § "Why TSH lies" for the full mechanism. Temperature + pulse are the actual readouts of cellular metabolic rate that the labs are trying to indirectly estimate.

## Counter-arguments

Mainstream endocrinology rejects basal-temperature diagnostics as too non-specific (infections, ovulation, ambient temperature, measurement error all confound). The Peat counter: those confounders are why you track *trends* over days, not single readings — and the trend reads metabolism more honestly than a single morning blood draw.

## Data gaps

- No published modern validation of Barnes-style basal temperature against gold-standard metabolic measurement (indirect calorimetry).
- Pulse-rate ranges are Peat-stated heuristics rather than population data — distribution at "metabolically healthy" baseline is unstudied.
