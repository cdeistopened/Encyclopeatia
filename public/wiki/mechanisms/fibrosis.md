# Fibrosis

The orthodox account of scar tissue is grudgingly admiring: parenchymal cells cannot divide fast enough to restore the original architecture, so fibroblasts step in and lay down collagen as a structural patch. Scar saves the wound from breaking open. The entire pathology of fibrotic disease then reads as a story of *too much* of an otherwise useful repair process — pulmonary fibrosis, cirrhosis, scleroderma, cardiac post-infarction remodeling, keloid scarring, fibroids — each scarring run to excess in a particular organ, with the therapeutic logic of inhibiting the excess without losing the underlying repair.

Peat read the picture the other way. Adult fibrosis is not excessive repair. It is *failed* repair — the body laying down scar because it has lost the energetic conditions under which the same injury would have regenerated. The fetus closes the same wounds without scar. Salamanders regenerate limbs. The membranes of the mouth, the bone marrow, and the thymus retain something like the fetal pattern into adulthood. What distinguishes these tissues and these life-stages is not the presence of an extra repair mechanism but the *absence* of the cytokine cascade that drives adult healing into scar formation. Fibrosis is the connective-tissue terminus of chronic [[inflammation]] — the same disorder traced from CRP elevation through fibroblast activation to the final replacement of functional cells by collagen.

The reframing has practical bite. If fibrosis were excess repair, the therapeutic move would be to suppress collagen synthesis (the modern strategy: pirfenidone, nintedanib). If fibrosis is failed repair, the move is to restore the conditions under which the organ would have regenerated — lift the substrate (PUFA), oppose the master cytokine ([[estrogen]]-driven TGF-β), restore the energy state (T3, CO2), and let the proteolytic enzymes that continuously remodel matrix clear the surplus collagen failed healing left behind. The toolkit Peat recommended — vitamin K, vitamin E, progesterone, T3, aspirin, lisinopril — does not act on a single anti-fibrotic target. It restores the metabolic environment in which the body's own remodeling enzymes can do their job.

This page assumes the inflammatory cascade covered at [[inflammation]] and the estrogen mechanism at [[estrogen]]. What follows is the fibrosis-specific layer: TGF-β as the master fibrogenic cytokine, the estrogen-fibrosis link across organs, the aldosterone-angiotensin axis driving cardiac fibrosis, the anti-fibrotic toolkit, and fibrosis as the aging trajectory Peat called "regeneration giving way to degeneration."

## The mechanism: TGF-β, myofibroblasts, and the chemistry of stiffening

The cellular finale of the inflammatory cascade is the conversion of resident fibroblasts (and certain epithelial cells, pericytes, and endothelial cells) into *myofibroblasts* — contractile, collagen-synthesizing cells that lay down the dense extracellular matrix of scar. The master cytokine that drives this conversion is transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β). Of the cytokines whose concentrations change between fetal and adult tissues, Peat singled out TGF-β as the one whose addition to the healing fetus converts a scarless wound into a scarring one: "Several cytokines (proteins that regulate cell functions) appear at much higher concentrations in adult tissues than in fetal tissues (PDGF A, three forms of TGF, IGF-1, and bFGF), and when one of these (TGF-beta1) is added to the healing fetus, it produces inflammation and fibrosis" ([[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration*]]).

The TGF-β cascade is straightforward in outline. Stressed cells release latent TGF-β bound to the extracellular matrix; mechanical tension, proteases, integrins, and reactive oxygen species liberate it; the active cytokine binds its fibroblast receptor and induces the Smad-2/3 transcriptional program, whose targets include type I and III collagen, fibronectin, PAI-1, and tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs). The myofibroblast phenotype — defined by α-smooth muscle actin expression — emerges; collagen synthesis goes up; degradation goes down; the net effect is accumulation.

What makes the accumulation pathological is its *chemistry* as much as its quantity. From [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis* (2001)]]: "Fibrosis is an abnormal progression of the normal formation of fibrous material between cells, and it usually involves an increase in the volume of the extracellular material, and an increase in the percentage of collagen in that matrix, and an increased rigidity of the collagen that results from chemical cross-links, that are analogous to the polymerization of a plastic, or the vulcanizing of rubber." The cross-links — formed by lysyl oxidase, by non-enzymatic glycation, by aldehydes liberated from PUFA peroxidation — are what make a fibrotic organ functionally inert. The matrix locks.

Mast cells are the underappreciated infantry. They release histamine, serotonin, tryptase, chymase, and TGF-β itself; the proteases activate latent TGF-β at the cell surface; the mast-cell-derived TGF-β stimulates surrounding fibroblasts. In every chronically inflamed tissue — asthmatic airway, cirrhotic liver portal tract, scleroderma skin, endometriotic lesion — mast cell density tracks fibrosis density, which is part of why antihistamines (on Peat's [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]] anti-fibrotic list) have measurable anti-fibrotic effects.

The sequence Peat sketched in the [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]] opening summary remains the cleanest statement of the cascade: "Excitotoxins are fibrogenic. Estrogen is excitotoxic and fibrogenic. Lactate is fibrogenic. Edema precedes fibrosis. Injury and inflammation are fibrogenic. Free radical peroxidation is a common pathway leading to fibrosis." Each of the listed inputs converges on TGF-β activation: estrogen induces TGF-β transcription directly; lactate stabilizes hypoxia-inducible factor and activates latent TGF-β; PUFA peroxidation products (4-hydroxynonenal, malondialdehyde) cross-link the matrix and activate fibroblasts; the prostaglandins generated from arachidonic acid amplify the cytokine signal. The chain is the inflammatory chain of [[inflammation]] terminating in collagen instead of resolving.

There is a final piece of the chemistry worth naming. Collagen is, atomically, a remarkably unusual protein. "Collagen is a very unusual protein, since it lacks tryptophan and cysteine, and contains a very high percentage of glycine," Peat wrote in [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]. The glycine richness is part of why collagen breakdown is protective — dissolving scar releases inhibitory glycine and consumes ammonia in the process — and why dietary [[glycine]] (from gelatin and bone broth) is itself anti-fibrotic. "When collagen is dissolved, a large amount of the antitoxic glycine is released, but not a bit of the excitotoxic antithyroid amino acids cysteine and tryptophan." The composition of the scar contains the seeds of its own dissolution, if the proteolytic enzymes are allowed to work.

## The estrogen–fibrosis link

The single most consequential factor in Peat's account of fibrosis is [[estrogen]]. The same hormone whose oxygen-wasting and lactate-driving and stress-axis-activating effects organize the [[inflammation]] page is the hormone that drives the connective-tissue terminus of inflammation in nearly every organ. The historical evidence for the link goes back a century. In the 1930s, Leo Loeb showed that large doses of estrogen produced massive collagen synthesis in the uterus and vagina, and observed that the effects resembled the collagen accumulation of aging. In the 1940s, Alexander Lipshutz showed that small but continuous doses of estrogen produced fibromas in many tissues besides the uterus, and that these fibromas tended to develop into cancers. Peat threaded these findings through his framework: "Estrogen stimulates collagen synthesis, and it has been associated with a variety of inflammatory and fibrotic conditions" ([[rosacea-inflammation-and-aging-the-inefficiency-of-stress|*Rosacea, Inflammation, and Aging*]]).

The link is not merely associational. Estrogen acts at every node of the TGF-β cascade: it directly induces TGF-β1 transcription in fibroblasts, activates mast cells to release latent TGF-β, increases collagen synthesis through both genomic and non-genomic pathways, drives the lactate production that activates latent TGF-β, and locally activates [[aromatase]] to synthesize more estrogen from androgens. Through its suppression of [[thyroid]] function and impairment of liver conjugation, it also removes the metabolic brake that would otherwise let proteolytic enzymes clear surplus matrix.

The organ-by-organ pattern is consistent across the corpus. **Uterine fibroids** are estrogen-dependent collagen tumors of the myometrium that regress under progesterone dominance and after menopause; Peat noted that retroperitoneal fibrosis "has been compared to uterine fibroid tumors, and it is commonly treated successfully with the antiestrogenic drug, tamoxifen" ([[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]). **Breast fibrosis** (fibrocystic disease) sits in the same category. The keloid finding from [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]] is particularly direct: "I have seen keloids regress quickly following the application of vitamin E with antiestrogenic hormones." The scar dissolves when the estrogen signaling holding it in place is interrupted.

**Hepatic fibrosis** runs the same cascade through hepatic stellate cells, which activate into myofibroblasts under TGF-β from Kupffer cells, PUFA-peroxidation aldehydes, and portal endotoxin. Estrogen amplifies each step: it raises endotoxin uptake by impairing biliary clearance, raises PUFA peroxidation by occupying antioxidants, and directly stimulates stellate cells. **Pulmonary fibrosis** clusters in postmenopausal women, smokers, and scleroderma; the bleomycin animal model is sensitized by estrogen and attenuated by ovariectomy and tamoxifen. "Scleroderma, like the other 'collagen diseases,' affects women (during their reproductive years) much more often than men" ([[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]).

**Endometriosis** is the limit case. The ectopic endometrial tissue is itself aromatase-expressing and locally estrogen-synthesizing; the peritoneal adhesions are driven by the estrogen the lesion produces. Progesterone regresses both lesion and scar where estrogen-receptor antagonism (Lupron, GnRH agonists) does not, because progesterone destroys the receptor rather than blocking it. See [[endometriosis]].

**Keloid and hypertrophic scarring** complete the catalogue. Susceptibility tracks female sex, pigmented skin (higher local aromatase), pregnancy, and puberty — the high-estrogen windows.

## The aldosterone–angiotensin link: cardiac fibrosis

The fibrosis that organizes around the heart has a partly different signaling architecture and warrants its own treatment. The cytokine remains TGF-β; the upstream amplifier is the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis (RAAS). The chain runs: stress, sympathetic activation, or sodium retention lowers renal perfusion; the kidney releases renin; renin cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I; angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II; angiotensin II constricts vessels, stimulates aldosterone secretion, and directly activates cardiac fibroblasts; aldosterone retains sodium, raises blood pressure, and *also* directly activates cardiac fibroblasts through the mineralocorticoid receptor expressed on those cells. Both angiotensin II and aldosterone induce TGF-β expression in cardiomyocytes and fibroblasts. The fibrotic outcome is the replacement of myocardial cells by connective tissue — diastolic stiffening first, then systolic failure, then in the worst cases the calcification that Peat described.

Peat's framing of cardiac fibrosis kept the metabolic substrate in view. From [[heart-and-hormones|*Heart and Hormones* (2015)]]: "In the stressed, energy depleted failing heart, muscle cells die and are replaced by connective tissue cells. The growth produced by over-exposure to adrenergic stimulation leads to stiffening and reduced functioning." And: "The calcification of blood vessels caused by phosphate excess and vitamin K deficiency involves the expression of a protein which has its proper place in the skeleton. The replacement of heart muscle by fibrous connective tissue and even bone is a basic biological problem of differentiation, and the responsible factors — stress, increased estrogen, deficient thyroid hormone, suppression of glucose oxidation by fatty acids, etc. — are involved in the problems of differentiation that occur in other degenerative processes, such as sarcopenia, dementia, and cancer."

Two clinical points fall out. First: the standard pharmacological intervention for hypertension and heart failure — ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril), ARBs (losartan, valsartan), and aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone, eplerenone) — is *also* the most evidence-supported anti-fibrotic regimen in cardiology. The RALES, EPHESUS, and EMPHASIS-HF mortality benefits exceed what blood-pressure lowering alone would predict, and the favored explanation is the anti-fibrotic effect on the myocardium. Peat endorsed lisinopril on essentially this basis: it interrupts the angiotensin-aldosterone-TGF-β chain without immune suppression.

Second: the cardiac picture connects directly to [[vitamin-k|vitamin K]] deficiency. Matrix Gla protein (MGP), the principal soft-tissue calcification inhibitor, requires vitamin-K-dependent carboxylation. Warfarin users show accelerated vascular calcification; the K2 menaquinones (MK-4, MK-7) are the soft-tissue active forms. Late-stage cardiac fibrosis progresses to frank calcification partly because uncarboxylated MGP can no longer suppress the osteoblast-like trans-differentiation program in the matrix.

## The anti-fibrotic toolkit

Peat's anti-fibrotic recommendations are not aimed at a single fibrotic target. They restore the metabolic environment in which the body's proteolytic enzymes — collagenase, plasmin, the matrix metalloproteinases — can do the matrix remodeling they were designed to do. The toolkit overlaps almost completely with the anti-inflammatory toolkit at [[inflammation]] (because adult inflammation and fibrosis are the same disorder at different stages), with several fibrosis-specific additions.

**[[Vitamin-k|Vitamin K]]** is the most fibrosis-specific entry. It carboxylates matrix Gla protein, the principal soft-tissue calcification inhibitor, and suppresses the prostaglandin generation that activates aromatase and TGF-β. The K2 menaquinones (MK-4, MK-7) are the soft-tissue active forms. K is the mandatory companion to chronic aspirin and an independent anti-calcification, anti-fibrotic agent — especially in cardiac fibrosis, where the matrix is at risk of progressing to frank calcification.

**[[Vitamin-e|Vitamin E]]**. The Shutes (father and sons) demonstrated in the 1930s that vitamin E "acts as an antiestrogen, protecting against the increased clotting problems caused by excessive estrogen" and "activates the proteolytic, fibrinolytic, enzymes that remove blood clots" ([[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]). The mechanism is partly antioxidant — protecting PUFA from peroxidation reduces the aldehyde load that cross-links collagen — and partly direct: it displaces PUFA from binding sites, suppresses prostaglandin synthesis, and stabilizes membrane redox. The keloid regression Peat reported from "the application of vitamin E with antiestrogenic hormones" runs through both arms.

**[[Progesterone]]** is the steroid lever. It opposes estrogen at the receptor (destroying the protein, not blocking it), suppresses [[aromatase]], inhibits the phospholipase that liberates arachidonic acid, and directly reduces collagen synthesis in estrogen-primed fibroblasts. "Various fibroses, including breast, liver, and mesentery, have been regressed by progesterone treatment" ([[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration*]]). For men, [[pregnenolone]] carries similar effects with less androgen-suppressive risk.

**[[Thyroid|T3]]** restores the oxidative metabolism that allows proteolytic remodeling of matrix. From [[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration*]]: "Thyroid hormone, progesterone, and carbon dioxide all reduce inflammation while facilitating normal tissue remodeling. Fibrosis of the heart and liver, which are often considered to be unavoidably progressive, can be regressed by thyroid hormone." A thyroid-sufficient cell oxidizes glucose to CO2 rather than lactate; the proteases that lactate would otherwise inhibit regain function; the surplus collagen failed healing left behind is gradually dissolved.

**[[Aspirin]]** intervenes at the prostaglandin step. It blocks COX-mediated conversion of arachidonic acid to PGE2, inhibits the phospholipase that liberates arachidonic acid in the first place, and inhibits protein glycation and lysyl-oxidase-mediated cross-linking of collagen — direct anti-fibrotic actions independent of its anti-inflammatory work. See [[aspirin]] for full mechanism.

**Lisinopril** (and the broader ACE-I / ARB / aldosterone-antagonist class) interrupts the angiotensin-aldosterone-TGF-β axis at its source. Peat recommended it most often in cardiac and hypertensive renal fibrosis, but the anti-fibrotic effect generalizes — angiotensin II is fibrogenic in lung, liver, and kidney as well. Spironolactone or eplerenone adds the aldosterone-receptor blockade that ACE inhibition alone does not deliver.

**Retinoids** are the underappreciated entry. Hepatic stellate cells store vitamin A in their quiescent state; loss of stored retinoids is a diagnostic feature of myofibroblast transition, and supplying retinoic acid drives the cell back toward quiescence in vitro. Peat treated vitamin A as part of the fat-soluble cluster (A, D, E, K) whose collective deficiency permits the fibrotic and calcific failures of aging.

The dietary layer does most of the work. Fibrosis-specific elements of the [[anti-stress-diet]]: depletion of dietary [[pufa]] (without which the cascade has no substrate); frequent sucrose-containing sugars (preventing the hypoglycemic episodes that mobilize stored PUFA); dietary [[glycine]] from gelatin and bone broth (substrate for matrix turnover, plus tryptophan-lowering); [[carrot-salad|raw carrot]] daily (blocking the intestinal recirculation that elevates tissue estrogen); adequate salt (suppressing the aldosterone retention that feeds cardiac fibrosis); [[carbon-dioxide|CO2]] elevation (the regenerative-state signal whose absence permits scarring). [[Niacinamide]] inhibits the lipolysis that mobilizes stored PUFA.

The crucial framing point is that no single agent in the toolkit "treats fibrosis" the way a pharmaceutical anti-fibrotic is meant to. Each acts on a node of the metabolic environment, and the combined effect is to allow the body's continuous matrix remodeling to clear the surplus collagen that failed healing left behind. As Peat wrote in [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]: "The first principle in reversal is to stop the pathogenic process, and to stop the stress-induced cell death. Then restorative processes can come into balance with degenerative." The order matters. Suppressing TGF-β with a kinase inhibitor while leaving the upstream estrogen, lactate, and PUFA signals intact buys time without resolving the disorder.

## Fibrosis as aging: from regeneration to degeneration

The most important integrative claim in Peat's account of fibrosis is that aging itself is the gradual replacement of regeneration by degeneration — the same fibrotic terminus playing out at the level of the whole organism. The 2012 newsletter [[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration: Types of Inflammation Change With Aging*]] is the canonical statement: "Fifty years ago, inflammation was seen as a necessary part of the healing process, but now it is recognized as a cause of heart disease, diabetes, cancer, and aging itself. During the development of the organism, the nature of healing changes, as the nature of inflammation changes. Early in life, healing is regenerative or restorative, and there is little inflammation. In adulthood as the amount of inflammation increases, healing fails to completely restore lost structures and functions, resulting in scarring, the replacement of functional tissue with fibrous tissue."

Peat returned to the opening of [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis* (2001)]] more than a decade earlier with what is still the cleanest statement of fibrosis-as-aging: "Fibrosis is an outstanding feature of aging: Skin, joints, liver, kidneys, heart and blood vessels, even the brain, undergo fibrotic changes in association with disease, injury, and aging." The sentence is a list of organs, but the underlying claim is structural: aging is the disorder of one process — the fibrotic-degenerative replacement of functional cells by collagen — expressed across many tissues. The brittleness of an old artery, the loss of elasticity of old skin, the rigidity of old tendons, the calcification of old heart valves, the cirrhosis of the alcoholic liver, the dementia of the senile brain — all are local readings of the same systemic shift.

The PUFA-driven mechanism organizes the trajectory. In adulthood "the PUFA and prostaglandins are known to increase collagen synthesis. Serotonin and estrogen, which interact closely with PUFA, promote collagen synthesis and fibrosis. In the fetus, hyaluronic acid, rather than collagen, is the main extracellular material in wound repair... PUFA also interfere with the turnover of collagen by inhibiting proteolytic enzymes that are necessary for tissue remodeling" ([[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration*]]).

The animal-experiment confirmation is striking. "When animals are made 'deficient' in the polyunsaturated fatty acids, their wounds heal, with normal or accelerated collagen synthesis, and with more vigorous collagen breakdown... All phases of development, from gestation to aging, are altered by the presence of the unsaturated fats, and these effects correspond closely to the loss of the regenerative capacity" ([[regeneration-and-degeneration-types-of-inflammation-change-with-aging|*Regeneration and Degeneration*]]). PUFA "deficiency" is in scare quotes because the body produces [[mead-acid]] as substitute; the deficient animal heals *better*, suggesting the entire premise of "essential" fatty acids is backwards.

The temporal sequence Peat sketched for an individual tissue scales up to a lifetime. From [[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]: "In the first stage of tissue injury, swelling stiffens a tissue; after prolonged edema, there may be an increased volume and concentration of collagen around cells; finally, the density of the collagen can increase, and can calcify, as in the sclera of the eye, the arteries, heart valves, pericardium, and even the heart muscle itself." The arteriosclerotic vessel, the calcified valve, the rigid lens — these are the late-stage termini reached at 70 if the cellular-edema, lactate-elevation, collagen-deposition sequence has been running uninterrupted since adolescence.

The link to cancer follows. "Fibrogenesis is related to carcinogenesis. Cancerization is a 'field process,' not primarily a unique 'genetic event'" ([[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]). The fibrotic field — chronic estrogenic stimulation, chronic prostaglandin generation, chronic TGF-β signaling — is the field in which cancer arises, and "antifibromatogenic hormones oppose the development of the fibromas and cancers that are caused by estrogen" ([[fibrosis|*Fibrosis*]]).

## Clinical conditions

The fibrotic terminus expresses itself in a recognizable catalogue of named diseases. The thread connecting them is mechanistic, not nominalist; what makes them clinically distinct is which tissue has reached the end-stage replacement of parenchyma by collagen, not which underlying disorder produced the replacement.

**[[Cirrhosis]]** — the hepatic terminus. Stellate cells activate into collagen-producing myofibroblasts under TGF-β signaling from Kupffer cells, endothelial cells, and PUFA-peroxidation products. Framework interventions overlap conventional management at some points (alcohol cessation, weight loss) and diverge at others (vitamin K, low-PUFA, thyroid, progesterone, raw carrot daily).

**Pulmonary fibrosis** — the alveolar terminus, clustering in postmenopausal women and long-term smokers. The bleomycin animal model is sensitized by estrogen and PUFA loading. Pirfenidone and nintedanib slow decline but do not reverse it; the framework predicts that adding the metabolic toolkit would do additional work.

**Scleroderma** — the cutaneous-systemic terminus, female-to-male ratio roughly 4:1, peaking in the reproductive years (the estrogen signal showing through). Tamoxifen and progesterone have shown signal in case reports.

**Keloid scarring** — the cutaneous limit case, common in pigmented skin (higher local aromatase) and in the high-estrogen windows of pregnancy and puberty. Topical vitamin E, intralesional progesterone, and tamoxifen all have published regression evidence.

**Uterine fibroids** — the myometrial terminus. Fibroids regress in pregnancy and after menopause; natural progesterone treatment produces regression without surgery. The mainstream confusion between progesterone and anti-progesterone synthetic "progestins" obscures this.

**[[Endometriosis]]** — the peritoneal terminus. The ectopic tissue is itself aromatase-expressing and locally estrogen-synthesizing; the peritoneal adhesions are the fibrotic outcome. See [[endometriosis]].

**Cardiac fibrosis** — diastolic dysfunction, restrictive cardiomyopathy, post-infarction remodeling, with the angiotensin-aldosterone axis as upstream amplifier. The one place where mainstream and framework recommendations align almost completely (ACE-Is, ARBs, aldosterone antagonists).

**Renal fibrosis** (chronic kidney disease) and **arterial fibrosis** (atherosclerosis) round out the catalogue, both running the same cascade with tissue-specific embellishments.

## Counter-arguments

**Pirfenidone and nintedanib.** The modern anti-fibrotic pharmacology is anchored on two drugs approved for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: pirfenidone, a pyridone derivative that suppresses TGF-β and TNF-α expression and reduces fibroblast proliferation; and nintedanib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting the PDGF, FGF, and VEGF receptor families. Both have produced statistically significant reductions in forced-vital-capacity decline in IPF trials (CAPACITY, ASCEND for pirfenidone; INPULSIS-1 and -2 for nintedanib), and both are now standard of care in IPF and have been extended to scleroderma-associated interstitial lung disease and progressive fibrosing interstitial lung diseases more broadly. The framework's response is not denial — the drugs work — but reframing: they suppress one node of a cascade whose upstream causes (PUFA-driven prostaglandin generation, estrogen amplification, oxidative-metabolism failure) they leave untouched, and the clinical effect is to slow decline rather than to reverse it. Mortality benefit in the trials is modest and concentrated in the early disease stage where decline is slow anyway. The framework would predict that adding the metabolic toolkit (T3, progesterone, K2, E, aspirin, low PUFA) on top of either drug should do additional work, though the controlled comparison has not been published.

**Anti-TGF-β biologics.** Direct neutralization (fresolimumab, anti-αvβ6 integrin antibodies) has shown signal in scleroderma and IgA nephropathy but has failed to deliver the dramatic effects expected from preclinical data, partly because TGF-β has so many other roles (immune regulation, wound healing, cell-cycle control). The framework predicts this — TGF-β is downstream of the metabolic disorder; suppressing it without addressing upstream signaling generates the same symptomatic-improvement-with-side-effects pattern as the anti-inflammatory biologics at [[inflammation]].

**The fibrosis-is-irreversible thesis.** Hepatology and pulmonology textbooks generally treat established fibrosis as irreversible. The thesis is undermined by the consistent observation that early cirrhosis reverses on alcohol cessation and weight loss, that hepatic fibrosis from HCV reverses on direct-acting antiviral cure, and that matrix metalloproteinases remain capable of dissolving cross-linked collagen given the right conditions. Peat's clinical claim that thyroid hormone and progesterone can regress hepatic and cardiac fibrosis is consistent with the contemporary recognition that irreversibility was an artifact of leaving upstream causes in place rather than of the matrix itself being inert.

**The estrogen-is-protective consensus.** Mainstream cardiology and rheumatology continue to treat estrogen as protective against fibrotic disease, citing the rise in cardiovascular events after menopause. The framework's full response is at [[estrogen]]; for fibrosis specifically, the postmenopausal cardiovascular rise tracks *tissue* estrogen (through ovariectomy-induced upregulation of peripheral aromatase), not estrogen withdrawal, and the female autoimmune skew is direct evidence of estrogen's fibrogenic action. The Women's Health Initiative results (HRT increases breast cancer, stroke, dementia, VTE) are at least consistent with the framework.

**Genetic and idiopathic frameworks.** The IPF literature increasingly emphasizes telomere-shortening (TERT, TERC, RTEL1) and surfactant gene mutations as causes of "idiopathic" disease. The framework grants the genetic predisposition while arguing that it acts through reduced metabolic resilience — a telomere-short alveolar epithelium reaches the inflammatory threshold faster, but the threshold is set by the metabolic environment.

## Data gaps

**The quantitative reversibility threshold.** Peat claimed that hepatic and cardiac fibrosis can be regressed by thyroid hormone and progesterone; the corpus does not quantify how much fibrosis, at what stage, in what time-course. Whether late-stage cirrhosis with established portal hypertension is reversible, or whether reversibility is confined to earlier stages, is not addressed.

**Comparative trial data.** The framework's claim that the metabolic toolkit (T3 + progesterone + K2 + E + aspirin + low PUFA) should outperform or complement pirfenidone and nintedanib in IPF, ACE-Is in cardiac fibrosis, or anti-fibrotics in scleroderma has not been tested in controlled trials. The clinical-anecdote literature is suggestive but not decisive.

**Sex-specific dosing.** The progesterone dose required to regress fibroids in a premenopausal woman, breast fibrocystic disease in a postmenopausal woman, or cardiac fibrosis in a man is not specified. Peat's general guidance (5-10 mg natural progesterone daily for women, lower doses or pregnenolone for men) is the floor; the ceiling and the titration logic are underdeveloped in the corpus.

**The TGF-β paradox in early vs late disease.** TGF-β is pro-fibrotic in late tissue injury but anti-proliferative and tumor-suppressive in early epithelial transformation. The framework does not engage this paradox directly; whether the anti-fibrotic interventions Peat recommended are pro- or anti-tumorigenic at different stages is not resolved.

**Mast cell biology.** The corpus mentions mast cells in the context of histamine and serotonin but does not develop the mast-cell-as-TGF-β-amplifier picture that contemporary fibrosis literature has built. The cromolyn-and-ketotifen mast-cell-stabilizer class would be predicted by the framework to be anti-fibrotic; this prediction has not been worked out.

**The aldosterone-vitamin K interaction.** Both aldosterone excess and vitamin K deficiency contribute to cardiac fibrosis and calcification; whether they act independently or whether one potentiates the other (and which direction) is not specified.

**Matrix metalloproteinase regulation.** The framework treats proteolytic remodeling as the rescue mechanism that the toolkit enables; the specific regulation of MMP-1, MMP-2, MMP-9, MMP-13 and their TIMP antagonists in the context of the toolkit interventions is not engaged. Some of the mainstream anti-fibrotic strategy targets exactly this layer.

**Specialized pro-resolving mediators.** As at [[inflammation]], the Serhan-school case for resolvins, protectins, and maresins as the active resolution-phase signals derived from omega-3 fats remains unengaged in the Peat corpus. Whether these mediators are part of the fibrosis-resolution machinery the framework grants or are PUFA-derived liabilities the framework would reject is unresolved.

**Stem cell and regenerative medicine.** The framework treats regeneration as the fetal-mode alternative to adult scarring, but the contemporary regenerative-medicine literature (mesenchymal stem cell therapy, induced pluripotent stem cells for fibrotic disease) is not engaged. Whether the framework would predict that exogenous stem cells succeed or fail depending on the metabolic milieu they are placed into is intuitively yes, but the prediction has not been worked through.
