# EncycloPEATia

This is the EncycloPEATia: a dense, interlinked encyclopedia of Ray Peat's bioenergetic framework, organized as a hypertext rather than a book. It maps the substances, hormones, mechanisms, conditions, people, and protocols Peat returned to across fifty years of newsletters, articles, emails, podcast interviews, and two books. Three downstream products draw from it — the encyclopedia itself, *The Ray Peat Diet* (an Amazon KDP book), and the EncycloPEATia plugin (an interactive Claude Code coach). The vault is maintained by Charlie Deist as part of the Creative Intelligence Agency portfolio.

## About Ray Peat

Ray Peat (1936–2022) was a physiologist whose framework inverts much of contemporary endocrinology and nutrition. He argued that nearly every chronic disease — cancer, dementia, heart disease, diabetes, depression, obesity — is a partial reversion away from oxidative metabolism, the controlled flow of electrons from sugar to oxygen inside the mitochondrion. The biographical hub sits at [[ray-peat]] (work in progress), with his intellectual lineage scattered across the [[people]] category: [[broda-barnes]], [[albert-szent-gyorgyi]], [[hans-selye]], [[gilbert-ling]], [[otto-warburg]].

## How to Read This Encyclopedia

### The wiki-link graph

Most entries are saturated with `[[wiki-links]]`. Follow them liberally. The encyclopedia is designed to be read laterally — the page you came in on is usually not the page you needed. Hub entries ([[oxidative-metabolism]], [[pufa]], [[thyroid]], [[estrogen]]) tend to be the destination of many incoming links; spoke entries open with a one-sentence anchor back to their hub. If a claim feels assumed rather than argued, the argument lives one link away.

### The trust gate (`explored: false`)

Every AI-built or AI-updated page ships with `explored: false` in its frontmatter. Only Charlie sets `explored: true`, and only after reading the article in full. This is the anti-slop trapdoor — a public marker that says "an agent wrote this; a human has not yet verified it sentence by sentence." Most articles in the vault sit at `explored: false`. Treat that not as a disclaimer but as a fidelity warning. A page with `confidence: high` and `explored: false` has been carefully assembled from citations but has not been Charlie-reviewed; verify load-bearing claims against the source documents linked inline before acting on them.

### Counter-arguments and data gaps

Every concept, mechanism, protocol, and condition page ends with two required sections:

- **Counter-arguments** — what pushes back against the claims on this page. Mainstream medical consensus, competing frameworks, places where Peat's position is disputed, contemporary clinical-trial data that disagrees.
- **Data gaps** — what we don't know. Where the corpus is thin. Where Peat hedged or contradicted himself. Where a clinical question has no clean answer.

These sections are honesty by design. Peat's framework inverts mainstream medicine in many places, and an encyclopedia of his views that omitted disagreement would be a hagiography. The counter-arguments are not boilerplate; they cite specific studies, clinicians, and frameworks that disagree.

### Citation discipline

Every quoted Peat passage carries an inline link to the source document — a specific newsletter slug, transcript episode, email exchange, or article. The reader can click through and verify the quote in context, or read the surrounding paragraphs to check whether the lift was fair. Newsletters and articles cite by title; transcripts cite by date and show name; emails cite by topic. The corpus indexed behind the vault includes 180 newsletters, 253 polished transcripts across 14 shows, 244 topical emails, 82 articles, two books (*Generative Energy* and *From PMS to Menopause*), and 48 newspaper letters from 1967 to 1991.

### Reference, don't repeat

Foundational mechanisms are not re-walked in every article. If a piece needs PUFA, it links to [[pufa]] in one sentence and moves on to its own distinct argument. The same applies to [[thyroid]], [[oxidative-metabolism]], [[estrogen]], [[stress]], and the other hubs. Each article should carry a unique signal beyond the universal Peat chain. This keeps the vault dense rather than redundant and rewards the lateral reading the link graph encourages.

## Five Reader Paths

The encyclopedia does not have a front-to-back sequence, but it does have shapes a reader can trace. Five common ones.

### A. New to Peat

Start at [[ray-peat]] for the biography, then read [[oxidative-metabolism]] for the central claim, then [[bioenergetics]] for the wider intellectual frame. From there go to [[pufa]] (the substance class Peat blamed for most of modern disease) and [[core-diet-protocol]] (what to eat, concretely, this week). At that point the vault opens out — follow the wiki-links from whichever pages you are reading.

A shorter version of the same path: [[oxidative-metabolism]] → [[pufa]] → [[core-diet-protocol]]. The first explains why; the second explains what to avoid; the third explains what to do instead.

### B. Women's reproductive and hormonal issues

Peat's most-cited book is *From PMS to Menopause*. The vault has a parallel set of entries oriented toward the same questions. A reader investigating PMS, menopausal symptoms, fertility, or PCOS should start at the condition page that matches their situation — [[pms]], [[menopause]], [[pcos]], [[endometriosis]], or [[fibroids]] — then move to the supporting substances ([[progesterone]], [[allopregnanolone]], [[estrogen]]), then to the protocols ([[fertility]], [[menopause-protocol]], [[pcos-protocol]]). [[Pregnancy-nutrition]] sits in the same cluster for those planning or carrying a pregnancy.

[[Allopregnanolone]] in particular is worth reading early. It is the GABA-A neurosteroid downstream of progesterone, and its physiology explains why so many women's symptoms — anxiety, insomnia, mood instability — track the luteal phase, postpartum window, and perimenopausal transition.

### C. Hypothyroidism, suspected or confirmed

The cleanest entry point is [[hypothyroidism]] — the condition Peat treated as the central health issue of modern populations. From there read [[thyroid]] (the hormone), [[broda-barnes]] (the mid-century clinician whose diagnostic Peat extended), and [[hypothyroid-recovery]] (the staged protocol). The under-the-radar hop most readers miss: [[heart-disease]]. Barnes's central clinical claim — that in thirty years of practice not one of his thyroid-supplemented patients died of heart disease — sits at the intersection of those four pages, and the [[heart-disease-protocol]] is built around it.

[[Temperature-tracking]] is the daily measurement protocol. It replaces the scale for most Peat-informed self-monitoring.

### D. Biohacker or optimizer

For readers who arrive at Peat through the longevity, mitochondria, and red-light corner of the internet, the entry sequence is different. Begin at [[mitochondria]] for the organelle. Read [[cardiolipin]] for the specific lipid Peat tracked as the marker of mitochondrial youth. Then [[methylene-blue]] (low-dose, on the 20–40 μg side, not the 15 mg biohacker convention), [[red-light]], and [[hormesis]].

Read [[hormesis]] last and read it carefully. It is the cautionary frame. Peat was skeptical of most "good stress" claims — fasting, cold plunges, exercise overdosed for adaptation — and treated them as forms of stress that compound damage rather than build resilience. If you arrived expecting an endorsement, the page disagrees with you, and the disagreement is the point.

### E. Clinician

A clinician reading the vault to evaluate a patient's labs or symptoms should start at the condition page that matches the presenting complaint, then read the relevant substance pages for the molecules in question, then read the counter-arguments at the bottom of each condition page. The counter-arguments are where the mainstream medical view is preserved — they make explicit where Peat departs from current standards of care and on what evidence.

A useful constellation for general workup: [[hypothyroidism]] + [[broda-barnes]] (temperature and pulse diagnostics that pre-date TSH and that Peat argued the TSH test obscured) + [[stress]] (the seven-step physiological cascade) + the counter-arguments section of whichever condition is in scope. The Blood Panel Analyzer plugin (separate product, lives in the GitHub repo) covers 38 markers and 21 metabolic threads at clinical depth.

## What's NOT Here

This is a Peat-framework encyclopedia. The editorial frame is Peat's. Counter-arguments are included on every relevant page for honesty, but the vault is a partisan reading of physiology — it reflects how Peat thought, not a neutral consensus of contemporary medical opinion. A reader who wants an evidence-based-medicine textbook should not use this as a substitute.

The vault is also not a substitute for medical care. The protocols describe what Peat recommended; the condition pages describe what Peat thought was happening underneath the symptoms. Clinical decisions belong to the reader and their physician.

A few large topics are still being prepared and have not yet shipped as canonical articles. The research briefs exist, but the entries themselves require splitting before single-pass synthesis: [[cancer]] (5,400-line brief), [[diabetes]] (8,100 lines), [[obesity]] (8,800 lines), [[warburg-effect]] (7,800 lines), [[nitric-oxide]] (10,900 lines). Tier 3 long-tail conditions — adenomyosis, scleroderma, cirrhosis, mast-cell disorders, post-finasteride syndrome and roughly ninety others — sit in the queue. The full ranked list lives at [[_queue]].

## More Reading Paths

The five paths above are the starter set — short, three-to-five-hop routes through the most common entry points. The expanded wayfinding layer lives at [[sequences]], which catalogs roughly eight reader-persona paths and fourteen topic arcs at ten-to-twenty-step depth. Reader-persona sequences ([[sequences/personas]]) organize by *who is reading* — the chronically fatigued, the woman with hormonal issues, the cancer patient, the parent of an atypical child, the biohacker. Topic arcs ([[sequences/topic-arcs]]) organize by *what concept cluster is being traced* — the PUFA arc, the thyroid arc, the estrogen arc, the CO₂ arc, the cancer arc. Both schemes index the same articles in different orderings; pick whichever framing matches your situation. The encyclopedia stays graph-shaped underneath — sequences are the scaffolding for the first traversal, not a replacement for the lateral reading the link structure rewards.
