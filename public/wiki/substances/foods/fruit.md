# Fruit

Fruit stands at the center of Ray Peat's dietary recommendations—not as a minor side dish but as a primary energy source. In his framework, the natural sugars in ripe fruit provide the most metabolically appropriate fuel for human physiology, superior to [[starch]] because of their fructose content and mineral profile. The anti-fruit sentiment that dominated nutrition advice for decades, driven by misguided fears about sugar and fructose, inverted the actual metabolic reality.

As Peat summarized his basic dietary recommendations: "Milk and honey or sugar. Fruit, oranges, tropical fruits, and gelatinous things such as consommé."

## Why Fructose Matters

The key to fruit's superiority over starch lies in its fructose content. As Peat explained in a [[2011-04-27-eastwest-healing-eastwest-healing-glycemia-starch-and-sugar-in-context|2011 episode on glycemia]], "Fruits usually have about a 50/50 balance of fructose and glucose. Besides that balance, fruits contain [[potassium]] and [[magnesium]], which help regulate glucose."

Starch is pure glucose; fruit sugar is half fructose. This matters because fructose has a fundamentally different metabolic effect: "When you drink [[orange juice]], the high potassium content reduces the amount of insulin you have to secrete to handle the sugar. That accounts for why they are much more smoothly handled than a similar number of calories in the form of starch."

The anti-diabetes effects of fructose were well-established decades ago before the low-sugar ideology took hold. As Peat noted in a [[2010-10-15-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-sugar-ii|2010 episode on sugar]], "30 or 40 years ago, there was quite a bit of publishing activity relating to the anti-diabetes effects of fructose because of that stimulating effect on the metabolism."

## Fruit vs. Starch

The glycemic distinction matters for [[insulin]] and stress hormone regulation. In a [[2022-11-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-november-2022|2022 episode]], Peat explained: "The difference with sucrose—the common sugar in fruits—is that it is only 50% glucose. So, it doesn't have the glycemic reaction that a digestible starch has. It's only half as able to stimulate insulin. The fructose component of the molecule actually has a mild insulin-inhibiting effect."

This means fruit "provides sugar energy much more gradually than starch does and tends to avoid the glucose-insulin-[[hypoglycemia]] stress reaction" that characterizes high-starch diets.

## Anti-Inflammatory Effects

Beyond energy provision, fruit supports liver function and suppresses [[inflammation]]. In email exchanges, Peat wrote: "Sweet fruits are anti-inflammatory and help to keep the liver functioning, including keeping [[cholesterol]] at an appropriate level."

The minerals in fruit—particularly potassium and magnesium—support cellular energy production. The organic acids help maintain appropriate pH. The sugars provide the fuel that prevents the [[cortisol]] and [[adrenaline]] surge triggered by [[hypoglycemia]].

## Tropical Fruits and Orange Juice

Peat's fruit preferences emphasized tropical varieties and citrus—particularly [[orange juice]], which he recommended drinking by the quart. These fruits tend to be higher in sugar, lower in problematic compounds, and richer in the minerals that support sugar metabolism.

The recommendation for juice over whole fruit reflected practical considerations: juice provides concentrated sugar and minerals without the fiber that can irritate sensitive digestive systems. For people with gut issues, the soluble fiber in whole fruit might be problematic, while the strained juice provides the metabolic benefits without the irritation.

## Fruit Selection

Not all fruits are equal. Peat noted that some fruits are starchier (like bananas before they're fully ripe), some contain problematic compounds, and some—particularly temperate fruits with their seeds—may contain more allergens or defensive chemicals than tropical varieties.

The general guidance: ripe, sweet, tropical fruits and citrus are safest. Fully ripe bananas, mangoes, papayas, and citrus fruits provide the cleanest sugar sources with the fewest complications.
