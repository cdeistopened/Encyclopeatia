# Potassium

Potassium is the mineral that makes [[fruit]] metabolically superior to [[starch]]. In Ray Peat's framework, potassium's role in glucose handling explains why fruit sugars don't cause the [[insulin]] spikes and fat storage that equal amounts of starch produce. This insight inverts conventional nutrition wisdom, which treats all carbohydrates as equivalent and ignores the mineral context that determines how sugars affect metabolism.

As Peat explained in a [[2011-04-27-eastwest-healing-eastwest-healing-glycemia-starch-and-sugar-in-context|2011 episode on glycemia]], "When you drink [[orange juice]], the high potassium content reduces the amount of insulin you have to secrete to handle the sugar. That accounts for why [fruits] are much more smoothly handled than a similar number of calories in the form of starch."

## The Insulin Alternative

Potassium functions as a partial substitute for insulin in glucose uptake. As Peat noted in a [[2014-01-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-questions-and-answers|2014 episode]], "Experiments showed that potassium was more involved in handling glucose effectively than insulin was. Since fruit juice has a lot of potassium, you don't need nearly as much insulin to handle the sugar."

This mechanism has profound implications. "Eating a piece of bread is much more disturbing to your insulin and blood sugar system than drinking a similar number of calories as fruit juice." The starch provides glucose without potassium; the fruit provides glucose with the mineral that helps cells absorb it without excessive insulin secretion.

## Fat Storage Prevention

The insulin-reducing effect of potassium directly affects fat metabolism. In a [[2014-06-02-its-rainmaking-time-it-s-rainmaking-time-energy-protective-materials|2014 episode]], Peat explained: "When sugar comes in the form of fruit like orange juice, the potassium takes on the function of insulin, so it is not fattening the way starch is. Starch is a powerful insulin stimulant, and insulin tends to turn carbohydrate to fat."

This is why Peat could recommend drinking quarts of orange juice without concern about weight gain—the potassium context changes how the body handles the sugar, preventing the insulin-driven fat storage that starchy foods promote.

## Stress Response

Potassium also helps break the hypoglycemia-stress cycle that drives metabolic dysfunction. In a [[2008-10-31-other-hope-for-health-hope-for-health-thyroid|2008 episode on thyroid]], Peat described how [[endotoxin]] "poisons our energy-producing systems" and "you can create hypoglycemia by exposing yourself to too many of these stresses."

The solution: "The quickest way to break out of the cycle is to eat a well-balanced natural diet, emphasizing a lot of fruits—orange juice, for example—because of the high potassium content. Potassium lets you handle and absorb blood sugar" without triggering the [[adrenaline]] and [[cortisol]] response that accompanies insulin-dependent glucose handling.

## Practical Sources

Peat's recommended potassium sources were fruits—particularly orange juice and tropical fruits. These provide potassium in the context of natural sugars and other minerals that support [[metabolism]].

The point in a [[2009-10-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-food-additives|2009 episode]] clarified the comparison: "The potassium content of fruit is what keeps the insulin much lower. If you eat bread, you get very little potassium. If you eat fruit with the same amount of sugar, the potassium acts somewhat like insulin, so you have very little stimulation of fat production."
