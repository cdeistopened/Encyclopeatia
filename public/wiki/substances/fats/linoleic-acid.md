# Linoleic Acid

Linoleic acid is the primary dietary villain in Ray Peat's nutritional framework. An 18-carbon omega-6 [[pufa|polyunsaturated fatty acid]] with two double bonds, it is the dominant fat in corn oil, soybean oil, safflower oil, and the other [[seed-oils]] that became staples of the modern diet in the mid-twentieth century. Peat considered it not merely unhealthy but actively toxic at the quantities Americans consume, and he spent five decades building a case that the entire "essential fatty acid" classification was a scientific error compounded by industrial marketing. In his view, linoleic acid suppresses [[thyroid-function|thyroid function]], generates the inflammatory [[prostaglandins]], feeds [[estrogen]] dominance, accelerates [[aging]], promotes [[cancer]], damages the heart, and accumulates in tissues over years in a way that progressively undermines the body's capacity for [[oxidative-metabolism|oxidative metabolism]]. "It was a marketing campaign to sell the idea that not only are those fats essential, but they are good for you and will prevent heart disease," he told listeners on a [[2011-11-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-energy-production-diabetes-and-saturated-fats|2011 *KMUD* episode on energy production and diabetes]]. "But very soon, people started producing evidence showing that linoleic acid not only causes heart disease but promotes cancer and immune problems."

If PUFA is the broad category of fats Peat warned against, linoleic acid is the specific molecule at its center. It is the precursor to [[arachidonic-acid|arachidonic acid]], the substrate for prostaglandin synthesis, the fat that displaces thyroid hormone from its carrier proteins, and the compound whose peroxidation products generate the [[age-pigment|age pigment]] lipofuscin. Understanding linoleic acid is understanding the engine of the entire inflammatory cascade that Peat's dietary recommendations were designed to shut down.

## The "Essential Fatty Acid" Myth

The story of linoleic acid's rise begins in 1929, when George and Mildred Burr published a paper claiming that unsaturated fats were essential nutrients. As Peat explained in his newsletter [[unsaturated-fatty-acids-nutritionally-essential-or-toxic|"Unsaturated Fatty Acids: Nutritionally Essential, or Toxic" (2008)]], "In 1929 George and Mildred Burr published a paper claiming that unsaturated fats, and specifically linoleic acid, were essential to prevent a particular disease involving dandruff, dermatitis, slowed growth, sterility, and fatal kidney degeneration."

The Burrs' findings were contradicted by evidence that already existed. Two years before they published, German researchers had demonstrated that a fat-free diet practically eliminated spontaneous cancers in rats. "I have always wondered whether George and Mildred Burr were aware of that study in 1929," Peat wrote. "Since 1927, others have demonstrated that the polyunsaturated fats are essential for the development of cancer (and some other degenerative diseases), but the Burrs' failed to even mention the issue at any time during their careers."

The definitive refutation came from Roger Williams' laboratory at the University of Texas. As Peat recounted on a [[2011-07-04-its-rainmaking-time-it-s-rainmaking-time-life-supporting-substances|2011 *It's Rainmaking Time* episode]], "Roger Williams' lab recognized the Burrs' rat disease as a Vitamin B6 deficiency. Vitamin B6 wasn't known at the time the Burrs were doing their study. Williams' lab gave the rats the exact same diet the Burrs used, created the deficiency symptoms, and then cured it with Vitamin B6 without adding any fats at all." The supposed essential nutrient deficiency was a misidentified vitamin deficiency.

The Burrs' other claims collapsed just as completely. "The claim that polyunsaturated fatty acid deficiency caused sterility of male animals was quickly dropped," Peat wrote, "probably because an excess of polyunsaturated fats was discovered to be an important cause of testicular degeneration and sterility." In practical terms, Peat argued that linoleic acid deficiency is essentially impossible outside of a laboratory. In his newsletter [[suitable-fats-unsuitable-fats-issues-in-nutrition|"Suitable Fats, Unsuitable Fats" (2007)]], he noted: "It has been shown that it is probably impossible to experience a detectable deficiency of linoleic acid outside of the laboratory setting."

## How the Seed Oil Industry Sold the Lie

The Burrs' questionable science would have remained an obscure footnote if not for the seed oil industry. For twenty years, most biologists ignored the Burrs' claims because the evidence against them was so strong. What changed was not the science but the economics.

"Nothing really assured the support of their position until the seed oil industry wanted to market their liquid seed oil," Peat explained on KMUD. "Cottonseed oil, linseed oil, soybean oil, and so on. They brought the Burrs out of obscurity. They said, 'Since they have proven that linoleic and linolenic acid are essential for life, we'll get the public to eat them in huge quantities and treat them as drugs rather than as trace nutrients.'"

By the 1970s, the evidence against linoleic acid had become too strong to ignore. Rather than abandon the essential fatty acid concept, the industry pivoted. As Peat described in his newsletter [[fats-and-degeneration|"Fats and Degeneration" (2009)]]: "In the 1980s the oil promoters were becoming more sophisticated, and were publishing many experiments in which the [[fish-oil|fish oils]] were compared with corn oil, or safflower, or soy oil." The omega-3 fats became the new "good" essential fatty acids. "Since they had sold the public on the idea of essential fatty acids, they just changed the story," Peat told listeners. "They said, 'Well, fish oils or linseed oil are a *different* kind of fat. They aren't the Omega-6 oils like the deadly linoleic acid; they're the Omega-3.' But those had already been incriminated with yellow fat disease and lipofuscin."

## Arachidonic Acid and the Prostaglandin Cascade

Linoleic acid's direct toxicity is only part of its danger. Inside the body, it is converted to [[arachidonic-acid|arachidonic acid]], a 20-carbon omega-6 fat that Peat considered "one of the most toxic stored fats." As he wrote in "Fats and Degeneration," "Arachidonic acid is made from linoleic acid, and so those two oils were considered as roughly equivalent in their ability to meet our nutritional needs." The concept of "balancing" omega-3 against omega-6 misses the point: the conversion to arachidonic acid happens inside the body regardless of what else you eat. The only way to reduce arachidonic acid's effects is to reduce its precursor.

Arachidonic acid, in turn, is the direct substrate for the [[prostaglandins]], the hormone-like signaling molecules that mediate pain, inflammation, immune suppression, and tumor growth. As Peat wrote in his newsletter [[aspirin-brain-and-cancer|"Aspirin, Brain, and Cancer" (2006)]]: "In 1970, when I was beginning to see the ways in which unopposed estrogen and accumulated polyunsaturated fats interacted with a vitamin E deficiency during aging and in infertility, I got some prostaglandins to experiment with, since they are products of the oxidation of linoleic acid."

The body's alternative to this inflammatory cascade is the [[mead-acid]] system. In a [[#64： Life ｜ Energy ｜ Estrogen ｜ Fibrosis ｜ Calcification ｜ Process Theology ｜ Altruism with Ray Peat|*Generative Energy* episode on estrogen and fibrosis]], Peat explained: "An Australian guy named Cleland found a series of prostaglandin-like substances that are made from the Mead acid, which we make if we aren't poisoned by external linoleic acid. He found that they are, without exception, anti-inflammatory and constructive, whereas all of the arachidonic acid derivatives end up being harmful." The Mead acid derivatives limit the inflammation response; the prostaglandins from arachidonic acid amplify it. "As it is with our present environmental foods," Peat said, "the prostaglandins amplify the inflammation, derange the healing process, and make scar tissue, keloids, or cancers."

## Thyroid Suppression

Linoleic acid suppresses [[thyroid]] function at every level of the system. In "Unsaturated Fatty Acids," Peat summarized the research: "A series of studies about 20 years ago showed that the functions of the thyroid hormone are all inhibited by unsaturated fats, with the inhibition increasing in proportion to the number of unsaturations (double bonds) in the fat molecule."

The mechanism is physical displacement. As he explained in his newsletter [[fats-functions-malfunctions|"Fats, Functions and Malfunctions" (2013)]], "Transthyretin (also called prealbumin) is important as a carrier of the thyroid hormone and vitamin A. The unsaturation of vitamin A and of thyroxin allow them to bind firmly with transthyretin and certain other proteins, but the unsaturated fatty acids are able to displace them, with an efficiency that increases with the number of double bonds, from linoleic (with two double bonds) through DHA (with six double bonds)."

On a [[2011-07-04-its-rainmaking-time-it-s-rainmaking-time-life-supporting-substances|2011 *Rainmaking Time* episode]], Peat traced the inhibition through the entire respiratory chain: "When you analyze the mitochondrial oxygen-using enzymes, you see that at this point they start absorbing the unsaturated so-called essential fatty acids. And as they absorb the unsaturated fats such as linoleic acid, their activity declines... at every stage conceivable, the unsaturated fatty acids, linoleic and linolenic, and it's worse in proportion to the number of double bonds."

The Burrs themselves observed this, though they did not understand it. As Peat wrote in "Fats and Degeneration," "Burr put one of his 'essential fatty acid deficient' rats under a bell jar to measure its metabolic rate, and found that the deficient animals were metabolizing 50% faster than rats that were given linoleic and linolenic acids as part of their diet."

## The Stress Hormone Vicious Circle

Linoleic acid creates a self-amplifying stress response. In "Fats, Functions and Malfunctions," Peat described the mechanism: "In good health, especially in children, the stress hormones are produced only in the amount needed, because of negative feedback from the free saturated fatty acids, which inhibit the production of adrenalin and adrenal steroids... But when the fat stores contain mainly PUFA, the free fatty acids in the serum will be mostly linoleic acid and arachidonic acid. These PUFA stimulate the stress hormones, ACTH, [[cortisol]], adrenaline, glucagon, and [[prolactin]], which increase lipolysis, producing more fatty acids in a vicious circle. In the relative absence of PUFA, the stress reaction is self limiting, but under the influence of PUFA, the stress response becomes self-amplifying."

The contrast between saturated and unsaturated fat is direct. As Peat told KMUD listeners: "If you compare stearic acid (butter/beef fat) and linoleic acid (corn oil), the butter turns off adrenaline and cortisol, while the corn oil turns them on."

## Heart Damage and Cancer

Peat presented evidence that linoleic acid is directly toxic to heart cells. The story involves rapeseed oil, whose heart-damaging effects were incorrectly attributed to erucic acid. As Peat explained in a [[2008-07-21-politics-and-science-politics-science-fats|2008 *Politics and Science* episode on fats]]: "Hans Selye gave purified linoleic acid and showed that *that* was the actual toxic agent to the heart, not the so-called special toxic fatty acid. It was the 'essential fatty acid' itself killing heart cells. He added chocolate fat, cocoa butter, to the diet, and that protected the heart."

A 2013 reanalysis of the Sydney Diet Heart Study, published in the British Medical Journal, confirmed what Peat had been saying. As Andrew Murray reported on a [[2013-06-21-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-heart-ii|2013 *KMUD* episode]]: the study "found that substituting dietary linoleic acid in place of saturated fats increased the rates of death from all causes, including coronary heart disease."

The cancer case is equally strong. "The amount of polyunsaturated fatty acids often said to be essential is approximately the amount required to significantly increase the incidence of cancer," Peat wrote in "Unsaturated Fatty Acids." The immune suppression mechanism operates through the thymus: "The weight of the thymus is reduced by PUFA in the diet. Thymus cells tend to be easily killed by a combination of stress and EFA... The effects of PUFA on the thymus were compared to those of radiation by Soviet researchers."

Linoleic acid also serves as a necessary cofactor for alcoholic liver damage. In "Suitable Fats, Unsuitable Fats," Peat cited research showing "animals fed tallow and ethanol had no liver injury, but even 0.7% or 2.5% linoleic acid with ethanol caused fatty liver, necrosis, and inflammation."

## The Estrogen Connection

Linoleic acid and [[estrogen]] form a mutually reinforcing pair. As Peat explained on a [[2020-07-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-july-2020-kmud|2020 *KMUD* episode]]: "Estrogen has many overlaps with polyunsaturated fats; they increase estrogenicity, and estrogen increases their accumulation in the tissues." The specific mechanism: "Estrogen activates the phospholipase which liberates arachidonic acid, and [[progesterone]] inhibits that phospholipase." In a person with high linoleic acid stores and high estrogen, the two substances amplify each other in a cycle: estrogen liberates arachidonic acid from cell membranes; arachidonic acid generates prostaglandins; prostaglandins activate aromatase; aromatase converts androgens to estrogen.

## Glycation: Blaming Sugar for PUFA Damage

One of Peat's most provocative claims was that the "advanced glycation end products" blamed on sugar are actually caused primarily by linoleic acid peroxidation. In "Unsaturated Fatty Acids," he wrote: "The so-called 'advanced glycation end products,' that have been blamed on glucose excess, are mostly derived from the peroxidation of the 'essential fatty acids.' The name, 'glycation,' indicates the addition of sugar groups to proteins, such as occurs in diabetes and old age, but when tested in a controlled experiment, lipid peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids produces the protein damage about 23 times faster than the simple sugars do." This means the tissue damage conventionally attributed to high blood sugar is overwhelmingly caused by PUFA breakdown products.

## The Four-Year Tissue Replacement

One of the most practically important facts about linoleic acid is how long it takes to clear from the body. As Peat wrote in his newsletter [[oils-in-context|"Oils in Context" (2007)]]: "The half-life of fats in human adipose tissue is about 600 days, meaning that significant amounts of previously consumed oils will still be present up to four years after they have been removed from the diet."

The timeline is not purely passive. On a [[2011-07-04-its-rainmaking-time-it-s-rainmaking-time-life-supporting-substances|*Rainmaking Time* episode]], Peat described how the process can be accelerated: "If you, for example, take half an ounce of coconut oil, you get a burst of thyroid-like activity, and your cells respire more intensely for about an hour until that fat is burned up. But after about 2 years of a changed diet, you've burned up roughly half of your stored inhibiting unsaturated fats, and your metabolism stabilizes at a much higher level."

The strategy involves frequent eating to avoid triggering stress hormones that liberate stored PUFA. "Frequent feeding, always with sugar and absolutely no PUFA, allows the slow disposition of the unsaturated toxic fats," Peat said on KMUD. "The liver treats PUFA like other toxins; if it has the energy, it detoxifies them for excretion." In his email correspondence, Peat outlined the specific supports for this transition: "[[Niacinamide]] helps to lower free fatty acids... A small amount of [[coconut-oil|coconut oil]] with each meal will increase the ability to oxidize fat, by momentarily stopping the antithyroid effect of the PUFA. [[Aspirin]] is another thing that reduces the stress-related increase of free fatty acids, stimulating metabolism. Taking a thyroid supplement is reasonable until the ratio of saturated fats to PUFA is about 2 to 1."

## Animals Without Linoleic Acid

Some of the most striking evidence involved animals raised on diets completely free of linoleic acid. In "Unsaturated Fatty Acids," Peat listed their characteristics: their metabolic rate is very high; their nutritional needs are increased; they are very resistant to many common causes of sickness and death; and they are resistant to the biochemical and cellular changes seen in aging, dementia, autoimmunity, and the main types of inflammation.

The endotoxin resistance is dramatic: "Shock, caused by the injection of endotoxin, which is 100% lethal to normally fed animals, is only 24% lethal to the deficient animals." The deficient animals also showed reduced inflammation, protection against colitis and nephritis, and larger bones with thicker cortex. In the Burrs' own human experiment, Peat noted, "a person on a diet with absolutely no measurable level of linoleic, linolenic, or arachidonic acid saw their chronic health problems, such as migraines and hypertension, disappear after about four months."

## Coronavirus and ACE2

In 2020, a pre-publication study implicated linoleic acid in the mechanism by which the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein enters cells. Peat discussed this on a 2020 KMUD episode: "It actually should be a revolutionary paper because it shows that linoleic acid is essential for forming the receptor binding site of the spike protein. The spike protein is the thing that makes this a more dangerous form of coronavirus than the common cold. If you can't form the crucial thing that makes it infective without linoleic acid, that has all kinds of ramifications."

He connected the finding to the broader suppression of linoleic acid research: "Since those studies have been essentially suppressed because they hurt oil sales, it is assumed that everyone has to have linoleic acid on the surface of their cells. Since the doctrine says it has to be there, they won't be able to accept that it is the cause of much of the infection of coronavirus." Animal experiments, he pointed out, had already shown "that a diet free of the essential fats prevented diabetes, cancer, inflammation from trauma, and sickness from poisoning. Resistance was vastly higher if they had been totally deprived of the unsaturated fats."

## Cell Structure and Excitotoxicity

Linoleic acid's effects extend to the physical structure of cells. In "Fats, Functions and Malfunctions," Peat cited research showing that linoleic acid disrupts the cytoskeleton more severely than colchicine, a drug specifically used to interfere with cell division: "According to Hoover, et al., it disturbed the structure of the cytoskeleton more than colchicine does; it caused the cell filaments to clump together, while saturated fatty acids didn't have such an effect."

The excitotoxicity data is equally notable. Peat reported in the same newsletter that among the polyunsaturated fatty acids tested for their ability to kill thymocytes (immune cells from the thymus) by increasing intracellular calcium, "the toxicity of DHA is greater than that of EPA, whose toxicity is greater than alpha-linolenic acid, and linoleic acid was the most potent." Linoleic acid being more cytotoxic than the more highly unsaturated fish oils in this particular assay underscores its unique danger as the most abundant PUFA in the modern diet. "Excitotoxicity is probably an important factor in Alzheimer's disease," Peat added.

Linoleic acid also damages the retina through the same oxidative mechanisms. As Peat described on a [[2009-09-01-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-the-ten-most-toxic-things-in-our-food|2009 *KMUD* episode on toxic foods]]: "They found that linoleic acid in particular, but all of the polyunsaturated fatty acids, created an inflammatory process that basically accelerates the degeneration of the retina. A new article showed that stearic acid or saturated fatty acids don't do that."

## Modern Meat and Hidden Sources

Linoleic acid is not only a problem in bottled seed oils. Industrial feeding practices have changed the composition of animal fats. As Peat wrote in "Suitable Fats, Unsuitable Fats": "Hogs fed soybeans can have fat containing over 30% linoleic acid. Considering that most of our food animals are fed large amounts of grains and soybeans, it isn't accurate to speak of their fats as 'animal fats.'" Pork and chicken, even without added cooking oil, can be significant sources of linoleic acid because the animals were fed soy and corn.

He also offered a memorable scale comparison. Applying Linus Pauling's reasoning about vitamin C to linoleic acid: "If we followed Linus Pauling's reasoning in determining optimal vitamin C intake, this study of the linoleic acid content of the tissues of an animal which can synthesize it would suggest that we are eating about 100 times more 'EFA' than we should."

## Aging: The Progressive Accumulation

Linoleic acid's role in aging operates through its progressive, self-reinforcing accumulation in tissues. Fat cells preferentially burn saturated fat for their own maintenance, leaving unsaturated fat to accumulate, so the ratio of PUFA to saturated fat in stored tissue worsens with every decade. As Peat described in "Fats and Degeneration": "As the fat tissues become more burdened with arachidonic acid, they release it more easily in response to moderately lipolytic stress signals. This could explain the increased levels of free fatty acids and lipid peroxidation that occur with aging."

The age-related change is specific to the energy-producing organelles: "The phospholipids of mitochondria and microsomes become more unsaturated with aging." This means the mitochondria become progressively poisoned by the linoleic acid derivatives accumulating in their own membranes. As Peat told KMUD listeners: "In recent years, people are seeing that the level of free fatty acids, which in our population means mostly unsaturated fats, has an extremely close connection to your likelihood of dying from just about anything: shock, aging, cancer, heart disease, and infection."

In an email exchange, Peat connected the aging of tissues to the fetal state where PUFA is absent: "Before birth, injuries heal without inflammation and don't leave a scar. Two important causes for that difference are the high concentration of CO2, which limits lactic acid production, and the absence of the n-3 and n-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids."

## Conjugated Linoleic Acid: The Protective Isomer

One notable exception in Peat's treatment of linoleic acid is conjugated linoleic acid (CLA), a naturally occurring isomer found in dairy fat and ruminant meat. In "Fats and Degeneration," he explained: "When meat is grilled at a high temperature, the normally spaced double bonds in PUFA migrate towards each other, becoming more stable, so that linoleic acid is turned into 'conjugated linoleic acid.' This analog of the 'essential' linoleic acid competes against the linoleic acid in tissues, and protects against cancer, atherosclerosis, inflammation and other effects of the normal PUFA." CLA is protective precisely because it interferes with normal linoleic acid's actions. In his email correspondence, Peat noted: "They aren't necessary, but the CLA in cream and [[butter]] are probably responsible for some of their good effects."
