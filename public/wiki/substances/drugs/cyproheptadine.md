# Cyproheptadine

Cyproheptadine (brand name Periactin) is the anti-[[serotonin]] drug Ray Peat most frequently recommended—an antihistamine that also blocks serotonin receptors, addressing two of the inflammatory mediators central to Peat's understanding of degenerative disease. While Benadryl and other antihistamines block histamine alone, cyproheptadine's dual action makes it particularly useful for conditions where serotonin excess drives pathology.

As Peat explained in a [[2013-05-17-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-heart-i|2013 episode on heart health]], "The drugs that are most helpful in my experience are [[aspirin]] and antihistamines—Benadryl, for example, or cyproheptadine, which is an anti-serotonin antihistamine."

## The Serotonin Problem

In Peat's framework, serotonin is not the "feel-good" neurotransmitter of pharmaceutical marketing but a stress mediator that becomes chronically elevated in aging and disease. Excess serotonin promotes [[inflammation]], intestinal problems, pulmonary issues, and the general stress state that accelerates degeneration.

Cyproheptadine's ability to block both serotonin and histamine addresses the common pattern where these inflammatory mediators rise together—as they do under [[estrogen]] influence, since estrogen activates both histamine and serotonin pathways.

## Heart Safety

Unlike some antihistamines that can affect heart rhythm, Peat considered cyproheptadine safe for cardiac patients. In a [[2020-02-17-one-radio-network-one-radio-network-menopause-estrogen-thyroid-coronavirus-and-glaucoma|2020 episode]], he distinguished between antihistamines: "Benadryl is safe if not taken in excess. Ma Huang (Ephedra) doesn't have the risk of stopping the heart. I think Cyproheptadine, which reduces both histamine and serotonin effects, is safe for the heart."

This matters because people with metabolic problems often have cardiovascular concerns, and cyproheptadine offers anti-inflammatory benefits without the cardiac risks of some alternatives.

## Specific Applications

Peat mentioned cyproheptadine for several conditions:

**Intestinal problems**: In a [[53-what-is-inflammation-the-cia-s-finders-operation-using-progesterone-co2-and-stem-cells|Generative Energy episode]], he noted that "one of the most frequent symptoms is diarrhea. Anti-serotonin agents like cyproheptadine have been helpful."

**Hyperventilation syndrome**: "The hyperventilation syndrome leads to the inability of platelets to transport serotonin, so they leak it, poisoning systems." Cyproheptadine can help by blocking the leaked serotonin's effects.

**General inflammation**: Any condition involving serotonin and histamine excess may respond to cyproheptadine's dual blocking action.

## The Hormonal Context

Peat's broader advice emphasized that drugs like cyproheptadine work best alongside hormonal optimization. As he explained in a [[2016-03-18-ask-the-herb-doctor-ask-the-herb-doctor-allergy|2016 episode on allergies]], "The real solution is to adjust your [[thyroid]] and [[progesterone]]. Progesterone has a broad variety of antihistamine effects. [[Estrogen]] turns on both the multiplication of mast cells and their tendency to release histamine and serotonin."

Cyproheptadine addresses symptoms while thyroid and progesterone optimization addresses root causes. Both approaches work together in Peat's therapeutic framework.
