# Progest-E

Progest-E is Ray Peat's own formulation of USP [[progesterone]] dissolved in [[vitamin-e|vitamin E]] oil, designed to deliver the most protective hormone in his framework without the pitfalls of synthetic progestins or poorly absorbed pharmaceutical preparations. It's not just a supplement; it's the practical embodiment of Peat's hormonal philosophy—anti-estrogenic, anti-cortisol, anti-inflammatory, and pro-energy—delivered in a form that maximizes bioavailability while adding vitamin E's own protective effects.

## Why Progesterone in Vitamin E

The combination is elegant: progesterone is fat-soluble and notoriously difficult to deliver effectively. Oral micronized progesterone in peanut oil (the pharmaceutical standard) is absorbed, but much of it is rapidly metabolized by the liver before reaching systemic circulation. Synthetic progestins (medroxyprogesterone, norethindrone) avoid this problem but come with their own risks—many have androgenic or estrogenic side effects and lack progesterone's full protective profile.

Peat's solution was to dissolve natural, bioidentical progesterone (USP grade, chemically identical to what the body produces) in vitamin E oil. Vitamin E serves three purposes:

1. **Solvent**: Tocopherol is an excellent lipid solvent that keeps progesterone stable and evenly distributed.

2. **Absorption enhancer**: Vitamin E may improve progesterone's penetration through skin and mucous membranes, particularly when applied topically or sublingually.

3. **Co-protective factor**: Vitamin E itself is anti-estrogenic, protects against [[PUFA]] peroxidation, and supports mitochondrial function. The two substances amplify each other's effects.

This formulation allows for flexible dosing—drops applied to the skin (commonly gums, wrists, or feet), taken sublingually, or swallowed—without the need for capsules or injections.

## Progesterone's Central Role

Progesterone is the cornerstone of Peat's hormonal framework. While mainstream endocrinology views it primarily as a reproductive hormone (maintaining pregnancy, regulating the menstrual cycle), Peat positioned it as a master regulator of cellular metabolism and stress resistance.

Progesterone opposes [[estrogen]] at nearly every level: it reduces water retention, lowers inflammation, stabilizes mood, protects neurons, improves thyroid hormone action, lowers [[cortisol]], and shifts metabolism away from glycolysis toward efficient oxidative phosphorylation. Where estrogen promotes growth, excitation, and breakdown (catabolic stress), progesterone promotes structure, calm, and repair (anabolic resilience).

In Peat's model, estrogen dominance—whether from environmental exposure, obesity, stress, or aging—is a primary driver of degenerative disease. Progesterone is the corrective. It doesn't suppress estrogen production directly; rather, it competes for receptors, modulates estrogen's effects at target tissues, and improves the metabolic state that reduces estrogen synthesis (by lowering [[cortisol]] and improving [[thyroid]] function).

## Dosing and Application Methods

Progest-E comes as a liquid in a dropper bottle, with each drop containing approximately 3 mg of progesterone. Peat did not prescribe fixed dosing schedules; instead, he recommended titrating based on symptoms, context, and individual tolerance.

**Common application methods:**

- **Topical (gums)**: Rubbing 3-10 drops into the gums allows rapid absorption into the bloodstream while bypassing some first-pass liver metabolism. This is Peat's most frequently recommended method.

- **Topical (skin)**: Applying to thin-skinned areas (wrists, inner arms, feet) provides slower, more sustained absorption. Some people rotate sites to avoid local saturation.

- **Sublingual**: Holding drops under the tongue for 1-2 minutes before swallowing.

- **Oral**: Swallowing directly, though this subjects progesterone to more extensive liver metabolism and may be less efficient.

**Typical dosing ranges:**
- For menstruating women: 10-30 mg per day during the luteal phase (after ovulation until menstruation begins)
- For postmenopausal women: 5-20 mg per day, often cyclically or in response to estrogen-related symptoms
- For men: 5-10 mg per day (progesterone is not just a "female hormone"; men produce it naturally and benefit from its anti-estrogenic and neuroprotective effects)
- During pregnancy: Peat considered progesterone safe and beneficial throughout pregnancy, often at higher doses (30-100 mg/day), though this should be done under supervision

The goal is not to reach a specific blood level, but to resolve symptoms: anxiety, insomnia, PMS, water retention, headaches, inflammation, or estrogen-dominance signs.

## Safety and Context

Progest-E is remarkably safe. Unlike synthetic progestins, natural progesterone does not carry cardiovascular risks, and it does not suppress endogenous hormone production in the way that synthetic hormones or testosterone can. Peat frequently noted in correspondence that progesterone is "the body's most important anti-stress hormone" and that toxicity is essentially unheard of at physiological or even supraphysiological doses.

However, context matters. Progesterone works synergistically with adequate [[thyroid]] hormone. In hypothyroid individuals, progesterone alone may not resolve symptoms, and in some cases, it can exacerbate fatigue if metabolic rate is too low. Peat's typical recommendation was to address thyroid function first or concurrently.

Progesterone also requires sufficient [[vitamin-a|vitamin A]] for receptor function and sufficient [[cholesterol]] for synthesis. Deficiencies in these co-factors can limit effectiveness.

## Use During Pregnancy

Peat considered progesterone supplementation during pregnancy not only safe but often essential, particularly for women with a history of miscarriage, preterm labor, or pregnancy complications. Progesterone maintains the integrity of the placenta, prevents premature contractions, supports fetal brain development, and protects against maternal [[cortisol]] elevation.

In his pregnancy-related correspondence, Progest-E appeared alongside [[thyroid]] supplementation, [[vitamin-e|vitamin E]], adequate protein, and other protective measures. He did not view progesterone as a drug with risks to be minimized, but as a hormone often deficient in modern pregnancies due to chronic stress, poor diet, and environmental estrogen exposure.

## Why Not Other Progesterone Products?

Many commercial progesterone creams use synthetic bases, preservatives, or fragrances that can themselves be estrogenic. Others use "wild yam extract," which contains diosgenin (a progesterone precursor) but is not converted to progesterone in the human body—these products are pharmacologically inert.

Pharmaceutical progesterone (Prometrium, for example) is micronized and suspended in peanut oil, which is a [[PUFA]]-rich oil that Peat advised against. It's also typically encapsulated, limiting flexibility in dosing and application.

Progest-E avoids these problems: no synthetic ingredients, no PUFA carrier, no capsules, and the addition of vitamin E as a co-factor rather than a neutral base. It's the cleanest, most bioavailable form of progesterone available outside of compounding pharmacies.

## Broader Implications

Progest-E represents a shift in how hormones are conceptualized. Mainstream medicine treats progesterone as a niche drug for specific indications: luteal support in IVF, prevention of endometrial hyperplasia in women on estrogen replacement, or management of abnormal uterine bleeding. Peat treated it as a fundamental metabolic regulator, relevant to aging, neurodegeneration, cancer prevention, immune function, and stress resilience.

The vitamin E carrier amplifies this reframing. Vitamin E is not just a vehicle; it's a signal that hormones and nutrients work together, that protection is multi-factorial, and that the goal is not hormonal "balance" in some abstract sense, but metabolic resilience—high energy production, low inflammation, efficient repair.
