# Caffeine

Caffeine is one of Ray Peat's recommended metabolic supporters—a substance that increases metabolic rate and can help prevent fatty liver disease when used appropriately. Unlike the conventional view of caffeine as merely a stimulant to be moderated, Peat saw it as a useful tool for maintaining metabolic efficiency, particularly when combined with adequate food and [[sugar]].

As Peat wrote in email exchanges, "Caffeine increases your metabolic rate, so it's important to take it with food, including enough sugar."

## Metabolic Enhancement

Caffeine's primary value lies in its ability to support oxidative [[metabolism]]. By increasing metabolic rate, it helps prevent the accumulation of fat in the liver and supports the cellular energy production that Peat considered fundamental to health.

The mechanism involves caffeine's ability to inhibit phosphodiesterase, which increases cyclic AMP levels and promotes thermogenesis. This increased energy expenditure is protective against the metabolic slowdown that characterizes aging and [[hypothyroidism]].

## Coffee and Cocoa

Peat favored coffee and cocoa as caffeine sources because of their additional nutritional content: "Coffee and cocoa are very good [[magnesium]] sources. Cocoa contains both bromocriptine and caffeine, bromocriptine seems to be more stimulating to the heart than to the brain."

The magnesium content matters because caffeine increases metabolic rate, and higher metabolic rates require adequate mineral support. The traditional practice of drinking coffee with cream and sugar aligns with Peat's emphasis on combining caffeine with nutrients that support the increased metabolic activity.

## Dosing Considerations

Peat addressed dosing in email exchanges. For treating fatty liver, a question about 600mg tablets elicited: "That would be too much caffeine at once, unless it's with a big meal to slow its absorption."

On higher doses, Peat was less restrictive than conventional advice suggests: "As long as it isn't increasing [[free fatty acids]] and glycerol I think it's safe, and I suspect that there might be beneficial effects other than ergogenic at the higher doses."

The concern about free fatty acids relates to [[adrenaline]]—at very high doses, caffeine can trigger stress hormone release that liberates fatty acids from adipose tissue, counteracting its metabolic benefits.

## The Estrogen Question

Some research suggested high caffeine intake increases estradiol in women. Peat acknowledged this research but didn't see it as disqualifying caffeine use when overall metabolic function is supported. The context of diet, thyroid status, and other hormonal factors determines whether caffeine's effects are beneficial or problematic.

## Practical Integration

Caffeine works best as part of Peat's broader metabolic approach:
- Take with food, especially sugar, to prevent [[hypoglycemia]]
- Use coffee or cocoa for magnesium benefits
- Monitor for signs of excessive stress activation (anxiety, elevated heart rate without food)
- Consider [[thyroid]] supplementation if using caffeine to compensate for sluggish metabolism rather than to enhance already adequate function
