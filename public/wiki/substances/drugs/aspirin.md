# Aspirin

## Overview

Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is a derivative of salicylic acid, historically obtained from [[willow bark]] and meadow sweet. Ray Peat considers aspirin one of the most valuable and underutilized therapeutic substances available. He views it as a cornerstone of anti-aging, anti-cancer, and pro-metabolic intervention, with effects that parallel and synergize with [[thyroid]] hormone.

Peat often notes that if aspirin were under patent today, it would be hailed as a breakthrough drug. Instead, its low cost and generic status mean it receives far less attention than patentable pharmaceuticals.

## Peat's View

Ray Peat is strongly pro-aspirin, recommending it frequently for a wide range of conditions. He sees it as fundamentally protective because it addresses what he considers the core problem of aging and disease: the accumulation of [[polyunsaturated fats]] (PUFAs) and the [[inflammation]] they produce.

> "More and more people are recognizing the importance of aspirin and stopping inflammation... as we age we accumulate more and more of the polyunsaturated fats in our tissues and under stress we have those circulating in the blood and that means that they are available more and more chronically to produce the inflammatory [[prostaglandins]] and so aspirin is in a way an anti-aging defense against a lifetime of accumulating polyunsaturated fat."

Peat places aspirin alongside [[progesterone]], [[thyroid]], and [[vitamin E]] as substances that should not be obscured by drug company marketing:

> "I think it's important that we don't allow the drug publicists to obscure the broad importance of substances such as aspirin, vitamin E, progesterone, and thyroid."

He has noted that aspirin would be considered a "super antiviral" if it were patentable:

> "Aspirin, if it were under patent, would be considered a 'super antiviral' because it is active against a wide variety, including influenza, herpes, rhinovirus, and hepatitis."

## Mechanism of Action

Aspirin works through multiple complementary mechanisms:

### Prostaglandin Inhibition
The primary mechanism is blocking the conversion of [[arachidonic acid]] into inflammatory [[prostaglandins]]. Arachidonic acid is a highly unsaturated fatty acid that accumulates from dietary [[linoleic acid]] and becomes increasingly problematic with age and stress.

> "Prostaglandins are probably our biggest inflammatory problem... They're why aspirin is so great."

### Lipase Inhibition
Aspirin acts on multiple lipase enzymes to reduce the liberation of free fatty acids from tissues:

> "In the case of aspirin, it's both indirect and direct actions on at least two different lipase enzymes: phospholipase and the adipose hormone-sensitive lipase that insulin controls."

### Mitochondrial Protection
Aspirin protects [[mitochondria]] through multiple pathways, with effects similar to [[thyroid]] hormone:

> "Aspirin protects against several kinds of toxicity, including excitotoxicity (glutamate), dopamine toxicity, and oxidative free radical toxicity. Since its effects on the mitochondria are similar to those of thyroid (T3), using both of them might improve brain energy production more than just thyroid alone."

> "Caffeine and aspirin have multi-levels of defense for the mitochondria."

### Nitric Oxide Suppression
Aspirin suppresses [[nitric oxide]], which Peat considers "one of our central risky pro-inflammatory mediators that poisons mitochondrial respiration."

### Aromatase Inhibition
Aspirin inhibits [[aromatase]], the enzyme that converts [[testosterone]] to [[estrogen]]:

> "Aspirin is a major MTOR inhibitor and aromatase inhibitor."

### EZ Water Formation
Research by Gerald Pollack found that aspirin is one of the substances with the highest affinity for creating exclusion zone (EZ) water in cells.

## Uses & Benefits

### Cancer Prevention and Treatment
Peat considers aspirin one of the safest first-line interventions for cancer:

> "It's now known that it prevents essentially all kinds of cancer to some degree if used chronically but it's now being used therapeutically backing up other more conventional or toxic treatments."

> "For about 20 years I've been mentioning aspirin as probably the safest first thing that anyone who worries about breast cancer should start. It is a very effective way to turn off estrogen production and response to estrogen."

> "I always recommend aspirin for any cancer. It reduces inflammation, helps to restore the energy level, and has at least a dozen effects that help to control or eliminate cancer."

### Diabetes and Blood Sugar
Aspirin can help lower blood sugar by addressing the underlying metabolic dysfunction:

> "If you focus on stopping the liberation of fatty acids from your tissues and inhibit their conversion to prostaglandins, you can usually very quickly lower your blood sugar quite a bit and feel better. The two chemicals that are most practical for lowering free fatty acids and stopping the stress reaction are [[niacinamide]] and aspirin."

### Antiviral Activity
Aspirin has demonstrated antiviral effects against multiple virus types:

> "Aspirin has been found to have a mild antiviral effect against several types of virus... It has been proven somewhat effective for several different kinds of virus, including the herpes/chickenpox type."

### Iron Toxicity Protection
> "Aspirin is known to be very protective against iron. That was established 30 or 40 years ago. They think it might be acting as an antioxidant and maybe as an iron binder in some way. It is probably one of the most protective things, next to coffee and [[vitamin E]], for preventing the toxicity of iron."

### COVID-19 and Vaccine Injury
Peat included aspirin among protective substances for both COVID-19 infection and vaccine-related inflammation:

> "Anticoagulants and anti-inflammatory things that are successful for treating COVID patients... help for the symptoms of the vaccine damage. Aspirin, cyproheptadine, milk, and Vitamin D."

> "They warn people not to use aspirin before getting vaccinated because aspirin actually defends against the harmful effects of vaccines."

### Bone Health
> "Aspirin improves calcium retention in bones. It isn't as profitable as osteoporosis drugs like bisphosphonates, but it is very safe as long as you take [[Vitamin K]] with it."

### Allergies and Histamine
> "Aspirin is another antihistamine that works indirectly by reducing prostaglandin and nitric oxide production."

### Radiation Protection
Aspirin helps protect against radiation damage:

> "Progesterone and other things that stop the breakdown of polyunsaturated fats will protect to a great extent against ultraviolet and sunburn or against ionizing radiation."

### Sunburn Protection
> "Aspirin is very protective against sunburn."

### Intestinal Function
> "Aspirin, surprisingly, can act as a laxative because it suppresses nitric oxide and defends against [[endotoxin]]."

## Cautions & Interactions

### Vitamin K Requirement
The main caution with aspirin is its effect on blood clotting, which is addressed by ensuring adequate [[Vitamin K]] intake:

> "Especially if a person has combined antibiotics with aspirin, they might have lost the intestinal bacteria that make vitamin K. Eating lots of cooked greens, such as kale, and liver are the best sources. Some types of cheese are also good sources of vitamin K."

Peat and his collaborators recommend taking 1 milligram of Vitamin K2 per standard aspirin tablet (325mg) when using higher doses.

### Tylenol Contrast
Peat strongly distinguishes aspirin from [[acetaminophen]] (Tylenol), which he considers harmful:

> "Tylenol activates MTOR and increases inflammatory degenerative processes. Its antidote is aspirin."

> "Prenatal use of Tylenol is associated with an increased risk of autism."

### Additives in Commercial Aspirin
Commercial aspirin tablets often contain problematic fillers:

> "The fillers definitely aren't good. I mean, if you have access to nothing but the pills, I think you should dissolve them in hot water to let the junk settle out and then just drink the sour water."

Pure aspirin powder can be obtained from chemical supply companies or pet supply sources.

## Dosing

Peat discusses a range of doses depending on the purpose:

### Daily Maintenance
> "If your vitamin K is okay, I think it's fine to take 200 or 300 milligrams every day."

### Anti-inflammatory / Therapeutic
> "Usually, two doses of 500mg provide good systemic protection."

> "Up to several grams a day. Usually, two doses of 500mg provide good systemic protection."

### High-dose Therapeutic
For serious conditions like diabetes or chronic inflammation:

> "I know people who have temporarily taken 6,000 or 7,000 milligrams a day. Like 20 standard aspirin tablets."

### Vitamin K Protocol
When using aspirin beyond low doses:
- 1 milligram (1000 micrograms) of Vitamin K2 per 325mg aspirin tablet
- Baby aspirin (81-90mg) generally does not require supplemental K2

> "For every aspirin you take, you need one drop of Vitamin K2 to keep yourself from bleeding excessively."
