# T3

T3 (triiodothyronine) is the active form of [[thyroid]] hormone—the form that actually enters cells and increases oxidative [[metabolism]]. The inactive form, T4 (thyroxine), must be converted to T3 in peripheral tissues, primarily the liver. This conversion depends on adequate [[vitamin A]], [[selenium]], and liver glycogen reserves.

Ray Peat considered T3 supplementation (as Cytomel or liothyronine) often more effective than T4-only preparations (Synthroid) because many people have impaired T4-to-T3 conversion due to [[PUFA]] exposure, [[estrogen]] dominance, or liver dysfunction.

**For full coverage of thyroid physiology, supplementation, and Peat's framework, see [[thyroid]].**

## Key Distinctions

- **T4 (thyroxine)**: Storage/transport form, requires conversion
- **T3 (triiodothyronine)**: Active form, directly increases cellular respiration
- **Armour/desiccated thyroid**: Contains both T4 and T3 in roughly 4:1 ratio
- **Cytomel/liothyronine**: Pure T3
