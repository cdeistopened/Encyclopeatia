# Reading Sequences

An encyclopedia is a pile of articles by default. Roughly 295 entries sit in this vault, each carrying its own thesis, citations, and counter-arguments. A first-time reader who lands on [[pufa]] without context will find themselves four hops deep — into [[lipid-peroxidation]], [[arachidonic-acid]], [[prostaglandins]], and back — before they have a frame for why any of it matters. The graph is dense by design, but density without a path is the encyclopedic equivalent of a library with no shelves.

Sequences are the editorial layer that turns the pile into a journey. They are not new content; they are curated orderings of existing articles, with brief connective tissue explaining why this article follows that one. Two organizing schemes are maintained in parallel. [[sequences/personas]] indexes paths by **reader archetype** — the woman investigating PMS, the parent of a struggling child, the biohacker arriving via mitochondria. [[sequences/topic-arcs]] indexes paths by **concept cluster** — the PUFA arc, the thyroid arc, the CO₂ arc — for readers who want to trace one idea from first principles to clinical application.

## How to use sequences

Pick the scheme that matches your situation. If you arrived at this vault because of *who you are* (a tired person, a worried parent, a clinician), start with [[sequences/personas]]. If you arrived because of *what you want to learn* (how PUFA works, what Peat actually said about cancer, why CO₂ matters), start with [[sequences/topic-arcs]]. Many readers will use both — the persona path frames the why; the topic arc deepens the how.

A few notes on reading them:

- **Sequences are scaffolds, not prescriptions.** Each step links to a canonical article, and that article links onward into the wider graph. Stop reading the sequence whenever a side-link calls more loudly. Come back later.
- **Order matters but is not enforced.** The orderings reflect a deliberate pedagogy — mechanism before substance, condition before protocol, hub before spoke — but the graph is non-hierarchical underneath. Skip steps you already know.
- **Backtracking is expected.** If step 4 references a concept introduced in step 2 that you skipped, hop back. The encyclopedia is built for lateral movement.
- **The persona paths and topic arcs intersect.** The "chronically fatigued" persona path and the "thyroid arc" share most of their middle. The "cancer patient" path and the "PUFA arc" share their foundation. Pick the entry point that names your situation; the overlaps will surface as you read.

The five short paths in [[README]] are the starter set — they cover the most common entry points in three to five hops each. The sequences below extend that scaffolding: roughly **eight reader-persona paths** and **fourteen topic arcs**, each running ten to twenty steps with a clear destination.

## By reader persona

See [[sequences/personas]] for the full set with rationale and step-by-step orderings. Quick map of the persona archetypes covered:

- **The new arrival** — someone who heard "Ray Peat" on a podcast and wants the framework in 90 minutes
- **The chronically fatigued** — low energy, cold extremities, gut trouble, possibly undiagnosed hypothyroidism
- **The woman with hormonal issues** — PMS, perimenopause, fertility, PCOS, endometriosis, fibroids
- **The cancer patient or caregiver** — looking for the Peat reading of oncology and what the framework says is actionable
- **The parent of an atypical child** — ADHD, autism-spectrum, learning differences, mood instability
- **The biohacker or longevity optimizer** — arriving from the mitochondria/red-light/methylene-blue corner
- **The thyroid person** — already knows it's thyroid; wants the Peat framework for diagnosis and recovery
- **The bioenergetics deep diver** — wants the framework itself, not its applications
- **The Soviet-Peat philosophy reader** — arriving for *Mind and Tissue*, Vernadsky, Ling, the long intellectual lineage

Each persona path opens with a paragraph naming the situation, names the destination (what the reader will be able to do or decide by the end), and runs ten to fifteen steps with one-line transitions.

## By topic arc

See [[sequences/topic-arcs]] for the full set. Quick map of the arcs covered:

- **The PUFA arc** — from chemistry to lipid peroxidation to age-pigment to clinical implications
- **The thyroid arc** — from the hormone to Barnes's diagnostic to the recovery protocol
- **The estrogen arc** — from synthesis to aromatase to estrogen-driven disease to detox
- **The aging arc** — Peat's bioenergetic theory of aging, from mitochondria to cardiolipin to lifespan
- **The CO₂ / respiration arc** — from the Bohr effect to bag breathing to altitude longevity
- **The serotonin arc** — gut-derived serotonin, anti-serotonin tools, the inverted SSRI story
- **The endotoxin / gut arc** — LPS, the gut-thyroid-liver loop, the carrot salad, activated charcoal
- **The cancer arc** — Warburg, the metabolic frame, the Peat oncology brief
- **The diabetes arc** — Randle cycle, fatty-acid oxidation, the inverted "sugar causes diabetes" story
- **The obesity arc** — the energy-availability frame, why low-carb backfires, what Peat recommended
- **The Soviet-Peat philosophy arc** — Vernadsky, biosphere thinking, *Mind and Tissue*, the Ling lineage
- **The regulatory-capture / political-Peat arc** — Peat on the FDA, the medical establishment, the unsaturated-oil industry
- **The metabolic-rate / clinical-measurement arc** — temperature, pulse, the Barnes diagnostic, what to actually measure
- **The brain-energy / neurodegeneration arc** — from mitochondria in neurons to dementia to the Peat brain-energy frame

Each arc opens with a one-paragraph thesis (what the arc is about and why it hangs together), names the entry point, and runs ten to twenty articles in causal-then-clinical order.
