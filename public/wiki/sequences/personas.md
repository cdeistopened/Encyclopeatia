# Reading Sequences — by Reader Persona

The encyclopedia is built to be read laterally, not front-to-back. But most readers arrive carrying a particular problem — a diagnosis, a symptom cluster, a worldview they want stress-tested — and they need a path through the link graph rather than another invitation to wander it. The nine sequences below name the archetypal readers who land at the wiki and stake out an ordered reading list for each. Every page named here exists; every annotation explains the next-step logic. When two sequences overlap (the chronically fatigued reader and the thyroid reader share a long middle stretch, by design), the order and emphasis still differ — fatigue starts at the energy substrate, thyroid starts at the diagnostic.

A sequence is a starting move, not a prescription. The "where to go next" branches at the bottom of each are escape hatches into the deeper graph. Charlie's encouragement: read in order until a page connects, then follow its wiki-links wherever they pull you.

## Sequence 1: The new person discovering Peat

**Who this is for:** Someone who heard about Peat on a podcast, a Reddit thread, or from a friend, and wants the conceptual frame before any food list or supplement protocol. The reader who reasonably asks "what is this man's actual claim?" before deciding whether to drink orange juice for breakfast.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ray-peat]] — the man, the lineage, the arc from forestry to physiology. Start here so the framework has a face.
2. [[bioenergetics]] — the worldview before the diet. Energy creates structure; structure does not generate energy.
3. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the central concept of the entire encyclopedia. Every other page is downstream.
4. [[mitochondria]] — the organelle where the central claim happens.
5. [[carbon-dioxide]] — the diagnostic product of the framework working, and the protective signal Peat returned to most often.
6. [[lactic-acid]] — the marker of the framework failing. Read these two together; they are a single concept seen from opposite sides.
7. [[thyroid]] — the master regulator that turns up or down the rate of the whole system.
8. [[pufa]] — the substance class Peat blamed for most of modern disease. The villain of the framework.
9. [[estrogen]] — the second villain, and the one most readers least expect.
10. [[stress]] — Selye's seven-step cascade, which Peat used as the unifying physiological story.
11. [[core-diet-protocol]] — what the framework asks you to eat, concretely.
12. [[anti-stress-diet]] — the same protocol restated as the foundation under every condition protocol downstream.
13. [[where-peat-evolved]] — the honesty layer. Eleven verified positions Peat changed his mind on. This is where you learn the framework is not dogma.

**Where to go next:**
- For the lineage: [[broda-barnes]] → [[hans-selye]] → [[otto-warburg]] → [[albert-szent-gyorgyi]] → [[gilbert-ling]] — the five clinicians and scientists Peat built on.
- For the counter-arguments: read the bottom section of [[oxidative-metabolism]], [[pufa]], and [[thyroid]] — every concept page ends with mainstream pushback.
- For a problem you actually have: pick a [[conditions/README|condition]] page and follow its protocol link.

## Sequence 2: The chronically fatigued person

**Who this is for:** The person who has tried sleep hygiene, magnesium, an elimination diet, intermittent fasting, cold plunges, and whatever the latest podcast said, and still wakes up tired. Often female, often early-thirties to mid-fifties, often told their labs are "fine." This sequence routes the symptom back to its metabolic source.

**Reading order:**

1. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — what energy production actually is, before you treat its absence.
2. [[metabolic-rate]] — fatigue is a low-metabolic-rate problem more often than a sleep problem.
3. [[thyroid]] — the regulator. Underactive thyroid is the most common upstream cause of unexplained fatigue.
4. [[hypothyroidism]] — the condition. Most readers in this category have it, undiagnosed.
5. [[broda-barnes]] — the mid-century clinician who solved the diagnostic Peat extended. His finding: TSH misses most of the people who are actually hypothyroid.
6. [[temperature-tracking]] — the practical replacement for the TSH lab. You can run this from your kitchen tomorrow morning.
7. [[adrenaline]] — when thyroid is low, adrenaline compensates. Wired-but-tired is the signature.
8. [[cortisol]] — the second compensating hormone. High morning cortisol with low afternoon cortisol is the classic fatigued-but-can't-sleep pattern.
9. [[pufa]] — why the metabolism slowed in the first place. Stored seed-oil from years past is still suppressing thyroid signaling today.
10. [[free-fatty-acids]] — the mechanism by which stored PUFA breaks down under stress and inhibits glucose use.
11. [[randle-cycle]] — the glucose-fat switch. If fat is being burned, sugar is not, and the brain runs poorly.
12. [[hypothyroid-recovery]] — the staged protocol. Don't jump to thyroid hormone first; restore the substrate first.
13. [[energy-restoration]] — the broader protocol layer for rebuilding the metabolism the symptoms point to.
14. [[sleep-restoration]] — the sleep-side intervention, downstream of the metabolic-side.
15. [[morning-routine]] — what the first ninety minutes of the day should look like to anchor the whole pattern.

**Where to go next:**
- If labs look hypothyroid: [[thyroid-dosing]] — Peat's prescribing patterns (Armour vs Cytomel, T3-only, TSH as a trailing indicator).
- If the fatigue clusters with anxiety: [[anxiety-depression]] + [[anxiety-depression-protocol]].
- If postmenopausal: cross-reference [[Sequence 3]] in this file.
- For the dietary foundation: [[carbs-ranked]] + [[fats-ranked]] + [[proteins-ranked]] — the food triage.

## Sequence 3: The woman with hormonal issues

**Who this is for:** Perimenopausal, PCOS, fibroids, endometriosis, PMS, low libido, sleep disruption, mood swings — the cluster of symptoms women in their twenties through fifties carry that mainstream medicine routes to SSRIs, birth control, or "wait and see." This sequence is the long-arc framework Peat built across *From PMS to Menopause* and forty years of newsletters.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ray-peat]] — the biographical hub. *From PMS to Menopause* is Peat's most-cited book; the man behind it matters.
2. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the metabolic substrate every reproductive symptom rides on top of.
3. [[thyroid]] — the master regulator for women, and the one most overlooked by reproductive endocrinology.
4. [[hypothyroidism]] — often undiagnosed in women in this cluster; commonly the first domino.
5. [[estrogen]] — the central villain in Peat's framework for women's chronic symptoms.
6. [[progesterone]] — the antidote, the missing teacher, and the hormone Peat returned to most often in clinical advice.
7. [[allopregnanolone]] — the GABA-A neurosteroid downstream of progesterone. Explains why so many symptoms (anxiety, insomnia, mood instability) track the luteal phase, postpartum window, and perimenopausal transition.
8. [[prolactin]] — the stress-and-estrogen co-villain that complicates the picture, especially after pregnancy and during perimenopause.
9. [[serotonin]] — Peat's contrarian read on the "happy chemical" most relevant to women on SSRIs.
10. [[pms]] — the entry-point condition for younger readers.
11. [[pcos]] — the same hormonal cluster expressed earlier in life.
12. [[endometriosis]] — what high estrogen and low progesterone build inside the body.
13. [[fibroids]] — what the same imbalance builds in the uterus.
14. [[menopause]] — when the cluster typically peaks.
15. [[estrogen-detox]] — the practical intervention layer (foods, fiber, liver support).
16. [[menopause-protocol]] OR [[pcos-protocol]] — the protocol matching the dominant symptom set.
17. [[anti-stress-diet]] — the dietary foundation underneath everything else.

**Where to go next:**
- For pregnancy or trying-to-conceive: [[fertility]] + [[pregnancy-nutrition]] (both-partners protocol).
- For the partner / male side: [[testosterone]] + [[dht]] + [[post-finasteride-syndrome]].
- For deeper hormonal mechanism: [[aromatase]] (estrogen synthesis) + [[xenoestrogens]] (environmental load).
- For the cancer-risk arc that comes with high lifetime estrogen: [[estrogen-and-cancer]] + [[cancer]].
- For action: [[temperature-tracking]] + [[progest-e]] (Peat's preferred topical progesterone).

## Sequence 4: The cancer patient (or family member)

**Who this is for:** The reader who has a diagnosis in hand — theirs or a loved one's — and wants a framework alongside conventional oncology, not in place of it. Peat treated cancer as a metabolic disease in the Warburg lineage. This sequence walks that argument and the adjunct protocols that follow from it.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ray-peat]] — the biographical context. Peat's father died of cancer; the framework was personal.
2. [[otto-warburg]] — the 1931 Nobel laureate whose central finding (cancer cells ferment glucose to lactate even with oxygen present) anchors the whole Peat-on-cancer position.
3. [[warburg-effect]] — the mechanism named after Warburg, restated in Peat's terms.
4. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the healthy state cancer cells have lost.
5. [[mitochondria]] — the organelle whose damage initiates the reversion.
6. [[lactic-acid]] — what cancer cells produce instead of carbon dioxide. The diagnostic.
7. [[carbon-dioxide]] — the protective signal, and the basis for Peat's recommendation of bag-breathing and high-altitude residence in some cancer contexts.
8. [[cancer]] — the condition page; the consolidated Peat-on-cancer position.
9. [[estrogen-and-cancer]] — the estrogen-as-mitogen argument central to breast, ovarian, uterine, and prostate cancer.
10. [[cancer-treatment-iatrogenesis]] — the honest accounting: where conventional treatment helps, where it harms, and what the survival-curve data actually says.
11. [[hardin-jones]] — the Berkeley statistician whose 1956 paper Peat returned to. His finding: untreated cancers often had survival times comparable to treated ones, and the difference was masked by selection effects.
12. [[methylene-blue]] — the electron-shuttling dye Peat treated as a cautious adjunct.
13. [[aspirin]] — Peat's most-recommended adjunct; the anti-prostaglandin layer.
14. [[progesterone]] — the anti-estrogen layer for hormone-sensitive cancers.
15. [[thyroid]] — the rate-of-metabolism layer underneath every adjunct.
16. [[cancer-protocol]] — the integrated framework, designed to sit alongside conventional treatment rather than replace it.
17. [[anti-stress-diet]] — the dietary substrate.

**Where to go next:**
- For the mechanism layer: [[fibrosis]] (the tissue change cancer rides on) + [[dedifferentiation]] (the cell-state shift) + [[foreign-body-carcinogenesis]] (the long-tail carcinogen story Peat extended from Bischoff).
- For the political layer: [[regulatory-capture]] + [[science-as-ideology]] — why the metabolic theory of cancer was buried mid-20th-century.
- For the family member supporting the patient: [[Sequence 7]] in this file (parent of an atypical child) and the practical care-and-feeding patterns transfer.

## Sequence 5: The parent of an atypical child

**Who this is for:** The parent of a child with autism, ADHD, allergies, eczema, asthma, recurrent infections, behavioral difficulties, or a cluster the pediatrician keeps explaining as separate problems. Peat's framework treats this cluster as a single metabolic-and-inflammatory story expressed across organ systems.

**Reading order:**

1. [[bioenergetics]] — the worldview. Children are growing nervous systems; metabolic substrate matters more for them than for almost anyone.
2. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the engine the child's brain runs on.
3. [[thyroid]] — often subclinically low in atypical children, often missed because the pediatric reference ranges are wrong.
4. [[hypothyroidism]] — the upstream condition for many of the symptoms below.
5. [[stress]] — Selye's cascade as it expresses in a small body. Children's stress responses are larger and faster than adults' and the recovery margin is narrower.
6. [[serotonin]] — Peat's contrarian read, especially relevant to the SSRI-for-children question.
7. [[endotoxin]] — the bacterial-toxin layer behind many "behavioral" symptoms. The gut-brain axis read through Peat.
8. [[histamine]] — the substance behind the allergy/eczema/behavior cluster.
9. [[allergies-asthma]] — the inflammatory respiratory expression.
10. [[eczema]] — the inflammatory skin expression.
11. [[adhd]] — the cognitive-and-attention expression.
12. [[autism]] — the developmental expression, treated here as a serotonin / endotoxin / thyroid story.
13. [[child-feeding]] — the practical protocol. What to feed a young child for metabolic support.
14. [[gut-restoration]] — the gut-side intervention; reduce endotoxin load.
15. [[immune-protocol]] — the upstream protocol for recurrent infections.

**Where to go next:**
- For developmental physiology: [[developmental-gradient]] (Peat's reading of Child's gradient theory — how growing tissues organize themselves).
- For the genetic-determinism counter: [[genetic-determinism]] — why Peat rejected the "it's just genetic" explanation for childhood conditions.
- For ingredient-level care: [[carrot-salad]] + [[milk]] + [[orange-juice]] + [[gelatin]] — the four staples for kids in Peat's framework.
- For maternal context (if the mother is also symptomatic): [[Sequence 3]] in this file.

## Sequence 6: The biohacker / longevity optimizer

**Who this is for:** The reader who arrived at Peat through Huberman, Attia, longevity Twitter, or the mitochondria-and-red-light corner of the internet. Already supplementing; already tracking. Wants the contrarian takes — the places Peat would push back on the standard biohacker stack.

**Reading order:**

1. [[mitochondria]] — the organelle. Most longevity readers already think they understand this; the page reframes it.
2. [[cardiolipin]] — the specific lipid Peat tracked as the marker of mitochondrial youth. If you have not heard of this, your longevity reading list has a hole.
3. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the goal state. Most "longevity" interventions either support this or unwittingly subvert it.
4. [[respiration]] — the cellular process, not the breathing pattern.
5. [[cellular-electron-flow]] — Szent-Györgyi's framing of biology as electron flow; the substrate under everything else.
6. [[methylene-blue]] — low-dose, 20–40 μg, not the 15 mg biohacker convention. The dose-response story matters.
7. [[red-light]] — what Peat actually said about red and near-infrared light. Calmer than the marketing, but real.
8. [[carbon-dioxide]] — the protective gas; the bag-breathing argument; the altitude argument.
9. [[bag-breathing]] — the practice that follows from the CO2 argument.
10. [[hormesis]] — read this carefully. It is the cautionary frame. Peat was skeptical of most "good stress" claims; fasting, cold plunges, exercise overdosed for adaptation. The page disagrees with you, and the disagreement is the point.
11. [[cold-plunge-dont-do-it]] — the specific intervention Peat opposed most clearly. Read after [[hormesis]].
12. [[intermittent-fasting]] — the second intervention Peat opposed. Same logic.
13. [[pufa]] — the dietary intervention biohackers most often get wrong (fish oil, omega-3 supplementation).
14. [[fish-oil]] — the specific case. Peat's argument is not the mainstream argument and not the keto argument either.
15. [[saturated-fat]] — the rehabilitation.
16. [[coconut-oil]] — the practical intervention; medium-chain triglycerides without the seed-oil load.
17. [[peatmaxxing-cut]] — the bodybuilding-adjacent protocol if performance is the goal.

**Where to go next:**
- For the deep substrate: [[Sequence 8]] in this file (bioenergetics deep diver).
- For the philosophical substrate: [[Sequence 9]] in this file (the Soviet-Peat reading).
- For the actual data on what works long-term: [[where-peat-evolved]] — the eleven positions Peat changed his mind on across fifty years. A test of intellectual honesty against your own stack.
- For the practical drop-in: [[morning-routine]] — what the first ninety minutes of the day looks like.

## Sequence 7: The thyroid person

**Who this is for:** Diagnosed hypothyroid (Hashimoto's or otherwise), suspected hypothyroid with "normal" labs, on levothyroxine and not feeling well, or curious about thyroid because the symptoms fit. This is the heaviest single-symptom cluster on the wiki and the cleanest place to start clinically.

**Reading order:**

1. [[hypothyroidism]] — the condition Peat treated as the central health issue of modern populations. Start here, not at the hormone.
2. [[broda-barnes]] — the mid-century clinician whose diagnostic Peat extended. His finding: TSH misses most of the people who are actually hypothyroid.
3. [[temperature-tracking]] — the daily measurement protocol. Replaces the scale for most Peat-informed self-monitoring.
4. [[tsh]] — what the lab actually measures, why it can be normal in a hypothyroid person, and why Peat treated it as a trailing indicator at best.
5. [[thyroid]] — the hormone itself. T4, T3, reverse T3, and why the ratio matters.
6. [[hashimotos]] — the autoimmune presentation, treated here as a downstream consequence of long-running hypothyroidism plus PUFA-driven inflammation, not a primary disease.
7. [[adrenaline]] — what compensates when thyroid is low. Explains the racing-heart-but-cold-hands paradox most thyroid patients live with.
8. [[cortisol]] — the second compensation. High morning, low afternoon.
9. [[pufa]] — why the metabolism slowed in the first place. Storage PUFA from years past suppresses thyroid signaling today.
10. [[heart-disease]] — the under-the-radar hop most readers miss. Barnes's central clinical claim: in thirty years of practice, not one of his thyroid-supplemented patients died of heart disease. The intersection of hypothyroid + heart disease is the most underrated cluster in the wiki.
11. [[heart-disease-protocol]] — the protocol that follows from that intersection.
12. [[thyroid-dosing]] — Peat's prescribing patterns. Armour vs Cytomel, T3-only, why split doses matter, why most levothyroxine-only patients still feel bad.
13. [[hypothyroid-recovery]] — the staged protocol. Substrate first (sugar, salt, gelatin, milk). Hormone second.
14. [[liver-restoration]] — the under-recognized leg. The liver is where T4 becomes T3. Fix the liver, sometimes you fix the conversion.
15. [[anti-stress-diet]] — the foundation.

**Where to go next:**
- For the diet side: [[carbs-ranked]] + [[proteins-ranked]] + [[fats-ranked]] + [[vegetables-ranked]] — the food triage.
- For the female-specific overlay: [[Sequence 3]] in this file.
- For the chronic-fatigue overlay: [[Sequence 2]] in this file.
- For complications: [[osteoporosis]] (the bone-density consequence of long-running hypothyroidism) and [[dementia]] (the cognitive consequence).
- For the political layer: [[regulatory-capture]] — why TSH replaced temperature as the diagnostic, despite Barnes's data.

## Sequence 8: The bioenergetics deep diver

**Who this is for:** Graduate-level engagement with the physics and chemistry substrate. The reader who wants to understand Szent-Györgyi on muscle, Ling on the cell, Warburg on respiration, and how Peat synthesized the three into a single framework. This is the longest sequence in the file and assumes a strong stomach for primary mechanism.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ray-peat]] — the biographical hub. Peat's PhD was in biology; the framework is downstream of a specific reading list.
2. [[albert-szent-gyorgyi]] — the Nobel laureate whose work on muscle, ATP, and structured water Peat absorbed completely.
3. [[gilbert-ling]] — the cell physiologist whose association-induction hypothesis replaces the membrane-pump model. The cell as a structured-water-and-protein gel, not a bag of solution.
4. [[otto-warburg]] — the respiration laureate whose 1920s work on mitochondrial poisoning anchors Peat-on-cancer.
5. [[hans-selye]] — the stress physiologist whose seven-step cascade Peat used as the unifying physiological story.
6. [[bioenergetics]] — Peat's term for the synthesis. Energy creates structure; structure does not generate energy.
7. [[cellular-electron-flow]] — Szent-Györgyi's framing reframed for the modern reader. Electron flow is biology.
8. [[mitochondria]] — the organelle where the central claim happens. Read with [[cardiolipin]].
9. [[respiration]] — the cellular process. Not the lungs.
10. [[energy-production]] — the synthesis page; how ATP, CO2, and heat fall out of one process.
11. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the central concept, now legible against its substrate.
12. [[glycolysis]] — the alternative pathway, what cancer cells revert to, what stressed normal cells partly revert to.
13. [[lactic-acid]] — the marker, the signal, and the toxin.
14. [[carbon-dioxide]] — the diagnostic product and the protective signal. Henderson, Bohr, and Yandell-Henderson's work, read through Peat.
15. [[warburg-effect]] — Warburg's specific finding restated.
16. [[randle-cycle]] — the glucose-fat switch at the mitochondrial gate.
17. [[pyruvate-dehydrogenase]] — the specific enzyme complex at the gate. The most technical page in the vault, and worth it.
18. [[lipid-peroxidation]] — the PUFA-driven free-radical cascade.
19. [[mead-acid]] — the omega-9 fatty acid that appears when the body is genuinely essential-fatty-acid-deprived. Peat used its presence in the diet of healthy populations as evidence against the "essential" framing of linoleic acid.
20. [[methylglyoxal]] — the glycation by-product downstream of fructose. The biochemical detail that lets you read Peat-vs-anti-sugar arguments correctly.

**Where to go next:**
- For the Soviet-Peat layer that runs in parallel: [[Sequence 9]] in this file.
- For the political layer: [[regulatory-capture]] + [[science-as-ideology]] — why this lineage was buried.
- For the practical layer once the substrate is legible: [[Sequence 6]] in this file (biohacker), but read against the substrate rather than the marketing.

## Sequence 9: The Soviet-Peat philosophy reader

**Who this is for:** The reader who has noticed that Peat keeps citing names that don't appear in standard physiology textbooks — Ukhtomskii, Anokhin, Bekhterev, Pavlov's actual students, the dominant principle, conditional reflex, group energy. This sequence walks the Russian and Soviet lineage Peat absorbed, which most American readers find only after they've read everything else.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ray-peat]] — the biographical hub. The Russian reading list was not incidental; Peat learned Russian to read these figures directly.
2. [[hans-selye]] — the Austro-Hungarian stress physiologist who carried the Pavlovian inheritance into the West. The bridge figure.
3. [[bekhterev]] — Pavlov's contemporary and rival, the founder of reflexology, and the figure whose group-psychology work Peat returned to.
4. [[ukhtomskii]] — the physiologist who described the *dominant* — the way the nervous system organizes around a current task, suppressing competing inputs. Peat used this to explain attention, learning, and pathology.
5. [[dominant]] — the principle, restated. Why a chronic illness becomes self-sustaining: the dominant pattern stabilizes itself.
6. [[anokhin]] — the developer of the *functional system*, an alternative to behaviorist stimulus-response that Peat treated as closer to how organisms actually work.
7. [[conditional-reflex]] — Pavlov's mechanism, but read in Anokhin's and Ukhtomskii's frame rather than Skinner's.
8. [[orienting-reflex]] — what happens before the conditional reflex. The "what is that?" response. Why novelty matters physiologically.
9. [[inhibition]] — Pavlov's other half of the picture, often missed in American readings. Inhibition is not absence of activity; it is its own physiological state.
10. [[image-and-perception]] — Peat's bridge between Russian psychophysiology and the visual system.
11. [[memory]] — Peat's reading of memory as a tissue-state, not a synapse-state.
12. [[dreaming]] — the dream as oriented physiological work, not random firing.
13. [[consciousness]] — the page that ties it together. Consciousness as the organism's relation to its own energy field.
14. [[effect-of-person]] — the empirical observation Peat returned to: presence changes physiology. The Russian and Soviet psychophysiology made this respectable.
15. [[group-energy]] — Bekhterev's collective-energy thesis carried forward. The reader who finishes here is reading a Peat most American Peat-readers never encounter.

**Where to go next:**
- For the developmental layer: [[developmental-gradient]] (Child's gradient theory, read in the same lineage) + [[regeneration]].
- For the practical-clinical bridge: [[learned-helplessness]] + [[anxiety-depression]] — what the Soviet psychophysiology makes visible in the clinic.
- For the political layer: [[science-as-ideology]] — why this lineage was filtered out of the American textbook tradition.
- For the synthesis: re-read [[bioenergetics]] now that the philosophical substrate is in place. The page reads differently.
