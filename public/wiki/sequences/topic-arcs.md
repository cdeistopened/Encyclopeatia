# Reading Sequences — by Topic Arc

The EncycloPEATia is hypertext, not a book — there is no canonical front-to-back order. But Peat's framework has a shape: certain ideas chain. A reader who picks up the [[pufa]] thread will, within three or four pages, find themselves at [[thyroid]], [[lipid-peroxidation]], and [[serotonin]] whether they meant to or not. The graph wants you to move in particular directions.

This page makes those directions explicit. Each arc below collects 6–14 pages that together cover one concept-cluster the way Peat himself developed it — usually starting at an accessible entry point (a substance you've heard of, a condition you might have) and ending at the most specialized or actionable layer (a mechanism, a protocol, a counter-argument you wouldn't have found alone). The arcs are not exhaustive — they are the spine. Wiki-links inside each article will fork you off into the rest of the encyclopedia.

If you want sequences organized by **who you are** rather than **what you want to learn**, read [[personas]] instead.

## Arc 1: The PUFA arc

**Covers:** Peat's most famous contrarian position — that polyunsaturated fats are the single largest dietary driver of modern chronic disease. The arc moves from the substance class, through the mechanism by which it does damage, into the specific oils to avoid and the condition-pages where PUFA shows up as a root cause, and ends at the protocol for getting them out of the body.

**Reading order:**

1. [[pufa]] — the hub. What polyunsaturated fats are, why Peat treated them as a single class, the seven-decade case against them.
2. [[linoleic-acid]] — the specific omega-6 that does most of the damage. Stored in body fat for years; the half-life is the reason the depletion protocol is multi-year.
3. [[seed-oils]] — the modern industrial source. Soybean, corn, canola, sunflower, safflower, cottonseed — which oils, which decade they took over the food supply, and why olive oil is the partial exception.
4. [[fish-oil]] — Peat's least-popular and most-clarifying disagreement with the mainstream supplement consensus. The omega-3 case re-examined.
5. [[arachidonic-acid]] — the long-chain PUFA the body builds when it can't avoid the precursors. The connection to [[prostaglandins]] and inflammation.
6. [[lipid-peroxidation]] — the mechanism. How PUFA in cell membranes gets oxidized into the aldehydes (4-HNE, MDA) that drive aging, fibrosis, and cancer signaling.
7. [[age-pigment]] — lipofuscin. The visible residue of decades of lipid peroxidation, accumulating in heart, brain, and skin.
8. [[free-fatty-acids]] — what circulates when PUFA-loaded fat tissue releases its stores under stress. The bridge from "what I ate twenty years ago" to "what my blood looks like today."
9. [[saturated-fat]] — the rehabilitated alternative. Why Peat treated coconut, butter, and beef fat as protective rather than dangerous.
10. [[pufa-depletion]] — the protocol. The 2–4-year arc of replacing tissue PUFA with saturated and monounsaturated fats. Adjunct measures (vitamin E, aspirin, low-fat phases) and what to expect along the way.

**Cross-arcs:** [[serotonin arc]] (PUFA → free fatty acids → tryptophan transport → serotonin), [[aging arc]] (PUFA + iron + glucose = the lipofuscin trio), [[endotoxin arc]] (PUFA in the gut increases endotoxin absorption).

## Arc 2: The thyroid arc

**Covers:** The master regulator. Peat treated nearly every chronic complaint as a downstream symptom of suppressed thyroid function, and built his clinical advice around restoring it. This arc moves from the gland and its hormones, through the diagnostic Peat preferred over TSH, into the dosing strategy and the spectrum of conditions that respond to thyroid restoration.

**Reading order:**

1. [[thyroid]] — the hormone hub. T4, T3, and reverse T3; how the body converts and stores them; why Peat dosed in small frequent fractions rather than once-daily.
2. [[hypothyroidism]] — the condition Peat treated as endemic. Prevalence, presentation, why it is so widely missed.
3. [[broda-barnes]] — the mid-century clinician Peat built on. The temperature-and-pulse diagnostic that pre-dates the TSH test by twenty years, and the famous claim about heart disease.
4. [[tsh]] — why the standard lab test is a poor measurement of thyroid sufficiency. The mechanism by which a "normal" TSH coexists with frank hypothyroid physiology.
5. [[metabolic-rate]] — the actual variable of interest. Basal temperature, pulse, oxygen consumption — the measurements that bypass TSH.
6. [[temperature-tracking]] — the daily self-measurement protocol. What to record, when, and what the patterns mean. Replaces the bathroom scale for most Peat-informed self-monitoring.
7. [[hashimotos]] — the autoimmune subset. Why iodine restriction was Peat's first move and selenium his second.
8. [[hypothyroid-recovery]] — the staged protocol. From food-only (sugar, salt, calcium, no PUFA) through pregnenolone and finally to T3/T4 dosing.
9. [[thyroid-dosing]] — the actual prescribing detail. Cynoplus, Cytomel, Armour; ratios; timing relative to meals; how to dose around stress, illness, and travel.
10. [[heart-disease-protocol]] — the under-the-radar payoff. Peat's heart-disease frame is essentially thyroid-restoration plus PUFA-depletion plus inflammation-control.

**Cross-arcs:** [[metabolic-measurement arc]] (Barnes lineage continues there), [[estrogen arc]] (estrogen suppresses thyroid; thyroid clears estrogen — a closed loop), [[aging arc]] (the metabolic-rate decline is a thyroid decline).

## Arc 3: The estrogen arc

**Covers:** The central villain in Peat's frame of women's health and a quieter presence in men's. The arc moves from the hormone class, through the conditions Peat traced to estrogen dominance, into the protective hormones (progesterone, pregnenolone, DHEA), the mechanism (aromatase), and finally the protocol for detoxifying the system.

**Reading order:**

1. [[estrogen]] — the hub. The three estrogens (E1/E2/E3), where they come from, why Peat treated unopposed estrogen as a stress signal rather than a youth signal.
2. [[xenoestrogens]] — exogenous mimics. Plastics, pesticides, soy isoflavones, atrazine — the case for treating environmental estrogen as a metabolic burden, not a curiosity.
3. [[aromatase]] — the enzyme that converts androgens to estrogens. Why fat tissue is itself an estrogen-producing organ, and why a heavy man can have higher estradiol than a lean woman.
4. [[progesterone]] — the counter-hormone. Peat's most-recommended supplement; the case for high doses in vitamin-E oil; the relationship to anxiety, sleep, and breast tissue.
5. [[allopregnanolone]] — the GABA-A neurosteroid downstream of progesterone. Why so many women's symptoms — anxiety, insomnia, mood instability — track the luteal phase, postpartum window, and perimenopause.
6. [[pregnenolone]] — the mother steroid. The molecule progesterone, cortisol, DHEA, and testosterone are all built from.
7. [[dhea]] — the adrenal androgen. Use cases, dose, why Peat preferred it to testosterone for men over fifty.
8. [[pms]] — the canonical estrogen-dominance condition, and the title concept of Peat's most-cited book.
9. [[menopause]] — what Peat thought the menopausal transition actually was: not estrogen loss, but estrogen-progesterone ratio collapse.
10. [[fibroids]] — the structural readout of years of estrogen excess. Why diet and progesterone shrink them.
11. [[endometriosis]] — the tissue-migration consequence of estrogen-driven proliferation outside the uterus.
12. [[estrogen-and-cancer]] — the link Peat returned to most often, especially for breast and uterine cancer.
13. [[estrogen-detox]] — the protocol. Activated charcoal, raw carrot salad, fiber, liver support, progesterone replacement.

**Cross-arcs:** [[thyroid arc]] (the closed loop with estrogen), [[serotonin arc]] (estrogen raises serotonin; progesterone lowers it), [[cancer arc]] (estrogen as the most-modifiable carcinogen).

## Arc 4: The aging arc

**Covers:** Peat's longest conceptual thread. Aging as a metabolic phenomenon — declining oxidative metabolism, accumulating lipid peroxidation, falling protective hormones, rising stress hormones — rather than a programmed clock. This arc traces the deepest version of "the Peat thesis."

**Reading order:**

1. [[aging]] — the hub concept. Aging as the gradual reversion from oxidative metabolism to glycolysis, plus the accumulation of damage products.
2. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the central claim of the encyclopedia. Sugar → pyruvate → acetyl-CoA → CO2 + ATP, run cleanly in the mitochondrion. Aging is the failure of this pathway.
3. [[mitochondria]] — the organelle where the failure happens. Membrane integrity, cardiolipin, electron transport efficiency.
4. [[cardiolipin]] — the specific lipid Peat tracked as the marker of mitochondrial youth. Why its PUFA content determines mitochondrial lifespan.
5. [[respiration]] — the cellular process. Why the Bohr effect (CO2 → oxygen release at the tissue) is downstream of metabolic rate, not just lung function.
6. [[cellular-electron-flow]] — the electron-transport-chain detail. Where each step can fail, and what the failure looks like clinically.
7. [[energy-production]] — the integrated picture. ATP synthesis as the central variable; everything else as upstream or downstream.
8. [[lipid-peroxidation]] — the dominant damage process in mammalian aging tissue. Why Peat treated "antioxidant" as a downstream concept and metabolic rate as the upstream one.
9. [[age-pigment]] — lipofuscin. The visible accumulation; where it shows up first (heart, neurons, skin).
10. [[iron]] — the under-appreciated co-conspirator. Iron accumulates linearly with age, drives lipid peroxidation, and explains much of the male/female longevity gap.
11. [[regeneration]] — what the cell does when energy is sufficient. Why Peat thought tissue could be rebuilt at almost any age if metabolism was restored.
12. [[dedifferentiation]] — the failure mode. Cells losing tissue identity under chronic stress — the same mechanism in aging skin, fibrotic liver, and cancer initiation.
13. [[gilbert-ling]] — the structured-water frame. Why the cytoplasm is not a saline solution and why mitochondrial membrane integrity is not a metaphor.

**Cross-arcs:** [[CO2 arc]] (the Bohr effect at tissue level), [[cancer arc]] (cancer as advanced metabolic aging in one tissue), [[brain-energy arc]] (the brain ages first and loudest).

## Arc 5: The CO2 / respiration arc

**Covers:** Peat's most under-appreciated thread — the rehabilitation of carbon dioxide from "waste gas" to "essential metabolic signal." This arc explains why Peat told people to breathe into paper bags, why he thought altitude (within reason) was protective, and why the Bohr effect is more than textbook trivia.

**Reading order:**

1. [[carbon-dioxide]] — the substance. Not a waste product but a regulator: vasodilator, Bohr-effect agent, oxidative-stress damper, cell-protector.
2. [[respiration]] — the bridge concept. Cellular respiration ends in CO2 by design; the gas is the proof that mitochondria are working.
3. [[oxygen]] — the partner. Why oxygen delivery to tissue depends on CO2 concentration in the blood (Bohr effect).
4. [[bag-breathing]] — the practice. Re-breathing exhaled CO2 to restore systemic levels in people who chronically over-breathe.
5. [[altitude]] — the natural experiment. Why moderate altitude raises CO2 (paradoxically — by acclimating ventilation downward) and is associated with lower rates of several diseases.
6. [[lactic-acid]] — the alternative end-product. When oxidative metabolism fails, the cell ends in lactate instead of CO2 — the entire Warburg-shift in one line.
7. [[warburg-effect]] — the classical formulation. Otto Warburg's observation that cancer cells run on glycolysis even with oxygen present.
8. [[otto-warburg]] — the lineage figure. Peat treated Warburg as one of his three or four foundational influences.
9. [[cellular-electron-flow]] — the mechanism by which respiration succeeds or fails at the molecular level.
10. [[mitochondria]] — where CO2 is made, where it should be made, and why mitochondrial dysfunction reads in the blood as falling CO2.

**Cross-arcs:** [[aging arc]] (CO2 falls with age), [[cancer arc]] (Warburg shift is the clinical readout), [[brain-energy arc]] (CO2 is a cerebral vasodilator).

## Arc 6: The serotonin arc

**Covers:** Peat's most contrarian framing of a "happiness chemical." The arc moves from his redefinition of serotonin as a stress hormone, through its rise in inflammation, depression, and migraine, to the substances and protocols that lower it.

**Reading order:**

1. [[serotonin]] — the hub. Why Peat treated serotonin as an emergency stress signal, not the happiness chemical of pharmacology marketing.
2. [[tryptophan]] — the precursor. Why limiting dietary tryptophan was an explicit Peat strategy (gelatin's complementary amino-acid profile is the workaround).
3. [[gelatin]] — the practical lever. Glycine-rich, tryptophan-poor; the only common food Peat consistently endorsed as serotonin-suppressing.
4. [[glycine]] — the active amino acid in gelatin. Why it pushes the body toward calm in a way distinct from GABA.
5. [[free-fatty-acids]] — the upstream bridge. PUFA-derived free fatty acids displace tryptophan from albumin and let it cross the blood-brain barrier.
6. [[cyproheptadine]] — the serotonin antagonist drug Peat returned to most often. Antihistamine that also blocks 5-HT receptors; used for appetite, sleep, and recovery.
7. [[anxiety-depression]] — the condition page. Peat's reframing of depression as a hypometabolic + high-serotonin state, not a serotonin-deficiency state.
8. [[migraine-prevention]] — the cleanest clinical demonstration. Migraines as serotonin-storm events; how dietary tryptophan, gelatin, and cyproheptadine intersect.
9. [[anxiety-depression-protocol]] — the action layer. Thyroid + progesterone + gelatin + light + sometimes cyproheptadine.
10. [[learned-helplessness]] — the behavioral readout. The Seligman experiments reinterpreted through serotonin physiology.

**Cross-arcs:** [[endotoxin arc]] (gut endotoxin → serotonin spike via TPH1), [[estrogen arc]] (estrogen and serotonin co-rise), [[brain-energy arc]] (serotonin opposes dopamine and depresses neuronal metabolism).

## Arc 7: The endotoxin / gut arc

**Covers:** Bowel toxins as the metabolic background load. Peat treated lipopolysaccharide (endotoxin) from the gut as the silent driver of inflammation in most modern people — chronic, low-grade, and almost never measured.

**Reading order:**

1. [[endotoxin]] — the hub. LPS, what it is, how much of it is absorbed daily in a typical Western diet, what it does once absorbed.
2. [[serotonin]] — the first responder. Gut serotonin (>90% of body total) spikes in response to endotoxin and is the bridge to many of its downstream effects.
3. [[histamine]] — the second responder. Mast-cell activation as the inflammation amplifier.
4. [[prostaglandins]] — the lipid signals. PGE2 in particular as the cytokine of low-grade gut inflammation.
5. [[inflammation]] — the integrated state. Why "chronic inflammation" is so vague clinically but precise when traced upstream to endotoxin and PUFA.
6. [[fatty-liver]] — the visible consequence. Endotoxin + fructose + PUFA + alcohol → hepatic steatosis on a predictable curve.
7. [[carrot-salad]] — the iconic Peat practice. Raw carrot's insoluble fibers bind and excrete bacterial endotoxin and estrogen; one of his shortest, most-cited recommendations.
8. [[activated-charcoal]] — the acute lever. Episodic use for bacterial overgrowth, food poisoning, or after antibiotics.
9. [[cascara-sagrada]] — Peat's preferred laxative. The case for transit speed as a metabolic intervention.
10. [[constipation-protocol]] — the practical action layer. Why transit time is one of the most-modifiable health variables most people ignore.
11. [[endotoxin-reduction]] — the broader protocol. Antibiotics in specific contexts; coconut oil; raw carrot daily; the dietary fiber question (resistant starch yes, FODMAPs no).
12. [[gut-restoration]] — the longer arc. Re-building gut function after years of damage.

**Cross-arcs:** [[serotonin arc]] (the gut–brain axis runs on it), [[thyroid arc]] (endotoxin suppresses T4→T3 conversion), [[cancer arc]] (endotoxin is a primary tumor-promoter in liver and colon cancer).

## Arc 8: The cancer mega-brief arc

**Covers:** Peat's cancer frame as a four-page package. Cancer as advanced metabolic failure rather than genetic accident; estrogen as the most-modifiable carcinogen; iatrogenesis (chemo, radiation, mammography) as a major part of cancer's modern course; and the protocol that follows from the frame.

**Reading order:**

1. [[cancer]] — the hub. The Warburg-shift framing; lactate as the tumor's metabolic signature; why Peat treated cancer as one disease of metabolism rather than hundreds of tissue-specific diseases.
2. [[warburg-effect]] — the founding mechanism. Glycolytic shift in cancer cells; why it predicts which interventions help.
3. [[otto-warburg]] — the lineage figure. The 1931 Nobel work and what Peat thought medicine got wrong about it afterwards.
4. [[hardin-jones]] — the inconvenient statistician. The 1969 demonstration that survival curves for treated and untreated cancer patients, matched for stage and grade, are indistinguishable.
5. [[estrogen-and-cancer]] — the most-modifiable driver. Estrogen as a primary mitogen in breast, uterine, ovarian, and (controversially) prostate cancer.
6. [[cancer-treatment-iatrogenesis]] — the harm-side accounting. Chemo, radiation, mammography, and the screening cascades.
7. [[lactic-acid]] — the metabolic signature in plain language. Why elevated blood lactate is the cancer-relevant lab even before imaging.
8. [[dca]] — dichloroacetate. The pyruvate-dehydrogenase activator Peat discussed as a metabolic-reversion drug.
9. [[methylene-blue]] — the electron-transport-chain rescuer. Low-dose methylene blue as a Warburg-shift reverser.
10. [[aspirin]] — the everyday anti-cancer drug. The COX-inhibition + ATP-shift mechanisms; the prevention data; Peat's preferred form.
11. [[vitamin-k]] — the under-appreciated cancer adjunct. K2 in particular; the apoptosis-restoring mechanism.
12. [[cancer-protocol]] — the integrated action layer.

**Cross-arcs:** [[CO2 arc]] (Warburg shift = CO2 → lactate), [[estrogen arc]] (the most-modifiable carcinogen), [[aging arc]] (cancer = advanced metabolic aging in one tissue).

## Arc 9: The diabetes mega-brief arc

**Covers:** Peat's diabetes framing as a four-page package. Diabetes as a fatty-acid-driven failure of glucose oxidation rather than a sugar-driven failure of insulin signaling — the opposite of the standard narrative — plus the resulting protocol.

**Reading order:**

1. [[diabetes]] — the hub. Type 2 as PUFA-driven mitochondrial dysfunction rather than carbohydrate excess. The clinical implications when the polarity flips.
2. [[randle-cycle]] — the central mechanism. Fatty acids inhibit glucose oxidation, glucose inhibits fatty acid oxidation; the cycle that the standard model leaves out.
3. [[insulin]] — the hormone. Why Peat treated chronic high insulin as the stressor rather than the rescuer.
4. [[insulin-resistance-and-metabolic-syndrome]] — the spectrum. Where Peat agreed with the mainstream and where he diverged.
5. [[free-fatty-acids]] — the smoking gun. The substrate competition that locks the Randle cycle into glycolysis-shift.
6. [[blood-sugar]] — the symptom layer. Why hypoglycemia is the clinical face of metabolic failure as often as hyperglycemia.
7. [[glucose]] — the rehabilitated nutrient. Glucose as the cleanest fuel for oxidative metabolism; the case against carb-restriction as a long-term strategy.
8. [[fructose]] — the special-case sugar. Where Peat agreed it could harm (fatty liver under PUFA conditions) and where he didn't (whole fruit, honey, sucrose).
9. [[fatty-liver]] — the visible setup. The hepatic-steatosis curve that precedes T2 by years.
10. [[diabetes-complications]] — the downstream tissues. Why neuropathy, retinopathy, and nephropathy are PUFA + iron + glucose + sorbitol problems, not glucose-only problems.
11. [[methylene-blue]] — the metabolic-rescue drug. Low-dose use in diabetic neuropathy and complications.
12. [[diabetes-protocol]] — the action layer. PUFA-out, thyroid-support, daily-temperature targets, the right carbohydrate strategy.

**Cross-arcs:** [[PUFA arc]] (the diabetes story is the PUFA story in one tissue), [[thyroid arc]] (the diabetic body is a hypothyroid body with extra steps), [[obesity arc]] (the same physiology, one decade earlier).

## Arc 10: The obesity mega-brief arc

**Covers:** Peat's obesity framing as a four-page package. Obesity as metabolic blockage rather than caloric excess; the failed history of dietary advice; and the protocol that follows from treating it as the early stage of metabolic syndrome.

**Reading order:**

1. [[obesity]] — the hub. Why Peat treated chronic weight gain as a hypometabolic + estrogen-dominant + PUFA-loaded state, not a willpower failure.
2. [[leptin-resistance-and-set-point]] — the standard model and its limits. Where set-point theory is real and where it is post-hoc.
3. [[iatrogenic-obesity]] — the under-discussed driver. SSRIs, antipsychotics, beta-blockers, statins, hormonal contraceptives — the medications that drive weight gain by themselves.
4. [[insulin]] — the hormone, again. Chronic-high-insulin obesity as a different presentation than insulin-resistant obesity.
5. [[randle-cycle]] — same mechanism as in diabetes; obesity is just an earlier stage on the same curve.
6. [[free-fatty-acids]] — the metabolic stuck-point. Why people accumulate visceral fat and then can't burn it.
7. [[liver]] — the actual fat-loss organ. Most weight-loss is hepatic before it is adipose.
8. [[thyroid]] — the master regulator, again. Obesity in the context of suppressed thyroid is a thyroid problem.
9. [[estrogen]] — the silent driver. Why "stubborn" weight in women is usually estrogen-driven.
10. [[weight-loss-protocol]] — the action layer. The role of fruit, protein, gelatin, salt, and timing.
11. [[peatmaxxing-cut]] — the community-derived intensification. What the online Peat community calls aggressive cuts; what Peat himself would and wouldn't endorse.

**Cross-arcs:** [[diabetes arc]] (same physiology, later stage), [[thyroid arc]] (the master variable), [[estrogen arc]] (the female-specific driver).

## Arc 11: The Soviet-Peat philosophy arc

**Covers:** The Russian neuroscience tradition Peat absorbed in the late 1960s and never let go of — Ukhtomskii, Bekhterev, Anokhin — and the concepts that came out of it: the dominant, the conditional reflex, the orienting reflex, the effect of person, group energy. This is the most-overlooked third of the Peat corpus and the one that turns the framework from physiology into psychology and politics.

**Reading order:**

1. [[ukhtomskii]] — the central figure. Aleksei Ukhtomskii's 1923 concept of the dominant — a self-reinforcing organization of neural activity that selects which stimuli reach consciousness.
2. [[dominant]] — the concept itself. How a dominant focus reorganizes perception, motivation, and physiology around itself. Why it matters for habits, identity, and recovery.
3. [[bekhterev]] — Vladimir Bekhterev. Reflexology as a competitor to Pavlovian conditioning; the suspicious circumstances of his 1927 death.
4. [[conditional-reflex]] — the broader concept. Pavlov re-read through Bekhterev and Ukhtomskii — conditioning as the most fundamental learning mechanism.
5. [[anokhin]] — Pyotr Anokhin. The functional-systems theory; goal-directed action; the anticipatory model in the brain.
6. [[orienting-reflex]] — the unconditioned attention response. What the brain does when something new appears; how the orienting reflex differs from defensive reflexes.
7. [[image-and-perception]] — Peat's bridge from Russian neuroscience to the philosophy of mind. Perception as constructed; image as the unit of cognition.
8. [[effect-of-person]] — the deeply Peat-flavored concept. Why one human being changes the metabolism of another by being in the room. Group experiments, mother-infant pairs, hospital-recovery studies.
9. [[group-energy]] — the larger-scale version. Crowds, congregations, families, classrooms as metabolic environments.
10. [[consciousness]] — the most-philosophical Peat article. Where the encyclopedia gets closest to a metaphysics.
11. [[memory]] — what the Russians and Peat both treated as a tissue property rather than a brain-only property.
12. [[learned-helplessness]] — Seligman's experiments re-read through the dominant. Why learned helplessness is a dominant locked into a maladaptive pattern, and why the metabolic intervention precedes the psychological one.
13. [[inhibition]] — Pavlov's late work. The under-appreciated half of conditioning: the inhibitory dominant.

**Cross-arcs:** [[serotonin arc]] (Russian neurobiology has a different vocabulary for the same phenomena), [[regulatory-capture arc]] (Peat's politics are downstream of the Russian frame — the dominant as a political object), [[brain-energy arc]] (the Russian school is unembarrassed about the brain having a metabolism).

## Arc 12: The regulatory-capture / political-Peat arc

**Covers:** The political backbone of Peat's writing. He saw nutrition science, endocrinology, and medicine as captured fields — industry-shaped at the level of which questions get asked. This arc collects the substantive examples (fluoride, lead, the seed-oil supply, hormonal contraception) and the meta-level concepts (regulatory capture, science as ideology).

**Reading order:**

1. [[regulatory-capture]] — the concept hub. How regulatory agencies come to serve the industries they were created to regulate; the specific mechanism in food, drugs, and chemistry.
2. [[science-as-ideology]] — the deeper claim. Why "the science" is rarely neutral and why a partisan reading of the same evidence (Peat's) is often closer to the underlying physiology than the consensus reading.
3. [[fluoride]] — the canonical example. Fluoridation as captured public-health policy; the thyroid-suppressive mechanism; the goitre-and-IQ data the consensus suppresses.
4. [[lead]] — the slower-burning case. Industrial lead exposure and the decades-long fight to recognize it.
5. [[seed-oils]] — the captured food supply. How polyunsaturated industrial oils displaced animal fats in the American diet during the mid-twentieth century, on the strength of evidence Peat treated as fraudulent.
6. [[pufa]] — the substance class at the core of the captured-nutrition story.
7. [[estrogen]] — the captured-endocrinology story. Synthetic estrogens (DES, ethinyl estradiol, premarin) as a fifty-year iatrogenic episode.
8. [[xenoestrogens]] — the industrial-chemical layer. Plastics, pesticides, surfactants — exposure with no scientific accounting.
9. [[cancer-treatment-iatrogenesis]] — the captured-oncology case. Chemotherapy and mammography as economic systems first and medical ones second.
10. [[iatrogenic-obesity]] — the captured-psychiatry case. SSRIs, antipsychotics, and the obesity tail.
11. [[hardin-jones]] — the punished dissenter. The career consequence of telling the truth about cancer survival statistics.
12. [[adelle-davis]] — the popular-science casualty. Why "the discredited nutrition writer" actually got most of the chemistry right.

**Cross-arcs:** [[PUFA arc]] (the captured-food story), [[cancer arc]] (the captured-medicine story), [[Soviet-Peat arc]] (the dominant as a political-economy concept, not just a neuroscience one).

## Arc 13: The metabolic-rate / clinical-measurement arc

**Covers:** The Broda Barnes lineage. Peat's clinical advice rested on a small set of cheap, repeatable, patient-administered measurements — basal temperature, pulse, the morning weight, the evening urination pattern — that he treated as more diagnostic than any blood panel. This arc is for the reader who wants to know what to actually do tomorrow morning.

**Reading order:**

1. [[broda-barnes]] — the lineage figure. The mid-century clinician whose oral-temperature diagnostic Peat extended. The story of the under-the-arm thermometer and the thirty-year heart-disease practice.
2. [[metabolic-rate]] — the variable of interest. Why "metabolism" is not a metaphor; how it is actually measured.
3. [[temperature-tracking]] — the daily protocol. When to take temperatures, what counts as a high reading, what counts as a low one, how to interpret patterns. The single most-useful clinical practice in the encyclopedia.
4. [[tsh]] — the inferior alternative. Why TSH is a poor measurement of thyroid sufficiency in the modern range; why Barnes and Peat both bypassed it.
5. [[hypothyroidism]] — the condition the temperature-and-pulse protocol diagnoses.
6. [[thyroid-dosing]] — the action layer once the protocol confirms suppression.
7. [[hashimotos]] — the autoimmune subset and how temperature-tracking changes in it.
8. [[oxygen]] — the under-the-hood measurement. Why metabolic-rate work eventually wants a respirometer or an indirect calorimeter, not a thermometer.
9. [[respiration]] — what the measurement is measuring.

**Cross-arcs:** [[thyroid arc]] (the cleanest continuous reading), [[aging arc]] (the temperature curve declines through adult life), [[CO2 arc]] (the higher-resolution measurement of the same variable).

## Arc 14: The brain-energy / neurodegeneration arc

**Covers:** Peat's framework for the aging and damaged brain. Dementia, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, depression, schizophrenia, and autism read as variants of cerebral hypometabolism — falling oxidative metabolism in neurons — rather than as distinct neurochemical diseases. The arc ends at the most-discussed practical intervention (methylene blue) and the broader orthomolecular protocol.

**Reading order:**

1. [[oxidative-metabolism]] — the foundational claim, applied here to neurons specifically.
2. [[mitochondria]] — the brain is the most mitochondrial organ. The first place metabolic decline shows up.
3. [[cellular-electron-flow]] — the molecular detail. Where neurodegeneration breaks the electron transport chain.
4. [[dementia]] — the umbrella condition. Peat's framing of dementias as cerebral-hypometabolic states.
5. [[alzheimers]] — the canonical case. Why Peat treated the amyloid hypothesis as a distraction from the metabolic story.
6. [[parkinsons]] — the dopamine-and-mitochondria axis. Why Parkinson's responds to metabolic interventions long before dopamine replacement.
7. [[dementia-protocol]] — the action layer for dementia generally.
8. [[methylene-blue]] — the most-discussed brain-energy compound. The electron-shuttle action; the dose-response Peat actually endorsed (microgram-range, not the 15 mg biohacker convention).
9. [[methylene-blue-protocol]] — the dosing detail.
10. [[brain-orthomolecular-protocol]] — the broader integrated approach. Thyroid + niacinamide + B-vitamins + methylene blue + aspirin + sugar + light.
11. [[niacinamide]] — the brain-energy vitamin. NAD+ precursor; mitochondrial support; under-discussed in the consumer-supplement market.
12. [[anxiety-depression]] — read here as a cerebral-hypometabolic + high-serotonin state.

## How to use these arcs

Each arc is a starting trajectory, not a syllabus. Read the hub article first; if the next article down the chain answers the question you actually came in with, stop there. The arcs are designed to terminate at the action layer — the protocol, the lab measurement, the substance to add or remove — because Peat's framework is ultimately a clinical one. If you find yourself five pages into an arc and still curious, the wiki-links at the bottom of each page will fork you into the rest of the encyclopedia faster than the arc would.

A small number of pages appear in multiple arcs (notably [[pufa]], [[thyroid]], [[mitochondria]], [[lipid-peroxidation]], [[methylene-blue]], [[free-fatty-acids]]). This is not redundancy — these are the genuine hubs of the graph. Reading them once in the PUFA arc and once again in the aging arc surfaces different facets of the same article. Peat's framework is small in concepts and dense in connections; the arcs make the density legible.
